# M08 — The Missing History

## Scene 1 — An index that remembers

At fifteen, the hero returns from the twin mountain caves with the historian's drawing. The archive is a modest public building behind Wolfveil's fountain, not an imposing palace. Afternoon light falls through high windows onto sloped reading desks. The smell of dry paper and wax contrasts with the cave's wet limestone. Lyra is putting a stack of borrowed maps back into a cabinet when she sees him.

Lyra (with ordinary curiosity): “Did you find what you were looking for?”

Hero (unfolding the drawing): “I found something I shouldn't know.”

Lyra: “The mark?”

Hero: “The scratch underneath it. I remember making that.”

Lyra examines the drawing without pretending to know the answer. She turns it toward the window, noticing a small crest recorded by the historian beside the mark, on the adjacent cave wall, not on the hero's personal carving. This distinction is crucial: the crest is an independent clue, not a magical symbol the hero invented. She recognizes its broad shape from an archive catalog and offers to look for a matching record.

Player choices: ask for historical records, ask about the crest, or explain only the geographical familiarity. If the player withheld the personal claim in M07, Lyra says “The historian sent a sketch” and the hero does not repeat a confession. She searches the same shelf in every branch. No mandatory faction allegiance, Oath Tree pledge, S13 or D26 required.

The catalog is an interactive object on the reading desk, with readable titles, marginal numbers and an accessibility transcript. The player chooses the index entry matching the crest. Incorrect selections produce useful short descriptions, not failure or repeated clicking. Lyra identifies the correct volume after a request for help. The matching manuscript has a torn binding and one missing leaf. Adjacent ink is intact, while a single line at the margin is scraped away. Dust outlines the empty space where a second book once stood. The player can inspect each sign independently. The scene makes removal plausible without implying that every damaged book was deliberately altered.

Lyra: “The index lists a page that isn't here.”

Hero: “Someone took it.”

Lyra (tracing the intact binding): “Someone took something. We don't know who.”

Archivist (approaching quickly, then lowering his voice): “Leave that subject alone.”

Hero: “Why?”

Archivist: “Because the last person who borrowed that volume didn't bring it back. And the people who asked about him did not ask politely.”

The archivist's hands are steady, but he keeps glancing toward the open door. His fear is not proof of guilt. The player can reassure him, ask for the remaining copy by permission, or return later when the reading room is quieter. No intimidation or theft required. In all routes the archivist makes a lawful reading copy available; if the player leaves, the catalog retains the lead and Lyra waits at the fountain or desk depending on schedule.

## Scene 2 — The intact copy

The intact copy is a bound transcript rather than an implausibly identical pristine original. The outer folio is newer; its contents include a dated transcription of the missing leaf. Lyra places the two volumes beside one another, weighting their corners with plain wooden blocks. A three-lobed insignia is drawn above a route instruction. The surviving text identifies an abandoned sanctuary near a split ridge, with a narrow approach along a stone channel. It does not name the hostile goddess or explain the moon seal.

Lyra: “Here. Same heading. The copy calls it a shelter.”

Hero: “The other book calls it a shrine.”

Archivist: “Different translators. Different years.”

Hero: “But the road is the same.”

The player compares three record features: matching heading, matching crest, and route description. Two observations are enough to establish the location; all three unlock an optional journal annotation. A comparison interface highlights corresponding lines on request; no reading speed timer or compulsory fine text recognition. It displays observed facts separately from speculation. Suggested journal: “A leaf is missing from the archive manuscript. A transcript names a sanctuary near the split ridge. The crest resembles the one at the mountain cave.” Do not state the archive was altered by a deity.

When the hero reaches for the transcript, the sound of paper becomes the rustle of a notice pinned to a wooden post. The transition is an authored migration at a safe noncombat checkpoint, not a skill that allows voluntary world-hopping. The age-eighteen world takes control only once its terrain, collision and camera are ready. Reduced-motion mode uses a simple audio crossfade and a short fade to black; no flashing imagery.

## Scene 3 — Directions for the living

Age eighteen. A roadside shelter in the original reality stands beside a split ridge, with rough timber beams and tarpaulins that flap in the wind. The same insignia appears on an evacuation notice. The older hero knows it as a practical direction marker. He has seen other notices like it, and he knows which way people should go, but he has no conscious reason to connect it with a manuscript. Smoke obscures distant hills; the route itself remains well lit by low lamps.

Shelter attendant (straightening the notice): “Don't take the lower path. There's a cart stuck in it.”

Hero: “The sign says the upper road meets the channel.”

Attendant: “Yes. We've got water at the next shelter.”

The player can inspect the notice, walk the marked route to the next safe pull-off, or help the attendant explain the directions to a traveler. No escort obligation or enemy quota. At the pull-off, a mother asks whether the sign will still be there if her brother comes after dark. The older hero secures its loose edge rather than taking the notice away.

Mother: “He won't know which way we've gone.”

Hero: “Leave him a note beside it. Not over the directions.”

Attendant: “I'll keep a lantern here.”

The scene establishes that a symbol can be ordinary administrative information in the original world and mysterious historical evidence in the preserved world. No claim that the two shelters have identical histories or that the original notice was made by the Moon Goddess. The optional helping action adds a local gratitude line but does not change the required notice flag. The hero traces the crest with his eyes, feeling a sudden familiarity with its proportions. A migration returns the player to the archive at the saved desk, not the middle of a crowded NPC path.

## Scene 4 — What is missing matters

Lyra is still comparing the volumes. The hero draws the channel and fork before looking at the illustration in the transcript. He recognizes the route with the same unnerving certainty as in M07, but there is no accidental carving this time; the corroboration is a mundane document. Lyra checks the outline and turns the copy around so he can compare it himself.

Lyra: “You hadn't seen this side of the page.”

Hero: “I saw a sign. People were following it.”

Lyra: “Here?”

Hero: “I don't know.”

Archivist: “The sanctuary is closed. It has been for years.”

Hero: “Then why did someone need to remove its directions?”

The archivist cannot answer. He offers a paper copy of the route and asks that the books remain in the archive. Lyra warns the hero that a missing page can indicate fear, carelessness, censorship or an attempt to protect someone; the physical evidence does not settle motive. Player may promise discretion, ask Lyra to record the discrepancy publicly without names, or keep the copy for private investigation. The public record contains no accusation. Each decision changes a small follow-up line, not the required next objective.

Lyra (quietly): “If you go, tell someone where you're going.”

Hero: “I'll follow the channel.”

Lyra: “And if it isn't there?”

Hero: “Then I'll come back.”

The quest completes when the hero accepts the route copy. M09's sanctuary approach unlocks; no hostile goddess appears until M09, and the older hero does not identify her yet. S13 remains a separate optional binding investigation with its own evidence and completion. S13 completion may give the hero a corroborating remark about the complete insignia, but the archive transcript always provides the required location. Journal records observations and unanswered questions independently; no time travel or reincarnation terminology.
