# S34 — A Wall for Everyone
R58 | Catalog: Remoria fort; persuade guards to admit refugees regardless of faction and repair the damaged gate; age eighteen holds an evacuation gate against invasion. Links M15 and D25. New staging, mechanics, rewards and minor NPC details are proposals. No mandatory D25 completion.

## Scene 1 — The queue outside the wall
Late afternoon, age fifteen. Rain has softened the road outside the fort into a paste of clay and straw. A wagon with a cracked wheel leans against the ditch; people have arranged their luggage beneath its canvas so that the smallest children can stay dry. The hero hears an argument before he sees the gate. A guard repeatedly checks a folded order against the colors sewn into the refugees' clothing. The guard is not enjoying the exclusion: his glove is trembling, and he keeps looking toward the stairs where his captain might appear. Inside, a narrow gate lane is intermittently obstructed by a damaged guide rail.

Guard (trying to sound official): “Orders say only our own.”
Hero (looking past him, lowering his voice): “Look at the children.”
Guard: “I have. I've also got forty people inside and a gate that won't close properly.”
Refugee parent: “We can leave the cart. Just tell us where to stand.”

Trigger on witnessing the argument or interacting with the public gate desk. The player may ask to inspect the mechanism, request the captain, or offer to organize the waiting families. Declining leaves the quest available; do not close the main road or make the protagonist responsible for a timed massacre. The faction restriction is an actual local order, not a universal characterization of Remoria. Keep ambient reactions restrained: no crowd chants or camera forced toward a child's face.

## Scene 2 — What the captain can change
The captain comes down from the rampart with wet papers tucked inside his coat. He initially reads the queue as a security problem: every extra person slows the inspection, and a failed gate could leave everyone exposed. The hero can show the damaged rail, point out unused interior space, or ask the parent to describe what the family needs. Each route provides enough reason to seek a workable solution; optional extra evidence changes delivery rather than creating a hidden persuasion stat gate.

Captain (after listening, turns toward the gate): “Open a second lane.”
Guard: “For everyone?”
Captain: “Check the people, not the colors of their coats. Keep the passage clear.”
Hero, if asked to help: “I'll see what is holding it.”

If the player challenges the order angrily, the captain asks for an actionable proposal; if the player speaks cautiously, he invites the guard to explain the obstruction. Both dialogue paths converge on admission without faction exclusion. The captain retains authority and accountability; the hero does not gain command of the fort. The parent offers to carry light supplies but is not sent into danger. The choice affects whether the guard later thanks the hero personally or simply reports the successful opening.

## Scene 3 — Repair while people wait
At the public maintenance bay, a carpenter shows the hero a distorted wooden guide and a loose brace. The player identifies the obstruction, retrieves a replacement already stored on site and holds the mechanism while the carpenter seats it. Present these as three distinct contextual interactions, not a real-world mechanical construction tutorial. A safe bypass allows one lane to operate while the other is serviced. Optional hostile stragglers appear only on the outer road if the campaign state permits; guards retain responsibility, and the repair can be completed peacefully. A dropped component returns to the bench, not to the bottom of a ravine. The visual test is a slow gate cycle with an empty lane, followed by the admission of refugees. Do not close either lane on an occupied navmesh segment.

Carpenter (wipes his sleeve across his forehead): “Try it now. Nobody under the beam.”
Guard (watching the gate slide): “That'll hold.”
Parent: “Where do we go?”
Guard (steps aside and points inward): “The dry shelter. Someone will meet you there.”

The hero accompanies the first willing family as far as the shelter threshold or reports to the captain. The camera remains behind the hero; show the queue moving and the relief of the guard through animation rather than a compulsory cinematic. The captain records the new local admission order. Completion changes this gate's queue, repair and dialogue states, not the empire's allegiance or the outcome of M15.

## Scene 4 — The gate in the other life
Age eighteen, original reality. The same ridge notch rises beyond a fortified evacuation passage, but the structure is not a copy of the preserved fort. A damaged wall exposes the road to the invasion. The older hero already carries his shield. A living officer directs civilians toward a covered ramp while a defender asks him to protect the opening. Let the player move between two short defensive positions; a readable enemy approach and friendly warning precede each wave. Defending, redirecting civilians through cover, or assisting a friendly defender all contribute to the evacuation objective. Never require optional D25 equipment, and never grant a second shield.

Officer: “We need another minute at the opening.”
Older hero (glances at the people coming through): “Keep them moving. I'll stay here.”
Civilian (hesitates at the threshold): “My papers—”
Officer: “Inside. We'll sort it out when you're safe.”

After the last local group reaches the shelter, the older hero touches the familiar shield strap and briefly recalls the sound of a gate sliding in rain. He checks the road, puzzled by the momentary quiet. The younger hero later notices the pressure of a strap across his hand although he is not holding a shield. Neither explains the connection. This is a genuine original-world experience, presented only during an approved migration opportunity; exact local order relative to M05 is unresolved. Defeat retries the local defense, never overwrites the canonical death. The gate scene is not a second M05 evacuation or proof every civilian survived.
