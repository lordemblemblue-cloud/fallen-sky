## D27 Scene 1 — What the rooftops leave between them

Age fifteen. At the border town's upper market the streets narrow beneath sloped tile roofs. Rain has just stopped, and water drips from three uneven chimneys into separate gutters. A soldier is visible through an open guardroom door below the market terrace, speaking with a guard rather than standing in chains. The guard holds a folded report. The player hears a short fragment of conversation on passing: “Above the roofs. Not on the road.” This organically offers S40 without forcing it. If the player passes by, an independent rooftop survey route remains accessible. A lookout tending a weather vane notices the hero studying the chimneys.

Lookout: “Mind the tiles. Some of them were laid without pins.”

Hero: “Can you see the far street from up here?”

Lookout: “From the landing. Don't walk the ridge just for a view.”

The broad landing offers a voluntary first focus point. Three negative spaces between chimneys and the edge of a parapet align into a thin downward ray, terminating at the corner of a sealed balcony wall. In daylight a pale plaster strip makes the shape legible; in overcast weather the silhouette itself is enough. Do not depend on a reflective surface, exact sun angle or impossible rooftop leap. Player can align from a safe roof walkway or accessible landing via equivalent sightline assist. The hero hears a short whistle motif when the shape settles. Subtitles describe it; no source is shown. The motif is a sensory echo, not a new signal activation.

## Scene 2 — The signal through the smoke

Age eighteen. The corresponding hill overlooks an evacuation lane, but some buildings have collapsed. Three gaps in the broken roofline preserve the geometry of the preserved-world chimneys without duplicating the town's architecture. A signaler stands on a sheltered rooftop platform. Below, people are waiting for a signal before moving across an open street. The hero can approach along a stable stair or interior gallery. The signaler raises a silver-covered lantern above the parapet; its light descends through the gaps, indicating the evacuation route. This is an ordinary rescue signal reflecting light, not an explanation of the Moon Goddess's seal.

Signaler (checking the lane): “Keep the lamp above the broken roof. They need to see it from below.”

Hero: “Who's waiting?”

Signaler: “Families from the west street. Two carriers. The rest are behind the stone.”

Hero: “I'll stay until they move.”

The older hero already owns a small whistle, kept on a strap under his shoulder. He checks its tether, then watches the signaler. If S40 is active, inspect the roof wreckage and the signal station records for verification. If not, the same light is available as a normal authored environmental event. Player aligns the downward ray from the stable parapet. The motif returns, this time from the signaler's ordinary whistle. The older hero feels a sudden cold spot where the tether touches his fingers. The two focus flags persist independently; either order is supported.

## Scene 3 — The balcony nobody can see from the road

Back in the preserved reality, the outline of the sealed wall becomes a recessed passage once both focus views have been recorded. The player sees a shallow arch and an intact floor beyond it. Maintain focus until crossing the threshold or use toggle mode. If focus is released outside, the entrance closes; if inside, retain the interior collision lease and a permanently reachable exit latch. This must never be a disappearing platform. The hidden route passes through a small masonry service room, a narrow lookout corridor with a wide accessible gallery option, and the balcony. A hanging shutter has a loose hinge; dust and a visible sway warn of its sweep. It can be held open with a simple interactable stop, or bypassed along the gallery. No compulsory falling damage or precision timer. The shutter resets when the player leaves or retries before the shortcut opens; bypass never locks.

At a dry shelf beside the balcony door lies a worn signal whistle with a flattened ring at one end. The hero turns it over, finding a nick where its tether rubs. He remembers the pressure of a cord around his fingers. At the same instant the older hero closes his hand around his already-owned whistle. The older inventory remains unchanged. He feels as though someone has just placed it in his palm and looks briefly toward the empty stair. No duplicate reward, no physical item teleport explanation.

Hero (age fifteen, quietly): “That's the light he described.”

If S40 has not been heard, use alternate “That's the light I saw.” Record the catalog line for the verified testimony variant; offer a later journal connection after S40. ## Scene 4 — The light means go

At the far edge of the balcony, a second focus aligns a split ridge, a surviving roof pier and the lower street bend. The view shows the corresponding original evacuation lane. The older protagonist waits until the signaler checks the last family, then uses the whistle to acknowledge their passage. The whistle is not the cause of the light; both are independent parts of the evacuation protocol. If S40 was skipped, use this ordinary signal moment; if active/complete, show the verified details the player has actually established. Never imply that the detained soldier is present in both realities as the same identity.

Signaler: “They're looking up.”

Older hero: “Let them see us.”

Signaler: “Ready?”

Older hero: “Ready.”

The light crosses the roof gap. Below, a child looks up and waves. The younger hero lifts two fingers without realizing why. The vista fades, leaving the ordinary border town below. The player can remain on the balcony, read a small lookout tally or leave via the latch and exterior ramp. The optional tally names no cross-reality identities. Completion records the second vista even if the player leaves and returns; whistle grant occurs once on claim. The lookout resumes ordinary vane maintenance, no forced public discovery announcement.

## S40 Scene 1 — A story the guard cannot put in a report

The soldier in the guardroom is seated at a bench. His coat is folded beside him. The guard explains that he stopped the soldier after he described creatures above the town roofs; a superior interpreted the account as spreading panic. The guard is uncertain, not automatically malicious. The soldier is tired and avoids the street-facing window. The player may ask the guard for permission to speak, speak from the open doorway or request a private interview with a trusted witness. Do not allow a public crowd to gather or treat his confusion as proof of guilt.

Soldier (looking at the folded coat): “I saw things above the roofs.”

Hero: “What color was the light?”

Soldier: “Silver.”

Hero: “Where did you see it?”

Soldier: “There was a broken chimney. And people waiting behind a wall. I don't know what street it was.”

Guard: “He says it happened here. We haven't had a fire on that street.”

Hero: “Then write down what he saw, not where you think it happened.”

The player can ask for consent to record the details, offer to investigate without recording his name, or decline to participate. The soldier may permit only the roof shapes and light color to be shared. The journal stores evidence categories and consent, not a public confession. Declining leaves the quest available and the guard agrees to keep the soldier somewhere safe while reviewing the report. No arbitrary timer or punishment. The soldier is an NPC recovering memory through convergence, not migrating between playable lives. His fear of being disbelieved is the human stake, not a diagnosis or magical possession.

## S40 Scene 2 — Evidence without an accusation

The player investigates the current border town roofline. It is intact, contradicting the literal claim of a recent disaster. A lookout confirms the chimney layout, and an optional street ledger confirms that no fire has been recorded during the relevant period; neither proves the soldier fabricated his account. Inspect the three roof gaps, talk to the lookout, and record the absence of wreckage. If D27 is active its alignment remains independent and can be ignored. The quest requires no secret balcony or whistle. The hero asks the lookout whether the geometry resembles a signal route.

Lookout: “If someone stood on that landing, a light would reach the street below.”

Hero: “And if the roof were broken?”

Lookout: “More of the street would see it.”

Age eighteen, the protagonist visits the corresponding destroyed roofline during an ordinary playable transition. Inspect the broken chimney, a damaged evacuation direction board and the silver signal. The signaler confirms the families waited behind the wall. No recovered personal name or identical face is needed. The player sees evidence that fits the soldier's account; the younger hero can later report that he found matching details, but cannot show the original world's fresh physical objects as if they were transported into his current inventory. Use a journal comparison with clearly distinguished source observations. If the player hasn't reached the original segment, pause the evidence objective at a recoverable state; no forced reality switch. Optional physical roof route is safe and bypassable; ordinary stairs reach evidence.

## S40 Scene 3 — Let him leave without a crowd

Return to the guardroom. Three outcomes: seek safe release with the recorded inconsistencies and a trusted witness; arrange a private witness account while the superior reviews the case; or preserve the soldier's anonymity and provide only environmental corroboration. The guard agrees that the report is not evidence of deliberate panic-making. If testimony is insufficient, the guard asks for one more independent observation rather than failing the quest. The trusted witness can be the lookout or another approved local keeper; no faction reputation requirement. A private escort to the courtyard is optional, not a combat mission.

Guard (putting the report down): “I can write that there was no threat to the town.”

Hero: “And that he didn't threaten anyone.”

Guard: “I can write that too.”

Soldier: “What if I remember something else?”

Hero: “Then tell someone you trust. You don't have to tell everyone.”

If the soldier consents to the private account, the lookout records the roof shape and light color without identifying him publicly. If release is selected, the soldier collects his coat and leaves by the courtyard. If anonymity is selected, the guard records only the verified environmental details and arranges safe lodging rather than indefinite detention. All outcomes preserve his autonomy and create a trusted witness account for M16; optional evidence is never mandatory. Proposed S40 reward ordinary XP and local trust, not a whistle duplicate. Later ambient line from the soldier: “I slept last night.” If D27 is later completed, the catalog line becomes available and journal links the signal without retroactively asserting an identity.
