# M05 — The Silver Path

Revision R25. Catalog: original evacuation road; silver strands, late arrival, original body's death and preservation of life force; younger self absent. M04 precedes this chapter and M06 follows it. Shared life force, actual migrations and different chronologies are mandatory canon. Supporting characters, terrain and interaction timings below are proposed. No new deity, ending, resurrection mechanic or explanation of cosmology is established.

## Scene 1 — A road still open

Age eighteen. The captain's final instruction from M04 is still audible as control returns. The sky is crimson, not because the sun has set, but because unnatural light covers the city. Fine gray ash settles on the hero's sleeves. Between broken rooflines a thin silver strand bends around the river, moving against the prevailing wind. It has the same quality as the light in the woodland clearing, yet he cannot remember how he got from there to here. He knows the current road well enough to recognize a stone bridge before seeing it. His contradictory familiarity should be unsettling, not amnesia about every part of his life.

A woman at the roadside tries to untangle a blanket from a cart. A younger traveler grips its handle. The hero slows when he hears the wood crack. They recognize him if their M04 encounter was witnessed; otherwise use a neutral greeting.

Traveler (breathless): “Is that where we're going?”

Hero (following the strand): “I think so. Stay on this side of the water.”

Woman: “They said the bridge was gone.”

Hero: “Then we'll take the bank.”

Offer to walk with them to the next holding point, ask a retreating guard to accompany them, or point out the marked pedestrian path. Each is valid; do not punish a player for choosing a particular weapon role or arriving without optional equipment. Preserve M04 survivor flags rather than spawning duplicate rescued NPCs. As the family moves out, the camera remains player-controlled. Objective: follow the silver strands toward the city barrier.

## Scene 2 — The river takes the road

The river curves around an inclined slab of pale rock. The left bank has partially collapsed; a service path rises on the right. The strand can be seen through smoke but does not itself form a solid bridge. A suspended cloth marker points toward a walkable path. Players climb a low broken stair and cross a short gap, or use a longer sloping bypass. No D18 Air Recovery or glider requirement. Low rumble and falling dust precede each debris event, with a visible contour usable without audio. Traversal hazards are retryable, not the canonical catastrophe.

Guard (at the bend, waving a cloth): “This side! Keep coming!”

Hero: “Can everyone get through?”

Guard: “As long as the light holds.”

Hero (looking upstream): “Who's holding it?”

Guard: “I don't know. She's been there since the sky opened.”

At the high bank the player sees several families pass into the silver boundary. Their silhouettes resolve into ordinary people on the other side, not ash or disappearing enemies. The Goddess is protecting them by moving them to a preserved reality; from the hero's position the destination is only a briefly visible green horizon and uncertain shapes. Do not imply she is sacrificing the refugees or that they all become spirits. The player can pause to hear a mother calling to her child or continue. Story beats trigger at approach anchors, not a real countdown.

## Scene 3 — There is still one

At the city approach, strands run from the Moon Goddess's outstretched hand to separate passages in the barrier. Her other arm is held close to her body; she is fatigued from maintaining the connection. A distant shock moves through the city and the luminous curtain begins to contract. She turns toward a voice calling from the last checkpoint. Its identity is deliberately withheld.

Voice (distant, urgently): “There is still one.”

Goddess (turning toward the road): “Where?”

Hero (raising his hand): “Wait!”

Goddess (reaching toward him): “Hold on to me.”

The hero runs across the final stable terrace. The strand reaches across the space between them. The world's devastation is a narrative event, not a consequence of taking too long. The route must be completable without advanced movement or combat. At the threshold transition into a short, skippable authored sequence; any ordinary approach can lead into the reaching animation. Never fake a failed quick-time event.

## Scene 4 — The hand that did not let go

A catastrophic pressure wave reaches the threshold before the hero's original body can cross. His knees give way, his hand falls and the sounds of the city recede. The image does not need graphic injury: a dropped weapon, the loss of his breath and a body that no longer responds establish that his original physical life has ended at this instant. His fingers do not complete the grasp. Instead, one silver strand remains connected to him as the Goddess pulls toward the preserved world. The camera settles on his open palm, then cuts to light moving across the river's curve. This is not an ordinary character death or respawn. His life force is preserved, not a repair of the dead body.

Hero (voice fading): “I can't—”

Goddess (with effort, not triumph): “Hold on to me.”

The strand remains visible after his hand has gone still. Fade gradually, without flashing or camera roll. End on a sensation of warmth in the palm rather than an explanatory voice-over. M06 opens on the younger protagonist's hand with a corresponding mark. The younger self does not participate physically in M05; this is not the later hostile-goddess encounter where both selves overlap.

## Narrative truth and player belief

Developer truth: the Goddess saves humans by moving them to an alternate reality. The original eighteen-year-old body perishes at the threshold; the strand preserves his life force, shared with his fifteen-year-old counterpart. Subsequent playable age-eighteen experience genuinely exists in its own chronology and continues independently, with gear and survivor choices retained. It is not a recording, ghost-only mode, dream sequence or ordinary resurrection. The temporal relationship between individual older-life scenes and the catastrophe need not be explained yet. Do not show a second replacement body materializing at the barrier. Other survivors do not migrate between worlds as he does. The hostile goddess's identity and motives remain hidden; no Celestial Sight or optional sacrifice ending here.

Player-facing understanding: he remembers the forest, fighting in a city and reaching toward a woman. At M06 he suspects he dreamed the city, but the silver mark, muscle memory and future discoveries contradict that assumption. The rescue and death should be visible enough to make later revelations coherent; the full mechanics are intentionally withheld. No UI labels revealing shared soul, timeline or reincarnation.
