## D32 — Twelve Pieces of Dawn (S47)

**Age fifteen — Pilgrim caves.** A hillside footpath ends at a shallow limestone alcove. The midday light is warm, but the air inside is cool; drips punctuate the distant calls of birds. Three unrelated mosaic fragments sit at different depths: a curved tile by the floor, a broken radial pattern on the wall, a fragment on the far arch. From a worn stone shelf they form an incomplete circular motif. He traces the nearest tile with his thumb, squints, and steps backward rather than asking the camera to do it for him. “There ought to be more of this,” he mutters, with the impatience of someone trying to remember a word. He has no reason to know the pattern.

**Age eighteen — The survivors’ mural.** The corresponding hillside shelters people from the invasion. Their rough-painted mural uses the same motif, assembled by different hands on a timber screen and stone supports. A child asks him to hold a lamp while an adult repairs the painting. “Keep it there,” the painter says, gently but without looking up. The hero shifts the lamp and sees the same three shapes resolve. His attention wanders; the peaceful sound of dripping water seems to replace the distant thunder for an instant. “I thought I’d seen that somewhere.” He returns the lamp rather than abandoning the people.

**First alignment and exploration.** Both focus states resolve the pilgrim gallery entrance. The younger player enters a sloping chamber with small niches, broken tesserae and a dry cistern. A direct path passes loose stones with audible warnings; a wider side gallery bypasses them and leads to a mosaic repair bench. The relic is his pilgrim notebook, not a new deity revelation. He opens it and runs a finger over an unfinished sketch. “I know these drawings, not their names.” Speak quietly, brows drawn together, then let the player inspect rather than force a cutscene. The older hero already owns his notebook; when the younger touches it, he opens his own at a previously familiar page, momentarily feeling like he has just received it. No duplicate notebook. Keep individual identities of the Twelve unresolved.

**Second focus.** Beyond the bench an upper opening frames a ridge notch, three upright stones and the outline of the mural wall. Focus reveals the corresponding shelter, with the painter and child still working. In the older reality the hero hears an inexplicable echo of the younger self turning a page. He looks down, smiles briefly, and returns to helping. Journal records observed similarity, not dimensional truth.

**Claude blockout:** D32_APPROACH → FOCUS_A (three depths) → ENTRY → JUNCTION → (direct loose-stone route / wider mosaic gallery) → RELIC → FOCUS_B → EXIT. Suggested 2–3 m elevation rise to vista, 2.5 m minimum corridor width subject to avatar scale. Use modular limestone and tiles, warm surface light and cool chamber lighting; older variant retains ridge and arch silhouettes, with temporary timber and fabric. Loose stones telegraph with dust/audio, reset on checkpoint, safe bypass. Provide independent exit from inside. Test each focus independently, both aligned, cancellation, return, notebook single-claim, camera clearance, mobile alignment tolerance. Reward: notebook lore entries, not a mandatory progression gate. S47 optional lead.

## D33 — The Spring’s Reflection (S48)

**Age fifteen — Lunar spring.** Mist lies across a shallow pool at the foot of a cedar slope. The player hears a trickle before seeing the water. Two rocks and a distant crescent-shaped branch reflect into a bowl-like outline from a low bank. He kneels, steadies his hand against a stone, and murmurs, “I know the sound of this water.” His voice is puzzled, not certain. Optional S48 healer encounter provides a clue to the spring but is not required.

**Age eighteen — Field basin.** A medic works at the corresponding spring, drawing water into a dented basin while evacuees shelter nearby. The older hero carries water to the medic, who says, “Not too much at once. Let him breathe.” He sees the basin rim and the same rocks form the counterpart symbol. For an instant he feels cold clear water against his fingers although his own hands are dry. He checks the basin, then continues helping. The player experiences an actual ongoing scene, not a replay.

**First focus / grotto.** The aligned viewpoint reveals a side opening behind the spring. The younger player walks on wet shelves into a clear-water grotto. An optional low tunnel leads to an offering niche; the main route remains above water. A shallow surge channel has visible waterlines and a pulsing sound; wait for low flow or use the dry ledge. At a raised stone shelf he finds the old healing vessel. “I carried water in this,” he says, then shakes his head as if embarrassed by the statement. The older protagonist touches the vessel he already uses, surprised by the familiar weight becoming newly meaningful. “Funny. I could have sworn I'd only just picked this up.” He resumes the task. Reward is the proposed healing-vessel ability modifier, balance TBD; no duplicate equipment.

**Second focus.** Above the grotto a natural window lines up the pool, a cedar fork and the ridge. The younger sees a brief view of the original field infirmary, including the medic and patients. He grips the shelf, concerned rather than triumphant. The older hears the calm splash of the preserved spring and takes a deliberate breath. Neither names the other reality.

**Claude blockout:** D33_APPROACH → FOCUS_A (bank/reflections) → ENTRY → dry shelf / timed shallow surge → RELIC → uphill FOCUS_B → independent EXIT; optional offering niche. Use stable reflective cue geometry readable even on low settings (no expensive reflection dependency), water collision, low-flow bypass, no forced swimming. Shared spring lip and cedar/ridge anchors, different structures and props. Test focus while water moves, color-independent feedback, no drowning softlock, save/retry, claim once. Suggested 3–5 m local vertical range; no final coordinates. S48 optional.

## D34 — The Open Palm (S49)

**Reconciliation:** Retain the prior roadside compassion draft as approach scene. The catalog specifies a deity grotto, five stone fingers from three groups, ration token, pantry cave, and camp-sharing utility; the keepsake is therefore a ration token, not an undefined new relic.

**Age fifteen — Roadside shelter.** Morning light filters through leaning trees. A small animal struggles under a branch beside a collapsed shelter. The protagonist lays down his weapon, lifts the branch, then watches the animal disappear. He studies his open hand, slowly closing it. “Why did that feel so familiar?” An optional traveler mentions offerings in a nearby grotto. The shelter's existence does not prove the same civilization occupied this place in both worlds.

**Age eighteen — Evacuation shelter.** The corresponding ridge overlooks a busy road; cloth covers a timber shelter. The older hero offers a tired traveler part of his ration. “You might need that yourself.” He extends his hand a little farther. “Then I'll find something else.” As the traveler takes the food he feels the pressure of the branch against his palms; he looks into the trees, distracted. The traveler sees nothing. Compassion is the shared lived moment, not a supernatural exposition speech.

**First focus / pantry.** At the grotto approach five finger-like stones resolve from three groups (two upright rocks, two weathered ribs, one distant projection). In the older world hands arranging supplies, shelf ribs and a door jamb complete the matching silhouette. The younger enters a hidden pantry: sleeping alcoves, empty jars, modest offerings and a ration-token shelf. A creaking damaged support warns of a risky shortcut; the storage corridor bypasses it. He pauses over the token and then lifts it. “I remember giving this away.” His voice drops with recognition, not certainty. The older hero touches the ration token he already has and feels the gratitude of first receiving it. Reward: proposed camp-sharing utility; keep S49 optional and no duplicate token.

**Second focus.** An upper exit frames the ridge notch, forked tree and foundation; the shelter is momentarily occupied in the original world. He sees travelers sharing food and making room for strangers. After silence, he whispers, “I hope they're all right.” The older counterpart looks back toward the shelter, unable to explain why he wants to return.

**Claude blockout:** D34_APPROACH → FOCUS_A → ENTRY → JUNCTION → (unstable shortcut / safe storage corridor) → RELIC → FOCUS_B → EXIT. Five fingers must be distinguishable without a forced zoom. Rough stone, shallow storage recesses, reused wood, clear sightlines. Use 2–3 m passages where practical, audible and visual trap cue, resettable collapse that never seals both paths. Independent egress. Validate collision, navmesh, repeated focus/claim, checkpoints and mobile. S49 optional. No mandatory fighting.

## D35 — The Missing Span (S50)

**Age fifteen — A gorge with no bridge.** The protagonist reaches an exposed mountain shoulder after following a stream upstream. Wind lifts grass sideways; pale rock drops into a narrow blue river far below. On his bank are two weathered rope anchors, spaced apart and pointing toward a distant upright pillar. There is no bridge here, not even a convincing ruin of one. He walks to the edge, instinctively reaches for a rope that is not on his belt, then steps back, startled. “There should be a crossing.” Deliver the line with an uncertain laugh that fades into a frown. He cannot explain why he has reached for equipment he has never owned in this life.

**Age eighteen — A crossing under pressure.** The older protagonist stands on the corresponding bank during a storm and the continuing evacuation. A damaged road forces survivors to construct a temporary crossing; timber trestles and rescue ropes occupy the same silhouette as the natural rock features in the younger world. He is already carrying his own climbing rope. An engineer asks, “Can you hold that end while I check the far support?” He braces the line at the anchor and looks toward the pillar. “It's not going to reach if we leave it there. Move the anchor closer.” The player can help move the temporary support or check the alternate route. No mandatory civilian death for puzzle failure. The three elements form the matching straight-span motif; he pauses as he hears the quiet stream instead of the storm. “For a second I thought this place was empty.” The engineer replies, “Not today. Eyes on the line.”

**First focus — A bridge becomes visible.** The younger aligns the two foreground anchors and distant pillar at a voluntary view position on stable ground. The older resolves the supports during a safe pause. When both conditions are met, the younger sees a narrow stable bridge across the ravine, as if a route had been concealed in its shape. Maintain focus until crossing; do not force the camera or spawn lethal collision. Once the player steps onto it, keep the bridge physically stable for that crossing, with visual closure only after safe exit. Offer a lower ledge bypass to the same cavern for accessibility/retry, not an automatic reward. The player crosses with wind audio and subtle rope movement. He reaches a cave mouth beyond the pillar and looks back. “We built a crossing here.” He says it with certainty, then glances at his own hands, confused.

**Cave and relic.** A natural crack slopes to a dry chamber, its terraces resembling the platforms of the older world's construction yard without literal matching buildings. The main route passes a low stone arch to a rope shelf. A side route climbs to a ventilation window and abandoned anchor; a loose scree shortcut is telegraphed by sliding pebbles and can be bypassed. The younger lifts a weathered climbing rope from its storage niche, tests its knot, and makes a familiar movement without instruction. “I know how this goes.” Cut no camera; let the hands demonstrate the technique. In the older world, he pauses and grips the rope already attached to his belt. The material feels new in his hands even though he has used it repeatedly. “Why does this feel like the first time?” He checks the knot and goes on helping. The younger's reward is the proposed grapple traversal unlock at the approved progression tier, with a safe practice anchor; the older does not receive a duplicate.

**Second focus — The route that once led home.** From a high cave exit, the younger aligns a split ridge, the pillar and a river bend. He sees the original bridge and the people crossing it, and a route leading toward the town he remembers. The scene is an overlap with a lived moment, not proof the preserved world once contained that town. He leans against the stone, quietly: “I think I've walked that road before.” In the original world the older looks downriver, briefly sensing the peace of an unoccupied gorge. He exhales, then turns toward the survivors. The player should feel accomplishment mixed with longing rather than tragedy.

**Claude blockout:** D35_APPROACH (stable shoulder, view space) → FOCUS_A → BRIDGE → FAR_LEDGE → ENTRY → JUNCTION → RELIC → PRACTICE_ANCHOR → FOCUS_B → EXIT; optional ventilation window / lower ledge bypass. Suggested 12–18 m span, 1.5–2 m deck width, 8–15 m visible drop (subject to traversal/camera survey); use shared rock silhouettes and watercourse, older temporary timber supports and storm props, younger natural erosion and sparse ancient anchors. Preload bridge collision before reveal, preserve crossing lease until both feet land on safe geometry, no midair despawn. Use wind as atmosphere rather than unpredictable physics; telegraph scree, checkpoint at each bank, provide safe exit and alternate return. Test focus release on approach and midcrossing, migration midquest, mobile controls, grapple progression gating, one reward claim, NPC navigation, low-spec rendering. S50 optional; no main-story gate.
