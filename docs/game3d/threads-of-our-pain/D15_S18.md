# D15 — The Chalk Road / S18 — The Road of Chalk

R18 · Source: verified v1.3 R17 master catalog, D15 and S18. Mandatory catalog beats: cave crossroads; faded chalk arrows aligned into one arrow; fresh original-world evacuation arrows in corresponding tunnel; blocked branch opens to rest station and worn route map; Wayfinder map upgrade; “I wrote those directions.” S18: original mine exit, children imitate faded marks, refugee escort, “Which way?” / “Follow the white marks.” / “We call it the old trail.” All additional dialogue, characters, route mechanics and geometry below are proposed. Neither entry gates the main campaign.

## Scene 1 — The old trail (age fifteen)

Late afternoon light filters through a low cave mouth. The surface road is dry but a thin ribbon of runoff darkens the limestone inside. Three children have placed pebbles in shallow niches and are taking turns following a path between them. An adult who gathers firewood keeps watch from the mouth. Nobody is in immediate danger; the children remain inside a broad, protected daylight chamber rather than wandering an active mine. Their game is about reaching the next white arrow without stepping over the imaginary river. The player hears their argument before seeing them.

CHILD A [crouching beside a pebble]: “That one's a bridge. You can't just jump it.”

CHILD B [arms folded, amused]: “It's a stone.”

CHILD A: “Not when we're playing.”

The hero notices three old white markings scattered across a ruined wall, an angled stone rib and a recess farther back. The marks appear pale against dark calcite; one has a short diagonal stroke beneath it. His eyes settle on that stroke rather than the larger arrow. He reaches toward it and stops before touching the fragile surface.

HERO [a question more than a statement]: “Why's that one crossed out?”

CHILD B [matter-of-fact]: “Means don't go that way.”

HERO: “Who taught you that?”

CHILD B: “We call it the old trail. Everyone knows.”

Player may ask the adult about the markings (local tradition, no reliable author), join the children's game (three short safe movement interactions), or inspect the original marks directly. The child calls the hero back if he approaches the dangerous branch, but does not follow him. Finishing the game is not required for D15. If the player has already completed D07, mention that some walls conceal rooms, without implying that the gloves are necessary here. If S18 is completed first, the hero briefly recognizes the white marks as familiar but cannot explain when he saw them.

## Scene 2 — Which way? (age eighteen, S18 opening)

The corresponding mine exit is crowded with refugees. Dust rises from the stone floor every time a cart rolls across it. A storm outside has made the main path unreliable; a steward is trying to keep the families together without blocking the passage. Fresh chalk marks on the wall have been rubbed out by a cart. One family has turned toward a drainage branch, believing the old mine sign points to the surface. The protagonist carries a folded route map already marked in his own handwriting. The player can hear the dispute from the entry landing and walk away without accepting the quest; the steward keeps the party at a safe holding point.

REFUGEE [holding a child's hand, eyes moving between the passages]: “Which way?”

STEWARD [too quickly]: “The upper road. No, wait. That's the old sign.”

HERO [unfolds his map on a dry ledge]: “Give me a minute. Don't move the cart yet.”

The player scouts two candidate exits from the holding point. The shorter path has a low timber brace, a shallow puddle and a sound of loose stone. The longer path is wide enough for carts but has an unmarked turn where the old mining sign conflicts with the newer exit. A third optional pocket contains a misplaced satchel that belongs to a refugee; returning it changes a later thank-you line, not the main outcome. The player checks each route using visible airflow ribbons, light at the far end and stable floor, then reports to the steward. No forced combat. A stray hostile creature can occupy the short route only if existing region rules allow it; clear it or use the long route, never spawn it on civilians.

STEWARD [examining the proposed detour]: “That bend's wide enough for the cart?”

HERO: “If we go one at a time.”

If choosing the short route, the steward asks anyone carrying bulky loads to take the wide route with a second guide. If choosing the wide route, the hero marks the turn and adds a crossed-out arrow at the unsafe branch. If asking for help, the steward holds the junction while the hero scouts; the player may delegate the rear of the escort. These choices change the sequence and dialogue, not whether the family deserves safety. The player is never required to abandon anyone to finish the quest.

## Scene 3 — Marking the road (S18 escort)

The hero uses his own chalk to draw three navigational marks on accessible wall surfaces. The third mark is placed beside a diagonal seam; he adds a short cross-stroke because the previous direction now leads to unstable ground. The animation should show his wrist stopping briefly at the edge of the cross-stroke, not an elaborate magical gesture. These marks are his work; preserve their uneven proportions in the preserved variant. The player can inspect the map at will and choose a wall from approved marker sockets, with automatic placement available for accessibility. The three target marks used for D15 remain authored at stable anchor positions; extra player markers are local S18 guidance and do not modify the puzzle.

HERO [speaking toward the refugees, not the camera]: “Follow the white marks.”

REFUGEE [checking the crossed arrow]: “Even if the old sign points the other way?”

HERO: “Especially then. That sign's for the mine, not for us.”

At the first junction the lead family stops to check the wall. At the second, the cart carrier pauses to tighten a wheel strap. The player can wait, guide the family forward, or ask the steward to lead while checking behind. No escort timer runs while menus are open or when the party waits on a safe anchor. If the player leaves or migrates, refugees stay at the last completed holding point. If an optional hazard is triggered, they stop behind the safety barrier and the player can reset or redirect the route. Death restores the last checkpoint with civilians safe; do not create deaths solely to punish a missed chalk interaction.

At the exit, the refugee looks back toward the tunnel.

REFUGEE [relieved, almost embarrassed]: “I could have sworn we'd come through that bend already.”

HERO [folds map, tired smile]: “You weren't the only one.”

STEWARD: “Leave the marks. There'll be more coming.”

The player sees the steward place a lantern at the first marker for the next group. This persistent change is meaningful even after the quest ends. S18 completes after a valid route is marked and the party reaches the exit. Suggested rewards: local trust, modest EXP and ordinary chalk supply; quantities and balance TBD. No Wayfinder upgrade from S18 alone. If D15 is discovered but S18 is skipped, the age-eighteen protagonist still has his map and authored marks at a safe observation point during his normal journey; S18 remains an optional later interaction.

## Scene 4 — One arrow, two places (D15 first focus)

In the preserved cave, the player stands on an open shelf near the ruined wall. A near arrow shaft, middle angled rib and far arrowhead become one direction when viewed together. The child's route game points toward a useful viewing area, but does not solve the focus for the player. In the original mine, the same rock rib, bend and three newly drawn evacuation marks form the counterpart. The older self pauses while checking the map, outside active escort movement; focus never blocks a moving crowd. Both alignments can be completed in either order and persist across the protagonist's normal migrations. No forced zoom, forced body rotation, or spontaneous teleport to solve the puzzle.

At the original junction, the older hero hears a child's laughter echo off the wall. He turns toward the empty bend, then checks the map again. He believes he mistook a refugee's voice for someone farther away. In the preserved world the younger hears a cart wheel rattle against stone, despite the quiet chamber. He steps to the side as if making room, realizes there is nothing to pass and looks down at his own boot.

Once both alignments register, the younger sees the blocked branch open into a narrow doorway. The visual obstruction reforms when focus is lost. Collision is stable throughout threshold traversal, and an interior side latch provides independent egress. If the player does not enter, the opening can be revealed again. The children and adult remain outside the restricted branch. They do not migrate or acquire the hero's knowledge.

## Scene 5 — The rest station

Beyond the entrance, the atmosphere changes from bright daylight to cool blue-grey reflected light. A low bench lines the wall, with shallow sleeping niches, a hook for a lamp and a stone basin. The room seems made for people who were waiting to travel farther, but its ancient use is not established by the original world's recent mine activity. A faded strip of markings leads to a junction. The direct path crosses an old counterbalanced stone gate: movement of the exposed pivot produces a scraping sound and dust before the gate swings. A lever on safe ground holds the gate open. The alternate path follows a wide bench gallery and returns behind the gate; no jumping or acquired equipment required. A storage recess contains blank scraps of parchment and a small charcoal drawing of a forked trail, optional inspectable lore only.

The player may take a wrong turn into a shallow dead end with a visible return marker. This is a purposeful navigation lesson, not a maze that punishes memory limitations. The journal records visited junctions and permits manual pins even before Wayfinder. Optional navigation assists show already observed marks, never the hidden relic position automatically. The only mandatory interactions are reaching the map and completing the second focus. Any optional region combat remains outside the rest station so the space feels like a refuge.

At the central bench, an old route map lies under a stone weight. Its ink is faded at the corners. The path includes the same cross-stroke as the wall. The hero places a finger on the crooked bend, moves it to the next mark and then stops. He can trace the sequence without reading the legend.

HERO [voice low, no triumphant music]: “I wrote those directions.”

He turns the paper over, looking for an explanation on the back. There is none. His older counterpart touches the map he already carries; he unfolds it to the same bend and experiences the pride and relief of having drawn a useful route for the first time. His inventory and existing navigation knowledge remain unchanged. The younger receives the catalog Wayfinder map upgrade: proposed function is persistent visited-junction notes and optional directional hints to previously surveyed exits, not automatic revelation of hidden dungeons, enemy positions or story solutions. Exact interface and balance TBD. Claim once; allow inspection again without duplicate reward.

## Scene 6 — The second focus: a road for the next person

A rising passage opens into a natural overlook inside the cliff. The younger aligns the split tunnel bend, a ridge notch seen through a narrow daylight opening and a distinctive curved wall. These anchors frame the corresponding original mine exit. He sees the steward and the last family arriving at the surface. If S18 is incomplete, use a separate ordinary traveler who follows the already authored marks; do not show the player's unfinished escort as completed. If S18 was completed, reuse its recorded route and party state. The older protagonist stands aside as a newcomer pauses at the white arrow.

NEWCOMER [to steward]: “Someone's been through here?”

STEWARD: “Someone made sure we'd know the way.”

The older hero takes one last look at the mark. He hears the children arguing about the imaginary bridge. He almost laughs, then catches himself. The younger hears the cart roll out of the tunnel and the silence after it. He checks the old map again, folds it carefully and leaves the chamber. No mandatory final line; allow the player to hold the view as long as desired and disengage voluntarily. Journal: “The old marks match the directions on the map. I recognized the route before I saw it.” This is observation and confusion, not a solved chronology or proof of reincarnation. Return dialogue with the children is optional: “Your trail goes farther than I thought.” They ask whether there is another bridge; the hero replies, “Maybe.”
