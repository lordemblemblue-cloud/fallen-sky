## D24 — Scene 1: A hand without a banner

Age fifteen. A white canvas shelter stands on a low terrace above Tameri's irrigation road. Dust turns the hems gray; the physician has pinned two conflicting military insignia to the same notice board, not as trophies but so the respective patrols can identify their wounded. Under the awning a soldier grips a cup with both hands, while a patient across the aisle tries to sit up.

Physician (to a nervous assistant): “Put the clean cloth here. Leave the doorway clear.”

Hero: “Where are the supplies?”

Physician: “Under the rear awning. If you're going that way, tell me if the rain got in.”

The player can offer help, ask about the clinic, or walk around to the terrace. If S33 is declined, the assistant carries supplies and the discovery remains available. Three poles at different depths and the edge of a hanging cloth resemble fingers and a palm when viewed from the dry terrace. The younger hero notices that he knows where the medicine should be stored, even though the physician is pointing elsewhere. He presses his thumb into his palm as if opening a familiar case.

Hero (under his breath): “There should be a shelf behind that one.”

Assistant: “Behind the cloth? Nothing but the wall.”

The player can focus from the marked safe standing area without any forced camera zoom. Use optional non-color line hints and a caption as the hand shape closes. The symbol is abstract and does not display a divine identity. First alignment stores the age-fifteen flag, independent of S33 state.

## D24 — Scene 2: Keep the middle clear

Age eighteen. The original geography has a makeshift field hospital on a terrace supported by timber braces. Distant smoke hides the farms. The older hero helps a medic sort fictional game supplies into clearly marked categories. A stretcher arrives; the medic stops talking and directs the carrier to a bed. The older hero knows the arrangement, including a niche used to keep a personal medicine kit dry.

Medic (tired but firm): “Keep the middle clear. We need room to turn.”

Hero: “Do you want the kit here?”

Medic: “The one you carry? Keep it. Put the spare on that shelf.”

Hero: “I'll bring the rest over.”

The distinction is essential: the older hero has his own medicine kit already. It is never confiscated or dropped to justify recovery. The spare kit is a separate prop. Three support poles and the edge of a canvas divider match the preserved-world hand silhouette. The player aligns the original view, from a stable bay away from patients. Both flags reveal the ancient infirmary entrance at fifteen; if either is missing, the other remains stored and the player may return during the next permitted reality visit. No forced migration.

## D24 — Scene 3: The room under the terrace

At fifteen, a wall seam between two stone buttresses becomes a visible door while focus is held. Warm daylight gives way to cool limestone; a strip of moss ends where the old paving begins. The player crosses the threshold into a dry reception alcove. There is no battle in the infirmary itself. A collapsed storage rack can be passed by holding an authored brace interaction until its hinged section locks, or avoided by taking a wide gallery around it. Dust and a wooden creak telegraph the optional moving obstacle; the rack never closes on an occupied passage. A short return lever releases it from inside. Both routes reach the medicine niche.

In the treatment chamber, a stone bench has a shallow recess beside it. The younger hero lifts an intact medicine kit, with a worn handle and the same familiar latch. He opens it by instinct. His older counterpart pauses beside the field hospital shelf and closes his fingers around the kit already on his own belt. The two animation beats match but neither inventory transfers physical items across realities.

Hero (fifteen, surprised rather than triumphant): “I know where they kept the medicine.”

Hero (eighteen, barely audible): “Right. Here.”

The younger protagonist learns Emergency Recovery, a proposed self-recovery ability with a cooldown. Use a clearly labelled game-only healing animation, no real medical procedure. ## S33 — Scene 1: The physician's truce

Two soldiers from rival armies arrive nearly together. One sees the other's insignia and refuses to enter. A companion insists that their own wounded should be treated first. The physician stands in the doorway, hands empty, speaking without raising her voice.

Physician: “Blood doesn't carry a flag.”

Soldier: “You'd help him too?”

Hero: “Where are the supplies?”

Physician: “Rear awning. And I need the aisle clear.”

The player may challenge the soldier's demand, ask the physician to explain the clinic rules, or offer a practical task to both groups. Firm variant: “Nobody draws a weapon in here.” Soldier: “You don't give me orders.” Physician: “I do, while you're under this roof.” Practical variant: “Help me move the supplies and we'll get both beds ready.” The soldier hesitates, then helps. Neutral variant: “Let the physician decide.” Physician: “The person who needs help first gets it first.” No requirement to choose an empire. The physician assesses patients; the player does not make real medical diagnoses.

## S33 — Scene 2: Keep the clinic open

The player witnesses supplies delayed at a road barricade. A driver is unwilling to bring the cart near arguing soldiers. The player chooses an approach: negotiate with the road guards using the clinic's neutral seal; carry a small game supply bundle along a sheltered terrace route while the assistant coordinates the rest; or escort the cart through a wider guarded road. The paths converge at a receiving bay. Optional combat against hostile raiders occurs outside the hospital boundary, telegraphed by guard calls and visible approach; a defensive escort or guarded alternate route remains possible. The physician and patients never become required combat targets. No timer on treatment; the clinic remains staffed on decline or interruption.

Driver: “If they take the cart, I've got nothing left to bring.”

Hero: “Then we'll get you to the awning.”

Guard (if negotiated): “I'll hold the road until the cart clears.”

Assistant (if foot route): “I'll have the other bundles brought round. You've got enough to start.”

After delivery the soldiers watch the assistant move between beds. One hands the other a cup without looking directly at him. This is a modest local truce, not an end to the war.

## S33 — Scene 3: No insignia in the original hospital

At eighteen the protagonist helps a medic receiving people injured by the invasion. The same geography, but no rival empire uniforms are necessary. The player can help clear the aisle, guide a stretcher carrier to the receiving bay or deliver a marked supply crate. These are fictional interactions with no real-world treatment tutorial. The medic handles actual care. If this optional counterpart is visited after the preserved quest, it adds a journal recollection, not a second reward or a dependency for S33. The older protagonist recognizes the labor, not the other reality's soldiers as identical people.

Medic: “He's with the next group. Can you show them where to wait?”

Hero: “Of course.”

## S33 — Scene 4: A place that stays open

Back at the neutral clinic, the physician removes a muddy crate from the doorway. The player may ask whether the soldiers will keep their agreement.

Physician: “I can't make them agree outside. I can make this place stay open.”

Hero: “Then keep the sign up.”

The clinic receives a persistent local trust state. M15 may reference it if completed, but the main quest has fallback evidence. Proposed ordinary XP and local goodwill, no automatic faction-wide alliance or price discount. No duplicated D24 ability. Declining or abandoning leaves the physician and assistants treating patients, and the supply task may resume from the last safe bay. Failed escort resets only the current leg, not recovered supplies or earlier dialogue. No civilian death as a punishment for unrelated exploration.
