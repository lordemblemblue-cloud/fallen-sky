## Scene 1 — The fountain without its merchants

Age fifteen. On returning from Hollow Mountain the hero finds the fountain square quieter than usual. A bread seller folds a striped cloth, leaving two loaves on a bench for the people waiting at the hall. A guard directs a delivery wagon away from the broad east road. The ordinary fountain water sounds unusually loud because the bargaining voices have stopped. The player can walk the square, ask the guard what changed or go directly to Sable Vey. No forced faction selection or cutscene before the player can choose to approach.

Guard (looking toward the hall): “Keep the center clear. There are people coming through.”

Hero: “Where are the merchants going?”

Guard: “Home, if they've got one nearby. Sable's asking for you.”

Sable stands beside a table with three sealed reports and an unused fourth chair. She moves the chair out of the path when the hero arrives. She is tired, not theatrical; her fingers rest on the table edge while she gives the news. If the player has helped the three representatives in optional quests, Sable acknowledges the relevant relationships individually. If not, she introduces each representative and supplies a concise report of the underlying needs. No fictional claim that the player completed skipped quests.

Sable: “Three nations, three stories, one dead envoy.”

Hero: “When did it happen?”

Sable: “On the road between the meeting grounds and the hall. We closed the lane after the witnesses arrived. I want you to listen before anybody decides what they heard.”

Tome (folding his hands, voice strained): “Akitsu will demand answers.”

Henut: “Answers are not vengeance.”

Livilla (looking at the reports, not the other representatives): “Find out who benefits.”

Hero choice — ask about the person: Sable pauses. “He was bringing a draft agreement back to the hall. His family has been told. That's all I can say for them.” No name finalized. Choice — ask who is accused: “Each report points somewhere else. I don't know which claims will survive inspection.” Choice — ask why the hero: “You've been asking questions in places people stopped looking. I need someone who can do that without a uniform deciding the answer.” A faction-affiliated player receives “You can still ask a question your own people don't like.”

## Scene 3 — A road that used to be shared

The investigation takes place on the cordoned trade road, not an active crime scene simulation. The envoy's body has been respectfully removed; a guard stands by an empty bench and offers the verified notice. A surviving porter describes seeing people run toward the hall, but cannot identify the attacker. The party's abandoned travel bag is held at the guard station for return to family; the player inspects a publicly authorized copy of its route permit rather than looting personal effects. A witness points out that the road used to remain open to every trader. Wind moves the trade banners in the same direction, while new barriers divide the crossing.

Porter (holding a cup with both hands): “I heard people shouting. By the time I reached the bend they were already running.”

Hero: “Did you see who started it?”

Porter: “No. I saw who ran.”

Hero: “Then that's what we'll write.”

If pressed for certainty, the porter shakes their head. “I can't give you a face I didn't see.” The hero can apologize or continue, and the record remains unchanged. Inspect the notice, witness statement and authorized permit. The relevant comparison is that the permit's ordinary destination does not match the later redirection report; this is evidence of conflicting records, not proof of who produced them. Use neutral wording such as “account conflicts with record,” never “enemy exposed.” Optional memorial visit after briefing is purely respectful; no loot, no action reward.

## Scene 4 — Three camps, three human needs

The player may visit the camps in any order. At each, the representative's report is corroborated or narrowed by a civilian perspective. The main path requires the three short conversations or their equivalent accessible report station at the hall; all camp visits remain available. Optional objectives remain independent. Existing faction membership is respected, but no allegiance changes or irreversible faction commitments are required.

Akitsu's western camp: cedar shade, blue cloth and a drill lane set apart from the market road. A recruit repairs the strap of a grain sack rather than practicing a weapon. Tome shows a report signed by a courier who never claimed to witness the incident. Hero: “Did the courier say who fired?” Tome: “No. He said what was recovered.” Recruit: “My brother is across that road. I don't want to be told he's my enemy before I've asked him.” Tome closes the folder. “Then ask while we still can.” Optional player action: help carry grain to the neutral cart, a short non-repeatable interaction, no faction point penalty.

Tameri's eastern landing: sandstone, water jars, reed shades, barges on a channel. Henut holds a list of families waiting for deliveries. A dock worker lifts a jar and then sets it down when the gate closes. Henut: “We can replace a broken seal. We can't replace the water they're waiting for.” Hero: “Can you keep the civilian carts moving?” Henut: “If the crossings stay open. Tell Sable which families are waiting, not which flag they carry.” If S27 completed, acknowledge repaired water or neutral reserve only according to its actual state. Optional action: mark the neutral delivery route on a slate; no new repair objective.

Remoria's northern camp: pale stone, timber awnings and a banner rack. Livilla compares the recovered cloth report to the supply issue list. A seamstress explains that identical patterns are sold through ordinary trade; the material alone cannot assign guilt. Livilla: “Our people will want to believe the worst. Give them something that isn't a rumor.” Hero: “I can tell them what we don't know.” Livilla: “That is still something.” If S28 completed, its stitch comparison may strengthen the note; it is not essential. Do not reuse the same item or reveal a culprit. Optional action: help return a neutral banner to its proper storage, not a new collectible.

## Scene 6 — The fourth chair

Back in the preserved reality the hero returns to the hall. The fourth chair has been moved beside the wall, still empty. Sable takes each report and puts it in a separate folder. A messenger arrives with news that the roads are being closed despite requests to keep neutral routes open. Do not depict all leaders as agreeing to escalation; people argue over the response.

Hero: “The reports don't agree. None of them tells us who did it.”

Sable: “Then that's what we'll send.”

Tome: “There are officers who will say that isn't enough.”

Henut: “There are families who will say they need to get home.”

Livilla: “We should keep the civilian crossings open.”

Player final choice — public statement: “Tell them we have no verified culprit.” Sable issues a carefully worded public notice. Private evidence: “Send the findings to all three representatives first.” Each acknowledges receipt. Civilian access: “Keep the crossings open while you investigate.” Sable sends a request for neutral access; no guaranteed compliance. All choices preserve evidence, do not grant diplomatic immunity and lead to M15. A brief shot of traders waiting outside a checkpoint is optional; no forced camera. The quest ends with the report of a retaliatory mobilization, not proof of divine manipulation. Journal: “Three accounts disagree. One envoy is dead. People are closing roads before they know why.”
