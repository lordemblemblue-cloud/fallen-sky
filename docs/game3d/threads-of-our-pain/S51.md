## Scene 1 — The wall that cannot decide
Age fifteen. At the foot of a mountain ravine, a guide has tied two strips of cloth to a cairn. One points toward the ridge trail; the other hangs directly downward into a shallow opening. The air smells of wet limestone. A lantern stands behind the cairn, where its warmth cannot dry the rain-darkened stones. The guide is not staring at the sky but at a line of dust drifting from the rock face. He has been waiting for a second person to confirm what he saw, and he does not immediately ask the boy to enter.

Guide (half crouched, fingers spread against the rock): “There was a shelf here yesterday. I could see straight to the back of it. Today I can see a different wall.”

Hero (moves one step to the side; the secondary contour separates): “The wall looks like two places.”

Guide (lifts the lantern, then puts it down): “Don't chase the light. Follow the ground. I have marked the places that hold.”

The boy recognizes the shape of the opening with a certainty that unsettles him. He remembers carrying something heavy along a narrow road, but there is no road here and no evidence of road construction. He tests the guide's ordinary route markings rather than immediately trusting the sensation. Players may ask what the guide lost, inspect his drawing, or say they would prefer not to enter. Refusal leaves the adventure available. If D36 was completed, the boy can mention the deeper shelf without replaying the discovery or claiming the guide saw the original world. If not, no armor or outpost spoiler appears.

## Scene 2 — Where the ground remains
The guide explains that a survey crew left field equipment in a shallow side gallery when loose stones cut off their original approach. He needs the gear and a reliable account of the route, not a trophy from the anomaly. The guide marks a return cairn and stays at the entrance. The player enters through a stable inspection passage beside the rift; D36's paired-focus fissure may be visible only if its own conditions are met. The shallow path slopes to an intersection with an upper ledge and a lower switchback. Both are ordinary traversable routes, distinct from the hidden armor chamber. A scratched arrow points to a wall that appears to have a second, translucent face. It is a warning, not a door.

Hero (touches the stable wall, then withdraws his hand): “Cold. At least this part is real.”

Guide (from the entrance, calling without following): “Can you still see my lamp?”

Hero (turns toward the cairn): “I can see a way back.”

On the upper route, pale layers of limestone emerge from darkness as the player's eyes adjust. On the lower route, a shallow stream passes beneath the same shelf; its sound helps orient the player but does not substitute for visible markings. The player checks two survey markers in any order. The first is a polished rock rib with an unobstructed route behind it; the second is a stone pillar on solid ground beneath a fractured ceiling. The hero may sketch the apparent second wall or record only the stable path. The guide treats either report as useful and never demands a theory. No damaging trap, forced fall, mandatory grapple or combat encounter is added; D36's separately proposed optional loose-stone teaching hazard is outside the S51 public circuit. Noninteractive fissure effects must not change collision or produce a fake floor.

## Scene 3 — Gear for the return journey
In a broad alcove, a canvas wrapping lies beneath a collapsed wooden survey rack. The hero crouches, pulls a loose cloth edge free, and finds the crew's field gear: a spare lantern hood and a reinforced pack strap, proposed ordinary equipment distinct from the chest armor plate. He remembers fastening a strap while someone called to him from behind a door. The echo lasts only a moment. He checks his own equipment to reassure himself that he is still in the cavern. There is no forced camera turn or sudden migration at the pickup. The player may carry the kit to the guide, or secure it in the nearby marked cache if inventory is full. The quest item uses its own inventory slot and cannot be sold before delivery.

Guide (at the return cairn, taking the kit and inspecting the strap): “That's ours. I thought we'd have to leave it there.”

Hero: “There was another wall. I don't know what I was looking at.”

Guide (opens his sketchbook): “Show me where you were standing.”

The player selects the route used and can indicate the observed contour. The guide draws a dotted line rather than declaring the entire cavern safe. If both routes were inspected, he adds the second as a verified local return route. The public survey ends with a removable warning barrier across the unstable side branch, not the opening of a permanent dimensional breach. If a player has already entered D36's deeper chamber, the barrier cannot close its egress or disable its focus interaction. The guide leaves the stable inspection path open for later visits. Proposed reward: ordinary experience and repaired utility gear or a supply voucher of equivalent value; D36 alone grants the remnant chest armor component. The report is optional contextual evidence for M17, never a replacement for its astronomical observations.

## Scene 4 — A road made for people
Age eighteen, original reality; genuine playable experience. The corresponding ravine is cut by a military supply passage leading toward an outpost. Wet timbers support a stone lintel. A warning bell sounds from far away, but the immediate objective is to close a damaged section and direct a small group toward the safe service route. An engineer kneels beside a gate latch, listening to the rumble behind the wall. The older hero looks from a cracked beam to the edge of the stone; for a moment the pattern resembles a split diamond. He is already wearing his chest armor. The existing D36 focus may be completed if available, but it is not a condition of closing the service gate.

Engineer (holds up a hand to stop a porter): “Not through here. The upper braces have moved.”

Older hero (presses his palm against a stable pillar): “There's another way around the shelf.”

Engineer: “You know the passage?”

Older hero (hesitates): “I thought I did. Show me the route board.”

The player verifies the alternate service path, then signals the porters to move in small groups. The engineer counts each group into a stable waiting bay. The hero confirms the last active actor is clear before helping close the damaged supply passage. The gate is physical; closing it does not heal the cosmic fracture or alter M17's convergence revelation. A gate obstruction prevents closure and displays a clear explanation, rather than crushing an actor. Afterward the older hero touches the familiar pack strap and briefly hears the quiet drip of the younger cavern. The guide's voice is not transmitted as an intelligible instruction. At fifteen, the boy later hears a metallic latch click where no gate exists. Neither understands why ordinary work in two places feels connected. Preserve the separate D36 first-time breastplate recognition and second vista; this scene must not replay them or add older equipment.
