# R54 — Main campaign: the morning that belongs to them

All new dialogue and staging are proposed. The sacrifice/rebirth concept is owner-directed canon as an optional ending; the exact consequence contracts remain awaiting approval. No new quest IDs.

## M19.V01 — The report is not a verdict
At the M19 original-world assembly point, after the second passage is secured, a resident turns the shelter register sideways so that the older hero can read it without stepping into the guard's path. Three columns distinguish people seen arriving, reports received from elsewhere, and those whose whereabouts are unknown. The page is not a census of the planet. A runner returns a lantern to its hook and reaches for a cup; the caregiver steadies the cup with both hands before passing it over. A child can be heard asking an adult to help fold a blanket, but no child is placed in the foreground to demand a sacrifice. The lighting is warm near the worktable, gray at the stone exit, and the intermittent sounds of distant danger remain outside the shelter's playable safe area.
Guard (taps the first column): “These are the people we've counted ourselves.”
Hero, eighteen (traces the blank column without touching it): “And these?”
Guard: “We haven't heard. That isn't the same as losing them.”
Resident: “Leave the spaces. Somebody might bring news.”
Hero: “I'll remember which ones we know.”
If the player asks whether everyone is safe, the guard answers, “Everyone on this side of the gate is under cover. I can't answer for the other roads.” If the player asks what people want to happen next, the resident says, “Ask us about our own road. We can't speak for everyone else.” If the player remains silent, the guard turns the register back and resumes working. These responses do not add or remove anyone from the ledger. The player confirms the existing report by interacting with the table or reading the scout's summary. No optional quest determines the report's validity. The older hero believes he has done a finite, useful thing rather than earned a right to decide the fate of humanity. On return to the younger perspective, the journal contains testimony with provenance, not a physically transferred book.

## M19.V02 — A victory that is not an ending
At the raised ring, after the last tether is interrupted, the overlapping skyline separates just enough for the player to distinguish the broken original arch from the intact preserved arch. The hostile goddess withdraws as in R39. Her absence does not repair the lunar seal or tell us what happened to the original invasion. The Moon Goddess lowers her hands slowly; she has been holding the protection through the confrontation. A small piece of loose stone rolls into a dry channel. Then the ambient sounds of the active reality return. No triumphant boss-fanfare or loot shower should interrupt this moment.
Hero, fifteen (still facing the far side): “Is she gone?”
Moon Goddess: “Her hold on this place is broken.”
Hero: “Then why can I still feel it?” [He studies the silver mark, opens his hand.] 
Moon Goddess: “What she used was already there.”
Hero: “So stopping her wasn't the same as choosing what happens to us.”
Moon Goddess (turns to meet his gaze): “No.”
The player retains movement and camera control as the safe deliberation space becomes available. The threat outline disappears only when the combat state actually ends. Any active summon or hazard is removed before the journal opens; the checkpoint is committed after the encounter, not after a choice. If the player leaves the ring, the keeper directs them to the existing return path rather than repeating the goddess's entire explanation. The younger hero's recognition is now informed by M17–M18, but he does not gain an omniscient account of the hostile goddess's identity, nor a new ability merely because he defeated a tether. This beat preserves the difference between ending the immediate danger and choosing the future of the link.

## M19.V03 — Ask before choosing
The keeper brings the report to the near side of the ring, where a low stone bench and wide exit make it possible to deliberate without standing over a chasm. The two candidate paths receive identical pacing and emphasis. The hero asks about the people first, then about his own lives. The goddess answers only according to the contract actually approved for the build. The following lines are draft dialogue for the R39 survivor-preserving candidates, not approval of either outcome.
Hero: “If I keep living here, does that mean I abandon them?”
Moon Goddess: “Not if the separation leaves their lives in their own hands. It would end the forced overlap; it would not finish their work for them.”
Hero: “And if I release the thread?”
Moon Goddess: “The original existence you have known would end. A different life would carry the feeling, not the memory of everything you endured.”
Hero (looks at the keeper's record): “And the people in both worlds?”
Keeper: “That answer must be part of the choice. Not something we discover after it.”
For separation, the R39 candidate retains both existing lives and closes or makes dormant the involuntary link, without guaranteeing victory in the original invasion or immediate peace in the preserved war. The apparent tension with M05's physical death must be addressed in a writer-approved local chronology and continuation ontology before presenting any post-separation older avatar as a future event. It must not read as an unannounced resurrection. For rebirth, the original spirit merges into the reincarnated life, which has no conscious catastrophe memories; the third world has no invasion history. The R39 proposal keeps original and preserved inhabitants distinct and continuing, but the exact fate of the younger avatar remains unresolved. Do not convert that unresolved fate into a silent deletion or declare everyone saved. The keeper's proposed line is a production gate as well as a dramatic beat: if any essential consequence is not approved, the development preview may be read, but the final selection cannot commit. Do not display incomplete promises as a player-facing shipping choice.

## M19.V04 — Confirmation without a verdict
The hero may ask to hear either consequence again, return to the report, or leave for the safe checkpoint. A dialogue skip never commits a choice. The confirmation panel repeats the selected branch and its actual approved material consequences. A quiet visual pause separates reviewing the terms from the confirmation interaction. No countdown, no morality score, no special reward preview, no celebratory sacrifice badge. In the separation candidate, the hero looks toward the original horizon and says, “I hope they don't have to wait for me to start tomorrow.” In the rebirth candidate, he says, “Let the life that comes after be its own.” Both are delivered with uncertainty and resolve, not a declaration that suffering is desirable. The goddess answers, “Then we must honor what we have agreed.” If the player selects Back, she says nothing; the ambient scene resumes without resetting the confrontation. Once approved and explicitly committed, use R39's symbolic transition, not a player-controlled lethal action. The account's completion receipt is the same on either route. Missing contracts, target-cell failure, repeated confirmation or reconnect must return to a valid existing checkpoint or committed destination, never a half-written outcome.

## M20.V01 — The road is open a little further (separation candidate)
At the M15 road, two workers have laid the old barricade's timber flat. The damage is still present in the wood; the road is neither magically restored nor abandoned. A cart driver watches a worker turn the direction sign toward a newly surveyed path. The younger hero holds the sign steady or accompanies a traveler to the turnout using the existing choice of tasks. The camera stays behind the character at normal exploration height, with a clear view of the open road and the remaining obstruction beyond it. The Warden's report remains limited to the route the settlement has checked.
Worker (testing the sign): “A little left. There.”
Hero: “Will they know where to go?”
Worker: “If they read it. If they don't, we'll tell them.”
Traveler: “I haven't walked this road without a guard in months.”
Hero: “You don't need to hurry.”
The traveler does not declare the war over. The hero sees ordinary agency re-emerging in small, demonstrable acts. Optional S19/S29 consequences only appear if the underlying saved outcomes and disclosure rules permit them. No new faction allegiance or required escort is added. After the local task, the sanctuary rest point remains reachable. The optional original-world coda uses a separate authored context, never a realm selector; its local chronology remains blocked for writer approval where it would imply M05 was reversed.

## M20.V02 — The bench that needs fixing (original-world coda candidate)
The shelter is still lit. A resident runs a hand along the edge of a repaired bench, finds a rough patch, and sets a piece of cloth over it. A scout adds a new directional marker to the route board. If the separation contract and local chronology authorize the older avatar, he can help with the existing marker or bundle task; otherwise this is a noncanonical development preview until the contract is resolved. On the rebirth candidate, the same local activities are shown without the original protagonist as an active avatar. A resident, not a ghostly narrator, carries the final exchange. The scene is about their continued work, not the price the hero paid.
Scout: “The upper passage still holds.”
Resident (testing the bench): “This doesn't. Pass me the wedge.”
Caregiver: “Leave room for the water.”
Resident: “I will. We need a place for everyone to sit.”
If the older hero is playable under the approved separation contract, he answers, “Show me which end.” Otherwise another resident hands over the wedge. Do not introduce a new survivor ID if an existing verified actor is unavailable; use an ambient worker with no implied identity. The M19 ledger remains unchanged and read-only. New coda activity is not evidence that every evacuee survived, that the invasion ended, or that the original world was erased. The camera settles on the ordinary work rather than a portal or a face fading away.

## M20.V03 — The mountain has a different history (rebirth candidate)
Morning light touches the new world's shallow stream. The reincarnated protagonist walks at a normal pace beside a companion carrying a gardener's borrowed tool. The companion's face, name and age are not settled by the old life's casting. At the fork, the lower trail follows water and the upper trail opens toward the ridge. Neither is a challenge that tests remembered abilities. The player can offer to carry the tool or walk alongside; no item enters the old inventory. At the reunion junction a gardener is clearing leaves from a shallow stone channel. An arch in the distance has the same broad outline as earlier sanctuary landmarks, but its repaired mortar and flower beds reflect a distinct history in which the invasion never happened. Do not include invasion memorials, refugee notices or damage decals from the original world. The resemblance is for the player's recognition, not conscious recall by the new protagonist.
Companion: “The water comes down from the sanctuary. They keep it clear for the gardens.”
Hero: “It's quiet here.”
Gardener (glances up): “Wait until the birds start.”
Hero (smiles, after listening): “I think I'd like that.”
At the sanctuary a modest image or inscription identifies the Moon Goddess as healer and guardian among the Twelve. This is an optional readable detail, not a new identity reveal for other deities or a lore exam. A visitor leaves a small offering of flowers in gratitude for care received. The gardener explains, if asked, “People come here when they need healing, or just a place to rest.” The player can read the inscription or proceed to the rest stone. The new hero has no reason to connect the shrine with the catastrophe. The camera does not cut to an original-world flashback; an optional soft musical motif may echo the earlier score for the audience, with no in-character recognition line.

## M20.V04 — Where pain becomes peace
At the rest stone the protagonist stops because he feels unexpectedly moved by the view, not because the game forces him into a cutscene. The companion waits beside him instead of diagnosing the feeling. The existing R40 dialogue is retained exactly; the new stage directions and optional closing lines are proposed.
Companion: “Have you been here before?”
Hero: “I don't think so.”
Companion: “Are you hurting?”
Hero: “No. I don't think it's pain anymore.”
A pause. Water runs past the roots of a flowering plant. The protagonist breathes naturally and looks toward the path, where another visitor has stopped to help the gardener. If the player chooses Stay, the companion says, “We've got time,” and sits at a neighboring spot; control remains available. If Walk on, the companion says, “I'll show you the other side,” and waits for the player to move. Neither choice selects another ending. The relief is an emotional echo, not explicit memory or proof that the catastrophe was necessary. No apparition hands over the original hero's belongings. The world is not empty or built solely around the protagonist's grief. The player chooses to finish at the existing rest stone or exit terrace, with credits and supported postgame options only after a validated committed outcome. Show the separate inhabited original and preserved coda only under the approved contract. The hostile goddess's identity remains open.
