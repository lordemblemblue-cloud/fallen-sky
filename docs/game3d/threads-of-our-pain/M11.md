## Scene 1 — The air below

The older protagonist follows the attendant's directions from the sanctuary. At the mine entrance a woman holds a lantern sideways, watching its flame lean toward the tunnel. A queue of survivors waits beside a timber retaining wall. Nobody asks about the battle until he reaches the gate. His hand is still shaking from the confrontation; he hides it by tightening his glove.

Steward (hoarse, pointing down the passage): “If you're looking for somewhere to sleep, we've got floor. If you're looking for somewhere to breathe, we're working on it.”

Hero: “What's wrong with the air?”

Steward: “The upper vent stopped drawing. We moved the sick closer to the entrance, but the medicine is still outside.”

The player can ask to see the vent, offer to escort supplies, or ask about the sanctuary. The steward answers the last question with a practical admission: “I heard the stone come down. I don't know who was inside.” No one identifies the child or goddess. The player retains movement and camera control. The mine is a refuge, not a combat dungeon; people have arranged blankets to preserve walking lanes.

## Scene 2 — A family with a name

At a partition, a mother (provisional name Mira) sings quietly to her son (Tovin). The father (Renn) folds a damp blanket over a rail. The song has a short rising phrase and a descending three-note cadence; commission an original melody later. A chipped cup sits on a shelf beside a worn necklace. The child is asleep, not magically healed. The mother stops singing only when the hero speaks.

Mira (almost whispering): “He sleeps when I sing.”

Hero: “I can bring medicine.”

Renn (standing, then lowering his voice): “Come back safely. We've lost enough.”

Choice — ask their names: Mira introduces each person, then asks the hero's name using the player's selected name. Choice — ask what they need: Renn points out the healer's marked supply ledger; no improvised real-world medicine. Choice — leave quietly: the mother nods and resumes the melody. All paths preserve the names in the journal as an observed introduction, not an inferred family relationship. If S16 completed, the healer recognizes the earlier help and the family offers a brief greeting; no repeated delivery or duplicate reward.

## Scene 3 — Make room for breath

The vent inspection reveals a jammed wooden louver above a service landing. A strip of cloth hangs motionless. The steward explains that the counterweight has slipped, not that a supernatural force is choking the mine. The player may turn the ground-level release with a worker or climb the broad service ramp to clear the latch. A worker holds the gate open until the player returns. The mechanism moves slowly with an audible creak and visible warning stripe; it cannot crush an occupied player. A cloth streamer and lantern flame show the restored airflow. The healer moves the waiting patients deeper into the ward only after the path is clear.

Worker: “There. Hear that?”

Hero: “The air?”

Worker (smiling for the first time): “The boards stopped rattling.”

## Scene 4 — The supply corridor

A carrier waits at the outer fork with a sealed medicine crate. A small group of corrupted creatures approaches the abandoned service road, telegraphed by distant calls and disturbed gravel. The player can defend the carrier from the wide lane, signal the guard to escort along the ridge bypass, or clear the route first. No optional whistle, boots or salve required. Combat is bounded to the exterior, with no enemies spawning among patients. If the carrier is endangered, they retreat to the guarded shelter; the crate remains recoverable and the escort can restart. On arrival the healer checks the seal and takes responsibility for distribution. No instant recovery animation or promised cure.

Carrier: “I thought we'd have to leave it behind.”

Hero: “It's here now.”

## Scene 5 — A place at the table

The family offers a seat beside a low lantern. The player can eat, sit without eating, or decline and continue. Renn describes a tradition of leaving a place at the table for a traveler; Mira corrects him gently: “Not every traveler. The ones who have nowhere else.” Tovin wakes long enough to ask whether the hero will be there tomorrow. The hero may promise to try, admit uncertainty, or say the guards will stay. No choice guarantees survival or locks an ending. The family trust flag records whether the player helped and which conversation occurred; it never determines the child's medical outcome.

At a safe rest point, the younger protagonist wakes in his own world. He hears the final cadence of the song in memory, taps it against the windowsill, then stops. He believes it was a vivid dream. Journal: “I remember the song, and the smell of wet wood. I don't know where I heard it.” Do not show the M12 counterpart yet. The older believes he simply found a place to rest after the battle. Developer-only: shared life force permits the migration and sensory echo; no player-facing explanation or reincarnation label.
