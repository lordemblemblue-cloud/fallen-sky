# R14 — D11 Roots of Yesterday / S14 The Tree That Grew Too Soon

Status: expanded narrative proposal; ready for terrain blockout, not built. Catalog source: v0.9 R13. Mandatory anchors: river meadow, ancient branch/root/distant marker forming a sapling, original young tree and tied marker, root cave, worn personal marker and seed pouch, Herbalism Satchel. S14 remains independent; S13 is not renumbered.

## Scene 1 — A place to grow
Age fifteen. After a night of rain the meadow is bright enough to make the river look white. The road skirts a huge tree whose exposed roots have pushed the old boundary stones apart. A gardener kneels beside a shallow channel, lifting silt with a wooden scoop. The player can hear the scoop strike a buried stone before seeing her. No cinematic takes control of the camera.

Gardener (without looking up): “Mind the little ones. They look like weeds until they have leaves.”

The hero steps around the seedlings. A scrap of faded cord catches his eye high in the fork of the tree. He lifts his hand as though the branch were within reach, then lowers it. The gardener follows his gaze.

Gardener: “This tree is older than the road.”

Hero (almost interrupting): “I tied that knot.”

Gardener (studying his face rather than the cord): “Perhaps someone taught you.”

Hero: “Maybe.” He turns the end of his own belt over twice, attempting to reproduce the knot without looking at it. His fingers stop halfway through. The player may ask about the tree, offer to help the seedlings, or leave. Asking about the tree reveals that the gardener has never seen the cord replaced; she does not know its origin. Offering help starts S14, not D11. Walking away preserves the encounter and leaves the meadow accessible.

## Scene 2 — The shelter that might last
Age eighteen, during a playable interval in the original reality. The same bend in the river is recognizable by a split boulder and the distant notch in the ridge. Here the meadow holds a modest shelter and a young tree scarcely taller than the hero. Smoke from distant conflict hangs above the far bank, but the immediate scene is quiet. A shelter resident carries a bucket with a broken handle. The hero has a seed pouch and Herbalism Satchel already in his possession.

Resident (setting down the bucket): “You could put it nearer the wall. Less wind.”

Hero: “Then the roots will have nowhere to go.”

Resident: “You're planning for roots now?”

Hero (pressing soil with the heel of his hand): “Someone ought to.”

The player chooses to water the sapling or hold it upright while the resident waters it. Both lead to the same planting beat. The hero ties a personal marker loosely to the support, not tightly around living bark. The resident tests the knot, smiles and leaves it alone. A voluntary viewpoint near the split boulder aligns the young trunk and its marker with the distant ridge notch. The focus cue is a quiet change in ambient sound, not a revelation about time. The older hero believes he is simply remembering a place he has worked in.

## Scene 3 — The shape of a sapling
At fifteen, the player can inspect the old tree from a level path or climb to a low natural shelf. The branch, exposed root and distant boundary marker form the abstract silhouette of a sapling when viewed from the correct angle. The player freely rotates the camera; an optional directional cue, wider tolerance and hold/toggle setting support accessibility. Both reality alignments are required. If the older scene has not been played, the younger alignment is retained as a clue, with no forced migration or quest lock.

Once both are recorded, a seam beneath the root becomes visible. Soil and hanging roots separate just enough to reveal a dry stone-lined opening. The player may enter or walk away. Unfocus closes the exterior appearance, never the interior collision or escape route. The hero touches the root before stepping through.

Hero (softly): “It was small when I left it.”

He immediately looks back toward the gardener, uncertain whether he spoke aloud. She is occupied with the seedlings and offers no explanation.

## Scene 4 — Beneath the roots
The entrance slopes into a chamber with mineral-stained stone and roots threading through the ceiling. Water drips into a shallow basin; insects scatter from the lantern light. A fork offers a dry shelf route or a lower channel. The latter contains a loose slab that visibly rocks under pressure; stepping off resets it, and the dry route bypasses it. There is no mandatory combat. Optional small wildlife retreats when approached; do not add an enemy merely to fill the room.

In the rear alcove a worn personal marker rests beside an old seed pouch. The player examines the marker before collecting the satchel reward. Its knot is distorted by age but recognizable. The hero brushes dirt away with his thumb, then folds the pouch against his palm.

Hero: “There you are.” A beat. “Why did I say that?”

Cut only at a safe playable transition to age eighteen: he reaches for the satchel already hanging at his hip. The weight surprises him, as if someone has just placed it there. He checks the seam and smiles briefly, then looks toward the shelter. No duplicate item, inventory removal, or explicit explanation. The younger reward is Herbalism Satchel; proposed effect is additional dedicated herb storage, subject to economy balancing. The marker and seed pouch are inspectable story objects, not extra power rewards.

## Scene 5 — A promise without words
The second focus is on a dry ledge beyond the alcove, looking through a root-framed opening toward the original shelter site. The older counterpart's matching viewpoint shows the young tree, bucket and resident. In the original scene the resident asks who will water it when they leave.

Resident: “What if nobody comes back?”

Hero (after a long pause, watching water soak into soil): “Then someone else might find shade.”

At fifteen, the hero sees the immense canopy and hears leaves move overhead. He does not see a literal ghost or identify the resident. His expression changes from confusion to a small, unguarded smile. The player regains full movement immediately; optional journal entry records the physical resemblance and his uncertainty, not reincarnation or the mechanics of migration.

## S14 — The Tree That Grew Too Soon
Independent optional quest. The gardener's seedlings are wilting because the small irrigation channel has become blocked by silt and a displaced boundary stone. The player witnesses her effort before receiving a task. She fears digging near the old roots will damage them. The player can inspect the channel, follow the water upstream, and choose a shallow bypass or clear the existing obstruction by hand. Both are reversible environmental interactions; no cutting the ancient roots. A third dialogue route asks the gardener to show the safe channel, trading speed for instruction rather than punishing the player.

Gardener (if bypass chosen): “Water doesn't care which way it goes, as long as it gets there.”

Hero: “Will it reach all of them?”

Gardener (watching the first seedling bed darken): “Wait a moment. There. Now it will.”

If clearing the channel: she kneels beside him and warns him when a root is exposed. If the player asks for help: she demonstrates with the scoop and lets him finish. If the player leaves mid-task, the channel remains in a safe partial state; no seedlings die offscreen as punishment. On return, the gardener resumes the last unfinished step. Completion occurs when water reaches both beds and the player speaks to her or observes her acknowledgment. Proposed reward: modest experience and a common herb bundle, no exclusive discovery item. If D11 is complete, the hero can show the marker; the gardener says, “Keep it dry this time.” If not, she simply thanks him. Neither quest requires the other.
