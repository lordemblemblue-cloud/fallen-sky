## 2. Highest-risk writer decision — original chronology
M05's original-body death is a genuine event, not an ordinary defeat. M09–M19 original-world scenes are lived events, but their presentation after M05 cannot establish that their local occurrence is after the death. Existing sources specify partial orders: M04 precedes M05; M11 original refuge first visit precedes M12 revisit; M18 shelter survey precedes M19 original assembly; M09/M10 overlap and intervention must remain physically real. The exact total local order is not established. Without a writer-side answer, it is hard for actors to remember the correct events or for developers to know when a route can be used.

Candidate for discussion, not canon: place genuine older-body playable encounters within an earlier local chronology leading toward the original catastrophe, while the protagonist experiences them in a different presentation order. This could preserve a real death and avoid a resurrected body, but must be tested against every explicit recollection and the M09/M10 physical overlap, M05 silver-path references and M18/M19 convergence. In particular, M09's older hero currently recalls the pressure at the silver path; simply moving that scene before M05 may introduce a contradiction. Do not approve this mechanism or silently rewrite the recall. Alternative is a separately authored mechanism of shared existence consistent with natural law; no ghost, automatic rewind, newly granted portal power or cloned body is authorized. Build a complete local event/knowledge ledger and obtain owner direction before shipping affected scenes. This is an open writer problem, not a question the player must be forced to answer early.

## 3. Targeted proposed scene refinements
Each entry states what happens, why it matters, what the player experiences, and how the developer can deliver it. New lines below are optional editorial alternatives/inserts; use only after checking neighboring R51–R54 lines and approval, never duplicate dialogue. ### R88.01 — M04/M06: a life he wants to return to
In M04, the existing cart evacuation gives the older hero a practical reason to act even before he understands where he is. Instead of repeating another question about the strange city, he steadies a loose cart strap, recalling the ordinary forest encounter through body language without asserting a shared actor identity. In M06, the younger hero sees an ordinary strap or knot at the existing market and briefly checks his palm; the player feels the disturbing contrast between daily life and the city without being told it was a dream. Reuse the existing cart and market props; if M01 strap help was skipped, use a neutral familiarity gesture rather than a claim that he helped the merchant. No new mandatory task.
Proposed M06 optional line, replacing a redundant recollection if one exists: “Everything here is where it should be. So why do I keep thinking about that road?” Lyra need not hear it. Preserve her mandatory mountain lead and the Goddess's genuine uncertainty.

### R88.02 — M09/M10: the antagonist's method, not her name
The unknown woman already tries to open a passage and reacts when the mark disrupts her work. Strengthen the contrast with the Moon Goddess by showing that she prioritizes the opening over the protagonist's safety; the player sees a clear immediate objective without learning her final motive. In the existing M10 retreat, the older hero chooses to keep the child moving rather than chase the woman or seek a divine victory, making his protective instinct the central action. Reuse the established ritual surface, threshold and retreat geometry; no new attack, captive or identity clue.
Proposed M09 alternative to a redundant command: Unknown Woman: “Step away from the seal.” Hero: “There's someone on the other side.” Unknown Woman: “That is not your concern.” Keep the older sightline obscured and younger recognition asymmetric. Do not infer that she caused the assassination or original invasion. Writer still needs her actual objective, motive, knowledge and constraints in a private decision sheet; that question cannot be solved by this dialogue.

### R88.03 — M12/M16: people are not interchangeable
The two families already share a melody and emblem but live separate lives. Give each a contrasting ordinary action within its existing scene (original refuge family arranging bedding or the board; preserved family attending its domestic work) so the player remembers the people as individuals, not simply as clues to reincarnation. At the existing M16 witness hearing, an authorized person may describe a familiar melody without giving up their present identity; the player experiences empathy rather than solving a genealogy puzzle. Use separate actor rigs/IDs and one motif asset with different performance contexts; the memory speaker and music remain subject to writer review.
Proposed M16 optional alternate line if consistent with the private witness's consent: “I remember a song. That doesn't mean I remember whose life it was.” Hero: “You don't have to prove who you are to me.” Never reveal private testimony publicly, replace a person's identity or gate M17 on a side quest.

### R88.04 — M14/M15: the road becomes the cost of suspicion
M14 already portrays competing needs, unsigned accounts, absent merchants and a closed crossing. Make the loss visible through the same existing public route before and after escalation: the player sees a cart waiting at the boundary in M14 and a phase-valid cleared or blocked route in M15. This makes the political inquiry relevant to the hero's purpose of keeping people safe, without implying that local assistance ends the war. Claude should map existing fountain, road, camp and shelter landmarks; use actual waiting/arrival state and low-poly phase dressing rather than inventing a casualty or a new mandatory escort.
Proposed M15 contextual line at the existing route board: “We can argue about the report after these people are under cover.” Use only where the speaker and waiting group are valid. Preserve genuine S26/S27/S28 evidence, S29 scope, and the distinction between the preserved conflict and original external invasion.

### R88.05 — M19/M20: two outcomes of the same human promise
Use a familiar mandatory landmark, such as the surveyed split ridge or river bend already established in the campaign, to connect the finale to the opening. In sacrifice, the hero sees a safe ordinary landscape but learns through an aged record that the people he knew are history; in refusal, a passable route carries the physical scars and the next practical task. The player should recognize something worth saving in both endings rather than receiving a lecture about which choice was morally correct. Match sightlines with an existing anchor, use actual memory flags for any optional named recollection, separate current/historical actor layers and keep the twenty-year coda distinct.
Proposed alternatives for R65 review, not additional required lines: sacrifice, “The road is here. They're not.” Refusal, “The road is broken. We can start here.” Use sparingly if stronger than nearby existing lines; the original R65 realization and explicit consequence confirmation remain intact. No forced death action, revived acquaintance, new spirit power, reward advantage or universal survival.
