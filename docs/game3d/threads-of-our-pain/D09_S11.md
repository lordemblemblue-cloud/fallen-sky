# D09 — The Drowned Bell / S11 — The Bell Below

Revision R12 · 26 November 2026 · Source: Living Story Bible v0.7 R11, D09/S11 catalog. Expanded proposed narrative and blockout brief. Preserve the Warning Chime ability (optional nearby-threat cue), bell clapper, Sunken Temple, dry chamber and the evacuation warning. Diver dialogue and required hero line are retained. The flood's local cause, route details and supporting characters below are proposals, not final cosmology or surveyed geometry.

## Scene 1 — The sound below (age fifteen; S11)

At the Sunken Temple, a narrow trail emerges from reeds onto a terrace whose outer stairs disappear under green water. Sunlight breaks between passing clouds, leaving bands of gold on submerged columns. The roofline is gone, but the surviving masonry still channels sound. The player hears a single deep metallic note, then a long silence. On a dry landing a diver sits wringing water from his hair. A frayed line lies coiled beside him; his hands are shaking, not from a supernatural effect but from the cold.

DIVER [low, watching the water rather than the player]: “It rings without a hand.”

HERO [listening, takes one step toward the edge]: “Someone once used it to warn people.”

DIVER [turns sharply]: “How would you know that?”

HERO [after a beat, softer]: “I don't. It just sounds like a warning.”

The diver describes a hollow beneath the stairs. He recovered a fragment of an old sounding plate but could not reach its clapper; currents drove him back to the surface. He asks the player to discover what happened to the shrine, not to retrieve something valuable for sale. DIVER: “Bring that story back.” He points at a safe ledge before warning the protagonist away from the intake. The player can decline, ask about the current, or accept. If asked, the diver admits he has heard the note on calm days as well as stormy ones. He has no knowledge of the other reality and never migrates. Free-roam discovery remains possible without accepting S11; the diver stays on his safe landing and can be consulted later.

## Scene 2 — A warning nobody wants to hear (age eighteen)

The older protagonist approaches the same geological basin during an evacuation. The bell hangs above water inside a working warning frame overlooking a causeway. Timber ramps, rope rails and temporary supplies are recent additions; they need not correspond to the younger temple's architecture. A civil watchkeeper is counting families crossing the lowest path. Upstream, a rising line of water and floating branches make the risk legible before anyone speaks. The hero already knows how to sound the warning pattern. He hears the water's pitch change and looks toward the frame.

WATCHKEEPER [short, distracted]: “One more cart. Then we close the lower road.”

HERO [points at a floating branch]: “Look how fast that's moving.”

WATCHKEEPER: “If we ring now, they'll leave the cart.”

HERO [keeps his voice level]: “Then let them leave the cart.”

Player action: move to the stable bell platform, check the lower route or speak to the watchkeeper. Optional choice to help reroute a family before ringing; no irreversible deaths or inventory loss for waiting at the prompt. The warning sequence is two short notes and a sustained note, a fictional signal specific to this encounter. The player operates the bell through a single accessible interaction, not a timing minigame. NPCs move toward the upper causeway. The water subsequently covers the lower steps. The watchkeeper watches an abandoned cart drift away, then counts the people again. “Everyone's up. Good.” This is actual playable original-world time; it is not an illustration of a dream. Do not imply this particular flood caused the younger shrine's ancient inundation. The older hero believes he prevented a local disaster within a larger invasion.

## Scene 3 — First focus: a bell assembled out of distance

Age fifteen: a near bronze fragment forms the lip, a submerged column capital forms the shoulder, and a distant sunbeam against a stone slit completes the bell's crown. Provide dry-bank viewpoints and a shallow optional observation shelf. The light geometry must stay readable under clouds, night or low settings via a fixed stone slit and subtle non-color outlines. Age eighteen: the intact bell lip, frame support and ridge gap form the counterpart silhouette; the player aligns from the safe platform after the evacuees clear it. Allow either alignment first; preserve flags until the relevant playable world is accessible. The younger protagonist says the required line only after both views have been seen: “I heard this before the water came.” His expression is unsettled rather than triumphant. He tests the claim by looking back at the silent ruins, not by explaining it.

With both flags, hold/toggle focus to reveal a previously concealed dry stair inside the flooded complex. It appears along the inner wall, not through a sudden disappearance of the lake. The player crosses a stable threshold. Unfocus outside conceals the stair; after entry, an internal release opens a separate rising passage to the bank. No mid-crossing collision removal, forced camera movement or water damage at the threshold. If one perspective is missing, journal simply notes an incomplete bell shape and allows return.

## Scene 4 — The chamber where sound travels

Inside, the roar of water becomes a low pulse. Dry limestone blocks support a chamber above the lake's internal waterline, while a bronze resonance plate hangs near a shaft. The player discovers marks at different heights on the wall. A recent-looking scratch in the older frame can correspond to an ancient stain in the preserved world, but the script does not prove causation. The younger studies a relief showing figures moving uphill away from a watercourse. Nearby, a clogged relief channel explains how this local sanctuary could have become flooded: accumulated sediment and a blocked outlet, offered as a plausible local explanation rather than universal history.

Two routes: the dry gallery follows a spiral stair to the mechanism; an optional flooded side arcade leads through a short swim with an air pocket and ladder to an inscription rubbing. A loose hanging bronze plate warns through vibration, visible sway and a captioned knock. The player can wait for it to settle, secure it from a dry lever or bypass via the gallery. No boss is required. An optional environmental threat cue teaches observation without demanding the ability not yet recovered. A checkpoint at the dry junction allows retry; water resets to its local safe baseline, and no collected lore is lost.

At the mechanism, the hero finds the missing clapper resting in a catch recess. A diagram beside the bell shows the relationship between its frame and outlet. The player fits the clapper to the mechanism, frees the jammed outlet lever and sounds the bell once. The water level lowers only within the small side chamber; the external lake remains. The physical note and vibration reveal an alternate exit and confirm the shrine's warning function. The clapper remains installed as a world object, not a second wearable reward. The hero's fingers automatically fall into the same grip used to sound the warning in the original world.

HERO (15) [surprised, rests a hand against the plate]: “Two short. One long.”

He sounds the pattern, pauses at the familiarity of it, then listens until the last vibration disappears. The older protagonist, in his own ongoing scene, grips the rope he already knows and suddenly feels the confidence of learning that pattern for the first time. WATCHKEEPER: “You rang it right.” HERO (18) [looks down at his hand]: “I know.” He cannot explain why it feels like praise he has waited years to hear. No new ability is granted to the older self. The younger receives Warning Chime, a toggleable optional nearby-threat cue; it does not reveal exact enemies through walls, replace ordinary threat detection or become required for any main quest. Range and cooldown follow later balance review. Provide subtitle and visual pulse equivalents and mute controls.

## Scene 5 — The second focus: the upper road

Beyond the chamber a rising walkway frames the basin's split ridge, stair edge and bell-frame silhouette. After the ability is acquired, the player can align these elements and view the original warning platform during the moment when the last family reaches safety. The older protagonist turns the rope loose and supports the watchkeeper as he climbs the stairs. A child carries an empty basket; the parent stops to count their belongings and then laughs at how little remains. The player sees survival without an explanatory speech. The younger reaches instinctively toward the railing, then rests his hand on stone instead.

HERO (15) [a small exhale, not a victory shout]: “They heard it.”

In the original reality the older hero hears a distant bell decay after his own rope has stopped moving. He looks across the basin, feeling an unexpected grief for a quiet place he cannot identify, then returns to the families. Neither recognizes the other as himself. Journal records: “A warning bell. A road above the water. The same sound.” The player releases focus voluntarily and can remain at the vista without a forced cutscene. No Celestial Sight or cosmology is disclosed.

## Scene 6 — A story returned (S11)

At the landing, the diver sees the player's wet boots and offers a dry cloth. He wants to know why the bell was built and why the shrine flooded. The hero can report the blocked outlet as a probable local cause without claiming certainty. Three report variants: practical, “The drain was blocked. I freed it and fitted the clapper.” DIVER: “Then the next fool won't have to swim so far.” Compassionate, “It warned people to get uphill. That's what it was made for.” DIVER [looks toward the steps]: “A bell for the living, then.” Cautious, “The marks suggest a flood. I don't know if it happened the way I saw it.” DIVER: “Fair enough. Better an honest question than a good lie.” The diver posts a warning at the intake and returns to shallow-water work. Optional rubbing unlocks an extra line about the shrine's past, not another quest. S11 reward proposal: ordinary quest XP and local goodwill; D09 alone unlocks Warning Chime. Declined or late acceptance allows retrospective reporting. S11 does not require the player to surrender the installed clapper or repeat recovery.
