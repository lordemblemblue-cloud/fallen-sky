# M01 — The Journey Home

R21 | First expanded main campaign script. Source: R20 foundation and M01 catalog; successor M02 The Broken Seal. Mandatory: age fifteen only, Wolfveil forest, merchant warning, ordinary journey home, insects quiet and birds scatter, moon fractures and fragment falls beyond ridge, hero follows disturbance to lunar clearing. No migration or older self shown yet. Reserve river bend for later original-world evacuation. Additional dialogue, paths and props are proposals.

## Scene 1 — The familiar road

The opening begins with the protagonist already walking home. Do not open with an explanation of gods, war, death or reincarnation. A narrow path winds through a forest with patches of ferns, pale stones and roots crossing the trail. Late afternoon light softens into dusk. A distant roofline suggests home without establishing an unapproved family location or naming a village. The protagonist carries a small travel pack and ordinary equipment consistent with the eventual starter inventory. A bird lands on a branch and hops to another; water flows around a rounded stone. The world is busy with small things that have nothing to do with the protagonist. The player can stop and look.

Optional internal line, delivered naturally as he adjusts the pack: “Still a little light left.” No narrated destiny. Begin with control already enabled after a short fade. Movement and camera prompts appear only when needed, disappearing as the player uses them. Teach walking, turning and sprinting through a broad bend, not a test gate. A low root provides an optional vault or walking detour. A short streamside path loops back to the main trail. Keep dialogue and objective widgets unobtrusive; no floating destination marker over the moon fragment. Accessibility option: directional guide, journal clue or ambient landmark highlight. The player may spend time exploring with no consequence or forced timer.

The river bend is the first persistent geographical anchor. A split boulder sits inside the bend and a crooked tree grows on the higher bank. The camera does not turn automatically to show them, but the trail curves naturally toward them. In M04/M05 or a later surveyed evacuation route, the same silhouette may be recognizable among altered structures; do not show that alternate world yet. The opening's sound is close and ordinary: insects, leaves, trickling water and leather strap movement. A merchant's cart wheel creaks ahead.

## Scene 2 — The merchant's warning

The merchant has pulled his cart to the side of the road, tying a loose strap. He is not a mystical oracle, only someone who travels this road often. The animal pulling the cart hesitates and turns its head toward the distant ridge. The merchant tries to reassure it before speaking to the boy. His voice is conversational and slightly distracted.

Merchant: “The animals won't go near the ridge.”
Hero: “Have you seen anything?”
Merchant: “Not a thing. That's what bothers me. Usually there's a rabbit in every ditch up there.”

Player response A — Ask about the ridge. Hero: “Which side?” Merchant (pointing past the split boulder): “Beyond the trees. Keep to this road and you'll miss it.”
Player response B — Offer help. Hero: “Need a hand with that?” Merchant: “Hold the strap there, would you?” Brief contextual hold interaction. Merchant: “Thanks. Now get home before it gets dark.” The interaction gives no permanent power or essential quest item; it establishes ordinary kindness.
Player response C — Continue walking. Merchant calls after him: “Keep to the road, lad.” If the player never approaches, proximity voice and animal behavior communicate the warning; optional replay when returning to the cart. No mandatory dialogue menu to progress.

The protagonist can ask if the merchant has heard anything strange. Merchant: “Birds flew out of the trees this morning. Didn't settle again until the river.” Hero: “Maybe a storm.” Merchant: “Maybe.” The player initially has a mundane interpretation of the unease. Do not connect the merchant with a past incarnation or the three empires. A small journal note records that the animals avoid the ridge, not a prediction of catastrophe.

## Scene 3 — When the forest stops

As the player crosses a safe stretch of path beyond the merchant, the insects quiet in several successive patches. The wind continues. An animal freezes, then moves away from the ridge, and a few birds scatter from the upper canopy. The absence of ordinary noise is more unsettling than an orchestral alarm. The sky is visible through a broad gap where two tree lines separate. The moon appears pale and intact at first. A fine luminous seam spreads across it; the player can notice the event by looking up, by following the birds or through the optional directional accessibility cue. The seam is the visual sign of the lunar seal's rupture, but neither the hero nor the merchant knows this.

A luminous fragment descends beyond the ridge. Its apparent position, speed and scale should communicate distance without implying the entire moon physically falls. A low rumble arrives after the sight. The player sees the disturbance through an opening in the canopy; if facing away, the audio cue, reflected light on the path and merchant's reaction provide a second opportunity. Do not force the camera upward, cause a bright full-screen flash, or lock movement. Offer optional cinematic framing only through a deliberate look interaction.

Hero (sharply drawing breath): “Something fell beyond it.”
Merchant (calling from the roadside): “Then keep to the road.”
Hero (looking toward the ridge, not defiantly): “Did you see where?”
Merchant: “I saw enough.”

The merchant pulls his cart farther onto the familiar path. He will not accompany the boy into danger. If the player follows the road instead, a short safe loop toward the distant homeward landmark is available; the rumble and residual light remain visible at the forest fork. The quest journal says “Find where the light fell, when ready.” There is no invisible wall or punishment for delaying. Returning to the merchant yields: “Still there, is it? Be careful.” The protagonist ultimately chooses to investigate out of curiosity and concern. He is not compelled by a voice or supernatural instruction. If the player chooses dialogue expressing fear, Hero: “I don't like that sound.” Merchant: “Then don't go chasing it.” The player retains the decision to approach.

## Scene 4 — The ridge path

The investigation route turns away from the road through a narrow but navigable break in the trees. Dust has settled on fern leaves in the direction of the distant impact, and small stones vibrate once before resting. The player follows a intermittent silver glow glimpsed through foliage. Keep the sound localized rather than a constant blaring waypoint. A fallen branch lies across the path; offer a gentle vault or a walk around it. A fork has one route ascending to a vista and one following a stream toward the clearing, both connecting naturally to the same approach. The higher path offers a brief view of the fragment's glow; the lower offers a quiet environmental clue in the water. Neither is a mandatory combat tutorial.

Hero (at a distant glow, breath steadying): “That's not lightning.” If player delays, ambient music remains restrained, no repeated voice spam. Near the clearing, the forest becomes strangely still. Leaves closest to the light move against the prevailing breeze, while the river keeps flowing normally. This should look like a localized phenomenon, not a revelation of the alternate world. The hero slows only if the player chooses; no scripted forced walk. The final approach shows a silhouette near the luminous fragment, not a readable divine identity or detailed wolf transformation. M02 owns that reveal.

## Scene 5 — Threshold to M02

The player reaches the edge of the lunar clearing, where the bright light is partially obscured by a large tree trunk and an inclined rock. A faint voice is audible but not intelligible. The hero raises one arm briefly to shield his eyes only if standing idle; while moving, use a contextual face/eye reaction rather than interrupting input. The quest completes when the player voluntarily crosses the safe clearing threshold or chooses to investigate from the overlook. M02 activates with the objective “Find the source of the light.” Do not show the wolf's attack or have the Goddess deliver M02 dialogue before the transition. The player can pause, save or return to the forest path before approaching the fragment.

Developer-only truth: the fallen lunar fragment signals the broken protective seal. The Moon Goddess is already maintaining protection. The preserved reality is a real world and the opening is not a dream sequence; the boy has no conscious knowledge of original-world events. The older body and shared life-force mechanism are reserved for M04–M06. No Celestial Sight, hostile goddess identification, invasion explanation, names of future empires or third-world foreshadowing beyond the shared geography. No change to the user's established cosmology. The merchant's behavior, natural wildlife, and the boy's everyday helpfulness should make the eventual disruption meaningful. The wolf and its independent shadow belong to M02, transformation and defense to M03.

## Quest production and branching state

Required progression: start forest → pass merchant warning by interaction or proximity → witness fragment cue (visual, audio or accessible interaction) → voluntarily reach clearing → M01 complete / M02 available. Dialogue, strap help and scenic detour are optional persistent flags. If the user enters the clearing before the cue finishes, preserve the event by using a natural residual glow and short journal description rather than forcing a rewind. If a player saves before the rupture, replay once on return at the appropriate trigger. If after rupture, persist the sky seam and residual clearing glow; no duplicate cinematics or rewards. Death or navigation fall returns to safe forest checkpoint with story flags retained. No arbitrary time limit, mandatory collectible or combat. M01 teaches movement, camera and optional vault only; M03 teaches defense/combat, M04 weapon selection, as catalog specifies. Proposed modest main-quest experience or narrative checkpoint, no compulsory equipment changes or economy reward inflation. Optional merchant goodwill appears in later preserved-world visits without making him a cosmology source.
