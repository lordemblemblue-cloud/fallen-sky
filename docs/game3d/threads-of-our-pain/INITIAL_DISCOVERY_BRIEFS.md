## D01 — The Hill That Wasn't There (S43)
### Script
Age 15 glides across open valley; three sky glints align. He hears a wind like a familiar street: “There's nothing ahead of me. Why does it feel like I'm going home?” Age 18 sees matching shapes through a damaged watchtower during a lull; sword already at waist. “I could have sworn I was falling.” Both focuses reveal a mountain and safe shelf to the glider. Younger explores mineral-seamed cave with rock resembling a hearth, in a world where this town was never built. He finds his old sword: “I know this grip. Why was it waiting here?” Older grips the sword he already carries: “I've had this for years. Why does it feel new?” At the far overlook, arch-shaped rock, terraces and split ridge align with gate, rooftops and street of original hometown. Older briefly senses the silent landscape: “Why am I thinking about a place that doesn't exist?” Emotional arc: surprise, familiarity, longing.
### Terrain proposal
Open valley flight approach → revealed shelf (suggest at least 12 m wide, clear landing lane; tune to actual glider) → broad entrance → mineral-seam tunnel fork → hearth hollow/relic → ascending loop → overlook → ordinary exit or safe focus-assisted return. Optional fork contains a quiet resting alcove with environmental clues, no mandatory fight. Use the split ridge as shared landmark; preserve clear target sightline at overlook. Age 18 watchtower and hometown use matching ridge/terraces but inhabited street geometry. Age 15 natural stone, cool diffuse light, wind, no actual ancient town foundations required. Stream collision before reveal, allow loss of focus outside to hide threshold without disabling safe landing or trapping occupants. No forced camera or collision surprise. Test gliding from multiple headings, aborted focus, walking fallback, safe exit, item grant once and older inventory unchanged.

## D25 — The Gate That Held (S34)
### Script
Age 15 approaches old frontier fort near marsh; two broken battlements and distant mountain notch suggest shield: “There ought to be another way across.” Age 18 defends evacuation gate; brace, damaged beam, ridge align; he already holds shield. “I know the road. Why did I see water?” Paired focus reveals bridge and dry gatehouse-like cavity. Natural rock resembles masonry and equipment niches. Younger touches dented shield: “This feels like it belongs to me.” Older grips shield already worn: “I've held this through every fight. Why does it feel like the first time?” Upper ledge stone lip/outcrops/valley bend align with evacuation road and civilians crossing; older senses quiet: “For a moment, there was nobody here.” Emotional arc: unfamiliar responsibility versus unexpected calm.
### Terrain proposal
Marsh trail or rampart route → stable focus platform → revealed bridge → dry gatehouse cavity → slab and shield anchor → optional narrow equipment niche → upper ledge vista → safe ordinary exit to rampart. Suggested bridge width 3–5 m with safe edge handling and stable collision after crossing; tune to controller. Optional loose-stone hazard clearly telegraphed, bypass via dry ramp. Age 15 wet marsh, moss and old fort remnants, age 18 dry road with braced gate and evacuation staging; do not make age-15 fort literally the same historical gate. Use ridge and valley curve for both. Test two approaches, bridge visibility and traversal, focus release mid-crossing, safe return, optional vista and no older shield duplication.

## D36 — The Fracture Below (S51)
### Script
Age 15 mountain ravine; three fractures form split diamond: “There should be a way through here. Why do I keep thinking that?” Age 18 supply road cut through mountain links hometown to outpost; damaged beams and crack align; chest armor already worn. “I could almost see this place before anyone built it.” Both perspectives reveal fissure. Younger explores narrow entrance, shelf above dry ravine, broad lower chamber with ledges resembling shelves; dripping water over slab and old plate. Optional pressure-sensitive loose stones; no forced damage. On touch: “Why does this feel like something I lost?” Older touches breastplate: “I know this armor. So why am I noticing it now?” Far overlook aligns three rock shelves with supply road, lookout and roofs; guard waves cart through in the other life. Younger whispers “I know where that road goes.” Older feels quiet longing: “Why did I think of this place just now?”
### Terrain proposal
Ravine approach → focus ledge → fissure → upper narrow tunnel → shelf junction → lower chamber/slab → winding ramp → far overlook → safe exit. Add optional high shelf shortcut and lower bypass around loose-stone trap. Suggested three tiers separated by 3–5 m each, with walkable ramps or tested climbing affordances; adjust to avatar metrics. Trap telegraph: fractured floor, pebbles dropping and creak; trigger only on marked unstable section, reset after a short safe interval, bypass on stable edge; recovery ramp if player drops to lower shelf. Place relic out of trap zone. Age 15 cool mineral light and dripping water; age 18 smoke, wooden bracing, military supply traffic and distant alarms. Use the three shelves as shared visual anchors. Test camera clearance, collision, no fall-through, focus persistence, retry and return, single reward and unchanged older armor.

## Common implementation contract
Use stable local anchor IDs, not final global coordinates until terrain survey. Each first and second focus must be achievable with voluntary camera control and accessible tolerance/cues; no forced zoom. Save paired alignment, claim and vista independently. Opening may close on loss of focus, but never strand players. Render only active reality and relevant cells; use shared terrain mesh/LOD and separate dressing where feasible. Claude to return blockout adjacency/anchors, collision and traversal test results, measured WebGPU performance, and design discrepancies. No runtime changes in this handoff.
