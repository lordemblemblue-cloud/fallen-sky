## D19 Scene 1 — A place missing a piece

Age fifteen. Hollow Mountain opens into a sheltered terrace. The inner wall is carved into broad steps, but nearby villages disagree about the monument's origin. A historian kneels beside two loose fragments. An assistant has set up a canvas cover and low tray. They disagree about moving a recently detached piece before rain: the historian wants to record where it fell, the assistant wants to prevent another break.

Historian (pointing with a brush): “This break is fresh.”
Assistant: “And the rain's coming. If it gets wet, we'll lose the edge.”
Hero: “Can we mark where it was before we move it?”
Historian (looking up): “Yes. That would help.”

Accept S23 to mark the position and compare gouges against sheltered dust. If declined, the historian records it independently; D19 remains available. A carved curve on the fragment seems incomplete. The boy examines it in place or handles a survey replica, not stealing a memorial piece. Another curve appears on a second slab; they cannot simply be assembled because the third edge remains in the monument.

Hero: “It's missing something on the left.”
Assistant: “Most of the left side is missing.”
Hero: “No. Just that little bit.”

The assistant places cloth beneath the stone. The boy knows where his hand should rest but cannot explain why. The camera remains free; the two fragment silhouettes and monument edge are authored geometry rather than texture-dependent pixels. Wind through the mountain opening, rain on cloth and brush against stone establish the worksite. A fresh scrape interrupts old dust, suggesting disturbance but not proving deliberate historical revision. If the hero suspects theft, Historian: “A mark is not a witness. We have to look at what else survived.”

## D19 Scene 2 — A wall for the living

Age eighteen. Families rest beneath a new mountain sanctuary shelter. Rock steps and curved ceiling match the younger world's geography, but the monument is new, with fresh tool marks. The older hero already owns a personal chisel; its ultimate origin need not be decided. A survivor brings paper with names written in different hands: some living, some absent, some dead. This is a record of refuge, not just a grave marker.

Survivor (flattening paper): “Let my children know we lived.”
Hero: “Put the names here. I'll keep the piece safe.”
Survivor: “Even the ones who left?”
Hero: “Especially them. They were here.”

The player can copy the provided names, brace a panel or organize a protected work area. The survivor chooses how to record a missing relative: leave space, write their known name or include a family mark. The protagonist does not promise that missing people are dead or alive. A small offcut serves as a practice piece. He finishes a distinctive edge with his chisel while sighting the monument curve and two panels. The player may view from the workbench or safe scaffold landing. Default authored scene flags allow this observation if S23 is skipped; NPCs complete the work with the hero observing. The older hero believes he is performing ordinary work for people who may never return home.

## D19 Scene 4 — Evidence, not a verdict

The table holds a personal chisel with a distinctive shallow notch and worn grip. Beside it lies a protected copy of the missing inscription, separate from S23's detached fragment. Surviving text indicates that people recorded their passage through the sanctuary. Proposed partial line: “Those who came through this place shall have a place here.” The remaining words are unreadable. The protagonist traces the matching emblem and turns the chisel; his thumb finds its notch without looking.

Hero (anger and uncertainty): “This is what they erased.”

His “they” is suspicion, not proof. Cautious variant adds “Or what someone lost. I need to show this to someone.” The older protagonist reaches for the chisel already at his belt; its weight feels as though he has only just received it. He checks that it remains his and returns to his work. No loss, duplicate or explicit memory of the younger chamber. The younger hero unlocks inscription evidence and crafting tool cosmetic. The evidence can corroborate M13, never gate it. Cosmetic grants no compulsory combat stat or economy change.

## D19 Scene 5 — The second focus: leave a place

A narrow light shaft provides a second viewpoint toward the shared mountain arch, curved monument edge and recessed shelf. Align after recovery to reveal the original worksite. A child asks where to put names of people arriving later.

Child: “What if there isn't enough room?”
Survivor: “We'll make another stone.”
Older hero (moving a blank slab): “Leave this one empty for now.”

The younger protagonist sees an empty space on the surviving monument. He had interpreted it as a missing object; now it may also be a deliberate invitation to remember future arrivals. This does not explain the separate erased inscription. His hand moves over the blank area without touching it. The older hero hears a brief scrape of ancient stone where fresh stone should be, then returns to work. Both experience tenderness and uncertainty; neither announces reincarnation or the structure of their lives. Journal records observations rather than cosmological conclusions. Independent ramp returns to terrace. S23 fragment and D19 evidence remain independent objects.
