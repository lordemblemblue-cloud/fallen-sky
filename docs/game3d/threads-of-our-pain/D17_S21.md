# D17 — The Cooling Forge / S21 — The Smith's Last Order
R20 · 26 November 2026 · Expanded scene scripts and proposed blockout. Source: R19 catalog, D17/S21. Catalog requirements: mountain forge; anvil, chimney and slag form hammer; working equipment in original reality; ancient smithy annex and personal blade fitting; weapon upgrade component. S21 half-finished blade with matching forge stamps; metal delivery, finish a blade after tools. Preserve the three catalog dialogue lines and “I remember the heat.” All new characters, item specifics, route geometry and reward tuning are proposals.

## Scene 1 — A forge that has forgotten fire (age 15)
The mountain road climbs through scrub pine, its worn stones turning charcoal-gray where runoff carries old slag. The distant chimney stands against a shallow notch in the ridge. Wind through the broken roof sounds almost like a bellows. The player's approach is slow and exploratory, not an automatic camera sequence: distant hammer-like silhouette, a dark anvil, then the half-finished blade lying on a stone bench. There are no recent footprints near the cold hearth. A young metalworker is sitting on the outside step, rubbing charcoal across a piece of paper to capture the mark on a loose ingot. They are studying old craft methods, not waiting for the hero.
METALWORKER [holds the rubbing against the blade; puzzled]: “The stamp's the same. But this one was never finished.”
HERO [lays a hand on the cold anvil, withdraws it before touching the blade]: “It should be warm.”
METALWORKER [looks at the open roof]: “Nothing's been lit here in my lifetime.”
Player choices: inspect the unfinished blade (“Was this made here?”), offer to compare stamps (“Let me see the rubbing”), or simply examine the room. The metalworker explains that local makers reused the shape of the old mark without knowing who first struck it. In the short response, they point to the two stamps and leave the player free to investigate. The blade is not the reward and must remain a world prop. No demand to take it or to prove the maker's identity.
The player notices a very slight irregularity on the blade's unfinished shoulder: a shallow tool mark near the stamp. The hero instinctively puts his fingers in the position used to hold the piece while finishing it. He frowns at his own gesture. The metalworker notices only that he seems familiar with the work. The player sees evidence of a craft tradition, not evidence that this actual settlement necessarily existed in both realities.

## Scene 2 — The last order (age 18; S21 organic trigger)
The ridge is the same, but the original-world forge is working despite the evacuation. A canvas extension shields a queue of people holding broken hinges, cart brackets and tools. Sparks fall onto blackened stone, and a bellows rises and falls beneath the steady rhythm of the apprentice's foot. The older hero arrives carrying his already-owned blade fitting attached to his weapon. Its small stamp matches the forge mark; do not remove or unequip it to manufacture a quest reward.
An argument has broken out. A defender wants a finished blade before leaving; a cart driver needs a replacement pin so a family can move their belongings. The smith sets down a hammer, removes one glove and looks at both objects before speaking.
SMITH [tired, matter-of-fact]: “A blade is no use if the hands that need it are gone.”
DEFENDER [looks toward the road, grips the empty scabbard]: “If we don't hold the road, that cart won't get anywhere.”
DRIVER [does not raise their voice]: “And if this wheel comes off, we won't get as far as the road.”
HERO [sets the damaged cart pin on the bench]: “Make the tools first.”
The defender's shoulders fall, not in defeat but recognition. They offer to carry metal from the delivery yard instead of waiting. The player can also ask the defender to explain the road conditions first; then the hero says the catalog line after identifying the cart as the bottleneck. The smith prioritizes essential repairs and reserves material for the final blade. No mutually exclusive moral label or artificial countdown. The smith needs metal from the nearby safe yard, not an arbitrary remote collection point. The queue continues to move in the background only when staged animation and safe navmesh permit it.
SMITH [nods toward two bins]: “Good stock in the low box. Offcuts in the high one. Bring what we can use, not everything you can carry.”

## Scene 3 — Work worth doing (S21 branching sequence)
The player may carry the selected metal from the yard, recruit the defender to help move a heavy crate, or ask the apprentice to sort the usable offcuts at the forge. All three paths require the same finite, locally available order allocation. Carrying stock is a short contextual interaction, not an inventory grind. If the player leaves midtask the stock remains reserved and the NPCs take shelter; retry at the bench. The older hero is not punished for failing to help before a migration. The cart driver is not killed as a consequence of an optional quest being skipped.
At the bench, the smith sets out three workpieces: cart pin, tool bracket, and the unfinished blade. The player chooses which of the two civilian repairs to assist first. The apprentice completes the other, and the smith checks both before proceeding to the blade. Use authored interaction points and a clear step sequence: select workpiece, assist with a safe stylized work animation, inspect the completed stamp. No precise timing or crafting skill gate; an optional timing challenge may add presentation feedback only. The action is a game abstraction, not a realistic fabrication tutorial. Keep the hero's hands away from hot surfaces in all animations. A cancellation returns the workpiece to the bench rather than consuming material.
APPRENTICE [brushes soot from a finished bracket]: “Same mark on both.”
DRIVER [tests the repaired cart beside the bench]: “That's all I needed.”
DEFENDER [accepts the finished blade after the repairs]: “I'll walk with them until the bend.”
SMITH [holds the old stamp upright, turning the worn face toward the apprentice]: “Remember which side's up.”
APPRENTICE [smiles, then carefully wraps the stamp]: “The stamp survives.”
The last line does not imply that this original forge is historically identical to the ancient one. The player can remain for the completion conversation, return later, or decline the offered experience/material reward until later. If the player skipped the quest, ambient workers complete the civilian repairs and the smith's blade independently; the authored focus props and corresponding observation remain available. On completion, the repaired cart is parked in a departure bay and the defender and driver later appear at the safe road bend, if the region is active. Their survival is not dependent on a timer or on D17. Reward: proposed crafting XP and modest existing upgrade materials, balance TBD; no duplicate D17 fitting or new currency. The player can examine a rubbing of the stamp in either reality. The younger-world metalworker accepts the rubbing comparison as a separate optional report and offers craft lore; do not require this report to collect D17 or award S21 twice.

## Scene 4 — The hammer in the ruins (D17 first focus)
At age 15, the player stands in a broad observation area outside the ruined forge. A low anvil profile, the narrow chimney and a wedge of slag at the far wall align into a hammer silhouette. The age-18 counterpart aligns working equipment from a protected waiting area: anvil shoulder, chimney side and the dark edge of a stock bin. A short metallic resonance confirms each perspective without demanding an exact frame or forcing the camera. The player may choose either reality's focus first; the other flag persists through actual migrations. S21 completion is not required. If the forge is busy, the focus zone remains clear of NPC paths and active work surfaces.
When both flags resolve, a seam opens behind the ruined forge. A slab slides visually into an adjacent recess, revealing the annex. The older hero momentarily hears the wind in the ruined chimney, despite the warm working forge around him. He shakes his hand as though it has gone cold and checks that the glove is intact. The younger catches a trace of heat on his palm, looks at the dead hearth and says nothing. Both believe they have experienced an odd memory or dream sensation; neither calls it dimensional travel. Keep the physical source of the younger's heat ambiguous to the player. No forced view or involuntary migration on button press. Release focus outside and the seam closes after the threshold is clear; inside, a separate latch opens a dry return corridor.

## Scene 5 — The annex and the fitting
The annex descends into cooler stone. A shallow ash-filled recess holds broken tool molds, and marks on the wall indicate where an old rack once stood. The room is not an active forge; use cold residual slag, no molten metal, and a suggestion of former heat in the discoloration. From the junction the player can walk the broad maintenance gallery or investigate an optional hinged rack shortcut. The rack moves only in a bounded, telegraphed arc; a creak, shadow and visible marked sweep zone warn of movement. A lever in the safe zone lowers it into a bridge across a shallow recessed floor. The gallery bypasses the mechanism completely. Reset on reload or when both sides are clear; no crushed player, no locked return. The old rack is an interactive traversal prop, not a compulsory skill check. A small alcove off the main gallery contains an apprentice's sketch of a stamp; it provides context and an optional lore note without inventing a named civilization.
The central fitting rests on a stone finishing bench. It has the same irregular stamp and tool mark as the older weapon's fitting, but the younger sees the marks through wear and oxidation. He brushes the piece, then pulls his hand back, eyes narrowing. The sound design offers a single soft hammer ring followed by the normal cave ambience. The camera stays player-controlled. A short inspect animation holds the item at chest height with an optional skip; no full-body pose lock.
HERO (15) [under breath, testing the weight in his palm]: “I remember the heat.”
At the original forge, the older protagonist puts a thumb over the fitting already on his weapon and senses the warmth and pride of receiving it for the first time. He does not receive another item, lose the original or gain an extra stat. The smith asks, “Something loose?” The hero checks the fitting and answers, “No. Just checking.” He resumes whatever task was active. The younger reward is one weapon upgrade component, usable with the existing equipment/Blacksmith system subject to its actual progression requirements; the visual fitting is not a second weapon or compulsory craft. Avoid introducing a new enhancement system. Recovery does not depend on spending gold, using credits or completing S21. If inventory is full, use existing safe claim/overflow flow; keep reward pending until a successful authoritative transaction.

## Scene 6 — The forge that stayed open (second focus)
A ventilation terrace above the annex frames the ridge notch, chimney profile and anvil-shaped boulder. After the component claim, the second focus resolves the corresponding original-world view. The older protagonist watches the cart driver check the wheel and the defender shoulder the finished blade. The smith stays behind to repair another worn tool, although the queue is nearly gone. The apprentice sets a stool beside the smith and hands over water. The older hero reaches to take the hammer, but the smith shakes his head, smiling without looking up.
SMITH: “You've done enough for today.”
HERO (18) [looks at the door and the people outside]: “I'll leave the light on.”
The younger sees the scene from the safe terrace and instinctively shades his eyes against a burst of warm light. It is not a cutscene that relocates him physically. He turns back toward the cold chimney and lowers his hand. If S21 was not completed, the same core lived moment shows the smith and apprentice finishing work for the community without crediting the hero with repairs he did not perform; replace the smith's line with “That's the last of this batch,” and the hero answers with the same light-on line. No retcon of player choices.
HERO (15) [after the view fades]: “They kept it going.”
Journal: “The mark is the same. The forge was warm. I found a fitting beneath the old hearth.” Keep journal language observational. Returning to the metalworker allows either sharing the fitting's mark or keeping the experience private. If shown, the worker says, “Then the old pattern wasn't just decoration.” If private, “If you find anything else about the maker, I'll be here.” They do not identify the protagonist as the maker. The emotional conclusion is that useful work outlasts the moment it was performed.
