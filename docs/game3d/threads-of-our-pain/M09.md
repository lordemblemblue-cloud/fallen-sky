# M09 — Beneath the Silver Veil

R29 | Source: R28 catalog M09. Mandatory: abandoned sanctuary, unnamed goddess opening a passage, silver mark disrupts seal on contact, age eighteen recognizes her and witnesses her threaten a child through unstable threshold, two skies in one doorway, catalog dialogue. M10 owns physical interception and escape. Supporting ritual design, dialogue, timing and geometry are proposals, not established implementation. M08 required; optional discoveries never gate entry.

## Scene 1 — The road on the other side of the page

Age fifteen. The copied directions from Wolfveil's archive take the protagonist beside a narrow channel that bends around the foothills. It is late afternoon; warm light catches the thin upper edges of the reeds while the water below is cold and dark. The path is otherwise ordinary. A little farther uphill, a pair of abandoned stone posts frames a road whose paving disappears beneath grass. The first post bears the shelter insignia documented in M08, not the personal mark from M07 or the three-culture crest of D26. The protagonist stops at the same bend he remembers from the evacuation notice, anticipating the curve before he sees it. He is increasingly disturbed by the accuracy of his memories.

Hero (examining the route copy): “The channel turns here. There should be steps above it.”

There are steps, but their edges are softened by age. A folded corner of the route copy flutters against his thumb. Player can inspect the post, the stairs or a worn drainage arch in any order. The objective remains simply Follow the sanctuary road. A wide path around the broken stair is always available. A distant sound like a stone vibrating against another interrupts the ordinary stream noise. A second vibration follows as the player approaches the sanctuary, visible in the water in a small trough even with audio disabled.

Hero (under his breath): “Someone's inside.”

He has come expecting an explanation for missing history; he finds someone actively using a place that the archive said had been abandoned. Do not spawn pursuers here. Give the player time to inspect the entrance, stop or return to the road. An optional note in the margin of his copied route says the sanctuary's inner door was sealed; this is an authored annotation, not proof that the archivist deceived him. The player can read it, put the paper away or bypass the interaction. All choices converge at the entry threshold.

## Scene 2 — The woman who doesn't belong here

At the entrance a stone lintel hangs above a dry receiving chamber. Fallen plaster reveals a band of dark stone. Through an interior arch the hero sees an unfamiliar woman standing before an upright seal. The interior is cold even where the late sunlight touches the floor. Her attention remains fixed on the seal; one hand hovers over a narrow gap, the other traces a slow movement parallel to its edge. The seal is not an ordinary locked door. Its inner contour appears to sit a few centimeters behind the visible stone, producing a depth mismatch when seen from the side. Use a localized emissive boundary and portal surface, not a global screen distortion.

Hostile goddess (speaking to the seal, with controlled impatience): “Her protection still stands in my way.”

Hero (at a distance): “Who are you talking about?”

She stops moving, but does not turn immediately. A thin streak of red light appears on the stone behind her.

Hostile goddess: “You are not supposed to be here.”

Hero: “This place was closed.”

Hostile goddess (looking toward the outside corridor): “Then go back the way you came.”

The player has three responses: ask what she is opening, ask about the other protector, or withdraw to the arch to observe. For the first, she says “A door that should have opened long ago.” For the second, “You know nothing of her.” For withdrawal, she resumes the ritual and murmurs “It will not hold forever.” She is unnerving because she treats the protagonist as an inconvenience, not yet a known adversary. Her identity and the full motive of forcing convergence remain developer-only. No nameplate exposes her identity; dialogue subtitles label her Unknown Woman until the story earns a different label.

The objective becomes Inspect the seal. The player may examine its border from a safe shelf, observe the woman or leave and return before committing. Do not place a compulsory close-up or automatic camera orbit. A prompt explicitly offers Touch the seal; the player can cancel. The woman remains at her ritual station until that input. Her hand movement pauses naturally at inspection so the player never has to race a scripted animation.

## Scene 3 — The sky behind the stone

The hero reaches out. The silver mark on his palm warms. At contact, the seal's narrow line loses its regular shape. The woman lifts her head sharply, and a red-lit portion of the opening widens into a view of a ruined street. Through the broken stone arch the player can still see the green hill outside. For a few seconds both skies are visible in the same frame: a calm amber sky through the outer arch, a dark red sky through the disturbed threshold. The player retains camera control. The geometry at the edge vibrates slightly but the floor does not move beneath him. The visible event is a disturbance, not a skill activation.

Hero (staring through the opening): “That's the sky I remember.”

Hostile goddess (looking at his palm for the first time): “That mark. How?”

Hero: “Do you know what it is?”

She steps closer, stopping before his space. The expression changes from irritation to recognition of the mark, not recognition of his personal identity. She studies his hand, then the opening, and lowers her arm. The portal contracts in an irregular way. A faint double echo sounds from within the opening; a child coughs on the other side of the threshold. The younger hero turns toward the sound.

Hostile goddess: “Take your hand away.”

Hero: “There's somebody there.”

Hostile goddess: “I said take it away.”

Contact is ended by player release or a short authored completion after the three lines. Releasing early still records the disturbance once it has begun; the hero pulls his hand away, and the effect lingers until the safe checkpoint. No mandatory prolonged button hold. If reduced motion is selected, show the two sky colors, a steady edge outline and subtitles instead of tremor. No sudden whiteout. The threshold cannot be crossed by walking or rolling through before M10; use a visible solid rim/ward and a clear line of dialogue, not an invisible wall. The player may look through it and step back freely.

## Scene 4 — Someone on the other side

Perspective changes at the authored safe beat after the hero moves away from the seal. Age eighteen. He is standing in a damaged version of the sanctuary approach, where an old drainage channel runs past a collapsed outer wall. He can hear falling masonry and a muted call from a shelter attendant. He recognizes the goddess from the disturbed threshold, but has no name for her. On the opposite side of a broken arch he sees a child-sized figure standing in a lit patch of the unstable threshold. The younger self is never presented in a clean frontal close-up from the older point of view; the stone edge, light and posture conceal the resemblance without replacing either model. The older hero believes he is seeing a stranger in danger. The player may infer the truth, but the journal must not confirm it.

Older hero (to the attendant): “Who's in there?”

Attendant: “Someone's at the door. I couldn't get across.”

Older hero: “Stay behind the wall.”

He moves toward a broken arch and sees the woman raise a hand toward the child. The sky through the threshold appears green and ordinary, almost painfully so. He recognizes the red sky around him and the same pressure in his ears that he felt when the Moon Goddess held the silver path open, but he does not equate the two women.

Hostile goddess (to the child): “You will answer me.”

Older hero (calling across the chamber): “Leave him alone.”

She turns toward the sound. The older hero's hand closes around his weapon, a practiced motion. The player can approach by the broad fallen lintel route or sheltered side passage; both lead to a safe observation shelf. No attack or body collision between the two selves until M10. A short dialogue variant for the player who waits: Attendant says “Can you see him?” Hero says “Yes. I'm going in.” The attendant leaves for a safe rear alcove. Do not spawn him as a mandatory escort or collateral target.

## Scene 5 — The moment before the attack

Return to age fifteen at the same physical beat: the woman turns partly away from him at the older hero's call. The player hears the voice but cannot see its source clearly. The younger hero notices a silhouette standing across the opening; its features are obscured by the arch. The unknown woman raises her hand again. A thin line of energy gathers above the floor, clearly visible and audible, but the attack itself is reserved for M10. The younger hero instinctively retreats one step in the authored animation; the player is allowed to move back within the staging area before pressing Continue. The objective changes to Escape the sanctuary only when M10 starts. The last spoken line is the younger hero, barely audible: “Someone's there.” M09 completion saves both actors' placement and the threshold state. No cliffhanger death, combat reward or celestial power unlock.
