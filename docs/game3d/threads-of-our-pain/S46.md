## Scene 1 — Someone still on watch
Age fifteen. At the ancient shrine, rainwater runs from a stone lintel into a shallow channel. A narrow trail bends around the ridge and opens into a worn courtyard. Behind the arch are empty bunks visible through a cracked doorway. The hero hears a spear butt strike stone twice. An armored silhouette moves into the passage, placing its body between the visitor and the sleeping quarters. Its weapon is raised with the precision of habit, but its attention is directed toward the empty beds rather than the hero's purse or equipment.

Guardian (steady, hoarse): “No one passes while they sleep.”
Hero (stops outside reach): “Who is sleeping?”
Guardian: “Keep your voice down.”

The spirit advances only to a visible boundary in the courtyard. Trigger on approach or inspection of the watch marker, with clear threat indication and space to retreat. The hero can lower his weapon, ask about the watch, or engage the manifestation. Do not force combat on players merely approaching D31. An independent public shrine path leads to a fallen duty marker; the concealed D31 bunkroom stays a separate discovery.

## Scene 2 — The duty marker
The player can take the side path to an abandoned watch desk. A small bronze marker lies in a shallow dish beside an illegible rota. Its reverse bears a simple notch pattern matching the guardian's shield rim. The hero sees enough to infer the object belongs to the watch, but not enough to identify the person who used it. A loose spear shifts as he approaches; the movement is telegraphed and can be avoided by taking the wider approach. No mandatory damage or puzzle involving tiny texture details. The marker can be inspected, picked up, and presented; no paired focus is required. If D31 has already been completed, its testimony supplies a contextual dialogue alternative but does not replace the marker or grant another gauntlet.

Hero (sets marker on a stone rather than throwing it): “Is this yours?”
Guardian (weapon lowers a little): “The watch doesn't end until someone relieves it.”
Hero: “The people you guarded are gone.”
Guardian (looks toward the doorway, waits): “Then who will tell me to stop?”

The player may say “You've kept your watch,” offer to inspect the bunks together, or stay quiet. The spirit steps to the threshold and notices the empty beds. The hero does not declare that every original civilian died; he is speaking of the empty quarters in front of him. The guardian is permitted to decide to relinquish the watch. This is a compassionate ending, not an exorcism forced by a magic token.

## Scene 3 — The other way to end the watch
If the player chooses combat, the guardian manifests in the courtyard with a readable guard stance and limited attack zones. Its movements favor deliberate thrusts and broad shield pressure; telegraph through silhouette and distinct audio, never require parry timing alone. The player may retreat behind the boundary or disengage and return with the marker. Upon defeat, the manifestation falls to one knee and the weapon becomes translucent; it is not a second physical death for the same living guard. The post-combat scene still allows conversation. Combat does not automatically erase testimony or force a cruel answer.

Guardian (breathing like someone who has forgotten how to rest): “I couldn't let them through.”
Hero: “There is no one behind you now.”
Guardian: “Then let me rest.”

On the peaceful path, the final line is spoken after the guardian has inspected the room. On either path, the guardian places the weapon beside the door and its silhouette fades. Leave the courtyard quiet, with no loot explosion. A simple local remembrance note becomes available. Do not attribute the spirit's origin to the hostile goddess or identify it as the living guard from the original reality as a fact.

## Scene 4 — The living watch
Age eighteen. The corresponding ridge shelters a working guard post. Lanterns are low, damp cloaks hang from hooks, and people sleep on the bunks while a living guard watches the doorway. He checks the passage and asks the older protagonist to relieve him briefly so he can bring a cup of water to a civilian. The player holds the marked position, listens to the approaching footsteps, and directs an authorized refugee to the shelter. A short defensive encounter is optional if the established local threat permits, with a noncombat assistance alternative. The guard returns and takes the watch back.

Living guard (sets the cup down): “I thought I'd be frightened when it got quiet.”
Older hero: “Are you?”
Guard: “More than when it was loud.”
Older hero (nods, looks out): “Go sit for a minute. I'll call you.”

The older hero flexes his already-owned gauntlet, briefly remembering the chill of a stone threshold he cannot place. This is a sensory echo, not an acquisition or D31 focus completion. The younger hero feels a faint warmth at the wrist after the spirit rests. Exact chronology relative to M05 remains unknown; neither guard is assigned a definitive cross-reality identity. This scene illuminates M18's question of protective duty without declaring all guardians divine or duty inherently harmful.
