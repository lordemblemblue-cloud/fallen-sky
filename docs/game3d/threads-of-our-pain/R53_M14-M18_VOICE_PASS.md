## M14 — Three Arrows
### M14.V01 — A chair for someone who is absent (hall, before reports)
At the fountain the sound of running water continues even after the market has fallen quiet. Inside the diplomatic hall, Sable Vey reaches for the unused fourth chair, intending to put it away. She hesitates. It was set there for the envoy who was meant to bring back the agreement. Tome notices her hesitation; he begins to speak and stops. Henut puts a folder on the table without occupying the chair. Livilla makes room for the hero beside the table rather than placing him behind a faction representative. The camera remains over the player's shoulder, with a clear route to the report station.
Sable (controlled, no ceremonial announcement): “We set four places yesterday.”
Hero: “Was the agreement finished?”
Sable: “Not signed. He was bringing it back so we could read it together.”
Tome (after a breath): “We were going to argue about the words again.”
Henut: “I would rather be arguing.”
Livilla (looks at the chair): “So would I.”
If the hero asks for the envoy's name or family details, Sable declines gently, consistent with the primary script: “His family has had enough strangers asking about him today.” If the player asks who is responsible, she slides the reports forward: “We have three accounts. That's not the same as three answers.” If the player chooses silence, the representatives open the folders themselves. The existing report objective begins in every branch. No clue is awarded from grief alone.
### M14.V02 — The cost of an unsigned report (hall return)
The porter has given an honest but incomplete account; the camps have supplied contradictory records. At the table, Sable starts writing a concise summary. A messenger waiting by the doorway asks which accusation should be carried to the officers. His hand is already reaching for the paper. The hero sees how easily an uncertain sentence can become an instruction once the messenger leaves. No camera close-up of alleged attack implements is necessary; use paper, voices and waiting faces.
Messenger: “They'll want the name of the side responsible.”
Hero: “We don't have one.”
Messenger: “What am I supposed to tell them?”
Livilla: “What you were told. Not what they'll want to hear.”
Sable: “Write that the claims disagree. Leave the rest unsigned until someone can stand behind it.”
If the player selected public disclosure, the notice is copied for the public board. If private sharing was chosen, the same text goes to the three representatives. If civilian access was prioritized, attach the neutral-route request rather than replacing the findings. The messenger leaves by the existing door. Outside, a cart turns around because of a road closure unrelated to the player's wording. The hero cannot save the negotiation by choosing a perfect sentence. End with the existing M15 readiness cue, not an accusation against a deity.

## M15 — A War That Should Not Be

At the Gate Fields culvert a warden's assistant has drawn a line down a slate to separate waiting people from those who arrived. A traveler asks whether they should stand with people wearing their own colors. The assistant looks at the slate, then at the frightened family. A farm lane and raised service path are both visible, though the first may be closed by the disputed incident outcome. The hero's attention shifts from the distant armies to the narrow strip of mud on which the family waits.
Assistant: “Name, please.”
Traveler: “Which list?”
Assistant: “The one for people who haven't crossed.”
Traveler: “I'm not from here.”
Hero: “You still need to get across.”
A child has trouble lifting a small bundle. The player can help through one optional interaction, ask the assistant to take it, or continue to the existing escort prompt. No separate reward. A cart uses the compatible broad path or staffed transfer that preserves the original escort rules. The slate is updated only when the actual group reaches shelter; an interrupted escort is marked waiting, not lost.
### M15.V02 — The road under the other sky (original receiving yard)

The damaged signpost resembles the younger world's sign, but the sounds beyond it are entirely different. The guard is listening for unfamiliar creatures rather than human soldiers. An attendant carries a repaired lantern from the shelter. The older hero watches the light settle on a stone step he remembers from the other life, then makes room for the people coming through.
Attendant: “Can we close the door?”
Guard: “Not until we've checked the lower yard.”
Hero: “I'll check with you.”
Guard (noticing his attention on the step): “Something wrong?”
Hero: “I keep expecting to see a farm cart here.”

## M16 — When the Forgotten Speak
### M16.V01 — A memory with no accusation (municipal interview)
The detained soldier sits near an intact window, watching a roof whose tiles are undamaged. The interviewer moves a cup closer; the soldier thanks him without looking away. He is trying to establish which details belong to his current surroundings, rather than reciting a perfectly preserved account. The player may take a chair or stand beside the window. A quiet sound cue of falling water is captioned as remembered, never as actual structural damage.
Soldier: “I went home this morning. My door was there. My neighbor was hanging washing.”
Hero: “And the roof?”
Soldier: “Still there. I kept looking for the gap.”
Hero: “You knew it wasn't broken?”
Soldier (finally turns): “I could see it wasn't. That didn't stop me remembering it.”
If the player has consent to reuse S40 testimony, ask only the new environmental question rather than replaying the entire interview. If consent is absent or the soldier is unavailable, the municipal lookout offers an anonymous account of the same volunteered details; no private name is disclosed. The core card records intact current roof as observation and damaged remembered roof as testimony. No diagnosis, accusation, migration or reincarnation claim is attached. The player can defer further questions and still visit the family and shelter. The soldier returns to an ordinary seated idle, not a looping panic animation.
### M16.V02 — Let him go home (administrative review)
After verifying the original-world ruined roof and shelter conditions, the younger hero returns to the municipal office. The clerk has several forms waiting, but no form that explains why a person remembers a disaster the town has not suffered. The hero places only the present-world inspection and a consent-limited statement where the officials can read them. His firsthand older-life observations inform his own understanding; he cannot offer them as physical exhibits carried between worlds.
Clerk: “If he says it happened, do we put that in the record?”
Hero: “Put down what he said. Put down what we saw, too.”
Warden: “The roof is intact. Nobody has reported a threat from him.”
Clerk: “Then what are we holding him for?”
Warden (sets the form aside): “Nothing in this report justifies keeping him here.”
Where the existing outcome is protected lodging rather than release to the street, the warden adds, “We'll offer a room, not another locked door.” If the witness declines public disclosure, do not announce his identity when opening the exit. The player can ask for privacy or simply leave the officials to finish the paperwork. The family remains a separate household, with its own remembered sensations and present relationships. The subsequent observatory lead comes through the existing warden/guide route, not a new informant. The emotional gain is that the protagonist has protected somebody despite not yet understanding the memories.

## M17 — Two Skies, One Wound
### M17.V01 — The instrument did not make it happen (ridge observatory)
After checking the arch, peak notch and yard, the hero watches the keeper release the brass sighting ring. The mechanism falls still. At the fixed viewing direction the anomalous contour is faintly visible even without touching the apparatus. A gust moves the survey papers but not the distant outline. The keeper anchors the papers under a weight, checks her old drawing and deliberately turns away from the mechanism. This insert clarifies that the observatory measures a pre-existing anomaly; it is not the source of convergence or the means of travel.
Keeper: “I thought it was the instrument. I stopped using it for a day.”
Hero: “Did the shape go away?”
Keeper: “No. I could still see it from the terrace.”
Hero: “Then we're looking at something. We're not making it.”
Keeper: “That's what I wanted someone else to see.”

### M17.V02 — The people are not the fault (Moon Goddess, after comparison)
The primary revelation remains in this chapter: the hostile goddess is deliberately making the histories resemble each other to weaken their separation. The younger hero has witnessed human soldiers and external invaders causing similar displacement. He puts the two observations beside one another and sees people seeking shelter, rather than an abstract diagram of two worlds. He becomes angry at the human leaders, but the Moon Goddess redirects his anger toward the deliberate exploitation of suffering without excusing human choices.
Hero: “So if they stop fighting, she loses?”
Moon Goddess (looks out toward the valley): “She would lose something she is using. I cannot promise the rest would follow.”
Hero: “Then why won't you tell them?”
Moon Goddess: “Would they hear a warning, or only another voice telling them whom to obey?”
Hero: “I can't just watch them.”
Moon Goddess: “Then help them. Do not ask them to suffer less because their pain is dangerous to you.”
This is an optional follow-up to the existing reveal, not a second exposition scene. If the player asks about the two lives, preserve the primary answer that both are real, leaving the full life-force explanation to M18. Do not assert an exact threshold, a named collaborator or the guilt of a specific empire. The player returns the records to the keeper with the existing disclosure choice and may leave for the sanctuary. The two skies are not a playable third-world selector.

## M18 — The Twelve and the Price
### M18.V01 — Counsel without permission (inner gallery)
The younger protagonist stands near the damaged seal while the Moon Goddess continues attending to it. The twelve motifs around the dais remain architectural symbols, not assembled gods who vote on his future. The keeper leaves space between himself and the hero. After the primary explanation that his life force remained connected, the hero asks what protection actually preserved. The Goddess looks at his hand, then at the model; she does not claim that all survivors reached safety or that she knows every consequence of her intervention.
Hero: “When you reached for us, did you know what would happen to everyone who couldn't cross?”
Moon Goddess: “I knew what I was trying to prevent. I did not know every life I would reach.”
Hero: “And the people who were left?”
Moon Goddess: “They are not made less real because I could not reach them.”
Hero (after a pause): “Then don't ask me to call that world finished.”
Moon Goddess: “I won't.”
This follow-up is available after the three essential evidence stations. If the player expresses fear of either proposal, the keeper offers the same neutral testimony prompts rather than a morality score. No final option is confirmed, no sacrifice is described as inevitable, and no deity identity is added. The scene leads to the existing survivor consultation. Keep a short silence before the objective update so the emotional beat is not interrupted by a reward sound.
