## D23 — Scene 1: Three broken wheels

Age fifteen. A trade road rises toward a saddle between two ridges. Three abandoned wagons have been moved out of the main road by travelers. One wheel remains attached, another rests against a boulder, and a third has been mounted above a roadside shelter to hold a canvas awning. They are ordinary objects before the alignment, not glowing quest icons. A wheelwright checks a damaged axle while watching distant wagons approach. The road is dry and winds through low grass; a stream below the narrow bridge gives the route a familiar shape. Dust trails and canvas flaps indicate direction of travel without requiring audio.

Wheelwright (tapping a rim): “That's the third cart this week.”
Hero: “What happened to the first two?”
Wheelwright: “They left the wheels here. Carried what they could.”
Hero (studies three rims): “They ought to be further apart.”
Wheelwright: “What ought to?”
Hero: “Nothing. I thought I remembered where they stood.”

The player can ask about the road, examine a rim, or look from the ridge. The three wheel silhouettes form one circle when their arcs overlap, with a distinctive missing section at the top. Fixed rim segments or spoke hubs provide reliable alignment even when canvas moves. The focus prompt appears only near a usable viewpoint. No camera grab; adjustable tolerance, standing or seated-height anchors and an optional abstract alignment preview after discovery. The preserved-world half persists when the player leaves.

## Scene 2 — Keep the carts together

Age eighteen. The corresponding saddle lies along an evacuation road. Three refugee carts have been drawn into formation beside a shallow drainage cut; they create a protected walking lane rather than a barricade. The older protagonist already carries a small escort badge, a simple stamped metal disc with a broken-circle emblem that a quartermaster issued before this scene. Its purpose is to identify someone who has agreed to guide civilians, not faction membership. The player can inspect it before discovering the younger relic. Three cart-wheel arcs align with the same missing section seen by the younger hero. The formation is anchored by stones even if S30 has moved the carts; stationary spare wheels are a state-equivalent geometry fallback.

Guide (checking a cart strap): “If we spread out, they'll lose sight of us.”
Hero: “Then keep the carts together. Leave room to walk.”
Civilian: “Where do we go if the bridge fails?”
Hero: “The hillside. See that split in the rock? There's shelter there.”

The player sights along the carts from a safe ridge, records the original half of the symbol, and can help the group or leave the authored scene. The original world contains invaders, not human faction war; characters of several cultures are cooperating out of necessity. No requirement to complete S30 to obtain either half. The older hero believes this is a practical route, not a memory puzzle. A momentary pressure sensation in his hand is the only echo before recovery.

## Scene 3 — The road underneath the road

On loss of focus the visible seam closes, but an occupied interior remains solid and has an independent egress. If the player releases focus outside before stepping through, re-align; if streaming is incomplete, hold a nonblocking loading indicator and do not expose a false floor. The hidden supply tunnel slopes into a broad receiving chamber. Old shelves bear the rectangular discoloration left by removed crates. Stone troughs once kept runoff out of the walkway. A faded arrow points toward an unloading bay rather than a treasure room. The player can inspect a small ledger with route notes and a carved count of water jars; these are optional, not proof of a specific surviving empire or population. Age and materials show the place is ancient, even though the preserved world's road need never have been an evacuation road historically.

At the junction, a weighted cargo gate provides a short route. Its counterweight hangs behind a grate; a cracked wooden brace, squeak and visible travel arc warn the player. Move an adjacent lever to secure the gate before crossing or use the wider dry loading gallery. No timing puzzle is required. If the gate closes during the optional shortcut, a catch shelf and manual return latch allow safe recovery; checkpoint reset returns it to a known position. A side alcove reconnects to the gallery and contains optional supply records, not another mandatory relic. No enemy is required; if a future designer adds wildlife, do not spawn it in the gate or focus space.

The older counterpart touches the badge already fixed to his strap, and the sound of a wagon wheel comes into focus for him as if he has just been entrusted with the badge.

## S30 — Scene 1: Which empire do you serve?

The optional quest begins from a witnessed dispute on the trade road, not a board marker. A merchant with a damaged wheel has asked to travel with a mixed caravan. A nervous checkpoint guard insists the caravan is easier to inspect if the traders separate by origin. Another merchant offers to abandon the rival wagon to avoid delay. The scene is a disagreement about responsibility, not proof of a particular empire's cruelty. All factions include ordinary travelers and reasonable people. Names and specific emblems remain provisional; no forced allegiance.

Merchant (eyes the player): “Which empire do you serve?”
Hero: “The people on this road.”
Guard: “Then keep them moving.”
Wheelwright: “That one's not going anywhere until we lift the axle.”
Rival trader (quietly): “Leave my cart. I'll walk.”
Hero: “You can walk if you want. You don't have to be left behind.”

Choices: offer escort, mediate at checkpoint, ask guard and wheelwright to coordinate, or decline. If declined, caravan stays at a safe pull-off and can be re-approached later; there is no punitive offscreen massacre or ticking timer. The mandatory quest premise, if accepted, is refusal to abandon people because of their origin. Players may voluntarily consolidate loads with NPC consent, but no choice requires them to sacrifice a rival trader. If the player attempts to split the group, the guard suggests a separate inspection lane that reconvenes on the other side, preserving safety and agency.

## S30 — Scene 2: A narrow bridge

At the bridge, old wagons block the widest approach. A raised beam catches a wheel and forces a stop. Players can clear the obstruction with two NPC helpers, negotiate a staggered crossing with the guard, or survey a longer raised side route where carts can pass one at a time. Each route has different conversation, not a hidden morality score. Optional fictional road raiders may approach a designated outer area after a visible warning; use existing combat roles or persuade the guard to cover the group while the player moves the carts. Civilians retreat to authored holds, never serve as damage sponges. The peaceful route requires the same narrative responsibility but no combat win. No compulsory real-time escort timer; checkpoints segment progress.

If the player negotiates, Guard: “Two carts at a time. I'll count them through.” Merchant: “Even his?” Guard: “I said two carts.” If player helps lift: Wheelwright: “Wait until I'm braced. There.” Hero: “Tell me when to stop.” If the longer path is chosen: Trader: “It'll cost an hour.” Hero: “Then we'll arrive an hour later.” Deliver these as practical choices, not lectures.
