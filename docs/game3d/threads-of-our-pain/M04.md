# M04 — The Forgotten War

R24 | Expanded main campaign script. Source: original catalog in v1.9 R23, M01–M03. Mandatory: fifteen-year-old loses awareness after M03; playable age eighteen awakens beneath a burning sky in original city outskirts, tests melee/range/defense, fights invading entities with guards, escorts civilians, holds retreat corridor, preserves equipment progression. M05 owns the barrier crossing and original body's death. Supporting NPCs, layout and combat tuning below are proposals, not approved final assets.

## Scene 1 — Not the forest

Blackness follows the final sound of the Fractured Beast. Begin with a steady low rumble, then the rasp of a wooden wheel dragged across cobblestones. The protagonist opens his eyes at ground level beside an overturned cart in the outskirts of a city. Ash coats his sleeve. His hands look older, and his boots are unfamiliar to the player, but he moves with the competence of someone who has worn them before. Do not display a dream transition, a timeline label or a reincarnation explanation. The scene is a playable reality, not a vision.

A guard crouches near him with a bundle of spare equipment. The guard's face is turned away toward a gate where people are gathering; he is not astonished by the protagonist's appearance. He assumes the young man has been stunned. The older protagonist remembers the familiar road and the immediate duty in this life, yet the memory of the woodland clearing remains unnaturally vivid. He searches the sky for the moon instead of answering the guard immediately.

Guard (short breath, beckoning): “Grab a weapon!”

Hero (sitting up, scanning the road): “Where am I?”

Guard: “East of the city. On the road. Look at me. Can you stand?”

Hero (tests his footing): “Yes. There was a woman. A light—”

Guard (not dismissive, but urgent): “Tell me when we get inside. Come on.”

A child calls for a parent beyond the cart. The protagonist looks toward the sound before checking his weapon belt. The player regains ordinary camera and movement control on a safe paved patch. No enemies can reach this recovery patch. Optional inspect interactions: intact name tag on his own pack, an established weapon grip, a roadside waystone whose split shape recalls M01. These are evidence of an existing life, not proof of the underlying cosmology. If the player looks at his hands, play a short idle examination without blocking locomotion. Camera cuts are optional on replay; caption the guard and the cart sound.

## Scene 2 — Three ways to keep a road open

The guard leads the player to a sheltered supply yard. One wall has fallen outward, exposing a narrow view of the city roofs beneath crimson clouds. A captain stands beside a chalk plan, pointing people away from the damaged main arch. An equipment rack holds a melee weapon, a bow and a shield. The captain does not give a lecture about classes; each item is there because someone brought it back or has not yet collected it.

Captain (firm, not shouting at the hero): “Keep the road open. There are people behind us.”

Guard: “Which can you handle?”

Hero (looks at the rack, then at his own equipment): “Give me a moment.”

Choose practice in any order. Damage: practice a narrow melee strike against a padded post; recover, then strike again without requiring a wide arc. Range: draw and release a bow at a straw target, with a visible charge indicator and optional movement. Defense: raise the shield against a training partner's clearly announced padded tap, then move to protect a marked space. No friendly damage or ammunition cost in practice. Follow the existing weapon rules and control bindings; these are not new global stamina or lock-on rules. On touch devices show the matching controls, not keyboard-only prompts. A skip option grants tutorial completion, not items twice. The player's current equipped gear is preserved in the loadout snapshot; if starter equipment is needed, grant it only once and let the player select one role without deleting other owned items. Do not silently equip all three weapon types or hard-lock character progression to this first selection.

Guard, after melee: “Good. Keep your feet clear of the cart.”

Guard, after bow: “Watch the gap. Don't shoot through the people.”

Guard, after shield: “That's it. Make room behind you.”

Hero (if asked whether ready): “I think so.” / “Let me try another.” / “Show me where they need help.”

The captain accepts every role. He gives a route instruction rather than a kill quota. A civilian pushing a handcart stops at the yard's exit; her passenger is asleep beneath a blanket. She asks whether the road is clear. The captain turns toward the player instead of promising something he has not checked.

## Scene 3 — The road belongs to the people on it

The player enters a shallow street with two branching routes. On the broad main road, a broken wagon partly blocks the passage; the side route is sheltered by stone shopfronts but too narrow for the largest carts. The player can lift or mark the obstruction with a guard, guide walking civilians through the side lane, or take the raised firing position and signal the others to work. All choices converge at a covered refuge court. The captain's orders are localized; no player must defend two distant points simultaneously.

Cart driver (trying to keep a wheel from slipping): “We can leave the sacks. Just not the blanket.”

Hero: “Keep the blanket. Which end needs lifting?”

Guard (if player chose range): “I'll take the wheel. Tell me when the gap's clear.”

Civilian (if shield route): “Can I walk behind you?”

Hero: “Yes. Stay with the others. We'll move together.”

The first invading entity emerges in sight, not behind the camera. It is an unfamiliar nonhuman form with a dark core and distorted limbs; it should not be a copy of M03's wolf-dragon or imply that creature was the only invader. A guard warns the player and civilians stop behind cover. The player can defeat the entity, interrupt its advance while allies finish it, or protect the evacuation corridor until the guard secures it. Proposed encounter uses a small number of simultaneous enemies (start 2–3 on normal, adjust after profiling), one readable threat at a time during the first tutorial. Use clearly telegraphed attacks with recoveries, no forced target lock or camera snap. A ranged target is reachable from ground level; no weapon role can be softlocked by enemy placement. Keep civilian movement out of active attack volumes, no civilians used as damage targets in tutorial. The combat is real to the characters, but tutorial failure restarts the local encounter with the same civilian group at the last safe node.

At the refuge court the passenger awakens. The driver puts a hand on the blanket rather than making a speech. A guard counts the arrivals by looking at faces, not issuing an abstract objective tally. The protagonist recognizes an ordinary gesture from the forest but cannot place the person. He asks whether the road beyond leads home; the answer is practical rather than metaphysical.

Driver: “Which home?”

Hero (after a pause): “Never mind. Keep going.”

## Scene 4 — Hold until the last cart

A second group arrives at a junction with a cracked stone arch. The city gate in the distance appears to be lit by intermittent silver lines, but at this distance the protagonist cannot identify their source. The captain orders a retreat corridor. The player chooses a station: close approach with melee, rear line with bow, or civilian lane with shield. Stations are suggestions, not restrictions; any role may change position. A short defensive sequence advances after each civilian group clears a safe boundary and the current threat is resolved. No arbitrary survival timer if all groups are already through. Proposed 2 intervals separated by a safe pause to replenish practice supplies and speak to the guard.

Captain: “Not the arch. The road under it. Keep that open.”

Hero: “How many are left?”

Captain: “One cart and the people walking behind it.”

Guard (if the player has struggled): “Don't chase them. Stay here. I'll cover your side.”

The final cart passes. The captain gestures for the protagonist to withdraw. The player may help the guard carry a dropped bag, check the rear or proceed immediately. No extra reward for remaining in the danger zone after evacuation; threats despawn only out of sight, or withdraw visibly. The road does not collapse under the player without warning. A distant pulse of silver becomes visible along the river bend. The older protagonist turns toward it and hears a faint resonance resembling the Goddess's voice in the clearing. It is not yet intelligible speech.

Hero: “That light. I saw it before.”

Captain (looking toward the river): “Then follow it. The others are heading that way.”

Hero: “You're coming?”

Captain: “As soon as the rear is clear. Go.”

The player walks or runs toward M05_EVAC_ROAD. End with a playable transition, not a forced blackout; M05 handles the fatal threshold and the Goddess's strand. The captain's fate is deliberately unresolved. The age-fifteen body is absent from this segment and no new revelation is presented to the younger hero. Later recollection may be inaccurate because the protagonist mistakes the migration for a dream, not because the original events are fictional.

## Quest state and encounter implementation

State progression: M03_COMPLETE → M04_AWAKEN_SAFE → ROLE_PRACTICE_OR_SKIP → ROLE_SELECTED → COURT_GROUP_SAFE → RETREAT_GROUP_A_SAFE → RETREAT_GROUP_B_SAFE → ROAD_EXIT_CONFIRMED → M04_COMPLETE. Practice checks independent and repeatable, selection confirmation once per checkpoint. Player can backtrack to the supply yard before the final hold, leave the main quest at safe nodes, resume after reload with group states and loadout intact. On death restore encounter snapshot, don't duplicate supplies, completion or equipment. If an NPC gets stuck, reset to nearest validated safe hold node with an appropriate walk animation; do not teleport across visible combat. No failure for selecting the wrong role. Rewards: proposed standard progression XP and one initial equipment selection if not already owned; actual values determined by economy owner. Do not grant D18 Air Recovery, D21 lining or any optional relic by default. No quest requires previous optional discoveries.
