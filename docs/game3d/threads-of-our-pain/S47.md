# S47 — The Twelve's Broken Mosaic
R58 | Catalog: Pilgrim caves; recover mosaic fragments depicting different responses to suffering, no fixed pantheon identities. Original murals offer morale to survivors. Links M18 and D32. Proposed fragment art and interpretation are not identities, motives or approved divine history.

## Scene 1 — The missing pieces
Age fifteen. Sunlight reaches only the first recess of the pilgrim caves, where a traveler has spread a worn cloth across a stone bench. Small fragments of colored stone sit on the cloth, arranged by thickness rather than by hue. The main mosaic is an incomplete circle with twelve distinct motifs. Some show sheltering hands, others figures carrying burdens or standing at a distance; none has a nameplate. Water has loosened several pieces and carried them toward a dry cistern. The pilgrim keeps trying to join two tiles with edges that do not quite fit.

Pilgrim (holds a fragment up to the light): “Not every god answered alike.”
Hero: “What did they choose?”
Pilgrim (sets the fragment down): “Look at what survived.”
Hero: “You don't know either?”
Pilgrim (smiles faintly): “I know what people have said about it. That's not the same thing.”

Trigger on examining the damaged public mosaic or speaking to the pilgrim. The hero can ask to help preserve the fragments, read the traveler's notes, or leave. The quest is about conserving evidence and listening to traditions, not unlocking twelve deity identities. The pilgrim explicitly distinguishes personal interpretation from observed design. Players may complete the public conservation route without D32, and D32's hidden notebook gallery must not be opened by S47.

## Scene 2 — What the cave kept
The player explores three connected public recesses. In the first, a fragment showing a curved shelter motif lies near a low channel. A shallow puddle reflects the arch; a broad dry path reaches the fragment without a jump. In the second, a relief depicting hands passing an object rests near a fractured support. Dust falls before the player reaches the narrow route; a wider bypass remains open. In the third, a partially weathered motif lies beside the dry cistern. The player can copy its outline and recover the loose piece after inspecting the stable ledge, rather than climbing over an unsupported edge. These are conservation interactions with contextual prompts, not a separate focus discovery. Each recovered piece retains its provenance and orientation so the restoration can be done in any order.

Hero (at the second recess): “This one doesn't look like the others.”
Pilgrim, if accompanying within safe distance: “It may be another hand. Or another century. Don't scrape the edge to make it fit.”
Hero (at the weathered fragment, quieter): “There's barely anything left.”
Pilgrim: “Then keep the part that's left.”

If the player takes the wrong route, no fragment is permanently destroyed. A checkpoint at the bench preserves recovered pieces. Optional cave wildlife stays in an outer recess outside conservation stations and can be bypassed; no guardian attack, required combat or new divine power. Accessibility mode describes shape and edge pattern in text or audio, with selection-based matching. Avoid color-only recognition, timed camera angles and compulsory precision movement.

## Scene 3 — A circle with room for doubt
Back at the bench, the pilgrim helps the hero place the recovered pieces into compatible positions. The player rotates or selects each matching outline; unsuccessful placements return to the tray. The restoration deliberately leaves gaps where evidence is absent. A single scene shows the twelve motifs as distinct artistic shapes without naming their subjects. If the player has the D32 notebook, an optional line acknowledges a familiar sketch, but does not require or modify the notebook reward. The hero can choose which interpretation to record: people helping each other, uncertainty about divine intervention, or a neutral description of the image. None is marked correct.

Hero: “That one is reaching out. This one is standing apart.”
Pilgrim: “That's how I see it too. Another traveler told me the empty space was someone waiting.”
Hero: “Which one is true?”
Pilgrim (takes a charcoal rubbing of the edge): “Write down what we can agree we saw. Leave the rest open.”

The restored public mosaic becomes a persistent environmental change. A journal record distinguishes observed motifs, the pilgrim's interpretation and the player's selected reflection. M18 retains authority over its own revelations; no hidden identity or ending requirement is unlocked. The incomplete circle is more meaningful than a perfect restoration because the player can see the boundary between evidence and imagination.

## Scene 4 — The mural that helped people endure
Age eighteen, original reality. A corresponding hillside shelters civilians behind timber screens. Their mural is not an ancient mosaic: it is paint, cloth and chalk assembled by ordinary hands. The older protagonist already owns his pilgrim notebook. A painter asks him to hold a panel steady while a child adds a small shape to a damaged section. The player can carry the panel to a safe wall, help illuminate the work, or read the names of the contributors if they consent. Do not add a compulsory battle to the shelter. Outside, distant invasion sounds remain audible but never interrupt the civilian interaction with a scripted casualty.

Child (looks up at the panel): “Can I put this here?”
Painter: “If it fits, yes.”
Older hero: “What is it supposed to be?”
Child: “A place where we can all sleep.”

The older hero opens the notebook he already carries and sees a familiar curved shape. For an instant he thinks of a cool cave and wet stone, then returns to holding the panel. The younger hero later hears the soft rasp of chalk when he looks at a repaired tile. Neither assumes the painter and pilgrim are the same person. The mural records how civilians used symbolism to sustain hope, not evidence that particular gods answered the invasion. Exact local chronology is unresolved; genuine original visit only. If missed, leave correspondence pending. The third reality, if approved through M19, may have a public mosaic devoted to healing and ordinary care but cannot bear an invasion-history inscription.
