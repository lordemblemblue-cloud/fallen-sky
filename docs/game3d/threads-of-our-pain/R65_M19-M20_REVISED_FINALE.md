## M19.R65.01 — What the goddess could save
The last hostile tether breaks. The older hero's shelter report remains a record of the people actually observed, not a verdict on everyone in the world. On the younger side of the ring, dust falls from the arch and the sounds of the living settlement return. The Moon Goddess lowers her hands, looking at the intact stone rather than the hero. The keeper places the register on the bench, leaving the passage clear. Allow free walking, sitting or silent inspection; no damage, timer or forced choice.
Hero (15, rubbing the silver mark): “You took us away from it. Why is it still here?”
Moon Goddess (a long pause): “I could give you somewhere the destruction could not reach in the same way. I could not make that place yours forever.”
Hero: “But they lived there. I know them.”
Moon Goddess (looks directly at him): “Yes. Every day they lived was real.”
Hero: “Then what happens when it can no longer hold them?”
Moon Goddess: “That is why I cannot choose for you.”
If asked why she cannot simply make the refuge permanent, she answers, “There are limits even to what I can give. I could keep the door open, not change what belonged beyond it.” This is metaphor, not a finalized cosmic law or literal portal. If asked who caused the invasion, she says she cannot answer what she does not know; do not identify the hostile goddess or Hesper. If silent, the keeper closes the register gently and waits. Player knows the refuge was temporary, not imaginary; exact origin of the shared life force and permanent migration technique remain writer-only or unresolved.

## M19.R65.02 — The weight of either road
The goddess indicates the two horizons without spawning fully rendered alternate worlds. The hero first looks at the road from the preserved settlement, then at a bounded projection of the original damaged ridge. The two options concern his existence and which history will prevail, not a reward for dying or a test of generosity.
Hero: “If I give up the life I had at eighteen?”
Moon Goddess: “You will wake in the world you fought to restore. But the lives you knew here may be far behind you by then.”
Hero (fingers tighten on the bench): “How far?”
Moon Goddess: “Longer than the moment will feel to you. I cannot promise that they will still be waiting.”
Hero: “And if I stay with what happened?”
Moon Goddess: “This refuge can bear what the other world could not. Its land may survive, but it will carry the wounds. You will have to live with what follows.”
Hero: “We would have to rebuild.”
Moon Goddess: “Yes. I can give you that chance. I cannot do the work for you.”
The keeper distinguishes verified losses from unknown whereabouts. A plain confirmation screen must explicitly explain that sacrifice places the people known at fifteen in the ancient past, while refusal fuses the preserved landscape with the original devastation and allows society to recover. Neither promises universal survival. Equal emphasis and campaign recognition proposed; no branch-only power preview. Let the player review, leave to safe checkpoint or decline commitment. Missing equipment/save policy blocks shipping the confirmation, not reading the draft.

## M20.R65.A1 — Grass beneath his hands
Age eighteen. A hand presses into green grass. The camera starts close enough to see ordinary blades bending, then returns to normal exploration height without requiring the player to look upward. Birdsong replaces the distant alarms. The hero looks for smoke and finds a quiet, ordinary sky. He stands, steps forward, then looks back at the ridge as though expecting the fire to return. Let the player examine grass, the sky or the familiar split ridge; any order, no forced focus or equipment requirement.
Hero (almost a whisper): “It's still here.”
He recognizes a contour that was present during evacuation. His realization is not that the last years were a dream; he understands that he was fighting for the survival of his own reality and existence. He closes his eyes for a moment, hears no alarm, and says, “I thought I was trying to get home. I was trying to make sure there would be a home.”
The restored landscape has no active burning sky. Do not carry over the previous mandatory amnesia draft. Hero (sets his hands against a loose signpost): “Tell me where I can help.”
