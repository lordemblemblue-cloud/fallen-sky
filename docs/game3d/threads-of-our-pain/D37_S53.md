## D37 Scene 1 — Empty shelves, full marks

Age fifteen. A windswept ridge overlooks an abandoned frontier store. Three carved marks remain on a low crate end, a weathered beam and a stone threshold. They appear unrelated from ground level. A narrow shelf on the ridge provides a safe viewing angle; a small patch of grass moves across the lower carving in wind. The player notices empty storage recesses and a hand-written thank-you inscription protected beneath an overhang. These are distinct from the Hollow Mountain names and D26 coalition receipt. A traveler points out a broken cart road without knowing what the store once held.

Traveler: “There's no grain in there. I checked last winter.”
Hero: “Why are the shelves numbered?”
Traveler: “Someone must have expected to come back.”
Hero: “Or expected somebody else to.”

The player can inspect a crate mark, trace the old inscription or explore the public ruined store. On the ridge, voluntary camera movement aligns all three marks into an incomplete rectangular cargo symbol. The older hero already carries a ration bag with a distinct double repair stitch and a short strap. He steadies it while helping a worker check the crate symbols. The worker marks the load for a group whose food is running low. If S53 is skipped, he can observe the depot independently; NPCs continue the supply work. ## D37 Scene 3 — The storage cave

The passage opens into a dry receiving alcove. The rock walls have flat shelf sockets, and a groove slopes toward a drain. Old wax stains and wooden pegs indicate that supplies were stored here. The player can examine scratched tallies and a shelf that once held a lantern. At a junction, a suspended empty rack creaks and sways before moving across the short route. A broad sloping maintenance gallery bypasses it. The rack cannot fall invisibly onto a player; warning dust, audible creak, visible motion and a marked safe step precede movement. Reset after a short pause or reload. No mandatory combat or climbing skill. A side niche holds a protected thank-you note with a damaged corner, not a new historical verdict. The inner store holds a ration bag on a stone bench, with the same double stitch and strap as the older hero's.

Hero (lifting the bag, checking its weight): “I promised to bring these back.”

The player can keep the physical note or record a copy; preserve the original prop in the narrative. Cosmetic bag visibility independent of its utility. Proposed utility: +4 dedicated supply slots or a modest supply-only capacity increase where the inventory supports it; no new universal inventory system required, no combat advantage. If unsupported, cosmetic and collectible remain. Never allow transfer loops or stacking.

# S53 — The Last Supply Run

## Scene 1 — A quartermaster's last load

Original frontier. The quartermaster stands beside a rescue cart, ledger held under his arm. The camp has sent a request for food, water and medical supplies, but its upper route has become unsafe. The player sees tired carriers and a guard pointing toward the lower road. No timer starts during dialogue.

Quartermaster: “They won't last without this.”
Hero: “We'll take the lower road.”
Carrier: “The wheel's been making that sound since the ridge.”
Hero: “Then we'll check it before we go.”

Accept escort, propose guard-led convoy or decline. Declining leaves the camp and depot in a recoverable waiting state; no off-screen catastrophe imposed as punishment. Optional D37 is not required and cannot substitute for the supplies. The player can inspect cart and choose marked lower road or upper service bypass once assessed. A worker fixes the wheel offscreen or via simple interaction; not a mandatory crafting skill. The quartermaster authorizes the load and records it; no repeatable sale or inventory exploit.

## Scene 2 — A road that changes

The lower road initially offers shelter beneath a cliff. A recent rockfall blocks the second bend. Disturbed gravel and distant calls warn of corrupted entities outside the protected cart path. Player can clear small debris with a worker, defend while the guard reroutes the cart, or lead the group along a broad upper service route. Two optional exterior encounters proposed, telegraphed and bounded. No enemies spawn under the cart or inside camp. Escort progress recorded at guarded pull-offs; no timers while paused, in menu or loading. If combat defeat, recover at the last safe node with cargo intact; if path blocked or NPC stalls, guard relocates only to a validated nearby safe anchor out of combat and reports the delay. No mandatory loss of supplies or scripted death. When alternate route is chosen, verify cart clearance before moving it.

Carrier: “If we go up, we won't see the camp until the bend.”
Hero: “Then stop at the marker. I'll check the next stretch.”
Guard: “I'll keep the cart here until you signal.”

## Scene 3 — The other way out

At camp, the protagonist helps unload the supplies into a protected store. A child runs toward the familiar carrier before stopping near the safe rail.

Child: “You came back.”
Hero: “We brought what we could.”
Quartermaster's assistant: “We need another way out if the road closes.”
Hero: “Show me the service path.”

The alternate exit is a broad shallow gully leading toward a guarded ridge; clear a simple obstacle, mark two turns and escort a scout to the exit marker. No hidden discovery required. If player previously opened the route, acknowledge it instead of forcing repeated repairs. The camp receives supplies, visible crates change from empty to stocked, and the child places a cup on a shelf. A brief optional return visit to depot thanks the quartermaster. The player records the promise fulfilled, not a claim that every refugee is permanently safe. No reward multiplier for fighting rather than negotiating.

## Scene 4 — The store after the years

Age fifteen. The hero explores the public ruined frontier store and reads surviving thank-you inscriptions beneath the overhang. These are ordinary physical evidence of gratitude, not a new D37 relic or a guaranteed direct historical copy. The player can record a protected line, compare shelf layout, or ask a local traveler about the site. “Someone came back,” he says, quietly. If older quest is not completed, the record is evidence only; no false rescue status. Both flags independent, linked to M19 as optional trust/evidence. Completion does not require D37; D37 discovery does not complete escort. One proposed XP and supply goodwill award, no duplicate bag or repeatable supply grant.
