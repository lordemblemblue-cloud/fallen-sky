# M10 — Against the Divine

R30 | Catalog M10: overlapping sanctuary, older stranger intercepts attack, younger recognizes resemblance, older recognizes goddess but not child as himself, survival timer and retreat, no divine defeat, no Celestial Sight, next M11. M09 required; optional discoveries never gate this encounter. New dialogue, tuning and geometry are proposals.

## Scene 1 — Between the child and the light

Resume M09 at the saved beat: the unknown woman raises her hand, the young protagonist stands near the seal and the older calls from the broken arch. Amber daylight and the original world's red sky bleed across the hall. The floor is one continuous playable strip where the sanctuary cells overlap. A pulse gathers in her palm and draws a bright contour toward the boy. Before it reaches him the older hero steps across the threshold and physically intercepts the strike. A short authored montage uses his equipped weapon or guard posture; the impact knocks his leading foot backward. Player input resumes at the safe beat after contact. The interception is not a reaction-time challenge, does not consume a resource or force an equipment change.

Older hero (urgent, eyes on the woman): “Get behind me.”

Younger hero (seeing the man's face as he turns half sideways): “Who are you?”

Older hero: “Move! I'll hold her off.”

The younger sees his own brow, mouth and habitual tightening of the grip. He has never seen his adult face, so the resemblance is unsettling rather than conclusive. The older's eyes remain on the threat; from his angle the child is backlit and partly obscured by the seal. He believes he is protecting a stranger. No knowing smile, matching nameplate or explicit recognition. Both bodies occupy the same space as separate actors, never a morph or reflection. Accessible cinematic skip retains the interception, recognition beat and safe actor placements.

Unknown woman (looking between them): “If one falls, will the other follow?”

Older hero: “He's leaving.”

Her question is a threat and hypothesis, not proof that she understands the shared life force. She looks toward the boy's silver palm. The younger's playable objective is Reach the sheltered arch; either a stone pier or wall recess leads there, protected by the older's position. No compulsory roll or special ability.

## Scene 2 — Fight for time, not victory

At the safe arch, control transfers to the older hero. The younger waits behind the pier. M09's attendant remains behind the rear wall, not a combat target. The goddess's stance is upright and still. Her attacks originate from readable hand gestures; the objective is Keep her attention away from the child, not Defeat the goddess. A three-segment defense-progress indicator reflects elapsed active defense and exit readiness rather than her HP. Hits produce a guard flare, never a death, loot or victory state.

Older hero: “Look at me.”

Unknown woman: “You would stand there for someone you do not know?”

Older hero: “He's a child.”

Proposed encounter tuning: three 12-second active intervals with safe breathers. First, a straight pulse telegraphed by a raised hand and ground stripe (1.2 s); dodge sideways or use existing guard. Second, a widening arc (1.4 s anticipation, 1.6 s recovery) with a clearly visible gap. Third, a delayed ground ring (1.5 s anticipation) followed by a seal-side pressure wave. Contours, directional cues and optional sounds communicate danger without color dependence. All timings and damage values need playtesting. Damage role may counter in recovery, range may hit projected pressure nodes, defense may hold a guarded zone. Movement-only survival is sufficient. No forced lock-on, headshot requirement, optional equipment gate or permanent resource drain.

At interval completion the child advances only after the next safe waiting position and collision are ready. First pier, side hall, then exterior retreat. She cannot arbitrarily kill the child during intended play. Defense timer pauses in dialogue, accessibility menus, loading and when the player leaves the marked defense area; guide return rather than instant failure. On genuine defeat, restart the current interval from its health/resource snapshot; keep earlier intervals complete. Assist extends anticipation and reduces pressure without altering the story. A decorative slab drops into an unoccupied recess, never across the sole exit. Confirm occupancy before changing collision; dust does not obscure cues.

Unknown woman (after interval two): “You cannot keep that door open by standing in it.”

Older hero: “I'm not trying to.”

He moves away from the seal, giving the child room. The player sees the corridor before any prop moves. His purpose is to protect one person, not prove that he is stronger than a deity.

## Scene 3 — A road he knows without knowing why

Control returns to the younger at a safe interval. The older continues his defense as a staged NPC. The child sees a split pillar, low ledge and drainage channel. He knows the turn before taking it. His familiarity comes from older-life experience, including the M04 retreat and M07 route recognition, not a new divine power. A short ordinary step-over path and a longer ramp both lead to a stable exterior shelf. No Air Recovery, gliding or other optional reward is required. Optional guidance uses normal markers or spoken directions, not a celestial overlay.

Younger hero: “The channel. It goes outside.”

Older hero: “Then follow it!”

The boy looks back and sees the older shake dust from his weapon hand in a familiar gesture. If the player waits, the older says once, “Go. I'll be right behind you.” There is no punishment for looking. A visible ward protects the active strike lane; if the boy approaches again, the older says “Not this way.” The child still cannot name the relationship. His journal should describe the familiar face and route, not label reincarnation.

## Scene 4 — The door contracts

At the shelf, the child looks through the arch. The older sees him reach the safe bend and withdraws in stages. The final older-controlled objective is Retreat to the broken lintel. The woman remains standing and dangerous. The silver seam contracts irregularly as the disturbed ritual loses coherence; neither hero knows the exact cause. M09's mark interference is developer context, not a new ability. Do not announce a goddess defeat.

Unknown woman (quietly): “You cannot hide a door forever.”

Older hero: “Keep going.”

Younger hero: “Wait!”

Close the threshold only after both bodies occupy verified safe floor. The older hears the call but cannot see the child through the closed seam. The attendant points toward a converted mining refuge. The younger remains on the peaceful outer shelf, hand trembling. He wonders whether the face was familiar because of a dream, a relative or something he cannot explain. Journal: “Someone stood between me and her. I knew the way out before I saw it.” M11 begins at the original mining refuge; the younger's later lullaby memory belongs there, not an early physical visit to its counterpart.
