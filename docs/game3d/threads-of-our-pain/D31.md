# D31 — The Guard's Rest
Expansion 04 • Revision 0.5 • Optional • S46 • Remnant gauntlet

## Scene 1 — The place where nobody is waiting
Age fifteen. Late afternoon settles over the ancient shrine. Rain has stopped, but droplets still gather on the tips of the three ceremonial spear shafts beside its outer wall. The boy has been following the ridge trail, brushing wet leaves from his sleeves. He stops to tighten a bootlace on a low stone. From this height the shafts cast three broken shadows across a pale slab; when he stands and moves sideways, the shadows nearly form a blade lying flat. He feels the odd certainty that someone should be sitting beside him. He glances at the empty stone before looking back at the shrine.

BOY (quietly, with an embarrassed half-smile): “I almost asked someone to move over.” He rubs his knuckles against his sleeve. “That's ridiculous.”

Player control remains active. The optional focus prompt appears only when the player has inspected the slab or held the pattern near alignment. A second approach through the trees reveals the same slab from a different angle; both lead to a safe standing area. If the player leaves, the boy says nothing further. No quest failure or forced camera movement.

## Scene 2 — A moment between watches
Age eighteen. The older protagonist has just returned from patrol. His left hand is stiff from gripping his weapon; he flexes it inside a worn gauntlet while a guard stacks three spears against the bunkroom wall. Beyond the open doorway, a kettle rattles over a small fire. The guard notices him staring at the weapons.

GUARD (dryly, not unkind): “They're not going anywhere. Sit down before you fall down.”

OLDER HERO (absently, looking at the spear shadows): “Was there always a stone bench outside?”

GUARD (turning toward the doorway): “A bench? There's a crate. You sat on it yesterday.”

OLDER HERO (a short laugh that fades): “Right. The crate.”

The player aligns the spear shafts and their shadows with the matching resting-blade shape. The guard continues his ordinary routine; he does not see the other world. A momentary sound of rain replaces the kettle hiss. The older hero looks at his own hand, then returns to the room. The younger sees the shrine wall resolve into a narrow doorway. Both alignments persist independently; migration timing follows existing story rules rather than a new instant-travel power.

## Scene 3 — A room that remembers a duty
The boy steps through the doorway into a cool rock cavity. The floor slopes gently downward. Water has polished the stone into shallow ledges that resemble bunks; the ceiling is low enough to make the room feel sheltered but not cramped. A worn wooden rack, belonging to the shrine's own history, stands apart from the natural ledges. The boy hears a scrape and stops. A loose ceremonial spear rolls a short distance from a niche as water dislodges its support. He can take the broad dry passage or inspect the niche and brace the rack before continuing. Neither route requires damage.

At the inner chamber an ancient guardian presence appears beside a stone seat. Its voice is low and measured; it is not a named deity or confirmed reincarnated guard. The boy keeps his hands visible, uncertain whether to approach.

GUARDIAN: “People remember the ones who opened the gates. Few remember who waited until everyone had passed.”

BOY (looking at the empty ledges): “Were you waiting for someone?”

GUARDIAN: “For the last footsteps to fade.”

Player response A — “Did they come back for you?” The guardian looks toward the passage: “Some did. Some could not. I did not stand there to be repaid.”

Response B — “You could have left.” The guardian answers without anger: “Yes. That is what made staying a choice.”

Response C — remain silent. The guardian lowers its head; after a brief pause it gestures toward the seat. All paths preserve the testimony; no moral-stat penalty. The boy may leave and return before accepting the relic.

## Scene 4 — The hand that held
A gauntlet rests in a recess beside the seat. Its leather lining is worn smooth at the thumb; a repaired seam crosses the wrist. The boy reaches for it, hesitates, then slips his fingers against the lining. His shoulders drop as if he has finally put down a burden. He feels a familiar pressure across his knuckles, but cannot name its source.

BOY (barely audible): “Someone stood here until the end.” After a pause: “Why do I know how this fits?”

At eighteen, the hero is seated beside the kettle. He turns his already-equipped gauntlet over, tracing the repaired seam. The guard offers him a cup. The hero takes it with his other hand.

OLDER HERO (with a puzzled warmth): “It feels like somebody just gave this back to me.”

GUARD: “You haven't taken it off all day.”

OLDER HERO: “I know.” He looks at the seam again, then accepts the cup. No inventory change occurs in the older world. At fifteen, the reward is a remnant gauntlet or role-equivalent, granted once; stats remain a balance proposal.

## Scene 5 — The second focus: the empty seat
An ascending passage leads to a sheltered opening above the shrine. A horizontal ledge, a forked stone pillar and a distant ridge notch align with the bunkroom window, spear rack and courtyard in the original reality. While the player holds focus, the empty landscape briefly registers as an occupied guard station. A tired guard shifts aside on a crate to make room for the older hero. Someone laughs at a joke the boy cannot hear completely. The scene is ordinary, not a battle flashback. The boy reaches toward the stone beside him and stops before touching it.

BOY (softly, surprised by his own sadness): “I think I miss them.”

At eighteen the guard asks whether he wants another cup. The older hero looks at the occupied room and briefly feels the stillness of an empty shrine. “No. Just… let me sit here a little longer.” The guard nods and returns to his work. Release focus to return to the present view. The player can revisit the vista without replaying reward or mandatory dialogue. The emotional arc is embarrassment, curiosity, respect, then longing for ordinary companionship. Developer truth: a shared life-force echo, not proof that this shrine housed the original guards. The guardian's identity remains unresolved.
