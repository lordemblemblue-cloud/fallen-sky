# R16 — D13 The Remedy I Carried / S16 Medicine for the Deep

Status: expanded narrative proposal, ready for terrain blockout; not built. Source: v1.1 R15 catalog. D13 reward: Restorative Salve recipe. S16 connects to M11, but remains optional. The medicinal moss and recipe are fictional game objects, not real medical instructions.

## D13 — Scene 1: A price written twice (age fifteen)

Rain has stopped on the mining refuge ridge. The preserved world's road passes a small apothecary, not a refugee settlement. A wooden awning drips onto a slate counter. The proprietor is scraping yesterday's price from a board when a customer sets down a nearly empty purse. The hero watches the proprietor quietly turn the board around to reveal a lower price for a smaller portion. The customer leaves with a parcel. No quest marker appears until the player approaches or examines the remedy.

Apothecary (without looking up): “If you're waiting for the rain, it has already gone.”

Hero (looking at a squat clay container): “Does that come in a cloth bag?”

Apothecary: “Usually a pot. Why?”

Hero: “I thought there was a strap.”

The proprietor offers an empty sample container. The hero holds it sideways, as if checking whether a stopper will leak on a long journey. His thumb searches for a notch that isn't there. He sets it down before the proprietor can ask. Player options: ask about the remedy, ask about the ridge, or buy ordinary supplies and leave. The proprietor explains that pale moss grows in sheltered stone hollows, but knows nothing about a mining refuge. If asked about a child, the proprietor says, “There are children all over the valley. Which one?” The hero cannot answer. No invented shared identity is confirmed.

The player can follow a path behind the shop to three pale moss patches on different rock faces. They are ordinary vegetation until seen from a particular shelf where their edges form a leaf. A gentle outline and controller pulse are optional cues, not a forced camera move. The first alignment stores a fragment, not an entrance. If the older fragment is already recorded, the seam becomes visible once safe collision is ready. Otherwise the journal records a sketch without naming another reality.

## D13 — Scene 2: The weight of a small parcel (age eighteen)

The original ridge shelters a crowded mining refuge. Canvas partitions divide a former loading shed into family spaces. The air smells of damp rope and cold stone. A caregiver has set a bowl beside a child's bed; the child is awake but too tired to finish a game of stacking wooden counters. The older hero has a medicine pouch on his belt, visibly worn from previous journeys. A refuge healer asks for a fresh supply of fictional pale moss for the ward. The existing pouch stays in inventory; it is not the object to be delivered or lost.

Caregiver (trying to clear a chair): “Sorry. Everything's in the way.”

Hero: “Leave it. Where do you need me?”

Healer: “The ridge shelf. The last carrier turned back when the lower path gave way. Take the upper path if it's still sound.”

Child (looking at the hero's pouch): “Is that heavy?”

Hero (loosens the strap so the child can see): “Not yet.”

The player can ask the healer to mark the safe trail, volunteer to accompany a carrier, or decline and return later. S16 begins here or from the observed carrier at the loading gate. D13 remains available without accepting the delivery. At the ridge, the older hero can hold his pouch at a normal inspection angle; its leaf-shaped stitched seam and the living moss complete the complementary silhouette against the rock notch. This is an ordinary playable action, not a dream cutscene. Both alignment orders work. No other character sees a portal or migrates.

## D13 — Scene 3: The hollow that should be solid

When the younger hero returns to the shelf, the aligned leaf outlines the seam in the rock. A narrow passage appears behind a hanging root curtain. The player chooses to enter; unfocusing outside closes the visible seam. After crossing, a stable interior route remains loaded and a separate exit is always available. A small landing shelf offers a safe checkpoint. The chamber is cool, with pale mineral stains and fossilized moss embedded in the wall, not a preserved copy of the refuge. The floor slopes toward a shallow drainage basin. Water drops strike a hollow ceramic fragment at irregular intervals.

The player encounters a fork: a broad dry passage around the basin, or a short ledge past a loose stone shutter. The shutter rattles before moving and can be secured with an existing wedge; if missed, it blocks the shortcut briefly rather than crushing the player. The dry path remains open. A small optional alcove contains a scratched tally board whose meaning is unresolved; it gives no mandatory clue or reward. No enemies are required. The hero reaches a stone shelf with an empty medicine container beside fossilized moss. He lifts the container, feels its lightness, and turns the stopper notch away from his body exactly as he did with the sample at the shop.

Hero (barely audible): “I carried this to someone.”

At the same moment the older hero steadies the medicine pouch already at his waist. The strap feels unfamiliar for a heartbeat, as though it has just been adjusted for him. He looks down, then resumes the task. Do not remove or duplicate the pouch. The younger hero unlocks the Restorative Salve recipe once; the empty container is a narrative prop or journal keepsake, not a second stat item. The recipe is a fictional crafting unlock; balance and ingredients remain with gameplay design.

## D13 — Scene 4: The second view

Beyond the shelf, a narrow window frames the ridge notch and a flat stone below it. The second focus uses the container rim, notch and stone as foreground, middle and distant guides. The corresponding original view is the refuge delivery bench. The player sees the older hero put a fresh parcel on the bench, not the ancient empty container. A healer checks the parcel and takes responsibility for care. The child slides one wooden counter toward the hero.

Child: “You can have that one. It's the crooked one.”

Hero (turns it over): “It stands up.”

Child: “Only that way.”

The hero sets it upright on the bench. The caregiver stops folding a blanket for a moment and looks at the child, then returns to the task. The younger hero unconsciously balances the empty container on its uneven base. He smiles when it stays upright, then looks puzzled at the feeling. The echo is relief and the memory of being trusted, not a promise of recovery or proof that the child exists in both worlds. The player can leave immediately or examine the view again. If S16 has not been played, this is a brief lived-moment glimpse, not automatic completion of that quest; the playable delivery remains available. No forced migration or duplicate reward.

## S16 — Medicine for the Deep: playable quest

The loading gate encounter is an organic trigger: a carrier is arguing with a refuge steward about a damaged route. The steward wants the parcel delivered quickly; the carrier refuses to cross a visibly unstable shelf. The hero can inspect the shelf before speaking. Steward (frustrated, not villainous): “We have people waiting.” Carrier: “Then we need a path that will still be there when I come back.” Hero options: “Show me the upper trail”; “I'll walk with you”; “I'll tell the healer what happened.” The third option opens an assisted route after the healer arranges another carrier, not an automatic failure.

At the upper trail, the player gathers the marked fictional moss nodes or helps the carrier gather them. The player can use the dry ridge bypass, or clear a fallen lightweight barrier from the lower route using a lever. No timed gathering requirement. Optional hostile wildlife may appear at a separate clearing, with a visible avoidance path; escort defense is available if the player chooses the exposed route, but a safe path always exists. The carrier waits at marked safe anchors during combat and never follows into a lethal hazard. If separated, return to the last shelter and resume. The healer checks the parcel at the delivery bench; no player-administered treatment minigame.

Caregiver (after delivery): “I thought you were another person telling us to wait.” Hero: “The healer has it now.” If the player escorted: Carrier: “Next time I'll take the ridge.” If the player cleared the route: Steward: “I'll put a sign at the fork.” If the player sought help: Healer: “You did the right thing bringing the problem here.” The child offers the crooked counter in the optional conversation; the player may accept it as a non-stat keepsake or leave it for the game. The family trust flag unlocks a later optional greeting and meal invitation; it does not determine the child's health or gate M11. Proposed reward: modest experience and ordinary crafting materials, no second salve recipe. Age fifteen counterpart: the apothecary has no knowledge of the refuge; an optional return line about the container is available only after D13. S16 is completed in the original reality independently of D13.
