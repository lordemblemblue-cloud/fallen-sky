# D07 — The Door in the Sketch
Revision 2026-11-26-R10 | Catalog verified against v0.5 | Expanded scene draft, ready for proposed blockout, not built.
West Root cliff; Miner's Gloves, climbing utility; S09 optional, M07 clue only. No main-story gate.

## Scene 1 — A man arguing with stone (age 15)
Wind pushes fine grit along the cliff road. A miner has pinned a charcoal sketch under a rock and is comparing it to the wall, pacing between two footprints in the dust. The drawing shows a rectangular door where the player sees only broken sandstone. He has already scraped his knuckles looking for a seam.
MINER [irritated, to himself]: “The lower corner was right here.”
HERO [stopping at a respectful distance]: “What are you looking for?”
MINER [folds the sketch, then reopens it]: “I drew what I saw, not what was there.”
If asked when: “Yesterday, just before the light went. Thought it was an old mine entrance. Came back with tools and found a wall.”
If challenged: “You can laugh after you find me a better explanation.”
If offered help: “Here. Don't smudge the bottom edge.”
The player can compare the sketch from several positions, ask about the trail or approach without speaking. The miner does not enter a dangerous cave. If S09 has not begun, witnessing the scene offers it; D07 can be found independently. The hero believes the drawing may be an observation trick, not a memory.

## Scene 2 — The hatch (age 18)
The same cliff contour overlooks a supply corridor. Wet canvas slaps against a timber shelter. A quartermaster carries an empty crate to a functional metal access hatch set between stone supports. The older protagonist already wears his reinforced miner's gloves; their left palm is worn smooth from rope work.
QUARTERMASTER [bracing the crate on one knee]: “Hold that open, would you? Can't afford to drop another box.”
HERO [grips handle, then looks at the rock edges]: “The bottom corner looks wrong.”
QUARTERMASTER: “It opens. That's all I need it to do.”
The player can set the crate down before focusing. The lower hatch edge, split support and distant slanted ridge form the counterpart of the sketch. The older man briefly hears charcoal scraping paper; he rubs his gloved fingertips together. He thinks he is tired. This is genuine playable time in the original reality, not a narrated flashback. Save the alignment without rewarding anything.

## Scene 3 — A door without hinges
At age 15, a foreground rock lip, middle diagonal fracture and distant upright edge close the three-sided sketch outline from a broad standing zone. The hero invites the miner to share the angle after both perspectives resolve.
HERO [points rather than moving the miner physically]: “Stand here and look.”
MINER [voice catches; he takes one step, stops]: “There it is!”
The entrance opens only while the player holds or toggles focus and the paired flags are satisfied. No forced camera, no automatic entry. The miner can see the door at the shared angle but cannot migrate; he is observing a local manifestation. On release before crossing it becomes rock again. Once inside, a collision-safe interior latch provides permanent outward access; the visible front can close without trapping the player. If the player walks away, the miner waits at a sheltered camp and the sketch remains available.

## Scene 4 — Lockers that nobody claimed
The first room has waist-high stone benches and corroded locker hinges. Their arrangement suggests a place for preparing work, not proof that this exact mining company existed in both worlds. Old chalk arrows point toward a lift shaft whose platform has fallen. A chain ticks in the draft. The direct route crosses a broad shelf beside the shaft; a side corridor winds through a ventilation passage with stable steps. A hanging loose beam drops dust and creaks before its optional trap activates. Wait, brace it with an existing lever or bypass it; resetting the lever restores safe travel. No recovered climbing utility required. No compulsory enemies. An optional locker contains an unsigned shift tally; the player may take a rubbing without inventing the workers' identities.
At the far bench, the hero finds the Miner's Gloves. He flexes the fingers, ties the cuff in a practiced motion and looks at the fastening.
HERO [quiet amusement, then uncertainty]: “I didn't even have to look.”
The older self simultaneously presses his thumb into the worn palm of his existing glove. He experiences the comfort of a first fitting; he does not acquire a second pair.
QUARTERMASTER: “Too tight?”
HERO [shakes head, almost smiling]: “No. Just right.”
The younger unlocks climbing utility once, with numerical balance and exact mechanic subject to the existing traversal system. Provide a safe practice wall and an accessible walking route. No forced tutorial.

## Scene 5 — The view from the other side
Beyond the lockers a short rise reaches a natural window. The second alignment combines the cliff notch, hatch-support silhouette and distant ridge. Only available after recovery. The younger sees the older supply corridor during a lived moment: the quartermaster struggles with a crate until the older man holds the hatch and takes the heavier end. The younger instinctively leans forward as if to help; his hand meets the rock. On the other side, the older man hears the sound of a locker closing and holds the hatch an extra moment.
HERO (15) [after the view fades]: “The drawing was right.”
He does not identify the older man or explain the two realities. Journal: “The same cliff. A different doorway. The gloves fit.”
On return, the player may show the miner the gloves, describe only the room, or keep the vista private. MINER, for each choice: “Then it wasn't my eyes.” / “A room behind that? I'll mark the safe way.” / “You don't owe me an explanation. Keep the sketch.” Trust and camp dialogue vary; reward does not. S09 completes on reporting the visible doorway and safe route; D07 completes on relic + second focus. If already completed, return dialogue is a short acknowledgement. M07's carving remains independent.
