## Scene 1 — Three things that do not belong together

At fifteen the hero enters a limestone ravine beneath the Lunar Vault. An exposed arch stands against the night sky; a shallow curved relief is carved into a near pillar, a second arc is visible on a distant lintel, and a small round recess catches moonlight on a rock beyond the arch. From most angles they appear unrelated. A keeper examines an old drawing at a resting shelf. He knows the individual motifs but not their combination.

Hero: “These aren't words.”

Keeper: “Perhaps they are pieces.”

Hero: “Then they belong together.”

The player approaches a broad focus station set back from the ravine edge. The near curve forms the lower eyelid, the middle lintel the upper curve, and the distant round recess the pupil. The moonlit disc is a fixed authored local illumination, not dependent on time of day or weather. The visible crescent can be inspected by day as a pale inlay. Focus is hold or toggle with voluntary camera motion. Once the arcs and disc align, a short silvery contour confirms the eye; an accessible static guide and caption describe its geometry. Do not reveal the entrance until both reality flags register. The keeper can explain depth and sightline without naming dimensional travel.

Keeper (if asked): “Try looking past the nearest stone. Don't move it. Move yourself.”

Hero: “From here the gap closes.”

The hero can leave and return freely; the first flag persists. A safe overhang and ordinary walkable exit exist even before discovery. No mandatory glide or timed lunar event.

## Scene 2 — A shelter under a different sky

In the original reality, the same ravine is an emergency resting place. The sky is dark with ash, but the geology is recognizable. A broken carved stone lies at the edge of a sheltered landing; a lintel survives above an old store recess, and a circular polished stone reflects the lamp of a passing scout. These are matching carvings and shared geological anchors, not evidence that every building existed in both worlds. The older protagonist already carries a small silver charm on a leather cord at his wrist. Its shallow oval face has a single chipped edge.

Scout: “The wind cuts around the ridge. Stay inside the arch.”

Hero: “I've seen the shape above it.”

Scout: “The carving? Nobody here could tell me what it was for.”

Hero (turns his wrist toward the lamp): “It looks familiar.”

The older player can shelter, inspect his existing charm, or explore the viewing shelf. Matching the three carved elements forms the complementary eye. He briefly perceives the quiet vault chamber as a wavering outline beyond the arch, then it disappears. He does not enter the younger vault or lose the charm. The two flags can be recorded in either order through available playable visits. No forced zoom, paid mechanic, companion or special vision requirement.

## Scene 3 — The silent arches

After both alignments, the younger returns to the original focus shelf. The eye becomes a readable seam in the cliff behind the near arch. The player walks toward it under their own control. Reveal only after entrance, collision and interior return latch have streamed; maintain visibility while focus is held or toggled. When focus ends outside, the seam conceals. If the player has crossed the threshold, it may visually conceal behind them but cannot delete floor or trap them. An inner latch always opens the return passage; save this access lease on entry. If focus releases while straddling threshold, defer closing until safe.

The entry descends into a low-ceiling vestibule with pale stone ribs and a dry floor. Three empty niches face a central ring. There is no background enemy spawning in the vestibule. The keeper's drawing hints at aligning three rotating stone rings; each ring has a distinct tactile notch and shape. The first trial joins the outer arc, the second seats the pupil, and the final alignment opens the chamber. The rings click into safe detents, cannot damage an avatar, and reset individually if requested. Show readable failure feedback; no limited attempts or timed input. An accessible lever sequence sets the same physical ring state rather than skipping narrative rewards. Proposed bypass after first attempt: keeper's marginal rubbing indicates the correct notches, player still confirms each ring. No required combat.

Hero (as the first ring settles): “The pieces are the same.”

Hero (at the final click): “Someone meant for this to open.”

Beyond the rings, a gallery divides. One short route crosses a slow sliding slab with a visible track, dust and audible scrape; a wider maintenance passage bypasses it completely. The slab stops if occupied and returns to a neutral state after reset. A small alcove contains a drawing of a protective hand around an oval object, not an explanation of the Goddess's life-force mechanism. A guardian-shaped stone figure stands near the end; the player may choose to activate a voluntary challenge in S45 or simply pass along the marked side route. The relic is not locked behind combat.

## Scene 4 — The weight of something already mine

The inner chamber is circular but not monumental in scale. The roof has a narrow opening toward a silver-lit recess. On a low stone cradle lies a small charm, its oval face scratched and chipped on the same edge as the older hero's. A piece of leather cord rests beside it. The younger lifts it without a victory pose; the charm turns in his palm and he instinctively catches it with the same finger motion the older used to adjust his wrist cord.

Hero (quiet, staring at his fingers): “Why does this feel like my hand?”

At the same moment, the older protagonist touches the charm already attached to his wrist. It feels newly heavy. He looks at the cord to make sure it has not broken, then lets his hand fall. No second item appears, no existing gear is deleted, no magical inventory synchronization grants duplicates. Younger receives the Silver Protective Charm once. Proposed modest protection: 8% reduction to eligible fictional non-PvP incoming direct damage while equipped, subject to the existing mitigation cap and stacking rules; no immunity, no reduction of authored narrative events, no effect on environmental hazards unless separately approved. If compatible equipment hooks are unavailable, retain cosmetic/keepsake only pending implementation review. Do not introduce new combat systems or bypass server checks. Reward claim survives full inventory through a pending claim record.

## Scene 5 — A quiet place to wait

The second focus station lies on the inner raised balcony, framing a curved rib, a distant rock notch and the circular opening. It reveals the corresponding original shelter at an earlier ordinary moment: the scout is tying a cord around the older hero's charm after the hero has already received it, reinforcing a loose knot. The hero sits on a bench, holding his hand still while wind blows outside.

Scout: “Don't pull it too tight. You'll want to move your hand.”

Older hero: “I'll leave it like that.”

Scout: “Good. We've got a little time before we move.”

The younger feels the pressure of the cord and hears the soft rasp of leather. The older hears a brief echo of the younger chamber's calm. Neither gains an explanation or a new ability. The younger journal adds: “I remember waiting where the stone was quiet.” The second view is replayable without rewards. Afterward, an inner ramp leads to an independent exit above the original shelf. It opens from inside even when focus is off. Both main and alternate routes remain recoverable.

## S45 — The Silence of the Lunar Vault

S45 is an independent optional investigation, not a requirement for D30. Accept from keeper before or after discovery. If D30 has been completed, the keeper asks the hero to document the already inspected ring arrangement and the chamber's carvings rather than force a repeated relic claim. If S45 begins first, the player may record three external carvings and the ordinary shelter approach, then document the ring sequence when D30 access is available. Provide a separate keeper-operated survey entrance into the outer vestibule after the player has recorded the three motifs; this route ends at the public survey gallery and does not bypass the paired focus gate into the personal relic chamber. S45 can finish with external evidence and an outer-gallery rubbing alone. The catalog's personal charm remains the linked D30 reward, not a second S45 reward.

Keeper: “I've copied these for years. I've never seen them all from one place.”

Hero: “They're not separate drawings.”

Keeper: “Then let's record them as one.”

At eighteen, the older hero helps the scout secure the resting shelf by moving a lantern to a protected niche or marking an unobstructed exit. He notices the matching carvings and recognizes the calm chamber inexplicably. This playable shelter observation is available during normal migration visits and can be recorded retrospectively; no compulsory combat. If already observed during D30, count that observation once. In the younger gallery, the player makes three rubbings or selects an accessible inspection equivalent. Each confirms a motif with text, shape and tactile detent feedback. The final rubbing combines them, with no automatic cosmological explanation.

Optional guardian challenge: a stationary stone guardian animates only after explicit player interaction with the trial plinth. It is a training-style defense trial, not a hidden mandatory boss. Proposed two readable sweep/stomp patterns with generous windup, floor markings, captions and recovery; evade or interrupt three cycles to complete. Guardian cannot leave its marked chamber, pursue into the safe route or target the keeper. A surrender lever ends the challenge safely and returns the guardian to idle, with no reward penalty on the investigation. If defeated or disconnected, reset trial only, retain all survey progress. Guardian identity and origin remain unknown. No new lore claiming a named deity created it.

Keeper (after survey): “The silence is different in there.”

Hero: “There was someone waiting under the arch.”

Keeper: “Someone you saw?”

Hero: “Someone I remember.”

The player chooses to share the full rubbing, keep the personal observation private or supply only the architectural sketch. Each preserves the physical evidence and completes the investigation; no forced confession. Ordinary XP and keeper goodwill proposed, plus a non-spoiler public survey note. No duplicate charm, stat stacking or required main progression. Later visit shows the keeper cataloging the rubbing, not changing the architecture.
