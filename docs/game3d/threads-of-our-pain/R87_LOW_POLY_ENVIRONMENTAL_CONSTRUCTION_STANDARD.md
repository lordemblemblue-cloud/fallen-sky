# R87 — Mandatory low-poly environmental construction standard
27 November 2026 | The Threads of Our Pain / Fallen Sky | Owner explicitly requires low-poly construction for the detailed environmental prompt series. This is a design/production instruction for Claude, not evidence that assets have been constructed. Apply with R85/R86 and the complete stable-ID scripts. The exact budgets, materials and microgeometry below are proposed starting design targets, subject to survey and device measurement, not owner-approved final numerical limits.

## 1. Non-negotiable production direction
Build all new terrain, rock formations, architecture, caves, dungeons, bridges, vegetation, underwater environments, props and environmental variants as intentionally low-poly game-ready assets from the outset. Low-poly is the base asset construction method, not merely a graphics setting that decimates a high-poly scene after loading it. Preserve readable shape, purposeful wear, architectural identity, interaction geometry and the cinematic atmosphere. An intricate surface does not require a dense mesh: use large angular planes and purposeful silhouette breaks, broad vertex colors, shared material atlases, painted wear and selective baked normals where supported. No mandatory tessellation, displacement, dense individual grass geometry, expensive full-world reflections or high-poly collision meshes. Do not flatten critical footholds, actual entrances, navigational landmarks or paired-focus target geometry in the name of optimization.

Treat proposed measurements as blockout-local targets; map them to existing surveyed world anchors. Never invent exact global coordinates or restore deleted maps by default. Mandatory story/quest behavior from the primary scripts, R66 and R83/R84 has precedence over a decorative suggestion. If an aesthetic or traversal request conflicts with story, collision, mobile performance or accessibility, return the conflict and a compatible variant. Do not introduce compulsory jumps, climbs, combat, portals, new rewards or an underwater route into a location where the source does not permit them.

## 2. Asset construction guidance
The following triangle ranges are initial per-mesh LOD0 targets for ordinary environment modules, not a universal budget or hard limit on an entire terrain cell. Count triangles after triangulation, not modeling quads; document actual totals and justify exceptions. A large terrain tile, distinctive quest landmark or character needs a separately surveyed budget.
| Asset type | Suggested LOD0 triangles | Detail method |
|---|---:|---|
| Small scattered stone | 24–100 | Broad flat-shaded faces, 1–2 silhouette chips, vertex color |
| Ordinary boulder | 80–300 | Uneven angular profile; actual large notch if relevant, painted fine hairlines |
| Modular cliff panel | 200–800 | Few major strata and bevels; atlas-based creases and broad mineral bands |
| Cave wall/ceiling module | 180–650 | Authored structural ribs and recesses, dark wetness variation near floor |
| Simple structural wall/arch module | 100–500 | Actual doorway/arch opening; coarse blocks and silhouette damage; painted joints |
| Path/floor module | 30–160 | Walkable slope and step geometry, painted cracks/worn edges |
| Tree (ordinary trunk plus crown) | 150–600 | Angular branching, clustered leaf masses, shared atlas |
| Shrub/fern cluster | 12–100 | Few foliage cards or simple faceted leaf clumps, avoid overdraw |
| Small crate, lantern or bracket | 24–180 | Broad form and functional handle only; texture for tiny rivets and wear |

For ordinary distant assets, trial LOD1 at roughly 40–60% of LOD0 triangles, LOD2 at roughly 10–25%, and inexpensive silhouette proxy or culling beyond useful visibility. Preserve gameplay landmarks, approach/reveal silhouettes and collision regardless of visual LOD. Use hysteresis and stable transitions; no aggressive player-centered unloading that makes nearby terrain or floor disappear. Build region/cell budgets from actual measurements of triangles visible, draw calls, shader variants, material/texture memory, CPU/GPU frame time, peak memory, loading and stutter. These ranges are planning candidates, not measured FPS guarantees. Use shared atlases, texture resolution appropriate to on-screen size, mipmaps and compression supported by the existing project. Prefer opaque materials, limited transparency, local emissive surfaces, restrained particles and instancing/batching for repeat props. Keep directional lighting and inexpensive shadow alternatives; avoid adding per-prop dynamic lights. Use existing renderer paths, verify WebGPU and fallback rather than claiming both are implemented or optimal.

## 3. Every construction prompt must be location-specific
Split each location into bounded work packets. Do not give Claude all 125 entries as one enormous generation. Record shared anchors so neighboring packets reuse the same assets and geometry.
- **Terrain approach and contour:** route from prior mission, direction relative to surveyed landmarks, walkable width, length and elevation, cliff profile, slope transitions, soil/rock boundary and safe return. Define every deliberate landmark and obstacle by local ID.
- **Rock and masonry detail:** dimensions, shape, orientation, strata, dents, notches, creases, chipped corners, broad fracture planes, color/material, dry and wet zones, moss placement and degree of wear. Decide whether each feature is silhouette geometry, simplified collision, texture/vertex color or decorative noncollision. Never make a painted crack look like a walkable passage or climbable ledge.
- **Floor and ceiling:** pavement pattern, seam depth appearance, uneven edges, puddles, rubble, roots, ramps, gaps, steps, climbability and overhead camera clearance. Identify mandatory path versus optional jump/climb or scenic detour, actual movement prerequisites and alternate route where source requires it. Confirm with supported avatar/controller measurements.
- **Water and secrets:** shoreline slope, water depth and current representation, doorway size, entry/exit and breathing space, wetness bands, reflections or stable fallback, secret cue geometry, required quest/focus state, collision readiness and emergency return. Include only water/secret features supported by the location source; no new portal or mandatory swimming invented.
- **Architecture, lighting and sound:** modular stone/timber/metal/cloth, roofing, supports, floor/wall corners, signs, damage/repairs, local palette, light sources/shadows, weather, ambient audio and low-effects fallback. Describe why each visible detail is there and what the player learns.
- **Mission integration:** actual main quest route and optional adventure/discovery trigger, NPC identity/placement and phase guard, dialogue/event, objective receipts and reward owner, optional detours and junction back to main path; approved enemy/trap telegraph, reset, recovery, independent exits. Source-supported events only; do not add a random mission at an empty intersection simply to fill space.
- **Delivery:** surveyed transforms, blockout diagram, local asset IDs and LOD triangle/material count, navmesh/collision, tested traversal and interaction, screenshots if available, measured desktop/mobile performance, unresolved conflicts and exact source revision.

## 4. Acceptance gate
First inspect the existing world and assets. Return the survey and low-poly blockout plan before mesh production. Keep map cells and occupied exits collision-ready; avoid invisible floor, forced camera changes and unapproved route gates. Test optional versus required routes, keyboard/controller/touch, low graphics, actual wall and ground collision, camera clearance, LOD stability, quest triggers/reload, original/preserved phase variants where applicable and measured WebGPU/fallback performance. Existing guest-only entry, authoritative reward checks and managed middleware remain untouched. Do not spend credits, construct maps, merge or deploy as part of this documentation task.
