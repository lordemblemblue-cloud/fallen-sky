# S50 — The Unfinished Bridge
R59 | Source: R58 master, S50 catalog and D35 expanded script (paragraphs 771–775 and 1319 onward, zero-based). Compatibility design below is proposed: a short constructed crossing to the sealed cave apron, separate from D35's hidden span and grapple relic. Ready for blockout review; not built.

## Scene 1 — The far bank is not the problem
Age fifteen, mountain gorge. Wind runs along the grass rather than over it; loose leaves circle below a bare stone shoulder. A builder stands beside a short break in a footpath near the sealed cave approach, examining a pile of weathered planks and stone supports. The far side is visibly close. The broad ravine farther downstream still has no visible bridge; that is D35's concealed crossing. Keep the two gaps visually and spatially distinct so the player does not think the hidden span was already built.

Builder (holds a length of cord between two marks): “The other side is closer than it looks.”
Hero (tests the firm bank with a boot): “We need a support here.”
Builder: “That's what I thought. But the rock under the old peg is loose. Help me find a better place.”

The hero can ask why the path matters. The builder explains that a nearby cave used to offer shelter from sudden storms, and the path has become unsafe. He does not know what is inside the sealed inner chamber. The player can accept, postpone or decline; no character waits in danger on a timed countdown.

## Scene 2 — Make the route hold
The player inspects three candidate support points on the near bank and one accessible lower shelf. A loose point shows cracked stone, a shifting peg and a clear warning from the builder. A stable point is marked by intact rock and a fitted base. The player selects stable supports, then arranges two quest-only brace pieces on prepared sockets. This is an abstract fantasy construction interaction, not a real-world engineering simulation. Nothing falls while the player stands in the gap. On wrong placement the builder calls, “Leave that one. We can move it before we put weight on it.” Unconfirmed pieces reset safely; no equipment is lost. A slower guided placement mode shows matching shapes and reads the builder's instructions aloud.

Once the two verified braces are in place, the builder sets the final deck panel. The player tests the route only after collision and navmesh are loaded. The camera remains player controlled; wind and a subtle movement of suspended cloth convey height without shaking the view. A walkable ledge bypass remains available for return and for players unable to use narrow bridges. The crossing is permanently available after repair, not focus-dependent. D35's separate hidden span remains visually closed until its own paired conditions succeed, and remains safe during occupation.

## Scene 3 — A mark left by a traveler
At the far apron a sealed cave door is visible behind a weathered storage recess. The builder stops at the threshold. “That's as far as I can take you. The rest isn't mine to open.” The player can inspect the door without triggering it, then finds a small, worn trail marker in the public recess. This is a proposed personal relic from earlier journeys, not D35's climbing rope. Its edge is smooth where a thumb has repeatedly passed over it. The younger hero turns it in his hand, recognizes the movement, and frowns. “I think I've carried something like this.” The builder takes no notice, busy clearing the entrance. The player may document the marker or keep the physical keepsake with the builder's permission; both resolve the adventure. The seal itself belongs to the relevant discovery progression, not S50.

A returning traveler crosses the repaired footpath and rests on the far apron. “You built a way through,” she says, looking back with relief. The hero doesn't claim credit alone. “We did.” The reward is proposed ordinary XP and the marker or journal rubbing, plus permanent local route access. No grapple skill, climbing rope or duplicate D35 reward. A later visit can show the builder checking the deck or the traveler sheltering under the overhang.

## Scene 4 — Between the rain and the road
Age eighteen, original reality, actual playable visit. A storm obscures the corresponding ridge; the short gap has become a washed-out service walkway near an evacuation route. An engineer and survivors are preparing a temporary path. Beyond, the larger rescue bridge belongs to D35. The older hero helps check the stable staging area and pass the prepared support components to the engineer. No live structural load puzzle or forced timed rescue. When the path is ready, survivors walk across in small groups. The older hero already carries his climbing rope; do not grant or remove it here. He also has a worn trail marker, a proposed continuity detail. As he rubs its edge, he briefly feels dry mountain air against his face. “Strange weather,” he says. The engineer glances at the rain. “That's one word for it.”

The younger hero later hears a distant rain patter while standing on dry stone. He looks up and finds no clouds. The two moments establish the emotional connection of building a way to safety; the marker's precise provenance remains unknown. This is not D35's first-receipt rope reaction or second focus. Original local sequence relative to M05 remains unresolved, with pending state if not visited. The third reality may contain a maintained public footpath in the same rock silhouette, without evacuation damage or invasion history, subject to ending approval.
