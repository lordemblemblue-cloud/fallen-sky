## Scene 1 — The woman beneath the light

Age fifteen. Beyond the clearing's fallen trunk, a woman kneels on one knee in a shallow depression. Her hands remain apart as though holding an object too large to grasp. Between them a fractured silver stone turns slowly without touching the ground. Grass bows toward the stone even though the trees move in the opposite direction. The player can approach either side of the trunk, examining the disturbed soil before approaching. The protagonist has never seen anything behave like this.

Goddess (without turning, strained but gentle): “Child, how did you get through?”
Hero (looking over his shoulder): “Through what?”
Goddess (finally looking at him): “Stay behind me.”

She expected her protective boundary to exclude visitors. Her surprise is brief: the stone's motion draws her attention back before she can investigate the boy. If the player asks “Are you hurt?” she answers, “Not yet. Please, move away from the light.” If asked “Did that fall from the moon?” she glances upward: “Something that should have remained there.” If the player remains silent, she indicates a safe patch with her elbow. Never require dialogue selection to advance. The protagonist believes she is a powerful stranger containing a dangerous object. The UI identifies her as Woman in the Light until the authorized identity reveal; developer scripts call her Goddess. No recognition of his identity.

## Scene 2 — The edge of protection

The player can circle the depression on a broad path. Silver strands curve over fallen leaves and the forest sound becomes slightly muffled. Inspect a scorched crescent in the soil, a suspended shard of light or a twig casting two overlapping shadows; any one is sufficient. The others remain optional journal observations. These features show the barrier's uneven behavior, not a puzzle that gives the boy control of it. 

Hero (after inspecting the twig): “There's something wrong with the light.”
Goddess: “I know. Don't touch it.”
Hero: “Can I help?”
Goddess (restraining a sharp breath): “Keep your feet on the ground. Tell me if anything moves.”

She lowers one hand briefly and a gap flickers in the protection. Dust rises; she immediately returns her hand to its original position. The camera stays with the player; a restrained positional sound draws attention. No damage for standing on ordinary walkable surfaces. At the central edge, use a readable soft obstruction and warning, not an unexplained invisible wall. The player may backtrack or inspect the wolf from the perimeter. The helpfulness established in M01 becomes relevant: the hero chooses to observe rather than assume she has everything under control.

## Scene 3 — A wounded animal

An uneven breath comes from the low side of the clearing. A wolf lies beside a root, one foreleg curled beneath its chest. Its ears move at the sound of footsteps. Its fur is dusted with soil; avoid sensational injury detail. A short disturbed track indicates that it dragged itself before resting. The animal initially seems frightened rather than aggressive. Observe from a safe distance, crouch at a marked point or call the woman immediately. A cautious approach is not punished by sudden unavoidable damage.

Hero (softly): “Easy. I'm not going to hurt you.”
Goddess (eyes still on fragment): “What is it?”
Hero: “A wolf. I think it's hurt.”
Goddess (sharply): “Do not go closer.”

The wolf breathes in. Its shadow expands before its body moves. As it raises its head the shadow seems to turn another way. Concern becomes alarm. Use a distinct authored shadow silhouette or local projected decal, not a global shadow failure. A quiet double footstep comes from only one set of paws. Accessible observation: “The wolf's shadow moved first.” The boy may respond “It's scared,” “Its shadow moved,” or “Something's inside it.” The last is a frightened guess, not a diagnosis. All options warn the woman, who finally looks at the animal. No cosmic vessel explanation.

## Scene 4 — The choice to stay

The Goddess shifts her stance. The light bends toward her wrists as she straightens, still unable to release the fragment. The wolf struggles upright. Its silhouette briefly extends beyond its natural proportions, a pointed shape crossing a stone, but not yet a complete dragonlike transformation. A branch cracks and leaves stir inside the clearing. The forest beyond remains recognizable.

Goddess (controlled urgency): “Come behind me. Now.”
Hero (looking between her and the wolf): “What about you?”
Goddess: “I have to hold this.”
Hero: “Then tell me where to stand.”

For a hesitant choice, Hero: “I don't know what to do.” Goddess: “That's all right. Stay where you can see me.” For retreat she says, “The path is behind you. Keep to the trees.” The player can move to the retreat edge and reconsider; no cowardice score. Progression requires voluntary confirmation at a safe protective position, either near her or at the outer lookout. She extends a protective arc toward him. End on irregular breathing becoming an unfamiliar resonance. M03 owns full transformation, beginner combat and the Entity's spoken threat. Do not begin battle until the next arena has loaded and player control is available.
