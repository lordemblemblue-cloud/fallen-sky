## Scene 1 — Where the roof used to be

The younger hero follows the route from M12 toward Hollow Mountain. Through a break in the trees he sees the terrace between two curved pillars. One pillar leans but the original joint is visible beneath its broken roof. The historian has spread a sheet across a low stone bench. His assistant marks where fragments fell. Water runs along a groove where the roof once drained; grass grows in the collapsed canopy. The hero instinctively steps around an empty rectangle, as though a table still stands there.

Historian: “Mind the loose stones. We have enough pieces to count already.”
Hero: “There was a roof over this.”
Historian: “There was. See the sockets?”
Hero (looking at empty space): “And benches there.”
Historian: “Possibly. Have you been here before?”
Hero: “Not like this.”

If S23 complete, the assistant points to the protected tray and prior position sketch. If accepted but incomplete, the assistant invites the hero to return to it later; the main objective uses the fixed monument, not the loose fragment. If skipped, the historian has independently recorded the fallen piece. D19 completed adds the optional inscription rubbing and chisel examination; if not, the side seam remains unmentioned. No optional quest starts or completes automatically. The player approaches the fixed wall and inspects the ancient weathered lettering and fresh scratches beside it, preserving the actual evidence in the journal without disturbing it.

## Scene 2 — What the stone can tell us

The historian distinguishes three physical layers: old weathering across the raised stone, original lettering with mineral deposits in the grooves, and recent shallow scratches across several names. A sheltered section retains a legible name. Fresh fragments beneath the damaged area match its pale inner stone, but their presence alone cannot identify the person who caused the damage or motive. The player compares the protected area, the scratches, and the debris. Inspection is a free-camera interaction with a scalable close-read panel; it never requires high texture resolution. An optional tracing can be examined even when the sun is behind clouds.

Historian (kneeling without touching the wall): “The stone is ancient. Those scratches are not.”
Hero: “Who wants them forgotten?”
Historian: “We don't know that anyone does. Not yet.”
Hero: “Then why scratch across the names?”
Historian: “That is a better question. Keep it a question.”

If D19 evidence present, hero offers the rubbing; historian notes it refers to the use of the sanctuary, not necessarily the later damage. If S23 record present, assistant compares the earlier sketch: some fresh marks are consistent with the earlier disturbance; do not claim a second incident unless surveyed dates support it. If neither optional quest done, base evidence remains sufficient. The inscription reserved for future arrivals remains a deliberately empty panel and is not treated as erased. A single recoverable fragment contains part of a name; no reconstruction of missing letters without evidence.

## Scene 3 — A shelter that hasn't fallen yet

Migration takes place at a safe ledge after the player elects to rest or continue the examination; if combat or physics are active, defer until safe. The older hero arrives on the same mountain pass in the original reality. The pillars are intact. Timber beams have been installed between them, not as a grand temple but as a temporary refuge. Workers roll a cart beside the very bench the boy remembered. A survivor holds a small roll of paper with family names. Beyond the lower road, distant creatures force a group of travelers to wait behind a sheltered bend.

Survivor: “Write our names so someone knows we were here.”
Hero: “We'll put them where the rain can't reach.”
Survivor: “Some of them went ahead. Some I haven't seen since the city.”
Hero: “Then write what you know. Leave room for what you don't.”

This is the same monument worksite described in D19/S23, not another construction event. Establish three functional refuge tasks: escort the waiting group, brace the roof and prepare the names panel. The player can organize the order of roof preparation and escort, but names are recorded only after the survivors reach the safe worksite. If optional S23 work already recorded in older world, acknowledge the completed subtask and do not repeat its fragment or ledger rewards. Mandatory scene of monument participation remains available through an adjacent blank panel, with the survivor deciding what is recorded. If D19 already observed, use its dialogue once, not replay the exact first discovery. The player may contribute a carving, carry the slate to the mason or hold the panel while the mason works. No forced personal chisel acquisition. The panel includes a planned blank space, matching D19.

## Scene 4 — People, not cargo

At the pass a guard signals from a stone windbreak. The group includes a carrier with a heavy pack and a parent walking slowly with a child. A falling stone has obstructed the direct route. Two corrupted creatures are visible below, not inside the refuge. The player can defend the lower approach, signal the guard to lead a broad upper path, or move a lightweight fallen brace with a worker and take the direct road when clear. No role-exclusive skill or focus reward is needed. Proposed two exterior encounter waves, each one or two enemies; telegraph by audible calls and visible disturbed gravel. Keep civilians in protected nodes until the chosen path is safe. If a carrier falls behind, stop at the next shelter, never reset the entire mission for a slow NPC. On combat defeat restart at the last guarded node with the escort intact and no duplicate rewards. Optional route changes preserve names and prior repair state.

Carrier: “Don't wait for me. The rest can go.”
Hero: “We're going to the same place. We'll wait here.”
Guard (on safe arrival): “That's everyone from the lower bend.”
Hero: “Then close the gate after the cart.”

No forced death of named survivors. The player sees blankets laid out and the timber louver moved to vent smoke; M11 mining refuge is a distinct site, not this sanctuary. The roof repair uses a supported beam placement with a worker, no physics juggling or real-world construction procedure. A completed beam provides a visible dry strip. Ambient sound changes from exposed wind to muffled voices, hammer taps and water draining away. Pride comes from seeing a safe place become usable.

## Scene 6 — The names were here

The boy returns to the damaged monument. The recently scratched line crosses the very position where the older survivor pointed. He compares the shape and surviving letters instead of magically restoring missing text. He recalls a parent waiting by the bench, then sees the empty stone recess. His anger becomes focused: someone may have tried to remove a record, but he does not know who or why. If the player previously asked whether the marks were accidental, retain that uncertainty. The historian offers to keep a duplicate tracing at Wolfveil rather than leaving all evidence exposed.

Hero: “I remember the roof. I remember the people standing here.”
Historian: “I can record what you tell me, separately from what we can see.”
Hero: “Then record both.”
Historian: “I will. We should get a copy to Wolfveil.”

Sable Vey's messenger waits at the pass, carrying a summons to the diplomatic hall. The message is neutral: envoys have arrived and testimony is requested. Do not announce the assassination before M14. The main chapter closes after the hero receives the summons, with optional investigation still available. Journal: “The names were here. Some are missing now. I remember people who wanted them to stay.”
