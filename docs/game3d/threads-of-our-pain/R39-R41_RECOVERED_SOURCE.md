M19 — The Last Silver Thread
R39 | Main campaign expansion | Convergence threshold | Source: R38 catalog M19 and M18 full script. All
encounter choreography, alternative-outcome contracts, terrain dimensions and rewards below are
proposed. M18_DONE is the sole campaign prerequisite; optional quests, paired discoveries, faction
standings and equipment builds are not gates. The hostile goddess remains unnamed. Her part in the original
invasion and her deeper motivation remain deliberately unresolved. No final narrative lock until the writer
approves the outcome contract.

Scene 1 — The road that has two histories
At fifteen, the hero leaves the sanctuary carrying the testimony he gathered in M18. The keeper has set a
lantern near a split ridge, where the road to the convergence threshold should begin. A narrow seam in the
sky appears to touch the ridge. As the hero approaches, a ruined arch briefly becomes visible inside an intact
arch. Its outline matches but its masonry does not. A cart stands on the road in the preserved reality; a
broken wheel lies in the corresponding place in the original. Neither object changes the other's physical
collision. This is an observed overlap, not a free-travel portal. The player can walk around the intact cart and
look through the seam without losing camera control.

Keeper: “The other horizon is getting clearer.”

Hero: “Can the people there see us?”

Keeper: “We don't know. Don't send anyone through it.”

A group of civilians waits at a covered turnout because the bridge ahead is no longer considered safe. A scout
has identified a raised path and an old stone channel that connect to a protected courtyard. The hero
examines the route board, then accompanies the group through one of the two ways. The player opens a
jammed gate or assists the scout in taking the alternate path; neither route requires combat victory. People
walk in small visible groups, while those outside the playable area remain represented in a shelter register.
Do not use this as an infinite wave escort. The route closes behind the group only after all active actors and
the hero have passed through. A quiet person asks whether the ruined buildings are predictions of their own
future. The hero can answer that he has seen places like those, or admit that he does not know what will
happen. Both choices preserve the same evidence and objectives.

Civilian: “Is that what happens to us?”

Hero: “It happened somewhere. That doesn't mean it has to happen here.”

The courtyard is recognizably part of the same sanctuary geography introduced in M18. At the back, a pair of
stone channels leads to a raised ring that holds the visible seam. The Moon Goddess is maintaining the
protection, not waiting idly for the protagonist to solve everything. She asks him to listen to the original
shelter before proceeding. The player reaches a safe rest point where the older perspective begins through
an authored migration. Show the hero sitting near an ordinary wall, not stepping into a physical portal. No
free-form realm switching is added by this chapter.


Scene 2 — The last passage out
At eighteen, the underground survivors are organizing around the assembly point surveyed in M18. The scout
is checking lanterns and a caregiver is laying out blankets. The same family the player helped earlier has
packed their own possessions. They must have recognizable agency; the older protagonist is not rescuing
helpless background characters who only exist to validate his eventual decision. The shelter's route board
now indicates an obstructed branch, a passable branch and a safe assembly area. A resident who does not
wish to abandon their home may volunteer to help maintain the protected approach rather than be
compelled to leave. The player can read the board or ask the scout to summarize it.

Scout: “The lower passage is blocked. We've checked the upper one twice.”

Hero: “Who is still down there?”

Caregiver: “Two groups are waiting at the junction. They've got light and water.”

The first active task is to secure the junction. A damaged support has narrowed the approach, but an intact
bypass remains open. Hold an interaction with the scout to release a gate, or choose the bypass and have the
scout meet you at its exit. The second task is to accompany the last visible group through the selected route.
The older hero can defend the approach from the invading creatures if the encounter is active, or help
reinforce a barrier while the guard covers the route. The defense occurs outside civilian navigation space.
Use authored encounter rules and enemy families already present in the original reality; do not introduce a
new named species solely for the finale. A failed encounter restores the checkpoint and its local group
positions, not the entire evacuation ledger. No individual casualty count or arbitrary timer determines the
ending. Optional prior shelter help adds dialogue or supplies but cannot buy a more favorable metaphysical
outcome.

At the assembly area, each group reports in. A guard records verified arrivals, reported arrivals and people
whose status remains unknown. The player may ask for clarification but is not forced to find every missing
person in the world. The scope is this named settlement and its tracked evacuation parties, not all humanity.
The caregiver offers a place to rest. The older hero asks what would happen if the world stopped resembling
the other one. A survivor answers that they would still have tomorrow's work to do.

Resident: “We are not waiting here to become somebody else.”

Hero: “I needed to hear that.”

Caregiver: “Then remember it when you speak for us.”

The older protagonist places the route report beside his own name in the shelter ledger. He does not
physically send that book into the younger reality. On returning to the younger hero, a journal entry
identifies the report as remembered testimony, with the original shelter provenance attached. The report
proves that the tracked groups have a protected local assembly point; it does not prove that the entire
original world is safe or that the barrier will save everyone.

Scene 3 — The threshold confrontation
At fifteen, the hero enters the raised ring through a wide stone causeway. The moon's fractured outline is
framed between two broken uprights. The hostile goddess stands beyond the ring's center, with the Moon
Goddess visible at a separate anchor on the near side. The hostile goddess speaks of a history that was stolen
or hidden; she does not provide a definitive account of who launched the original invasion. The hero has no


obligation to deny that the cover-up hurt people. He challenges the conclusion that restoring what was lost
requires forcibly overwriting the lives that now exist. If the player completed S13, S29 or S41, short optional
lines cite the altered record, contradictory accounts or living identities respectively. The main dialogue
always remains understandable without those quests.

Hostile Goddess: “They will remember what they were.”

Hero: “That doesn't give you the right to erase who they are.”

Hostile Goddess: “You would defend a world built upon a forgotten one?”

Hero: “I would defend the people living in both.”

Moon Goddess: “The seam is still open. Keep it from widening.”

The encounter's goal is to interrupt forced convergence, not to kill every deity. In phase one, the hostile
goddess draws overlapping shapes across the ring. The active threat is shown by a solid ground outline,
animation anticipation and an audio cue with captions; the translucent scenery is never used as misleading
collision. Players move between clearly marked safe sectors and defend the near anchor. The hero can use
the established melee or ranged kit; an interaction alternative allows him to sever a visible tether after the
attack window. No particular weapon or optional discovery ability is required. The goddess's retreat to the
far side ends phase one and opens a stable checkpoint. Do not present the goddess as having died.

Phase two begins with an authored return to the older protagonist. The original shelter has felt a brief
disturbance, and the guard asks the hero to check the protected assembly area. The player inspects a
damaged brace, confirms the groups remain sheltered and helps secure the second passage from the
creatures. The task is to hold a safe connection long enough for the guard to finish the barrier, with clear
completion feedback and a restartable checkpoint. A distant broken ridge briefly resembles the intact ridge
from the other reality. The older hero recognizes the pattern without suddenly gaining all of the younger
hero's thoughts. The scout records the final local readiness flag. The player returns to the younger
perspective only after the older hero reaches the designated shelter checkpoint. No automatic transition
during a dodge, attack, dialogue or airborne traversal.

Phase three resumes on the near ring. The Moon Goddess explains that two anchor responses have become
distinguishable. The player disables the final forced-convergence tether using any compatible attack or the
accessible interaction window. The hostile goddess withdraws when her attempt can no longer sustain the
immediate overlap. She may speak one final line but must not expose an unapproved identity or swear
allegiance. The immediate danger is halted; the existing life-force link remains. This distinction makes a
nonfatal resolution possible and avoids treating sacrifice as the only means to stop an attack. The ring
becomes a stable deliberation area. No encounter timer, damage zone or NPC attack continues while the
player reads the outcome terms.

Hostile Goddess: “You think a different road will absolve the past?”

Hero: “No. I think people should be allowed to walk it.”

Scene 4 — Two alternatives, no hidden price
The Moon Goddess presents the options without praising one as morally superior. The hero asks how his
own link is involved. The explanation refers to the incomplete rescue, the silver mark and the already
observed connection between his lives. It does not suddenly add a requirement that every deity approve the


decision. The keeper reads back the shelter report and distinguishes what was verified from what is still
unknown. The decision is a deliberate narrative choice made after the danger subsides, not a quick-time
event. The player can return to the review screen or postpone at the checkpoint. Neither a hover nor a
dialogue-skip action selects a path.

Hero: “If the worlds separate, what happens to them?”

Moon Goddess: “Their lives remain their own. The shelter you helped prepare is still there. I cannot promise
what every tomorrow will bring.”

Hero: “And if the thread is released?”

Moon Goddess: “Your original existence would end. What comes after would be a different life, not the same
life returned. We must be certain that the people you helped are not made to pay for that choice.”

Proposed separation contract (requires writer approval): restore the moon's protection around the
preserved reality and disengage the forced overlap. Both current protagonists retain their respective
existence; the established involuntary migration connection closes or becomes dormant (exact future access
mechanics deferred). The original world continues independently, including its inhabitants, unresolved
invasion danger and verified local shelter. No global victory over the creatures is implied. Memories already
formed remain part of the people who formed them; nobody is compelled to forget the current
relationships. The preserved world still faces human political consequences of its war. M20 can revisit both
worlds using explicitly separated epilogue scenes rather than reopening the seal as a travel mechanic. The
ending's emotional cost is that the protagonists cannot rely on the shared thread to keep encountering one
another.

Proposed rebirth contract (requires writer approval): interrupt the forced overlap and release the
protagonist's original life-force link, establishing the third reality described in the core bible. His original
playable existence ends through a symbolic divine transition, not a combat death or player-controlled self-
injury. The preserved reality remains inhabited and distinct; its existing relationships are not retroactively
declared fictional. For a concrete production candidate, the original reality also remains independent, and
verified shelter residents continue their lives there. The third reality's inhabitants are distinct lives, not
replacements that extinguish existing people. The original protagonist's personal story does not continue as
an active avatar. The reincarnated protagonist awakens in M20 with emotional familiarity, without conscious
memories of the catastrophe. This proposed survivor-preserving interpretation must be approved because
the earlier bible deliberately left the original world's fate unresolved. If the writer instead chooses a different
cosmology, author its explicit consequences for every affected population before enabling this branch. No
claim that everybody survives, or that any real-world loss is required to obtain peace.

If the project has not approved both contracts, the production implementation must keep the final
commitment disabled in development builds, with a clear internal reason. Previewing candidate cinematics is
allowed in a noncanonical test mode; it must not write a completed campaign save. The narrative draft
proceeds to M20 with two candidate outcome records, not a locked canon. The hostile goddess's remaining
motives, identity and original-invasion involvement stay outside the confirmation text. The player should
know the material price of their decision without being told every unanswered mystery.

Scene 5 — The final choice and handoff to M20
The player may speak to the Moon Goddess again, inspect the report, or use the keeper's safe exit before
committing. The journal shows both alternatives with equal visual weight and plain-language consequences


for the younger hero, older hero, preserved residents, original residents and the potential third reality. The
unresolved survival of people beyond the surveyed settlement is explicitly distinguished from a known
outcome. The selected option opens a separate confirmation panel with the chosen name, the consequence
summary and an equally accessible Back action. Do not preselect either route or label one true, good,
cowardly or superior. Accessibility mode reads each consequence in order and requires an explicit
confirmation action; controller focus initially lands on Back or the heading, not commit.

Moon Goddess: “Choose knowing what it costs.”

Hero: “Then let the people who remain have a future of their own.”

For separation, show the two horizon outlines drawing apart while the original assembly remains visibly
occupied in a short epilogue preview. Do not represent people disappearing as the seam closes. The younger
hero sees the moon become whole; the older hero sees his own sky, then returns to ordinary work with the
survivors. For rebirth, keep the original shelter's lamps and voices present before the symbolic release; then
fade to a quiet view of a mountain with an unfamiliar light. Avoid a graphic death, explicit method,
resurrection reward or celebration of losing oneself. M19 ends at a transition boundary; M20 owns the
playable awakening and the full mountain visit. The camera does not reveal the third reality before
commitment. Neither outcome grants a combat advantage. The same noncompetitive campaign completion
reward is available once; route-specific keepsakes, if approved, are cosmetic.

D43 — The Soundless Arch
R39 | Independent paired discovery | Coastal cliff. Source: R38 catalog D43. Mandatory two sea stacks and

No M19 gate or early ending spoiler.

Scene 1 — Where the wind disappears
At fifteen, the hero approaches a low grassy coastal shoulder. Wind bends coarse grass and gulls circle
offshore. A weathered post indicates a safe approach to the viewpoint. Two irregular sea stacks rise from the
water; one has a notch near its crown and the other has a narrow gap near its base. A far stone arch frames
the middle distance. On the stable viewing shelf their outlines form a wave glyph. The arrangement is
geological rather than a bright magical icon painted across the sky. Focus assist outlines the three contours
on request. The hero notices the absence of echoes despite seeing waves break below. Sound remains
supplementary: a caption describes the sudden change, and the glyph is readable at minimum graphics or
with sound muted.

Hero (15): “I should be able to hear the water from here.”

A fisher remarks that winds sometimes carry sound away from the cliff. The hero can ask about the arch or
continue without speaking. The fisher does not know of the concealed cave. Record the first focus through
the normal observation action or its accessible toggle variant. A stable shelf and a clear path back make
accidental falls avoidable. No mandatory glide or timed camera challenge. If the player arrives from a later
chapter, use context-appropriate dialogue without changing the geometry or observation state.

Scene 2 — The noise of a harbor
At eighteen, the hero reaches an established evacuation route near a damaged harbor through an authorized
exploration opportunity. Two broken timber gantries and an outer loading frame match the outlines of the
distant rocks and arch. They are practical harbor structures with recognizable load beams and bracing, not
ruins installed by the hostile goddess. A guard calls directions toward the inland assembly route. The player
can help steady a loose sign or speak with a waiting traveler, but the focus lies outside the civilian path. The
evacuation cannot be reset by repeating this discovery; use the current harbor phase or an earlier safe
observation opportunity as appropriate to the campaign.

Guard: “Inland, past the third post. Don't wait on the quay.”

Hero (18): “The wind's wrong. I thought I heard waves from somewhere else.”

The older hero recognizes the wave shape from behind a stable rail. A brief silence makes the harbor feel
unexpectedly distant. He touches the place near his ear where a familiar charm would hang, but no item
physically transfers. Record F18 with the canonical glyph asset and original reality provenance. Return to the
younger life at the next authorized safe transition, never via unrestricted migration unlocked by this puzzle.

Scene 3 — The cave beyond the arch
When both observations are recorded, the younger hero may activate the focus. The edge of the far arch
resolves into an entry along a sheltered rock spur. Reveal starts only when destination floor, collision, entry


and exit are loaded. A stable stone causeway or walkable coastal ledge leads to the cave; no blind leap over
moving water. The route stays available while occupied, even when focus is released. The player enters a
curved passage with damp walls, salt-white deposits and a shallow pool reflecting daylight. Two routes lead
to the same chamber: a broad dry ramp and an optional short stepping-stone crossing with a scenic alcove.
The wet route is not required. Tide is a visual and audio motif rather than a compulsory time gate. Avoid
moving collision beneath the player.

Inside the relic chamber, a piece of driftwood has been fitted into a wall niche. The ear charm lies in a worn
pouch, protected from standing water. The player examines its uneven edge and simple attachment loop.
The object is personal rather than a sacred key: the hero remembers the sensation of adjusting it in the wind.
He can equip it as a cosmetic if an existing attachment slot permits; otherwise it remains inspectable in the
journal. The reward claim is distinct from memory interaction and can be completed later if inventory is full.
The cave is quiet but not ominous by default.

Hero (15): “The sea was louder then.”

Scene 4 — A quieter way to move
The relic inspection offers an optional movement tutorial in a dry chamber. A nonhostile shore creature
occupies a distant ledge. An on-screen indicator demonstrates the difference between ordinary walking and
controlled quiet steps. Practice uses an existing movement control or configurable toggle, not a mandatory
new key. Skipping the exercise does not withhold the reward. Proposed ability: reduce ordinary wildlife
hearing-detection radius by 25% while walking at controlled speed, with a balance-tunable cap and no bonus
during sprint, attacks, spellcasting or combat. It does not affect scripted detection, hostile boss logic, player
visibility or any competitive mode. If a matching perk is already owned, grant the personal keepsake and
ordinary discovery reward instead of stacking. Never require stealth to exit the cave or complete a main
quest.

Scene 5 — The second view
An ascending dry ramp leads to an overlook inside the arch. From here the younger hero aligns the notch of
the sea stack, an inner ledge and the distant shoreline. For a moment they correspond to the harbor rail,
gantry and evacuation route. The older hero's optional counterpart observation shows an ordinary coast
rather than the ruined pier. Neither character sees the third reality before the ending. The second vista
records recognition, not a third paired-focus gate; offer an assist interaction. Before M17 the journal
describes an unexplained experience; afterward it can reference convergence without attributing this relic to
the hostile goddess. The hero descends a separate dry return path. The fisher may ask whether he found a
sheltered way around the cliff. The answer can remain private. Completion leaves a durable record, not a
permanently open portal between worlds.

S13 — The Page That Wasn't Burned
R39 | Optional investigation | Wolfveil archive alley. Source: catalog S13 and M08 full R28 script. Mandatory
surviving scrap from bookbinder, evidence of deliberate alteration, complete insignia and phrase on original
notice, comparison of three records. Strengthens M08 without owning its route copy, location unlock or
migration. No identification of whoever removed the records. Newly quoted wording and binder staging
proposed.

Scene 1 — The binder's ordinary work
The younger protagonist notices a bookbinder repairing a worn volume near the archive side alley. The shop
opens onto the street, with a paper table, hand press and shelf of thread and cloth. It is a working trade
space rather than a hidden resistance headquarters. The binder separates ruined pages from usable ones
when a scrap with an unfamiliar crest catches the hero's attention. If M08 already introduced the damaged
manuscript, the hero recognizes its three-lobed outline. Otherwise he asks why the scrap was retained, and
the journal marks an unidentified fragment. The binder does not instantly trust the hero or deliver a
complete explanation. He points to a repair ledger and explains that the paper was brought in with
instructions to dispose of damaged leaves. Part of the instruction appears added later in a different hand.
This is evidence of deliberate alteration, not proof of who authorized it.

Binder: “They asked me to burn it.”

Hero: “Who?”

Binder: “I never saw their face.”

Hero: “Why keep this part?”

Act I — The fracture and first transition (M01–M06)
M01 — The Journey Home. Required event is the lunar fragment and arrival at the clearing; merchant

continuity are implementation prerequisites.

M03 — The Fractured Beast. Owns the transformation, defense and beginner boss. Defeating the beast does
not repair the lunar seal or reveal the older life before the authored M04 handoff. All supported weapon

once, not skip a fight the player has not completed.

M04 — The Forgotten War. First playable age-18 reveal; preserve the absence of a definitive dream label and

M05 — The Silver Path. Owns the catastrophe at the threshold, original body's death and preservation of life
force. Ordinary gameplay defeat is distinct from this authored narrative event, which commits only once.
M06's subsequent waking must not erase the original playable existence, reset its equipment or imply that
the player can repeat the transition manually. Do not introduce an ordinary revive prompt for this particular
authored event.

M06 — A Life Forgotten. Owns mark inspection, meeting Lyra and open-world access. The Oath Tree, local

optional service interactions are skipped.

Act II — Physical evidence and personal history (M07–M13)
M07 — The Mark of a Forgotten Age. The fresh carving at eighteen and aged counterpart at fifteen must use

activation. M10 consumes the active overlap; it must not create a separate new ritual or replay the seal-
