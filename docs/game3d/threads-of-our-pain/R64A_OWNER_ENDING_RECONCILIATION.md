# R64A — Owner-directed finale reconciliation (working narrative baseline)
27 November 2026. Source: owner's spoken decisions after R63. Owner confirmed the two directions and authorized continuation. Treat these directions as the working baseline, with final script sign-off and unspecified mechanics still pending. Supersedes conflicting ending proposals in R39–R41, R54, R61–R63; retain historical documents for provenance. Do not treat the former survivor-preserving Separation/Rebirth matrix as current.

## Shared foundation
The Moon Goddess could temporarily preserve/relocate existence by providing a parallel reality capable of surviving the catastrophe, but the laws of nature prevent her from making that borrowed placement permanent herself. The people and their relationships were real experiences, not a trick or fabricated illusion. Their presence in the borrowed reality is temporary. The hero's struggle was for the survival of his own existence and the destroyed reality. The prior sword example establishes asymmetric time: an object left moments earlier from his perspective may have been present for centuries in the other reality. The exact metaphysics, absolute dates, and causal order of all local scenes still need authoring. Keep M05 physical death as an experienced event in the original history; do not make that death an irreversible postgame rule. The owner explicitly confirmed that the fused outcome replaces the prior timeline.

## M19/M20 branch A — Sacrifice / restored reality
The age-eighteen sacrifice triggers a transition through his fifteen-year-old self: that self migrates into his eighteen-year-old existence and opens his eyes in the restored parallel reality. The younger self does not remain a separate active protagonist by default. Show green grass, ordinary sky and absence of the burning sky. His realization is that he fought for the very existence and reality that were destroyed, not simply for escape from danger. Do not apply the previous draft's mandatory memory-erasure rule: recognition and realization are now required; exact breadth of memories remains TBD.
The restored reality becomes the absolute/surviving timeline. Elements of the other reality can fuse into its history. The people he recently knew in the age-fifteen reality are now part of the past, deceased by the time he returns to its remnants. For him only moments may pass; for them the interval may be roughly a thousand years (illustrative, not a fixed chronology). Do not present the earlier draft's continuously inhabited age-fifteen village as the current post-ending world. The former realities remain available as narrative foundations for future expansion in a different manner, not an automatically unlocked realm selector.
Expansion direction: return to the former world to give the departed proper burials, recover or save their spirits, and attempt to migrate their existence permanently to the restored timeline. The Goddess could grant temporary placement only; the hero's task would be permanent restoration. Potentially children and other people could continue in a new life, reincarnate, or be restored/migrated to that timeline. Exact mechanism, eligibility, consent, identity, memory, successful outcomes and how the hero can act within natural law remain open. Do not promise all are immediately restored or grant a new migration power in the base ending.

## M19/M20 branch B — Refuse sacrifice / fusion and recovery
The protagonist accepts what happened at eighteen instead of escaping it and brings his existence into his eighteen-year-old self. He remains in the resulting reality. The two parallel realities fuse: the landscape/scenery of the age-fifteen preserved world carries the damage, scars, losses and aftermath of the age-eighteen original world. The temporary separate state dissipates; this is not two independently playable postgame worlds, nor a declaration that the borrowed people were illusions. The Goddess provided a reality capable of surviving the damage, not a reality that erases suffering. The owner confirmed the earlier death timeline is replaced for this ending and players continue playing after the finale.
Show a rebuilding society roughly twenty years later, a new generation working alongside survivors, visible repair and unrepaired scars, and acceptance of loss and confusion. Theme: confront and persevere through consequences rather than simply erase them. Do not imply universal survival or a complete cure, and do not automatically age the playable avatar to thirty-eight: the relation between the twenty-year epilogue and playable postgame start is TBD. No forced new quest or faction choice determines the ending.

## Claude: branch-specific terrain and cinematic direction (proposed implementation)
Use shared surveyed geography/landmark IDs, with distinct timeline/phase variants and actor lifetimes. For Sacrifice, stage the awakening on the intact grassy landscape with a normal sky, then preserve ancient remnants, weathered personal effects, graves and memorial sites as expansion anchors; do not create access or spirit-transfer mechanics yet. For Refusal, reuse age-fifteen landforms but overlay actual damage from original-world events at mapped corresponding sites: broken spans, scorched ground, damaged architecture and surviving structures. Add repaired routes, new generation NPCs, rebuilding work, retained ruins and memorials in the roughly twenty-year epilogue. These are proposed assets, not surveyed coordinates or authorized construction. Preserve accessibility and collision; active timeline only, low-poly reusable meshes and bounded effects. No automatic old-world travel. No new full maps or game code in this revision.
