# D18 — Steps of the Other Side / S22 — The Cavern of Returning Steps

R21 | Source: R20 catalog D18 and S22. Mandatory: Inverted Cavern, three stair rails versus broken stairs and support beams, sideways-looking corridor, safe platforms, Air Recovery technique, catalog dialogue. ## D18 — Scene 1: A staircase with two beginnings

Age fifteen. The entrance to the cavern lies in a ridge of layered gray stone. Afternoon light falls across a broad ledge, but farther inside, thin seams of white mineral catch the glow of a lamp that an explorer has set on a stone. She is holding a knotted survey cord, moving it from one railing to another. Her boots are dusty on one side and wet on the other. She has been trying to determine why a route that should lead to the lower chamber keeps returning her to the same landing.

Explorer (embarrassed, then irritated): “I came down that stair twice.”
Hero (checking the water dripping from the roof): “The walls haven't moved.”
Explorer: “Then what did?”

He walks toward the rail she indicates. Three rail fragments stand at different distances: one on the near ledge, one at a crooked intermediate stair, and one high on the opposite wall. Each looks ordinary on its own. Seen together from the safe ledge, the gaps appear to form a single bent staircase. Water still drips downward and dust falls toward the floor. The physical laws of the cave have not changed; the view is deceptive because the cave's ruined construction and natural terraces happen to align. Do not imply that a disorienting camera represents a change in gravity.

Player agency: examine the cord and markings, ask the explorer about her route, or continue without accepting S22. If the player starts with the far rail, the explorer points to the near ledge with her open hand: “Try standing where I left the lamp.” If asked whether she is lost, she responds, “Not lost. Just very tired of arriving here.” A successful partial alignment prompts the hero to put a foot forward, then stop because the imagined stair is not yet physically present. The player retains camera and movement control; no forced zoom, camera rotation or walk off a ledge.

## D18 — Scene 2: The stair that came apart

Age eighteen, original reality. The corresponding cavern contains a damaged service stair and a timber-supported passage leading toward an evacuation shelf. A scout has gone ahead to inspect the lower route; a maintenance worker braces a hanging board. The older protagonist can see the broken stair's support structure, which was concealed by stone in the other life. He understands where his weight can be placed, even when he cannot see the next tread.

Worker (without looking away from the brace): “Last two steps are gone.”
Hero: “The beam underneath is still there.”
Worker: “You won't get a cart across it.”
Hero: “Then keep the carts above. I'll check the footpath.”

The player examines a near broken railing, a diagonal support beam and the far stair bracket from a stable landing. These three parts complete the matching bent-stair shape. The worker will not advance into the gap; the hero can demonstrate the older self's already-known air recovery on a low practice platform, or take the adjacent ramp. The older technique is part of his experience, not a newly acquired power. This scene can be played during the appropriate original-world migration window, after M06, and its alignment flag is saved independently of the younger scene. No mandatory replay of an invasion encounter.

If the player aligned the younger site first, a faint familiar sensation accompanies the older pose: his foot anticipates the landing before he can see it. If older first, the younger notices his body leaning toward the matching rail. Neither character names the connection. Players may leave either scene and return later; the system stores the completed perspective without forcing the story to switch realities on demand.

## D18 — Scene 3: The corridor on its side

With both perspectives recorded, voluntary focus at the younger ledge makes a recess visible behind the lowest railing. Its doorway is a skewed trapezoid cut into the rock, aligned to read like an upright door only from that viewpoint. The reveal should feel like an overlooked route becoming apparent, not the entire cavern spinning. Loose pebbles continue falling straight down. The explorer, if nearby, can react to the opening but does not acquire cross-reality memories: “Was that there when we came in?” The player can step onto a broad entrance landing while focus is active. Release closes the exterior visual opening, but interior collision and an independent return passage remain available. No forced timing jump through the threshold.

Inside, a sideways-looking corridor carries a row of former wall niches along its apparent floor and traces of treads across one side wall. These are architectural remnants of a tilted stone structure embedded in stable geology. The actual walkable floor is a sequence of pale, worn platforms and natural rock benches. Use dangling cloth, falling dust, and a shallow runoff channel as consistent world-up references. The player chooses between a stepped lower route with two low, recoverable gaps, and an upper shelf with a broad ramp that avoids jumps. A third short side nook contains survey scratches and a broken sandal, adding history without a quest reward.

At the first gap the hero can inspect scrape marks and a footprint. A loose decorative tread shifts if stepped on, giving a visible tilt and low creak before it drops a short distance onto a catch shelf. The player lands safely below and can climb back via a ramp; no hidden instant death, no mandatory damage. If the player does not wish to use jumping, the upper route is clearly visible at the junction. The two routes converge at a practice bay where the smooth wall bears a carved diagram of a falling figure and a second arrow curving toward a landing. The diagram is not an exposition panel about gravity or other worlds. It is a practical mnemonic left by an unknown traveler.

## D18 — Scene 4: The next step

At the end of the bay, a handrail is polished where countless hands once steadied themselves. The younger hero puts his hand on it. His knees bend instinctively, and he catches his balance with a compact mid-air correction during a short guided practice movement. He does not need to successfully complete a skill challenge to recover the technique; examining the rail and choosing to learn grants it once, with practice optional and repeatable. Use a dedicated animation for a controlled tuck and extension rather than a teleport, snap or flight power.

Hero (surprised at how little he needs to think): “I know where the next step is.”

Cut to the older protagonist only if a safe narrative transition is available; otherwise queue the echo for his next playable visit. He is holding his familiar rail or bracing after a short jump. His shoulders ease and his body remembers the moment of first mastering the movement. He already knows Air Recovery; no duplicate unlock or loss of ability. Both selves experience a shared confidence rather than another identical shock reaction. The younger unlocks Air Recovery as an optional technique: proposed one controlled correction during eligible airborne movement, reset on stable landing, no chained flight, no damage increase and no bypass of locked geometry. Exact input, cooldown and availability require movement-system review. Provide a hold/tap alternative and a visible recovery prompt. Do not override existing dodge or jump bindings.

## D18 — Scene 5: A hand on the railing

The second focus lies on a broad balcony just beyond the practice bay. A chipped pillar, parallel rail and slanted beam form a distinct arch-like outline; from the equivalent original landing, a support and lamp bracket complete the same shape. The vista reveals the older protagonist guiding a nervous evacuee along the footpath. A short cinematic can play within the player's current camera framing without stealing control. The older hero demonstrates his route from a secure platform and waits until the evacuee grips the rail.

Evacuee (breath held, eyes fixed on the gap): “I can't see the floor.”
Older hero: “Look at the rail. Keep your hand there. The step's right below it.”
Evacuee (after crossing): “I thought I'd have to jump.”
Older hero: “Not everyone needs to.”

The younger hero instinctively extends his hand toward the empty rail, then lowers it when he notices no one is there. He remembers the feeling of helping someone steady themselves but not their name. This is not evidence that the evacuee migrated or that both scenes occurred at the same historical moment. A separate return ramp exits onto the original exploration ledge; the player can come back for practice without replaying the vista or receiving another unlock.

## S22 — Independent optional quest: The Cavern of Returning Steps

Trigger: see the explorer pacing between two familiar landings, hear her asking why she has returned, or inspect the survey cord. If D18 was completed first, her dialogue changes: “You found another way down. Can you show me where I went wrong?” Her task is to survey the ordinary cavern loop; it does not require the focus-revealed annex or Air Recovery. The two events share geology but distinct quest flags. She is a capable explorer, not a helpless escort, and the player may give advice without taking control of her movement.

Scene A — Establish a reference. The explorer stretches a cord along a fixed railing; the player places or selects a reusable marker on the ordinary loop's junction. The puzzle asks which passage changes height, which goes around a pillar, and which actually returns to the landing. The player can follow the visible water gradient, use railing shape or ask for her notes. An accessible text description identifies these same properties without relying on perspective or color alone. If the player guesses incorrectly, she follows the route with her eyes, not her body, and says, “That bends back to the lamp. Let's check the other one.” No consumable loss. No countdown.

Scene B — Test a route. Choose to walk together via the wide ramp; scout a lower platform loop and return with the result; or have her remain at the lamp while comparing the survey cord and visible terrain. Explorer (wide route): “That's a much kinder staircase.” Hero: “It still takes us down.” Explorer (scout route): “You came back from the left. I would have sworn that went up.” On the independent route, she traces the new line in her notebook and can explain the illusion to the player. The lower route has optional loose stones with a catch shelf and bypass; no mandatory monsters. If incidental wildlife is used, stage it outside the puzzle corridor, with disengagement space. NPC waits at the marked landing if player leaves, falls or migrates; resume from the last validated reference. Never make her leap a gap or walk onto an unloaded bridge.

Scene C — Close the survey. The explorer fixes her original map instead of discarding it. “I drew the stair from where I thought I was standing.” Hero: “Draw it from where you actually stopped.” She writes a note beside the misleading loop and leaves a copy on the public notice post outside. A revisit finds travelers reading the revised sketch. If the player refuses help, she remains on the safe ledge and can be approached later. Reward proposal: modest exploration experience, survey material and a friendly dialogue flag; no Air Recovery duplication or forced map upgrade. Complete on validated survey and handover; not on accepting D18. Optional original-world scene: the older hero helps the worker identify stable supports; S22 journal may record an ambiguous echo, but the older task is not required for S22 completion and his survival does not depend on the younger quest.
