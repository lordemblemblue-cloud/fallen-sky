# M06 — A Life Forgotten

## Scene 1 — The sound of ordinary things

At first there is only the sound of water. The boy's fingers close against damp grass as though attempting to hold another person's hand. The camera begins at ordinary third-person height, not a simulated first-person blackout. He sits up beside the inclined stone in the lunar clearing, where the grass has been flattened by his weight. A small insect moves across a leaf. He watches it for several seconds because nothing in its movement belongs to the city he remembers. His breathing still follows the rhythm of the final run in M05, then slows as the player regains control. There is no injury transfer from the older body's death, no corpse beside him and no second avatar visible.

Hero (looking at his palm): “I was holding on to something.”
Goddess (from a short distance, approaching only when he turns): “Can you stand?”
Hero: “I think so.”
Goddess: “Then take your time.”

The Goddess is seated against a stone, visibly tired. Her hair has lost most of its silver emission. She stands with effort rather than gliding. She offers a hand only if the player chooses to approach, so the player can first examine the peaceful clearing, the scar in the soil from M03 and the unbroken distant ridge. The scar is local to the younger world's beast encounter, not evidence of the city invasion.

Hero (voice becoming more urgent): “Why do I remember a city burning?”
Goddess (briefly stops): “Let me see your hand.”
Hero: “There were people there. They were trying to get across a river.”
Goddess: “Your hand, please.”

A fine silver mark lies across the palm, appearing only when he turns it toward ordinary light. The mark is a physical narrative clue, not a new spell button. The Goddess holds his wrist gently and notices the mark without declaring its meaning. Her expression changes from concern to recognition of an anomaly; she does not recognize the two lives as a fully explained mechanism in this scene. Her fingers tremble, then she releases him. Use a voluntary inspect interaction and caption describing the mark; no compulsory camera zoom.

Choice A, insist: Hero: “I remember falling. I remember not getting up.” Goddess: “I believe that you remember it.” Hero: “That's not the same as saying it happened.” Goddess (quietly): “No. It isn't.”

Choice B, ask about survivors: Hero: “The people on the road. Did they get through?” Goddess: “I saw people reach safety. I cannot tell you what became of everyone.” Hero: “There was a cart with a blanket.” Goddess: “Remember that, then. Don't let anyone tell you that remembering is worthless.” Do not imply she knows the fate of the specific family.

Choice C, avoid discussion: Hero: “Can I go home?” Goddess: “Follow the stream until it meets the road. If you feel unsteady, sit before you climb.” She does not punish silence or withhold the return route.

The Goddess asks him to return to the settlement. She does not provide a magical explanation, erase his recollection, promise that all humans survived, or reveal the hostile goddess's name. The camera remains on the boy's face after the exchange only if the player opts into the cinematic; otherwise use ambient dialogue with normal movement. When she leaves the interaction zone, do not show unexplained teleportation as a clue unless later approved; a bend in the path is sufficient to remove her from view. Her later appearances remain story-controlled.

## Scene 2 — A path that has no smoke

The walk to Wolfveil deliberately mirrors a few shapes from the older evacuation road. The same curved bank and inclined slab are visible through trees; the older road had damaged walls, while this one has living roots. The boy slows at the sound of a wooden wheel, looks across a stream and sees a merchant with a small cart of apples. The ordinary sound briefly resembles the damaged cart in M05. The sound cue is quiet and localized; no flashback overrides movement. An optional interaction lets the player inspect the bend and record “The river seems familiar.” The journal records observations, not conclusions.

Merchant (checking a loose basket): “You're staring at the wheel.”
Hero: “Sorry. I thought it was broken.”
Merchant (turns it once): “It's squeaking. That's all.”
Hero: “Good.”

If the player helps secure the basket, the merchant thanks him and points out the road. If they leave, the merchant resumes work and the main objective remains available. If they previously helped the M01 merchant, reuse that relationship where geography and actor availability permit; do not assign a new identity to the earlier merchant without confirmation. The encounter is a brief optional beat, not an additional counted quest or compulsory resource collection.

At the village approach, a wooden sign has a pale hand-painted direction mark. A child runs past carrying a loaf wrapped in cloth. The boy almost warns them to stay behind the cart, then stops. This restrained animation shows learned protective instinct rather than a paranormal ability. The player can walk to the gate or stop at a bench; resting does not switch realities on demand. A short voice line plays once after arrival, “I thought I knew this road.” No repetition on later travel.

## Scene 3 — Lyra and the open gate

Lyra the Seeker is standing near the fountain, checking the day's arrivals on a slate. She moves out of the central walkway before speaking, and a merchant passes between the pair without being frozen by a cinematic. The town should be active but not congested: a smith's hammer, ordinary conversation and flowing water replace the earlier battlefield noise.

Lyra (looking up): “You look as though you've walked all night.”
Hero: “It feels like longer.”
Lyra: “Where from?”
Hero: “The forest. I think.”
Lyra (sets the slate down): “Well, you're in Wolfveil. Water's there. Sit wherever there's space.”

If he mentions the city, she says, “Which city? There are a few names on these roads.” He cannot provide a reliable name. If he asks whether he is dreaming, she replies, “You're standing in my way, so I'll ask you to move either way.” Deliver gently with a small smile, not ridicule. If he shows the mark, she examines it only with permission: “The archive has records of strange marks. I won't pretend I know this one.” No identification of the mark as a known relic. All three branches converge on an invitation to explore, with optional archive information retained for M08.

Lyra: “The smith is along the lower street. The traders use the square, and the road by the tree leads out to the hills. If you need a place to begin, try the mountain trail. A surveyor's been asking about old passages.”
Hero: “What if I don't know what I'm looking for?”
Lyra: “Then you can start by looking.”

The player may inspect the Oath Tree. A local ribbon tradition offers a nonbinding promise to avoid hurting travelers under its shade; decline is respected, no faction membership, penalties or magical restriction. Lyra: “Some people tie a ribbon when they arrive. It's a reminder, not a toll.” Hero optional: “I'll remember without one.” Lyra: “That's fine.” Preserve the catalog's optional interaction, not the old War of Three Roads mandatory pledge system. No colors that assign allegiance automatically. The old BAND1 script's grounded aid and civic welcome inform the pacing; its mandatory oath and superseded terrain are not imported.

## Scene 4 — The world opens

Open-world access unlocks after the brief meeting, even if the player refuses the ribbon, skips dialogue or has no optional discoveries. A small unobtrusive journal entry points to the mountain trail for M07. The player can instead use the existing Blacksmith, Appearance, Companion, Spirituality, Character, Questbook, Map and Store service entry points where actually available; no new implementation assumed. Show context prompts one at a time, allow dismiss/reopen, avoid dumping all menus onto the screen. A local travel sign describes known roads, with unexplored regions remaining unmarked. S01–S08 and D02–D06 may become available only when their authored prerequisites and actual systems are ready; do not gate M07 on them. Gliding and focus are introduced according to their own unlocks, not granted by M06.

A final quiet moment at the fountain occurs on request, not a mandatory cutscene. Hero: “I remember dying. So why am I still here?” The line is whispered only after the player elects to reflect; the primary journal uses “I remember falling at the barrier.” His confusion is real, but the canonical death is developer knowledge, not a player-visible metaphysical answer. He resolves to look for physical evidence rather than simply trust dreams. M07 provides the first compelling chronological contradiction through a matching cave carving.
