# D08 — Behind the White Water / S10 — Water Behind Water

Revision 2026-11-26-R11. Source: inspected R10 master, D08 and S10 catalog entries. Expanded proposed scene script and blockout brief; no terrain built. D08 grants Waterproof Lantern; S10 is a separately tracked optional quest. The fisherman, his motives, sluice design and dialogue beyond catalog lines are new proposals. No main-quest gate.

## Scene 1 — The fisherman who stopped fishing (S10)

Age fifteen, Silent Waterfall. Late afternoon. The forest path narrows into a shelf of dark, wet stone. Water falls in a wide white curtain, its roar swallowing ordinary conversation until the player reaches the sheltered bank. A fisherman sits there with his rod lying unused across his knees. Every few moments he lifts his head toward the pool, waits for the surface to settle, then looks toward the cliff with increasing annoyance. His basket is empty. He is not waiting for a fish.

FISHER [gestures with the butt of his rod, without looking at the hero]: “The water shows a doorway.”

HERO [looks across the pool]: “But the rock doesn't.”

FISHER [matter-of-fact, then quieter]: “Look again.”

The player can move freely to a flat rock beside him. In the water, a dark recess appears where the visible cliff has no recess. A protruding slab and the curved root of an alder complete part of a key-shaped outline. Let the protagonist shade his eyes with one hand while retaining camera control; he is curious, not frightened. The fisherman explains he has watched for three afternoons because a loose float disappeared into the current at that point and emerged farther downstream. He wants to know whether a hidden watercourse could trap somebody, not to claim a treasure.

Choice — Help: “Show me where the float went.” Fisher: “I'll stay here. Don't go under the fall itself.” Sets S10 accepted and marks safe bank, not the hidden entrance. Investigate: “Does it happen every time?” Fisher: “Only from here, and only if the water settles.” Offers a non-spoiler focus hint; acceptance remains optional. Decline: “I've got somewhere to be.” Fisher: “Fair enough. If you hear anyone calling from the rocks, come and get me.” The fisherman remains available on later visits. D08 can be discovered without taking S10; free-roam players receive the same reward once.

## Scene 2 — Keep the oil dry (age eighteen)

Original reality, same river bend and cliff shoulder. Cold rain rattles on a wooden awning at the occupied supply tunnel. The waterfall is not a magical barrier here; a service ledge wraps around its edge toward a masonry mouth. Refugees are sheltering deeper inside, while two supply handlers carry sealed containers. The older protagonist is delivering lamp oil. The Waterproof Lantern already hangs at his belt, with a dent at the lower rim and a repaired clasp. Its small warm light shows the handrail through the spray.

SUPPLY HANDLER [raises a hand to stop him before a slick step]: “Put the crate here. I'll take it when the cart's clear.”

HERO [sets it on a dry stand, gently checking the lid]: “Keep it upright. Last thing we need is another lamp going out.”

A worker asks whether the outer passage could be used in heavy rain. The player may inspect the handrail and answer “Not until that rail's fixed,” or help move a crate away from the edge. Neither action is needed for the discovery. At the safe stand the tunnel's arch, ledge edge and curved support resolve the complementary key shape. He sees the line continue into the waterfall's reflection and stops speaking mid-sentence. For a breath, he hears a fishing reel clicking instead of the rattle of rain. He checks his lantern as if its clasp has come loose, then turns back to his work. This is playable original-world time; his belief is fatigue or a fleeting dream sensation, not an explanation of migration.

## Scene 3 — A key made of water (D08 first focus)

Return naturally to age fifteen through the existing migration system. At the bank, the pool's reflected recess becomes the key's shaft; the protruding slab forms its bit and the alder root closes the bow. The player aligns these three depth layers with ordinary movement and look input. When both reality flags are satisfied, the white curtain visually separates at the side, revealing a ledge that turns into the cliff. Do not stop the whole river or let the player walk through a lethal falling-water column. The effect is local. The fisherman, if present, rises and squints: “There. By that root.” He can perceive this local doorway, but does not migrate or see the original world's people.

HERO [backs away half a step, then looks between the reflection and rock]: “The reflection showed a different place.”

The player may cancel focus at any point. Before entry the visible side ledge closes from view; collision remains safe through threshold clearance. Once inside, a stone bypass door can be opened from within and leads to a permanent dry exit upstream. Never remove a platform under a player or lock a saved character inside. Re-entry uses the original alignment or the unlocked return passage. A low-effects alternative displays an etched outline of the same three shapes on a stable shallow-water proxy; it does not require real-time reflections.

## Scene 4 — A room filling with silence

The passage bends away from the falling water. Its roar drops into a dull vibration. Thin seams of light illuminate old waterlines, oxidized metal brackets and a row of shallow storage recesses. An empty hook rocks with the draft. The young hero notices the same worn shape as the lantern clasp in a dream he cannot place, but says nothing. The cave may have served a different community or no organized settlement at all; its architecture is not evidence of identical history.

A flooded depression bars the shortest route to a supply alcove. The player can drain it by opening a stone sluice from the dry platform, following a carved channel to its outlet. A jammed grate holds debris. Clear the debris with a nearby lever, then open the gate; moving water lowers to a visible marked line. The mechanism cannot flood the exit. On the optional swimming route, a shallow basin leads to an air pocket and ladder, with a visible rope and surface route available before submerging. A dry high shelf leads around the basin to the same lever and the alcove's rear stair, so swimming is never required. S10's drain objective can be completed from either route. If a player enters the current, a visible eddy and audio cue warn of drift; a catch shelf returns them to safe footing. No random drowning, compulsory combat or mandatory new equipment.

At a side recess an abandoned rack holds three empty jars and an unreadable tally; optional inspection records evidence of storage rather than giving a definitive history lesson. If a player tries to open the drain before clearing it, a low metallic knock and a visible stationary grate explain the failure. No damage. The sluice can be retried indefinitely. The objective reads “Find where the pool drains,” not “Recover your memories.”

## Scene 5 — The lantern that should not work

Beyond the lowered pool lies a small supply alcove, raised above the old floodline. On a stone bench rests a broken lantern with a dent at the lower rim and its clasp hanging loose. The player may inspect the hinge before touching it. The hero finds an intact spare pin nearby and closes the clasp using a practiced twist; an inner glass sleeve holds the flame away from spray. This is a fictional equipment repair, not a real-world survival tutorial. The proposed reward is the catalog Waterproof Lantern, not a duplicate unrelated item.

HERO [tests the handle, surprise replacing concentration]: “There. That's how it catches.”

He holds it above the wet stone and smiles at the steady light, then looks at his fingers. The motion felt like the hundredth time he had done it, yet he cannot recall the first. In the original reality, the older hero reaches down to the lantern he already wears. Its familiar repaired clasp seems new to him; the memory of a first useful light in a frightening tunnel arrives with a warmth he cannot name.

SUPPLY HANDLER [noticing his expression]: “Still working?”

HERO (18) [a small, genuine smile]: “Better than I remembered.”

The older inventory remains unchanged. Younger receives the Waterproof Lantern once, a utility light intended to remain active in spray and wet traversal under the game's approved rules. No unapproved damage or resistance statistics. Reward claim and quest completion are independent. If the player has already claimed it, the bench remains empty and the animation is replaced with a brief recognition gesture.

## Scene 6 — A light on the other shore (D08 second focus)

An uphill gallery opens onto a protected natural window. From a broad shelf the river bend, root arch and split cliff align again, forming a key turned sideways. The player must have claimed the lantern; the lantern itself need not be equipped to avoid accessibility or inventory friction. The second alignment reveals a corresponding view of the original supply tunnel in an actual lived moment. The young hero sees the supply handler pause to let a frightened child pass. The older hero holds his lantern at knee height so the child can see the wet step without being dazzled.

CHILD [barely audible over the water]: “Will it go out?”

HERO (18) [steady, unhurried]: “Not while we're walking. Watch the next step.”

The younger protagonist moves his own lantern toward the same empty step, then freezes at the absurdity of the movement. He withdraws his hand and breathes slowly. He does not recognize the older man as himself; the player can recognize the shared gesture. The older protagonist hears a single peaceful birdcall over the rain, looks toward the waterfall, then returns his attention to the child. The view fades on voluntary unfocus. Journal: “The cliff has another shape in the water. Someone was carrying the same light.” No cosmology, Celestial Sight or named hostile goddess.

## Scene 7 — What the fisherman wanted (S10 resolution)

Return to the bank via the independent upstream stair. If the fisherman is present he notices the repaired lantern before the player speaks. He stands, wipes his wet palms on his trousers, and asks whether the passage can be reached safely. He does not demand the lantern; his interest is the missing float and the danger to travelers.

Choice — Explain the current: “There's a drain under the pool. Your float probably went through it.” Fisher [relieved]: “So the river wasn't swallowing things. Just taking them somewhere else.” Sets local safety notice and cautious fishing routine. Share the doorway: “There is a passage. I opened a dry way out.” Fisher [thoughtful]: “I'll warn people off the slippery bank. No reason for them to risk it.” Sets alternate-path dialogue; he does not enter a still-secret unsafe route. Keep the strange reflection private: “The water runs through a cave. I made it safe to leave.” Fisher: “That's enough for me.” Sets same completion with restrained trust response. If D08 was completed before S10, a shorter retrospective report is available after the player accepts; no repeat lantern grant. If the player leaves without reporting, S10 remains active and may be completed later. Proposed S10 reward: modest local goodwill/quest XP at established balance, not another lantern. After completion, fisherman fishes from a stable bank; travelers avoid the hazardous wet edge. The optional lore tally may be mentioned, but no mandatory follow-up is invented.

## Quest and discovery implementation contract

D08 objectives: focus15 + focus18 → reveal and cross threshold → claim repaired lantern → second focus. S10 may complete before D08's second focus, or after it. No mandatory main-story flags; require only existing migration/focus availability, and if either perspective is inaccessible defer without failure. Persist flags across migrations, saves and retries. Older delivery scene and local NPC states resume at appropriate story availability. Third reality only conditional ending: healthy stream, no invasion supply history; optional civic lantern shelter is a proposed decoration, not mandatory evidence of a past war.
