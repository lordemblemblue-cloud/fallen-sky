## Scene 1 — The empty shelf (S12, age fifteen)

The woodland path rises through an irregular stand of birches. Rain has ended; drops continue falling from the leaves onto a roof of rough slate. The shrine is modest enough that a traveler might mistake it for a resting place. Its sandstone walls are worn smooth at shoulder height, where generations have leaned while removing their boots. Three candles burn in recessed niches, separated by thick ribs of stone. Their reflections tremble in a shallow brass bowl. The keeper is counting objects on a shelf, moving each into an empty space and then returning it to its original position.

KEEPER [embarrassed at being observed, not angry]: “The offerings keep vanishing.”

HERO [looking at the remaining coins]: “What did they take?”

KEEPER: “Cloth. A little oil. The wooden cup. Not a single coin.”

The player can inspect the shelf, examine the threshold or speak before investigating. The shelf has rectangular dust-free patches; the threshold has a small damp footprint and a strip of linen caught on a splinter. The clues support need rather than prove a particular culprit. The hero looks at the unused coins for a moment longer.

HERO [catalog line; practical, without accusation]: “Someone needs them.”

KEEPER [sighs, opens both palms]: “Then let's leave supplies.”

If the player suspects theft: HERO: “We should find out who's taking them.” KEEPER: “Find out why, too. I don't want anyone hurt over a cup.” If the player offers supplies immediately: KEEPER: “That's kind, but I'd rather know what they're missing. Cloth won't feed someone.” If declined, the keeper remains available; the shrine is not destroyed or depleted by an invisible timer. S12 begins on acceptance or inspection followed by consent. D10 remains accessible independently.

## Scene 2 — An ordinary injury (S12)

A faint cough comes from the covered woodpile below the shrine. Following a small trail of dropped linen threads, the player finds a young adult traveler sheltering with an injured companion. The traveler is using torn cloth to wrap the companion's wrist; the cup sits beside a kettle. The companion is awake and responsive. No graphic wound animation is required. The traveler rises abruptly and hides the remaining linen behind their back, expecting punishment.

TRAVELER [defensive, then quieter]: “I was going to bring it back.”

COMPANION [tired, but composed]: “You can't bring back something you've cut into strips.”

HERO [keeps hands visible]: “I'm not here to take it off you.”

TRAVELER: “They said the shrine was for people who had nothing. I thought that meant us.”

Allow the companion to speak for themself. They explain they lost their belongings crossing a muddy trail, not that a hidden villain attacked them. They have been avoiding the keeper because they assumed repayment was required. This local story must stand on its own; do not claim the travelers are counterparts of the original-world patients. The player may offer their own basic supplies if owned, ask the keeper to bring clean cloth, or escort the travelers to the shrine once the companion can walk. The quest uses generic fantasy aid items, not a medical treatment simulation; no required real-world medical procedure. The keeper can supply a starter bundle so low-inventory players are never blocked.

At the shrine, KEEPER [sees the cup, then looks at the travelers]: “Next time, knock.” TRAVELER: “I thought you'd send us away.” KEEPER [pulls a stool toward them]: “I might have been asleep. That's the worst of it.” The companion sits; the keeper offers water. The player may settle restitution by returning unused objects, helping sort supplies, or agreeing that the used cloth was a legitimate gift. All routes allow the same completion credit. The travelers keep necessary supplies. No morality points for humiliating them; aggressive dialogue ends the conversation temporarily, with a later apology or keeper-mediated alternative.

## Scene 3 — A place to put the clean cloth (age eighteen)

The original-world counterpart is a field aid station tucked beneath the same birch-covered slope, where a rock overhang protects a storage niche. Canvas partitions move in the wind, a kettle clicks on a brazier, and distant evacuation traffic is audible. The older protagonist arrives with his own field dressing already secured around a small kit. A medic is trying to separate usable supplies from damp boxes. The architecture is utilitarian timber and stone, not a copy of the shrine. The niche's use for supplies is older than the worship associated with its counterpart; the player is not told that this implies a shared building history.

MEDIC [without looking up]: “Clean cloth on the left. Used kit in the basin.”

HERO (18) [sets down a crate]: “This shelf's getting wet.”

MEDIC: “Then move it. If it's dry and I can reach it, I don't care what it looks like.”

The player helps move a light box onto a dry shelf. During a safe lull, the niche lip, its upright divider and an irregular rock projection form a folded-hand silhouette. The older player aligns it voluntarily. He hears a distant wooden stool scrape, rather than the canvas flapping. He looks at the empty corner where he thought someone was sitting.

MEDIC: “Need a rest?” HERO (18) [small shake of the head]: “No. Where do you want the next one?”

Record focus18 without granting loot. The older believes his attention wandered under pressure. This is ongoing playable reality, not a dream replay or simulated memory.

## Scene 4 — Three candles, one hand (D10)

Back at the shrine, the keeper has moved a bench to make space for the travelers. From the newly open standing area the player notices three candle shadows on separate stone ribs. One makes the knuckle arc, one the folded fingers, and one the palm edge. From other positions these shapes remain ordinary shadows. The player can inspect and align without the camera being pulled. Use fixed geometric silhouette helpers with subtle animated candlelight layered on top; the puzzle must work at noon, on low graphics, and with reduced flicker enabled. The keeper's decision to clear the bench is an optional hint, not a dependency: there must always be another reachable viewpoint.

HERO (15) [quietly, after alignment]: “That looks like a hand.”

When both focus flags are set, a narrow opening appears behind the votive wall. Candles continue to burn; the wall does not explode, slide or harm the people standing nearby. The player enters deliberately while focusing. Releasing focus outside closes the visible doorway. After safe threshold crossing an independent interior release and rising side passage allow exit even if the doorway is hidden. No NPC walks into the focus threshold. The keeper can observe the hero investigating the shrine, but does not follow him into another reality or explain the phenomenon.

## Scene 5 — The side chamber

Inside, the warm candlelight gives way to a narrow beam of daylight. Stone shelves follow the shape of the cliff rather than a formal temple plan. Shallow channels keep the raised storage area dry. The air smells of dust and cedar, and a torn piece of fabric moves in a ventilation slit. A small tally scratched into the stone is worn almost flat. An optional inspect prompt describes storage wear; no inscription names the hostile goddess or the Twelve.

The direct route crosses an old timber panel covering a shallow drainage trench. It creaks, bows and sheds dust before the player steps on its unsafe portion. The player can secure it using an adjacent lever, wait and use the reinforced edge, or follow a wider stone corridor around the trench. The optional hazard is a short, recoverable drop with a ramp back up, not lethal damage. The side corridor contains a niche with empty supply jars, revealing that practical storage and later worship can coexist without proving a continuous settlement history. No enemies are required. Keep the camera at the player's chosen angle and do not lock movement for inspection.

The hero reaches a raised shelf holding a preserved field dressing and its folded cloth pattern. He pauses over the bandage. His thumb smooths a crease; his fingers turn the cloth over in a sequence he has never consciously learned. Use a brief hand animation, interruptible after the recognition line, with no mandatory close-up.

HERO (15) [catalog line, softly, looking at his uninjured hand]: “I remember wrapping this around my hand.”

In the original aid station, the older protagonist adjusts the field dressing already attached to his kit. He suddenly recalls the nervous relief of receiving it as if the medic had just placed it in his palm. His posture relaxes and his grip changes. He does not receive a new dressing or lose the existing one. MEDIC: “Is that yours?” HERO (18): “Yeah. I just... haven't looked at it in a while.” He checks his existing supply and returns to work. The younger learns the Field Dressing recipe once; item quantities, costs, cooldown and healing values are balancing proposals, not new canon. Offer an optional safe practice interaction with a training kit; never consume the only reward to finish the discovery.

## Scene 6 — The hand that held still (second focus)

A short ascent reaches a natural slit looking over the woodland slope. Align the forked birch, niche-shaped rock lip and low ridge from a broad safe shelf. After the recipe is claimed, the vista reveals the original station during a lived moment: the medic's hands tremble from exhaustion while trying to tie a small bundle of clean supplies. The older protagonist sets down his own box and holds the bundle steady. MEDIC [slight laugh of embarrassment]: “I can do it.” HERO (18): “I know.” He keeps holding until the knot is finished, then lets go. The medic gives him a grateful nod, not a speech.

The younger hero quietly copies the gesture, palm open against the stone shelf. He does not identify the older protagonist. He hears a canvas flap in the wind even after the view fades. The older counterpart briefly smells candle wax and wet leaves; he closes his eyes for a beat, then hears the medic ask for the next box. Their emotional echo is the ordinary relief of being useful. The younger's journal reads: “Three shadows. A shelf of clean cloth. Hands I somehow knew.” Avoid repeated “I know this place” barks; silence and physical motion carry the scene.

## Scene 7 — Leave something for the next person (S12 return)

The travelers are settled beside the keeper, or waiting in the covered approach if the player has not yet escorted them. The keeper has set aside an unmarked basket. The player may donate a spare fantasy aid item, offer to write a sign inviting people to take what they need, or suggest keeping supplies under cover with no public sign. KEEPER: “I'll keep it filled as long as I can.” COMPANION: “When I can travel again, I'll bring you something that isn't already yours.” The keeper chuckles and waves away the promise. The travelers' story ends here; optional later ambient visits reflect the chosen resolution, never compulsory fetch follow-ups. If D10 is completed first, reporting the secret shelf is optional; the keeper does not require the recipe or claim ownership of it. If S12 ends first, D10 remains discoverable. S12 grants proposed ordinary XP/reputation and gratitude dialogue; no unique recipe duplication. D10 alone grants the recipe.
