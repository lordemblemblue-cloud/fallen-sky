# S07 — The Wolf at the Well
R55 | Optional adventure, Wolfveil woodland. Catalog: track wolf blamed for missing animals; poisoned spring, not inherently evil; original-world injured animal at contaminated supply well; purify spring or relocate animals; D05 optional. All names, staging and balance below are proposed; no identity link to the corrupted lunar vessel.
## Scene 1 — What the hunter saw
Age fifteen. Wet leaves cling to the edge of a livestock pen. A hunter kneels beside a gap in the fence, threading a strip of cloth through a splintered rail. Two empty feed bowls lie on their sides. Across the clearing a shepherd searches beneath a cart. The protagonist approaches naturally from the road; a call for help begins the adventure without a quest board.
Hunter (tight voice, stands too quickly): “It must be the wolf. Three animals gone since the rain.”
Hero: “Did you see it take one?”
Hunter (looks at the broken rail): “Saw it near the tracks. Isn't that enough?”
Shepherd (holding a length of rope): “I saw the goats leave. They wouldn't drink yesterday. I thought they'd come back.”
Choices: ask the hunter to wait; offer to follow tracks together; investigate without promising either conclusion. Hunter waits at the marked fork, no countdown. If D05 is already complete, the hero can mention the spring but does not claim it is clean. If the player leaves, the shepherd continues searching and the start remains available.
## Scene 2 — Two trails
A muddy track splits: small hoofprints toward a dry meadow, pawprints toward the spring. A bent wire snags wool, not flesh. The player can examine a damaged reed, cloudy water and an abandoned vessel without touching or consuming unsafe water. A low ridge offers a dry route around the bank. The hunter follows only if invited; otherwise meet at the overlook. Do not invent a confirmed poisoner. The observations establish local contamination, not whether deliberate or accidental.
Hunter (points with bow lowered): “There. Look at the prints.”
Hero (crouches beside a crushed reed): “They're going toward the water. Not away with a goat.”
Hunter: “You can't know that from a footprint.”
Hero: “No. But look at the water.” [Points at the discolored edge.] “Something else is wrong here.”
An ordinary grey wolf stands between the pool and a sheltered rock niche. It backs away when approached; it does not charge unless threatened at close range. Player can lower weapon, circle the ledge, or retreat to the meadow. If attacked, it flees to a safe habitat and the relocation route remains valid; no kill requirement or reward. The goat group is found alive near higher ground, with one weakened animal resting. A handler can lead it to safety without a carry animation prerequisite.
## Scene 3 — The supply well
Age eighteen. At the same split boulder a supply well is covered by a warning cloth. A thin animal shelters beneath a cart, breathing quickly. A caretaker lays an empty bowl outside its reach so nobody fills it from the contaminated well. Dust blows across a row of water jars; the protagonist recognizes the shape of the rock but is not sure why.
Caretaker (wipes hands on apron): “It keeps coming back. There used to be water here it could drink.”
Hero: “Can you keep people away from the well?”
Caretaker: “Already have. The clean jars are over there.”
Hero (sets a bowl down, steps away): “Give it room. It'll come out when it wants to.”
Player may assist with a fictional clean-water supply transfer or ask the caretaker to provide the bowl. No real purification instructions, no unsafe water interaction. If D05 already showed the child and caretaker, reuse the existing site and play only the animal beat. The older hero already owns the charm; it is not acquired here. He briefly feels wet woodland mud beneath his boots, then notices the dusty ground. The younger hears a wooden jar lid knock against stone. Neither treats this as proof of reincarnation. This optional original-world beat is genuinely lived and does not gate S07 completion if the opportunity is unavailable.
## Scene 4 — A choice about the spring
The shepherd brings the recovered goats to the dry meadow. The hunter unstrings his bow as the wolf disappears behind the boulder. The hero explains only what he saw. The player chooses local spring restoration or animal relocation. Restoration uses a clearly fictional sealed cleansing cartridge from the woodland caretaker; collect the damaged vessel as a non-sellable evidence token, install the cartridge at a marked upstream access point, verify the game's clean-water indicator, and report. No D05 charm or cave puzzle required. Relocation uses three safe holding markers and the shepherd's lead rope; walk the goats toward the alternate clean trough while the hunter keeps the trail clear. The wolf is never forcibly tamed or captured. Both outcomes protect the animals; relocation leaves the spring closed until a future approved repair.
Hunter (after seeing the herd): “Then we judged it too quickly.”
Hero — restoration: “Help me keep the path closed until the water's safe.”
Hunter: “I'll put a marker at the fork. No more shooting at shadows.”
Hero — relocation: “They need somewhere safe today. We can worry about the spring later.”
Hunter: “I'll walk the ridge with you. Leave the wolf a way out.”
Shepherd (quiet relief, rubbing the goat's neck): “I thought I'd lost them.”
On return, the spring is clear and the wolf may drink only after restoration; relocation shows warning sign, empty pool and distant tracks. D05 may still be entered independently; its cave basin does not clean the surface. Main campaign enemy states unchanged. Proposed modest XP, supplies or local reputation; no second clean-water camp utility. No reward for violence.
