# M07 — The Mark of a Forgotten Age

R27 | Reconciles the earlier M07 draft against the R26 catalog. Mandatory: twin mountain caves; age fifteen inspects an ancient carving with a historian; age eighteen makes that exact asymmetric mark to find his way; split stalactite and ledge; mineral crust versus fresh chips; catalog dialogue; next M08, optional S09. Supporting dialogue, routes, hazard and measurements are proposals. Neither D07 nor any optional discovery gates M07.

## Scene 1 — The mountain remembers

Age fifteen. The trail leaves Wolfveil's timber fences and climbs into limestone country. Rain has stopped, but drops fall irregularly from the pines. A historian crouches near the mouth of a cave, drying a slate with the edge of his sleeve. The path behind him curves around a split boulder; his assistant has put a lantern on the inside of the turn, not on the edge where a traveler might knock it down.

Historian (without looking up): “Mind the slope. It gets slick where the light stops.”

Hero: “Has anyone been inside?”

Historian: “The entrance was cleared last season. We've been mapping the old chambers since.”

The player sees the historian's sketch: two passages, an uncertain branch and a small notch on a ledge. No map marker identifies the carving before inspection. The main objective is to follow the historian to the marked chamber. The player can ask why he studies the place, look at the sketch, or enter first. If the player enters first, the assistant calls out that the historian will meet him at the wide landing; no escort failure.

Historian (if asked): “A good map tells you where the wall is. A better one tells you what people did beside it.”

At the chamber, the hero sees two joined angled lines, a shorter off-center stroke, and a thin diagonal scratch running beyond the lower line. Pale mineral growth crosses the grooves. The hero moves his hand near the mark, then stops just before touching it. His fingers almost reproduce the motion of making it. He has not yet seen the origin from his conscious perspective.

Hero: “That little line. Why did they put it there?”

Historian: “Could be the tool slipped. Don't rub it; the crust is part of the evidence.”

Player can inspect the mark, the mineral crust, and the nearby split stalactite from a wide safe shelf. Each inspection is optional in order, but the mark is the required story trigger. Use a voluntary close-look interaction rather than camera snapping. At the shelf the hero hears an ordinary stone scrape; the audio carries through the migration transition. No dream label or time-travel interface.

## Scene 2 — A mark for the return journey

Age eighteen. A shelter corridor in the original reality is dark except for a lamp carried by a runner. The same limestone ledge rises to the left, but the stone is bare and support timbers occupy a space that is empty at fifteen. People are sheltering farther back; the runner needs a route around a damaged passage. This is a lived episode in the original chronology, not a recording of the protagonist's death. No explanation of chronology is spoken.

Runner (looking into the dark): “We came from the other side, didn't we?”

Hero: “No. The split rock was on our right.”

Runner: “They all look split down here.”

Hero: “Then we'll leave something that doesn't.”

The player examines two paths. A low rubble corridor is the shorter route; a raised dry shelf is wider and easier to see. Both converge at the same ledge. A small loose stone clatters down the short passage before traversal; an accessible caption and visible debris warn the player. The loose-rock event is an optional environmental hazard, not a compulsory hit. If the player takes the dry shelf, the runner meets him at the junction. No optional climbing skill required.

At the junction the hero makes a deliberately asymmetric route mark using an authored interaction, not free drawing. His hand traces two joined angles and a shorter stroke. His tool slips on the final motion, leaving the small diagonal scratch seen at age fifteen. A short animation shows pale dust on his thumb, a freshly exposed groove and chips at its base. The player can choose to explain the symbol to the runner or simply point toward the correct passage.

Runner: “Couldn't you just make an arrow?”

Hero (looking at the scratch): “This one will be easier to recognize. The other arrows point to storage.”

Runner: “Right. If we see it again, we're going in circles.”

Hero: “Exactly.”

The runner tests the route by walking toward the exit while the hero follows. There is no timed pursuit; distant movement and a subdued rumble maintain tension. Optional interaction at the junction lets the player reassure a tired shelter resident: “We're checking the way out. Stay where the lamp is.” The resident remains in a safe holding node until the path is confirmed. The hero looks at the mark once more before departing. The scratch audio returns and the transition occurs at a safe authored checkpoint, not mid-jump or in combat.

## Scene 3 — The same mistake

Age fifteen. The historian is examining the ancient groove with a small lamp, careful not to touch it. The hero looks at the split stalactite and the shelf. He predicts the direction of the exit before the historian consults his notes. The camera remains under player control; the player can voluntarily walk around the shelf and compare the sightlines.

Hero (very quietly): “That one. My hand slipped.”

Historian: “What did you say?”

Hero: “I made this.”

The historian looks at the hero's hands and back at the mark, not with contempt but with genuine confusion.

Historian: “This chamber was sealed before my grandparents were born.”

Hero: “Then how do I know the way out?”

Historian (after a pause): “Show me.”

The player chooses between a truthful account of the fresh carving, a cautious account of recognizing the route, or deferring the conversation until outside. Truthful variant: “I was here with someone. The stone was clean.” Historian: “I can't account for that. But I can compare the marks.” Cautious variant: “I know what the next passage looks like.” Historian: “Then we have something to test.” Private variant: “Not here.” Historian: “All right. We'll go outside first.” None causes the historian to confirm a supernatural theory or ridicule the hero.

The player leads the historian to the exit by identifying the split rock and ledge. The historian sees the practical prediction succeed, but records only what he can observe: the hero knew a route he had not previously surveyed with him, and the mark is visibly older than the recent clearance. He offers a sketch of the carving and suggests visiting the archive in Wolfveil. No numerical age estimate until the historian has corroborating records. The hero folds the sketch in the wrong orientation, notices, and turns it without consulting it again. This is the small emotional ending: his memory is useful, but it is no longer comforting.
