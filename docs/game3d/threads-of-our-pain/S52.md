# S52 — The Village That Chose Peace
R60 | Optional adventure | Border village. Source: R59 master, original S52 catalog (787–792), M15/M17 and M19 R39/R54. Mandatory: prevent a local feud from escalating through evidence and mediation; corresponding rescue route easier to navigate at eighteen without proof peace repairs everything. Proposed: local overlap recedes, repaired road and independently recorded witness testimony. No mandatory ending gate or faction allegiance requirement.

## Scene 1 — The road with two names
Age fifteen. Late afternoon light falls across a border village built beside a broad bend in a hillside road. The same stream waters the northern gardens and southern orchards. A row of stones once marked the edge of a communal path; now two carts have been placed across it, facing opposite directions. The sound of an argument carries farther than the voices of the children waiting to cross. The boy approaches on the public trail and sees a neighbor trying to pull a broken wheel out of the ditch while an armed villager stands nearby. Nobody is attacking yet. The first objective is to learn what happened before offering a judgment.

Elder (between the carts, voice low with fatigue): “We can stop this fight. But not if every word is an accusation.”

Northern neighbor (points at a damaged crate): “They promised a delivery. We found this instead.”

Southern neighbor (keeps one hand on the cart): “And we found our road blocked. We thought you'd taken the rest.”

Hero (looks down at the broken wheel): “Who was here when it happened?”

The player can speak to either neighbor first or ask the elder to recount the sequence. The elder knows what each side claims but has not witnessed the incident. The boy hears a phrase he remembers from a frightened civilian in the other life: people waiting for a road to open. He does not announce that memory as evidence. If he has not experienced the corresponding older scene, the reaction is a vague unease rather than a remembered event. The quest can be declined and revisited; no automatic town combat or main chapter penalty.

## Scene 2 — Evidence with muddy edges
At the bend, shallow wheel ruts disappear beneath a small landslip. A snapped axle lies against a low retaining wall. The player inspects the damaged cart and a delivery ledger sheltered beneath a canvas flap, then speaks to a witness working at the nearby mill. Each observation has a separate journal entry: the damaged axle, the interrupted delivery, and the witness's account of the rain and blocked road. The witness saw a cart struggle through the bend and turn over; she did not see anyone steal its cargo. The surviving goods are scattered nearby, some damaged and some still usable. None establishes a wider imperial conspiracy. The player can recover the remaining crate, arrange its retrieval with a villager, or simply mark its safe location for later collection. The objective is a reliable account, not an inventory check.

Witness (wipes wet flour from her hands): “I heard the wheel break. By the time I got outside, the driver was trying to stand.”

Hero: “Did anyone take the supplies?”

Witness: “I can't tell you that. I was helping him.”

Hero (closes the ledger): “Then that's what we'll tell them.”

A faint additional line of paving appears beside the existing road at one viewing angle. The boy stops and looks; he does not walk onto the translucent line. It suggests a second geography, not a shortcut. No focus puzzle is required. If M17 is incomplete, the journal records an unexplained contour without naming convergence. If M17 is complete, the boy may note the resemblance to the ridge observations, but not claim this village controls the cosmic process. The player can ask the witness to speak at the meeting or offer to read her account with permission. She is free to remain at the mill. Both branches preserve the same facts.

## Scene 3 — A table instead of a barricade
The elder sets a small table on the wide common ground. Neither neighbor is asked to approach the other's cart. The player selects one of three opening approaches: acknowledge both losses, explain the material evidence first, or invite the witness to speak. A hostile accusation receives a grounded rebuttal and allows the player to return to the evidence; no single failed dialogue choice permanently locks peace. The neighbors are still angry, but their immediate goal becomes recovering the remaining goods and making the road passable.

Hero (lays the copied ledger flat): “This is where the delivery stopped. The wheel broke before it reached the gardens.”

Northern neighbor: “That doesn't tell me where everything went.”

Hero: “No. It tells us we don't know enough to blame the people across the road.”

Southern neighbor (after a long silence, loosens his grip on the cart): “I can help get what's left out of the ditch.”

Elder: “And the road?”

Hero: “Then stop it here. Start with the road.”

The player helps negotiate an agreement limited to the village: share the recovered goods, clear both approaches and have each household nominate a person to check future deliveries. Alternative resolution: agree to temporary separate supply times while both sides repair the common path; this remains a valid de-escalation, not a lesser ending. No personal donation is required. The player may help move a light obstruction using an interaction or ask the neighbors to do so while inspecting the route. After the carts are moved, the mill worker crosses first, followed by a child accompanied by an adult. Weapons are lowered and put away through ordinary animations. No grand speech or instantaneous friendship; one neighbor still checks the repaired cart before thanking the other.

## Scene 4 — The quieter horizon
The boy returns to the bend. The extra line of paving is less apparent, a proposed subtle visual change limited to this local anomaly. The elder notices the road is easier to walk but does not comment on a second world unless the player asks. A neighbor walks past with a basket and stops to help another person lift a gate. The ordinary work is the payoff, more important than the changing sky.

Neighbor (looking at the clear road): “We'll repair the road together.”

Hero: “Will you keep doing it when I'm gone?”

Elder (turns toward the two carts): “Ask us again next week.”

The player may record the agreement and the observed contour separately, or decline to speculate. The quest completes when both sides have accepted a local arrangement and the road is safe. Proposed ordinary XP and access to the repaired public route, plus optional testimony for M19. No special divine approval score, no reduction of the canonical final confrontation and no requirement for a sacrifice. Subsequent visits show the chosen shared or timed-use arrangement, not a generic reset to the argument. If M19 has already resolved but postgame access is permitted by the approved ending contract, the optional testimony remains a local record and does not retroactively change the ending.

## Scene 5 — The passage between the houses
Age eighteen, original reality, genuine playable experience. The same hillside bend lies among damaged homes that may never have existed in the preserved village. Smoke hangs over the distant ridge. A rescue worker points toward a narrow service road blocked by a broken cart and scattered timber. The older hero can inspect a raised path or help clear the stable portion of the road. As the rescue team marks the route, he notices that an earlier confusing outline of a wall is no longer apparent at the bend. The physical path is also easier to navigate because the crew has cleared it. The two observations remain separate; neither proves that peace in the preserved world caused the other event.

Rescue worker: “We can move them now. Keep the corner clear.”

Older hero (looks toward the hillside): “Was there always a path through there?”

Rescue worker: “I don't know. There is today.”

The player escorts the small active group to a protected yard or follows the worker along the verified bypass. A straggler returns to the waiting point if the path is obstructed; the player can retry without a forced casualty. The older hero hears the sound of a cart wheel moving freely and feels a moment of unexpected relief. The younger hero later catches the smell of wet timber under a clear sky. Their feelings are echoes, not a new physical meeting, and other survivors do not migrate. The local chronological relationship to M05 remains unresolved. Neither scene changes the existence of the original invasion or the protagonist's canonical physical death.
