# S48 — The Shrine of Healing
R59 | Source: R58 master, original S48 catalog and D33 expansion at paragraphs 755–759 and 1306–1311 (zero-based). New dialogue, local states and reward amounts are proposals. Ready for blockout review; not built.

## Scene 1 — The water that will not reach the garden
Age fifteen, lunar spring. Late morning; a narrow cedar shadow lies across the shrine's stone apron. A healer is carrying two shallow bowls, one full and one empty. Rather than announce a quest, she tips the full bowl into a channel that ends in a dry bed of herbs. The water vanishes through a crack before reaching the plants. She sighs, sets down the bowl and kneels to inspect the stone. Her sleeves are wet to the elbow.

Healer (matter-of-fact, not preaching): “A blessing means little without clean water.”
Hero (sets weapon aside): “I'll clear the channel.”
Healer (passes him a wooden scoop): “Then the blessing has hands.”

The player can offer to examine the leak, ask what the spring means to the community, or walk away. If asked, she explains that visitors leave water for others because local tradition remembers the Moon Goddess as a protector. She has never seen the goddess herself; she does not claim that the tradition proves an event. “My teacher used to say that if you have enough to wash your own hands, you have enough to help someone wash theirs.” The shrine remains open even if the player declines.

## Scene 2 — Follow the dry stone
The player follows the channel uphill, past an intact basin and a cracked stone junction. A cedar branch has lodged across a grate and diverted the flow toward an eroded shoulder. The visible waterline establishes what changed; the player need not guess the cause from a glowing marker. At the junction, inspect the blockage and the displaced stone guide. Remove debris from the safe bank, then seat the guide in the shallow groove. A low-flow test sends a thin stream into the garden. If the flow is still wrong, the healer calls, “It's reaching the first bed, not the second. Try the other notch.” The player may request assistance; no dexterity timing is necessary.

A second path runs above the channel on a dry ledge. It lets players inspect the grate without entering water. If a loose stone rolls when approached, dust and a brief scrape announce the harmless obstruction; the upper path remains available, and the obstacle resets on return. No boss, mandatory combat or deep-water hazard. The healer moves from the apron to the garden only after the flow succeeds, so she never blocks the work area. The player sees the plants' soil darken before any reward appears.

## Scene 3 — A place to rest
At the restored garden, a tired visitor arrives and sits on a bench. The healer offers a cup, asking permission before touching the visitor's injured wrist. The player may carry a clean cup, fetch a stool already nearby, or simply stay and listen. The healer finishes her work regardless; this is not a test of obedience or a medical simulation.

Visitor (quietly): “I thought the shrine would be closed.”
Healer: “It's the spring that's been troublesome. Sit as long as you like.”
Hero: “Do you think she hears the people who come here?”
Healer (looks at the water rather than the sky): “I don't know. I can hear them.”

The player can respond “I'll come back if it breaks again,” or “I wish more people thought that way.” The healer thanks either response without promising supernatural favor. Restore the surface channel, record her tradition as testimony, and complete S48 once. Proposed reward: ordinary XP and a local healer-service discount or rest benefit, subject to balance; not D33's healing vessel ability modifier. No mandatory contribution, no inventory drain, no main quest gate.

## Scene 4 — The basin in the storm
Age eighteen, original reality, actual playable visit. The same split rock and crescent-shaped branch frame a muddy field basin under cloth awnings. The older hero helps a medic move clean water from the corresponding spring to a covered supply table. The medic is tired, not mystical. “Put the clean one here. I'll mark the other so nobody mixes them.” The player carries a basin along a dry route or asks a volunteer to help if movement is constrained. There is a dry waiting bench, separate from the treatment station. Distant invasion noise is heard, but patients are not exposed to a surprise attack during this scene.

The older protagonist already owns the healing vessel associated with D33. He touches its handle and briefly feels the smooth edge of a stone channel against his fingertips. He looks down, puzzled, and sets the vessel safely on the table. This is a sensory echo, not the D33 relic acquisition; reserve the first-receipt reaction for the discovery. “Did you need another?” asks the medic. “No. I thought I'd set it somewhere else.” The older hero turns back to the task. In the younger world, the restored water sounds for a moment like rain on canvas. The healer does not hear it. Neither explains why. Exact original-world local ordering relative to M05 remains unresolved; no resurrection or dream-replay claim. If the original visit has not occurred, keep its flag pending rather than blocking the optional reward.
