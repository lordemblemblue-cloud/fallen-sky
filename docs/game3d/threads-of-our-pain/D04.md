# D04 — The Unlit Window

Draft expansion, 26 November 2026. Catalog verified against v0.5: Wolfveil rooftops; three windows form a crescent; refugee lookout frames; chimney doorway; Scout’s Hood and distant-clue sensitivity; free roam. No new quest ID.

## Scene 1 — A light that ought to be there
Age fifteen. At dusk, Wolfveil's market closes beneath roofs of overlapping slate. A baker shakes flour from his apron; the last cart rattles over uneven cobbles. The protagonist is returning a dropped scarf to a vendor when he notices a dark window above the street. He stops with the scarf still extended, staring at the empty opening. For a moment he expects someone to signal from it. The vendor clears her throat.

Vendor (amused, holding out her hand): “Are you giving me that, or keeping it?”

Hero (startled, then smiling): “Sorry. Does anyone live up there?”

Vendor: “Not that I know of. The chimney's older than my shop.”

The player can ask about roof access or leave and discover the stair independently. The vendor points to a public drying terrace, not a secret door. The scarf interaction is flavor, not a prerequisite. From the terrace, the hero notices three windows on different streets: a curved lintel, a broken shutter and a distant arched opening. Their edges form a crescent only from a small region of the roof. Let the player walk and rotate freely; the hero shades his eyes only when focus is held.

## Scene 2 — The lookout
Age eighteen. Wind pushes smoke between the surviving frames of a refugee lookout on corresponding terrain. The older hero already wears his Scout’s Hood. A lookout is counting arrivals below, tapping a chalk tally against a beam. A family stops at the road fork, unsure which path is open.

Lookout (without turning): “Can you see the lower road?”

Hero (leaning against the rail): “Two carts. A family behind them. They haven't seen the sign.”

Lookout: “Then we'll move it.”

The player can point out the family or help reposition the sign after descending; neither choice alters the focus requirement. At the rail, three surviving frames form the counterpart crescent. A momentary scent of baked bread interrupts the smoke. The hero pulls his hood back slightly, searching for the source. “Someone's cooking?” The lookout shakes her head. The older hero returns to the task; this is an ongoing playable scene, not a flashback.

## Scene 3 — The room behind the chimney
When both alignment flags are recorded, the younger hero can hold focus at the terrace to reveal a narrow doorway in the old chimney. The chimney is an architectural landmark, not an active flue. Behind it a short stair descends into a watchman's room set into the roof structure. Warm street noise becomes muffled; pale dust hangs in the light of a ventilation slit. A broken floorboard creaks before the player reaches it. The direct route crosses a marked weak section; a wider maintenance stair bypasses it. A fall lands on a lower storage shelf with a return ladder, not in the street.

At the desk, a folded hood rests beside a wooden sighting frame. The hero lifts the hood and instinctively turns the seam away from his eyes. He smiles at the unexpected fit, then looks at the empty chair. “Why do I know the view from here?” Deliver with curiosity rather than grief. The older hero adjusts the hood he already wears; the fabric suddenly feels like a gift he has just received. He rubs the seam between his fingers and quietly thanks the lookout for holding the rail steady. She assumes he means the rail. Neither understands the connection.

## Scene 4 — Watching for someone
A second focus position at the room's upper ventilation balcony aligns a roof ridge, the road fork and the crescent of frames. The younger sees the original lookout and the family below. The older points toward the relocated sign; the family changes direction. The younger follows them with his eyes until they disappear behind the ridge. “Good. They saw it.” He catches himself smiling at people he does not know. The older hears a brief market bell that does not belong to his surroundings, then continues counting arrivals.

Optional player response on returning to the vendor: “The view is worth the climb” (friendly), “Who used to watch the road?” (investigative), or leave silently. The vendor can offer local history but must not confirm a shared civilization or identify the older hero. No mandatory combat or additional reward. The hood grants the catalog's distant-clue sensitivity, with range and balance TBD; clues remain discoverable without it.
