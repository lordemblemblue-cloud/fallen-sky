## M14.POL01 — The price of a closed road
After the three core reports, before the existing camp check or equivalent hall station. A draft neutral delivery list lies beside the reports; Sable separates it with the edge of her hand. Rain dots the open shutters. The room smells of wet cloth and candle wax. Tome's sleeve is damp; Henut has been counting deliveries; Livilla presses a loose page flat instead of interrupting. No one is a cartoon villain. Use existing representatives; this is a replacement candidate for overlapping report banter, not a second briefing.

Tome (restrained): “They stopped our grain carts at the west turn. My orders say to bring the men home fed.”
Henut (without raising her voice): “And my list says three villages are waiting for water. If your wagons take the whole road, what do I tell them?”
Livilla (taps the margin): “We have people waiting on the other side as well. My commander won't open a crossing he thinks is under threat.”
Hero: “Does anyone here know who killed the envoy?”
[A beat. The fountain outside is audible.]
Sable: “No. And the carts are still waiting.”
Hero choice A, challenge: “Then why are the roads closing?”
Tome: “Because an empty storehouse doesn't wait for a council.”
Choice B, practical: “Can you agree to let the water through?”
Henut: “I can send the request. I can't promise every guard will obey.”
Choice C, defer: Sable: “Read the reports first. We have time for a question, not another accusation.”

## M17.POL01 — The pattern and the choice
After three genuine comparisons at the observatory, following the existing primary revelation. Optional replacement follow-up to R53.V02; never play both. The keeper's instrument is still. The hero looks out toward the actual valley, where a road disappears behind a ridge. A bounded outline of the other world is visible from the existing direction, but it does not become a road. The Moon Goddess stands beside him rather than above him. She is looking at the same people.

Hero: “I kept trying to find the person who started the war.”
Moon Goddess: “You found people who had reasons to be afraid.”
Hero: “And she used them.”
Moon Goddess: “She wants the histories to resemble each other. Every cruelty she can turn toward that purpose helps her. That does not make the people who suffer responsible for it.”
Hero (angry, not shouting): “Then I should stop the fighting.”
Moon Goddess: “Where you can. But do not promise that a single agreement can undo what has already happened.”
Choice practical: “I'll keep the roads open.” Goddess: “Start with the people who need them.”
Choice inquiry: “How did she begin it?” Goddess: “I know what she is trying to bring together. I do not know every hand she has used.” [Do not settle exact mechanism or named intermediary.]
Choice silence: allow player to turn toward the valley, then return to keeper.
