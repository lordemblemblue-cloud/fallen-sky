D49 — The Thread Above the Clouds
R45 | Independent paired discovery | Sources: D49 catalog; section 16 Paired Recollection; D01 aerial
mountain; R41 campaign audit. Preserve the highest accessible gliding ridge, three cloudbreak glints forming
a silver loop, three surviving towers in the original world, floating-looking shelf, silver sash, ancient city
panorama and glider handling reward. All specific transforms, dialogue additions, wind parameters,
architectural details and reward tuning are proposals. This shelf is not D01's mountain and is not a gateway
to the third reality.

Scene 1 — The last ridge before the sky
Age fifteen. The player arrives along a broad trail that gradually climbs above the tree line. Grass gives way to
short pale tufts, exposed grey rock and a few wind-shaped shrubs anchored in cracks. The ground changes
from rounded gravel to flat slabs with rough, broken outer edges. At the crest, an old stone waymarker
points toward a gliding departure platform. Its carved lines look like ordinary travel directions, not a divine
instruction. The view should be available to a player who simply walks here after unlocking flight; avoid a
quest giver who waits forever on the cliff edge.

A sheltered recess allows the player to stand still without being pushed by environmental wind. From here,
three openings in the cloud layer reveal thin silver glints. The nearest looks like a curved reflection on mist;
another is partly framed by a distant pinnacle; the third appears in the gap between the upper and lower
cloud banks. From ordinary positions the shapes have no obvious relationship. Moving along the ridge slowly
brings them into a recognizable loop. The wind remains ambient until the player deliberately launches; no
invisible force drags them off the platform.

Hero (15): “It looks as though something is caught in the clouds.”

Hero (15): “No. It's the space between them.”

The shared focus interface highlights the three pieces as a local discovery, using contours and different notch
patterns as well as brightness. The player aligns the shapes by moving to a marked safe observation area and
orienting toward the horizon. A touch/controller interaction offers a forgiving angular tolerance, and the
accessible alternative permits choosing the matching contour from three clearly described candidates after
visiting the viewpoint. Do not rotate the camera automatically or shrink the game view. The completed
younger alignment is persisted even if the player leaves, changes graphics settings or returns during a storm.
Before the original alignment, a journal entry notes an unfinished observation; it does not mark a destination
in another world that the protagonist has not yet encountered.

Scene 2 — The towers that survived
Age eighteen. During an approved playable interval, the older protagonist reaches a protected overlook
within the established evacuation network. The viewpoint is exposed to the sky but shielded from the drop
by a repaired parapet. A lookout has used three distant towers to orient travelers: one tall surviving tower,
one with a broken crown, and one with an external brace. Their recognizable profiles provide a human
reason for the landmarks to be noticed. No new town, faction or active invasion encounter is required. The
towers belong to the original reality, and their survival at this moment does not establish that they survived
every later event.


Lookout (optional): “Keep to this side of the wall. The ridge is open.”

Hero (18): “Those towers look different when you stand here.”

Lookout: “We use them to find the road. Even in the haze.”

From the protected platform, the three silhouettes align around a gap in the clouds. The hero feels an
unfamiliar recognition of wind against cloth. He may glance at his existing sash but does not take a duplicate
from a tower. The older alignment uses the same interaction and accessibility alternative as the younger one,
with different landmark meshes and lighting. If the lookout is absent, a fixed route sign provides equivalent
orientation. The observation can occur before the younger one; the two flags are independent. No use of
focus initiates an unscheduled transfer of consciousness, and neither hero speaks directly to the other.

Hero (18): “I know the feeling of being above that road.”

Hero (18): “But I haven't been flying here.”

Scene 3 — A shelf becomes visible
After both observations, the next eligible younger-world visit updates the local discovery state. Behind a bank
of clouds, a horizontal rock shelf becomes visible. Its underside tapers into shadow and a concealed
connection to the main mountain is hidden by a buttress and mist, giving the appearance of floating. Do not
create a physics-driven floating island or establish levitation as an ability. The player is shown the destination
by the shape of its pale landing stones and a cloth pennant, not by a forced cinematic flight. The reveal waits
for collision, navigation, terrain and the fallback ledge to be ready. If any load fails, the safe departure area
remains open and the journal offers retry.

The primary route is a short glide with a broad approach from the upwind side. A gentle rising current is
visible through sparse streamers and a subtle directional pattern in the mist; these are authored visual cues
for a predictable local flight volume. Crosswinds must be readable and bounded, never a random shove
toward a fatal obstacle. A lower recovery ledge leads by a protected switchback path to the original ridge or
shelf entrance. Players who lack the precision to make the upper landing can use this route without losing
their alignment, health or reward eligibility. No one must purchase the proposed glider modifier to finish the
discovery that grants it.

Hero (15): “There was nothing there a moment ago.”

Hero (15): “Or I couldn't see it.”

If the player starts gliding before the paired gate opens, they follow ordinary safe regional flight and the shelf
remains inaccessible. Do not enable invisible collision in a flight corridor. Once the shelf is ready and the
player has committed to the approach, temporary loss of focus, weather changes or a graphics toggle cannot
remove it beneath them. Occupancy holds the destination and return geometry resident until the player
leaves safely. Optional practice rings can indicate turning and braking outside the objective path; success
records a practice statistic only, not a quest dependency. No combat, timed collectible or forced jump is
inserted into the landing sequence.

Scene 4 — The cloth that remembers the wind
The landing platform leads to an opening behind a shoulder of stone. A low wall shelters the entrance; a
shallow floor groove carries rainwater away from the doorway. Inside is a small resting chamber with a


weathered bench, a plain stone shelf and the silver sash. The cloth is folded beneath a smooth stone, its
edges uneven from wear. A dark repair stitch crosses one end; its decorative thread catches a little light
without appearing to emit magical particles at all times. Keep the prop distinct from the older hero's existing
equipment but visually related through a shared motif. There is no new insignia identifying an empire, deity
or family unless already authorized by the costume owner.

The protagonist lifts the sash, instinctively folds it along its original crease and pauses when he realizes he
knew the movement without being taught. This is a short optional interaction animation; the item claim
remains available without waiting for its completion. The player can inspect the seam, the shelf and the view
through an aperture. Neither the object nor the environment proves exactly when the hero used it. The
discovery adds emotional recognition rather than a complete autobiographical flashback.

Hero (15): “The sky looks like two different skies.”

Hero (15): “I remember the wind pulling at this.”

Hero (15): “I don't remember where I was going.”

The reward receipt includes the silver sash as a keepsake or approved cosmetic and a proposed glider
handling modifier. If existing flight settings already provide equivalent steering assistance, the modifier must
not reduce accessibility for other players or make an essential movement feature exclusive to a reward. A
candidate balance test is a small reduction in noncombat steering response delay, capped at the current safe
maximum yaw rate; no new speed, stamina, invulnerability, attack or escape advantage. Exact tuning
depends on the flight owner's review. If a utility system is not present, preserve the reward as a design
definition rather than creating a fictional live item ID.

Scene 5 — A city beyond the cliff
A second corridor leads to an overlook with a broad opening between three stone ribs. From the correct
observation point, the local horizon aligns with an authored panorama of an ancient city. The city appears
distant and inhabited by history rather than by interactive NPCs. Its terraces, bridges and roofs should share
recognizable geographical anchors with the approved world map, not introduce a new civilization or a third-
world reveal. A simple parallax panorama with near silhouettes is sufficient. The player can inspect a few
identifiable architectural shapes, such as an arched bridge, a tiered roof and a road disappearing behind a
ridge; avoid new named landmarks until the world owner approves them.

Hero (15): “I can't tell whether I'm looking at a place or remembering it.”

The optional vista adds a journal entry and does not gate the sash claim. The original world may show a later
recognition reaction when the older protagonist adjusts his familiar sash at a safe checkpoint. Do not add or
replace any inventory there. The final return route emerges through a sheltered walkway that connects the
shelf to the lower recovery ledge. An inside latch opens a shortcut, but the normal exit must remain open
without it. The player can leave by foot or glide from a separate clearly marked launch pad. No cinematic is
required to exit and no automatic fast travel overrides the player's selection.

S38 — Hesper's Ledger
R45 | Optional investigation | Sources: S38 catalog; M14 Three Arrows; S26 The Broken Akitsu Seal; S29
Three Accounts; S35 The Price of a Rumor; M17 and R41 audit. Preserve payments connected to a broker
using Hesper's name, neutral records vault, forged signatures, permission or alternate access, and similar
ration ledger in the original reality. Hesper remains a proposed suspect only: the name alone does not
establish authorship, guilt, identity or a relationship to the hostile goddess. The amounts, local staff, routes
and document wording below are proposals.

Scene 1 — A name on the account
Age fifteen. Sable Vey asks the protagonist to visit a neutral records vault where merchants and authorized
clerks retain copies of payments connected to disputed border deliveries. The vault is not owned by any one
empire. Its public counter serves ordinary residents, so normal trade and inquiries continue while the
investigation occurs. The player is directed to a quiet side counter rather than an imposing interrogation
room. A clerk opens an account index and points to a broker entry carrying Hesper's name. The clerk is
concerned that a payment reference does not match the attached receipt, but does not accuse anyone.

Clerk: “The name is on the account.”

Hero (15): “That doesn't tell us who signed.”

Sable: “Keep the original.”

Sable's line is a request to preserve evidence, not permission to steal it. The clerk places the original ledger
under a protective cover and offers to provide an authenticated copy once the records are checked. The
journal registers a claim, its source, and the current uncertainty. If S29 was completed, the player sees a
contextual link to the prior evidence board. If not, the same explanation is provided locally. No optional quest
flag is required to begin S38. Before M17, the record is a political investigation; after M17 it remains a local
evidence source with no new divine confirmation.

Scene 2 — Two legitimate routes to the records
The clerk explains that the ledger's attached receipt is stored in a restricted annex. Players may request
permission at the counter by explaining the discrepancy and agreeing to the archive's handling conditions.
The clerk gives a temporary access token, allows supervised inspection and logs the visit. The alternative
route is to visit an existing public duplicate register at the neutral receiving office, then ask a records
custodian to retrieve the matching annex folio. A damaged archive corridor can remain blocked until a
custodian opens an approved service passage. This is a navigation alternative, not an instruction to break
locks or impersonate officials. Both routes yield equivalent evidence and rewards. No faction membership,
gold payment or character skill is necessary.

Custodian: “You can look at the copy here. The old paper stays where it is.”

Hero (15): “I need to know whether the two records agree.”

Custodian: “Then we can compare them together.”

The player checks the reference number, date within the present world's local calendar, account label and
signing mark. The proposed discrepancy is a repeated signature impression attached to two records with


different stated signatories. A clerk confirms that the duplicate lacks a reliable authorization trail. This
establishes that the signature records have been falsified or cannot be trusted, not who performed the
forgery. Do not provide a real-world forgery tutorial or require the player to reproduce a signature. Present
simplified fictional glyphs and an accessible text summary. The comparison is authored and deterministic; a
player may revisit any record without resetting the puzzle.

Scene 3 — What the evidence can say
At the reading table, Sable helps arrange three entries in the existing evidence interface: account label,
payment record, disputed authorization. A fourth optional entry records the custodian's testimony. The
objective is to identify the narrow supported conclusion: an account uses Hesper's name, related payments
are recorded, and the signing record is unreliable. Choices that claim Hesper personally paid the broker or
that the broker is the hostile goddess receive a correction explaining what is missing. The interface must
distinguish a reported claim, an observed inscription and a verified record discrepancy. There is no hidden
morality score for asking a question or changing an answer.

Sable: “We know the name was used. We know the payments were recorded.”

Hero (15): “And somebody wanted the record to look authorized.”

Sable: “Perhaps. We don't know whose hand did it.”

Hero (15): “Then we write what we know.”

The clerk seals an authenticated copy for the investigation while retaining the original in the neutral vault. A
journal note records its provenance and authorized storage location, not a new inventory item that can be
sold or destroyed. If the player leaves early, inspected records remain available and the clerk can reissue a
missing copy without duplicating rewards. The quest does not automatically arrest anyone, change faction
allegiance or broadcast an accusation to the world. Any later determination of Hesper's actual involvement
requires separately authored and approved evidence.

Scene 4 — The other ledger
Age eighteen. During a safe interval, the older protagonist visits an established survivor supply room. A
volunteer is comparing ration deliveries with a handwritten ledger; a crate is waiting to be marked received.
The page layout resembles the younger-world records: columns, repeating marks and a familiar way of
separating deliveries from authorization. It is an ordinary logistics format that could have been used by many
unrelated people. The account name Hesper does not appear as a required matching clue. The older hero
helps check three crate labels against the delivery record, or asks the volunteer to read them while he
confirms the matching entries. The result is improved record clarity, not a supernatural transfer of supplies.

Volunteer: “If we count it twice, someone gets nothing tomorrow.”

Hero (18): “This page looks familiar.”

Volunteer: “It's how we keep track of what reaches the shelter.”

Hero (18): “Then let's make sure it reaches the right people.”

A small missing or unreadable label can be resolved by inspecting the designated receipt or asking the
volunteer. Do not require real food to be consumed, redistribute scarce inventory based on a moral quiz or
expose personally identifying refugee records. The journal captures a generic layout sketch and records the


distinction between the two ledgers. The player may complete this lane first, and the later younger
investigation remains unchanged. Neither protagonist receives a message or physical ledger from the other.
Similar paperwork is thematic correspondence, not proof of a common author.

Scene 5 — A lead, not a verdict
The younger protagonist returns to Sable or the authorized neutral contact. The completed evidence packet
can be referenced by M17 as optional context, but the main campaign provides all essential revelations
independently. Sable files the copy alongside existing political evidence rather than displaying an accusation
on the town noticeboard. The player can review a concise final summary, including an explicit unknown field
for the identity of the authorizing person. If S35 or S26 has been completed, their evidence links appear as
separate corroboration categories, not automatic proof that the same actor caused every incident. No future
quest must treat an optional record as acquired when the player did not obtain it.

Sable: “A name can be borrowed. A signature can be false.”

Hero (15): “Then the person we're looking for may not be the person on the page.”

Sable: “Exactly. Leave room for what we haven't found.”

The optional reward is standard quest experience and a journal evidence entry. Any gold or neutral
reputation amount requires economy approval; both authorized routes and all corrected answer paths
receive equivalent rewards. No combat item, criminal guilt flag, divine identity reveal or faction punishment
is granted. The older supply task earns its own local acknowledgement without duplicating the younger
reward. A short ambient line can later indicate that the clerk is reviewing records more carefully, but the
vault remains open and ordinary NPC schedules continue.

D50 — The Garden Beyond the Stone
R46 | Independent paired discovery | Sources: D50 catalog; section 16 Paired Recollection; S55 (R42); M19–
M20 (R39–R40); R41 postgame audit. Preserve three flowerbeds and a moonlit arch forming a seed, the
original shelter's newly planted beds and arch, concealed garden passage, personal seed pouch, peaceful
post-ending sanctuary, garden keepsake and cosmetic reward. Every new line, dimension, material, tuning
and access contract is a proposed implementation, not approved canon. Neither S55 nor D50 is required to
reach an ending.

Scene 1 — Where the garden begins
Age fifteen. On the existing sanctuary approach, the younger protagonist can leave the main path at a low
stone wall. Three old flowerbeds occupy a stepped terrace. The first bed has a curved outer edge and an
incomplete row of small flowers; the second is narrow and follows a crack in the retaining wall; the third has
a rounded end with a low shrub. A distant arch stands beyond them, its pale upper stone catching moonlight.
Viewed from the normal entrance, each feature looks like ordinary landscaping. Viewed from a shallow
recess in the uphill wall, their shapes overlap into the outline of a seed. The discovery should reward
curiosity, not require the player to memorize an arbitrary pattern or return at a particular real-world hour.

An optional gardener's line directs the player's attention to the quiet terrace without revealing the secret. If
the player has restored S55's public garden, the gardener recognizes their work, but the focus features
remain distinct and the selected planting layout does not change the geometry of the seed. If the gardener is
absent due to a campaign phase, a weathered stone bench and a journal hint provide the same clue. The
player must be able to encounter this site without taking S55. The three beds used for the focus are
permanent masonry features, not the dynamically selected planting plan.

Gardener: “The path stops there, but the old stones seem to continue.”

Hero (15): “Was there another garden?”

Gardener: “Perhaps. No one has found a way through that wall.”

The focus affordance appears only when the player is standing safely on the observation terrace. A short,
user-controlled alignment action lets the player bring the three edges and distant arch into a single seed
motif. On low settings or daylight, a pale line on the arch gives the same contrast as moonlight; no
astronomical clock, shadow simulation or weather gate is required. The assist option highlights each of the
three elements sequentially. The feedback is a gentle pulse, an optional soft sound and a caption confirming
that the first observation is recorded. No world transition is forced from this location. The player can return
to the campaign immediately, or inspect a stone groove where a passage might once have existed.

Scene 2 — A garden made from what remains
Age eighteen. During a supported original-world shelter visit, the older protagonist walks past a small
arrangement of newly planted beds. Their containers are made of salvaged crates, repaired stone troughs
and flattened metal panels. They belong to people who are still learning to live beneath the mountain, not to
a long-abandoned civilization. A reinforced shelter arch stands behind the containers. Its function is practical:
it carries the wall above a safe passage. Three planter outlines and this arch form the corresponding seed


shape from a nearby standing point. The arch and containers should be recognizable from the younger
version through their broad composition, not identical materials or implausible pristine construction.

Resident: “We kept these away from the main passage. People need room to walk.”

Hero (18): “Are they growing?”

Resident: “A little. We have to be patient.”

If S55's shelter restoration has occurred, the resident can refer to the chosen location and existing plants. If
not, these baseline planters belong to another small ordinary effort; they must never fabricate completion of
S55 or take resources away from its activities. The observation can be completed before or after the younger
one. If the original opportunity is not currently available, journal text says that the corresponding place has
not yet been found rather than instructing the player to change worlds at will. Reserve an opportunity in an
approved shelter visit before M19's ending commitment, and retain already earned evidence across later
scenes.

When the player completes the second observation, the journal records a paired correspondence. The older
hero notices the arrangement and may feel a sense of recognition, but he does not receive a conversation
with the younger hero, learn that their worlds are converging before the established reveal, or directly
manipulate the other garden. The passage opens only in the younger reality when the local scene can safely
apply the state. The older hero can finish his current business and leave through the original shelter's normal
route.

Scene 3 — The passage beyond the retaining wall
On a subsequent supported younger-world visit, the wall's central panel is no longer solid-looking. A shallow

S01 — Roadside Robbery
R47 | Optional adventure | Source: R46 catalog S01 and earlier v0.4 S01 playable draft, page 68; M06 open-
world unlock, S02 and S03 outlines. Preserve lost cart wheel, three bandits, map rather than gold,
combat/negotiation/dropped-map recovery, optional loan, original supply crate insignia. The previous short
draft is expanded here and is not a separate quest. All additional mechanics, dimensions, dialogue and
rewards are proposals.

Scene 1 — Voices on Wolfveil's south road
Age fifteen, after M06. The player is traveling along an ordinary woodland road, not following a mandatory
quest marker. Beyond a gentle bend, a cart leans toward the ditch because one wheel has separated from its
axle. A cloth cover has been pulled back from the cargo. Two bandits search the goods while a third blocks
the merchant's retreat. The merchant is standing on the road shoulder, not in the middle of the combat lane.
The ordinary sounds of the forest continue, interrupted by raised voices and a box scraping across the cart
bed.

Bandit: “Where did you put the other sheet?”

Merchant: “Take the coin. There's nothing else!”

The player can hear the exchange before seeing all three figures. A fallen barrel and displaced cargo provide
a visual clue at the edge of the bend. No dungeon icon appears, and the encounter does not announce a
supernatural conspiracy. An accessible caption identifies the argument for players without audio. The road
remains passable by a clear shoulder path; an uninterested traveler is not forced to fight or talk. Entering the
encounter area records that the player witnessed the event, not that they accepted the quest.

The bandits are ordinary people wearing patched travel clothes and simple equipment. Their clothing is
intentionally distinct from the original-world invaders. Their immediate goal is to obtain the map, and their
nervous behavior should support negotiation as a credible alternative to combat. Avoid using an imperial
emblem or divine symbol on their clothing that would prematurely assign responsibility for the future war.

Scene 2 — The choice to intervene
Approaching the merchant from the safe side exposes two ordinary conversation choices: Intervene or Talk.
The player can still walk away. Selecting Intervene uses the original dialogue and begins a short confrontation
only after the player's explicit action; an enemy cannot attack during a noncombat conversation overlay.
Selecting Talk asks what they are looking for and opens the negotiation route. No random charisma roll or
required gold payment decides whether the quest can be completed.

Hero [Intervene]: “Step away from the cart.”

Bandit: “This is none of your business.”

Hero [Talk]: “What are you looking for?”

Bandit: “A map. He knows where it leads.”

In the combat route, the bandits respond through the existing humanoid encounter system. Tune the
encounter for a player newly released into Wolfveil, using readable attacks, space for movement and the
existing surrender or retreat behavior if supported. Combat ends once the encounter is resolved; do not


spawn infinite replacements or require the player to pursue fleeing opponents into another zone. Keep the
merchant outside the active enemy navigation lane, protected from unintended splash damage. If the
merchant is in danger, the authored resolution shifts him to a nearby safe anchor rather than changing his
canonical survival based on a physics accident. Players who retreat can approach again through the recovery
route.

In the negotiation route, the merchant explains that the map is an old sketch, not proof of a valuable cache.
The hero can offer to help separate the disputed map from the merchant's other possessions and suggest
that the bandits leave. They may reluctantly depart without a fight. The player is never forced to surrender
money or destroy the map. If an approved dialogue variation permits the group to drop the map while
leaving, the lost-property recovery path becomes available. All resolution routes grant equivalent base quest
rewards, and no route forces a moral label on the player's journal.

Hero: “Whatever you're looking for isn't worth hurting him.”

Merchant: “It's a drawing of an old mine. I don't even know if it's accurate.”

Bandit: “Then why was someone willing to pay for it?”

The last line may hint that another person commissioned the search, but it supplies no proof of Hesper's
involvement, no faction identity and no goddess reveal. Keep this line optional until its compatibility with
later investigations is approved. The map is a lead to an actual location, not a mandatory item for entering
the cave.

Scene 3 — The event moves on without the player
If the player walks past, the encounter eventually resolves locally. Use a deterministic persisted local
resolution when the area unloads after witnessing, not an irreversible countdown demanding immediate
action. On a later return the cart has been moved to the shoulder and the merchant or a replacement
witness can describe what happened. The merchant's core story and safe recovery remain available. If the
map was dropped, a stable quest pickup or a witness's copy leads to its location. Recovery must not depend
on a physics prop remaining at an exact position after streaming.

Witness: “They were arguing over paper. One of them dropped something near the cart before they ran.”

Hero: “Is the merchant all right?”

Witness: “He's shaken. He went toward the village to get his wheel fixed.”

The player can return recovered property to the merchant at the roadside or his authored safe village
anchor. If the player never witnessed the live robbery, the later witness route is still an optional way to start
it. If combat was interrupted by death, disconnect or exit, restore the last committed encounter phase, not a
mix of live enemies and recovered property. A player who abandons the encounter is not locked out of S02,
S03 or the main campaign.

Scene 4 — The map and the next adventure
After a successful intervention or recovery, the merchant checks his belongings. The conversation follows the
earlier v0.4 draft. He distinguishes his ordinary property from the map and makes clear that the old mine is a
lead, not a destination he has explored.

Hero: “Are you hurt?”


Merchant: “No. They wanted the map more than my gold.”

Hero: “Where does it lead?”

Merchant: “An old mine west of the roots. I never went inside.”

Merchant: “If you want that trouble, take the map.”

Returning the property completes S01. The merchant can loan the map, which is represented as a quest-
journal reference rather than requiring permanent inventory space. The player chooses Take a look or Leave
it for now. Accepting the loan adds a lead toward West Root Cavern; declining preserves independent
discovery of that site by following tracks or approaching its entrance. The player is free to keep exploring the
forest, return to Wolfveil or continue M06's subsequent main story objectives. No S02 acceptance animation
or cave transition is forced.

At completion, grant a modest existing early-game supply reward or payment and standard quest experience,
subject to economy review. The map itself has no sale value and cannot be consumed in crafting. The player
should see one completion notification. Avoid assigning a second reward for loan acceptance, reading the
map again or locating the cave. The optional map sketch can remain visible in the journal after the reward
has been granted.

Scene 5 — The road under a different sky
Age eighteen. During an ordinary supported original-world visit, the older protagonist passes the
corresponding river-road bend. The route serves an evacuation supply convoy. A crate bears an insignia with
a broad resemblance to the younger-world map's marking. It should share a recognizable simple shape while
allowing differences in wear, orientation and surrounding context. Do not show the map physically traveling
between realities or assert that the crate's owner is responsible for the robbery. The older hero knows only
the immediate evacuation purpose of the supplies.

Supply volunteer: “Those crates go to the shelter. Leave the damaged ones here for sorting.”

Hero (18): “What's that mark?”

Volunteer: “It's on a few of the boxes. I was told which ones to move, not who made them.”

The player can examine the crate from a safe shoulder while the evacuation route remains usable. The
observation is optional lore, separate from S01's completion and reward. It may happen before or after the
younger encounter. Before the campaign's evidence revelations, the journal simply notes a familiar-looking
marking; after those revelations, it can display the two recorded images without asserting a causal link. Never
award D03 alignment for examining the crate: D03 has its own complementary shelter token and arch.
