## Scene 1 — A table without a flag
Age fifteen. The square is busy at noon. Sunlight catches the fountain's mineral ring and the backs of three delegation stalls. Traders argue over the price of grain, not over the meaning of the moon. Sable Vey has placed a plain table where people from every approach can reach it. She moves a chair away from a banner so the seat is not mistaken for a faction's place.
Sable (indicates the empty seat): “Listen before choosing a side.”
Hero: “Is that why you've asked them here?”
Sable: “I asked them because they all used the road. They don't agree about what happened on it.”

## Scene 2 — Three reasons to be here
Tome rests a grain measure on the table if it was returned. He explains that people at home worry about the next harvest and demand secure trade. Henut places her wrapped jar away from the table edge; her concern is safe access to water and trade, not ownership of the whole valley. Livilla smooths her cloth before explaining that families need dependable land and roads. These motivations adapt the legacy War of Three Roads material needs without reinstating its compulsory pledge structure or old map.
Tome (looks at the spilled grain still caught in his sleeve): “A sack that never gets home doesn't feed anyone.”
Henut: “Nor does a road with a guard who decides whose water matters.”
Livilla: “We came to trade.”
Hero: “Then why were you attacked?”
Tome: “I heard somebody shout. I didn't see who started it.”
Henut: “I saw the wall give way. I don't know what came before that.”
Livilla: “I can tell you where I was standing. Not what somebody else intended.”

## Scene 3 — The basin in another life
Age eighteen. The corresponding rock shoulder is occupied by a ruined fountain and a temporary water point. A volunteer has laid boards across the broken basin to make a level surface for cups. The protagonist recognizes a worn curve of stone and briefly expects the sound of the younger market.
Volunteer (hands him a cup): “There's enough for the next group. Don't let the line block the road.”
Hero: “Was this a market before?”
Volunteer: “People used to stop here. Now they stop because they're tired.”
The player can carry a filled container to a seated civilian or observe the line from the sheltered approach. The older hero hears the scrape of a chair on stone; the younger feels the cool weight of water against his palm. No definitive name for the settlement, no implication that both fountains shared one construction history. ## Scene 4 — Leave room for the next person
Back at the parley, the three travelers discuss a neutral path for merchants. The conversation stops being about the attack and becomes a practical question about tomorrow. Sable asks the hero to summarize what can be agreed on.
Choice — “Keep the road open to everyone.” Sable: “Then we'll need people willing to keep it open even when they're angry.”
Choice — “Write what we actually know.” Sable: “Yes. The rest can wait until somebody has seen it.”
Choice — “Let the travelers speak for themselves.” Sable turns the slate toward them: “Then each of you gets a line.”
All three variants record a nonbinding discussion, not a signed treaty or permanent political resolution. The NPCs remain free to disagree. At departure, Sable mentions that a later formal envoy meeting is planned, without predicting an attack.
