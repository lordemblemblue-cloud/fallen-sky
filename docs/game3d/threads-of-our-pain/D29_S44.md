## Scene 1 — The rain that stops at a line

At fifteen, the hero follows a wet cliff path. Rain runs down three shallow ridges, but one strip of rock stays dry. A puddle reflects a blue sky that is not visible overhead. The hero crouches near the edge without touching the water. “That's strange. The wind's coming from behind me.” The player may inspect the reflection, seek shelter or leave. A scout at the ordinary overhang warns of slippery stone; the hidden entrance is not required to reach safety.

The first focus station is a broad shelf where three rock ridges and rain streaks form a wave. Hold or toggle focus while voluntarily adjusting the camera. A static contour assist represents the rain streaks at low graphics or in clear weather; no waiting for random weather. The alignment registers with a soft ripple, caption and optional vibration. Looking away removes the visible seam but preserves the completed alignment flag.

## Scene 2 — Ash on the shoulders

At eighteen, the same cliff silhouette shelters a scout from ash rain. The ground is gritty rather than muddy; the overhang is reinforced with a salvaged timber brace. The older hero already wears his weathered mantle. Its shoulder seam is repaired with dark thread. He shakes ash from the hem, then notices a blue reflection in a shallow basin.

Scout (pulling their collar up): “Stay under the rock.”

Hero: “That rain is falling the wrong way.”

Scout: “The wind changes around the bend.”

Hero (after a pause): “I know this shelter.”

The older alignment uses the same ridge silhouette, ash streaks and brace shadow to complete the complementary wave. Either alignment order is supported during available playable migrations; no arbitrary forced swap. The hero thinks the reflection is an effect of the storm. Developer-only: the shared geography and life force permit the echo, not a third freely accessible world.

## Scene 3 — A dry room inside the cliff

After both alignments, the younger returns to the first shelf and focuses. A dry passage becomes visible behind the wet rock. Collision and landing must be loaded before the reveal. Crossing the threshold records safe entry; unfocusing closes the exterior appearance but never the interior return path. Inside, the sound of rain becomes distant. A narrow drip channel runs along the wall, with a broad dry ramp beside it. A loose stone shutter rattles before sliding across the lower shortcut; wait for its reset or use the ramp. No mandatory combat. An abandoned peg holds a weathered mantle with the same repaired shoulder seam.

The younger lifts the mantle and rubs the rough thread between his fingers. He smells damp wool and something like smoke. At the same moment, the older touches the seam of the mantle he already wears. He draws his hand away, then checks it again, as if someone had just handed him the garment. No item removal or duplicate grant. The younger receives the cosmetic and proposed modest weather utility (reduced fictional exposure buildup where implemented, no immunity or real-world claim). Cosmetic visibility is independent of equipment stats.

## Scene 4 — The second wave

Beyond the peg, a second viewpoint frames a crescent of dry stone against a narrow shaft of daylight. Voluntary alignment reveals the original shelter and the scout's earlier gesture: the scout held the mantle edge away from a dripping brace while the older hero tied the repaired seam. This is a lived moment, not a new acquisition. The younger hears the other rain; the older hears ordinary rain under the ash for a breath. Both pause, neither explains it. Younger journal: “I can still hear the other rain.” The second view can be replayed after recovery without granting another reward.

# S44 — The Mirror of Rain

## Scene 1 — A scout's warning

The younger hero witnesses a traveler hesitate at a rain-slick fork. The traveler points toward a shelter where the puddles appear to reflect a different storm. “I can see the path in the water, but not in front of me.” The hero can investigate, guide them to the ordinary overhang or ask the scout to accompany them. The traveler waits at a dry marked bench if the player leaves. This quest is available without D29 completion; its reflection puzzle uses a separate ordinary shelter viewpoint, not the hidden mantle gate.

## Scene 2 — Follow the dry edge

The player compares three reflected rock outlines with their physical silhouettes. The correct route is a broad dry ledge; the misleading reflection is a clue, never a collision surface. The older hero shelters from ash rain and notices the unfamiliar blue reflection. The scout says, “Stay under the rock.” The hero replies, “I know this shelter.” If D29 completed, use a shorter recognition line instead of repeating the full exchange. The player can trace the physical ridges, use a captioned contour assist, or ask the scout to mark the dry path. No mandatory timed jump or combat. A small runoff surge is telegraphed by a rising waterline and rumble; wait on a safe bench or take the upper path. The traveler follows only when the next segment is safe.

## Scene 3 — What the water showed

At the shelter, the traveler compares the reflection with a charcoal sketch. “It wasn't the road I thought it was.” The hero may share the strange rain observation, keep it private, or simply confirm the safe route. The scout marks the ordinary trail for future travelers. The older protagonist thinks the storm played a trick on his eyes; the younger wonders why the ash-colored reflection feels familiar. Neither claims a dimensional explanation. Proposed reward: ordinary XP and a local shelter marker, not a second mantle. Revisit shows a dry-route sign. D29 remains independently available.
