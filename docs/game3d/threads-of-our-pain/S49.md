# S49 — The Trial of the Open Hand
R59 | Source: R58 master, original S49 catalog and D34 expansion (paragraphs 765–769 and 1312–1318, zero-based). New choices and balance are proposals. Ready for blockout review; not built.

## Scene 1 — An offering that stays within reach
Age fifteen, deity grotto. In the sheltered entrance a keeper sorts empty bowls into a wooden rack. An open stone hand has been carved above the arch; it is a public carving, not D34's spatial five-finger puzzle. A traveler sits in the shade, holding a torn bag. Beyond her, two people are preparing to leave for the road. The keeper watches the hero inspect the offering shelf and pushes a small basket toward him.

Keeper: “What would you keep for yourself?”
Hero: “Enough to return with more.”
Keeper (nods toward the travelers): “Then go.”

If the player asks what the trial is, the keeper explains that travelers arrive with different needs and that the shrine's supplies are meant to circulate. “Some have bread. Some have a dry blanket. Some can carry a basket when nobody else can.” No deity demands an offering, and the keeper cannot read the player's thoughts. Players can decline, ask for an alternative, or accept the trial. Do not open an inventory donation interface before consent.

## Scene 2 — Three needs, several answers
On the public supply table are a few quest-only bundles: food, dry cloth and a wrapped spare flask. These do not consume the player's normal gear, coins or potions. The tired traveler wants something to eat; a road worker has wet clothing; another traveler is gathering water for two companions. Speak to each or read an accessible request summary. The player can assign appropriate bundles, help carry shared supplies from the keeper's shelf, or ask the travelers to coordinate a trade. An incorrect bundle is returned gently rather than destroyed.

Traveler (takes the bread but leaves a piece on the plate): “My sister's still outside.”
Hero: “We can take it to her.”
Road worker (shakes out a damp cloak): “If you bring the cloth, I'll mend the bag. We don't all need a new one.”
Keeper: “Good. Now leave something here for the next person.”

The puzzle is generosity with a reserve, not a secret morality score. The keeper points out an available replenishment basket if the player gives everything away. A player who prefers to keep their own supplies can do the same work using the quest basket. The objective is a fair practical arrangement: each request has a viable solution and the shrine retains a modest reserve or a confirmed replenishment. Optional personal donations are cosmetic and capped, with explicit confirmation; no advantage for giving scarce gear. The shrine cannot strand a low-level or empty-inventory player.

## Scene 3 — The empty shelf
When the requests are resolved, the keeper asks the hero to place the remaining reserve on the open shelf. The player may choose a short reflection: “We can carry more together,” “I was afraid I'd need it later,” or remain silent. The keeper doesn't grade the answer. “That's a shelf, not a judgment. Come back when you need something.” One traveler offers to bring a repaired bag on the next visit. The return visit shows the repaired bag on a peg or a replenished basket, not a magical reward chest. The hero watches a stranger take a modest bundle without asking him who donated it. For once he doesn't need to understand an unseen mystery to know that something useful happened.

Complete S49 as a local act of cooperation. Proposed reward ordinary XP and an independent shrine favor/comfort benefit, subject to tuning; do not grant D34 ration token or camp-sharing utility. No special ending flag or forced sacrifice.

## Scene 4 — The last basket
Age eighteen, original reality, actual playable encounter. A shelter occupies the corresponding slope; the arch silhouette is made by timber and a forked rock rather than carved stone. A supply steward counts three bundles, then looks at the line of refugees. The older protagonist offers to help distribute them. A civilian hesitates when he sees the hero's own ration pouch.

Civilian: “You have to walk back out there.”
Older hero: “I know. We'll check what can be spared.”
Steward (points at the table): “There are other ways. Carry the spare crate over, or help me divide what's already here.”

Player chooses to share a quest ration, bring a nearby reserve crate, or arrange smaller portions and a replenishment run by the steward. No civilian is scripted to die for refusing to spend personal inventory. The older hero already carries the ration token from D34; while passing the basket he rubs it and remembers the smooth weight of a stone bowl, but cannot place the sensation. The younger later notices the same knot in a bag strap and feels an unexpected urgency. Neither labels this reincarnation or identifies the keeper with the steward. Keep D34's first-receipt echo and second focus independent. Exact original event position relative to M05 stays open; if not visited, correspondence remains pending.
