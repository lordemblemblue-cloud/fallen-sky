# M12 — A Song I Know

R32 | Expanded narrative proposal. Mandatory catalog beats: age fifteen hears the M11 lullaby in Wolfveil, meets a different family with distinct names and faces, inherited emblem, no knowledge of catastrophe; age eighteen revisits Mira, Renn and Tovin; compare records without declaring reincarnation. M11 required; S17 optional. Next M13. New names, dialogue, props, staging and measurements are provisional.

## Scene 1 — The last three notes

Sunlight lies across the market street in narrow bands. The younger hero passes a house where somebody is shaking crumbs from a blue cloth. A woman sings from an open window. Her voice is brighter and her tempo a little quicker than Mira's, but the rising phrase and the descending three-note cadence from M11 are unmistakable. The protagonist stops of his own accord at the end of the authored arrival animation; control remains with the player. He begins to hum the ending, then looks toward the window as the singer finishes the same phrase.

Hero (softly, to himself): “I know the rest.”

An older villager at the doorway notices him. She is provisionally named Lessa; her adult daughter Neri and grandson Calen are not visually modeled as Mira, Renn or Tovin. Their voices, faces, clothing, occupations and biographies are separate. The family has lived in this ordinary Wolfveil house, not an evacuation mine. Lessa is sewing an apron; Neri is sorting bread for a neighbor; Calen is trying to balance a wooden hoop. No one is frightened by the sky.

Lessa: “You sing it faster than my daughter does.”

Hero: “Where did you learn that song?”

Lessa: “My grandmother sang it.”

Hero: “Have you ever lived underground?”

Lessa (sets her needle down, puzzled): “Never.”

Neri: “Unless you count the pantry. It's barely deep enough to stand in.”

Calen laughs. The hero attempts to smile, then looks at his hands. The joke makes the difference between the two places feel more substantial, not less. He has heard the same melody in circumstances these people have never experienced.

Player options: ask to hear the ending again; explain that he remembers a mine; or apologize and ask about the cloth. Lessa can repeat the melody in its entirety at the player's request; use one original composition asset with a separate performance, not a second tune. If the hero mentions the mine, Neri says, “That sounds like a hard place to sleep. But it wasn't here.” No forced disclosure or loss of trust. If the player walks past, a journal cue records the familiar cadence and a voluntary return prompt; do not mark the conversation complete until it occurs. Captions indicate the matching rhythmic contour, and an optional visual rhythm sequence allows comparison without hearing or perfect pitch.

## Scene 2 — What the needle leaves behind

Neri invites the hero to the public porch. A folded apron bears a small embroidered emblem identical in design to the worn necklace in M11, although the thread, size, orientation and wear differ. It is a family emblem, not the M07 carving, M08 sanctuary crest, D26 coalition mark or D30 eye. The player can examine it without taking it. Lessa shows the underside where the threads are knotted. The hero remembers Mira turning a pendant in her hand, not a magical flash that reveals the answer.

Hero: “Who made this?”

Lessa: “My mother taught me the pattern. I couldn't tell you who taught her.”

Hero: “Was it ever made in metal?”

Neri: “Could have been. We put it on cloth because we have cloth.”

Hero: “Do the lines mean anything?”

Lessa: “My grandmother said it meant you'd leave a place for somebody. She never told me who.”

Calen (pulling an empty stool toward the table): “That's for the neighbor.”

The action echoes the M11 invitation without making the two families identical. The hero can help carry the stool, simply listen, or ask permission to copy the emblem. Each route produces an observation, not an ownership transfer. If S17 was completed, the hero can mention a repaired wooden bird; Neri remarks that craftspeople often repeat old designs, not that the child is hers. Never identify the S17 child as a counterpart without owner approval. The emblem sketch may be copied into the journal with permission or described in words if the player declines.

## Scene 3 — A record with ordinary answers

Lyra helps the protagonist compare the family name in a public household register with the historian's index. The register contains Lessa's family, the mother's name and a dated repair entry for the home. It does not contain the original family's names. The record is a small mundane source, not an omniscient genealogy. The player chooses to ask the clerk, compare the visible entries, or have Lyra read them aloud. No requirement to obtain private documents or solve an exact transcription puzzle.

Clerk: “That's their house. The previous entry is the roof repair.”

Hero: “Is there a Mira here? Or Renn? A boy called Tovin?”

Clerk (runs a finger along the index): “Not in this household. I can only tell you what's in this register.”

Lyra: “A name being absent isn't the same as a person never having existed.”

Hero: “I thought if I saw it written down, I'd know.”

Lyra: “You know the song matches. You know the names don't. That's a place to start.”

The journal distinguishes observed details (melody, emblem, distinct names and faces, no mine history reported) from hypotheses (shared tradition, coincidence, an unknown historical connection). The protagonist is not asked to choose a canonical explanation. If the player selects an inaccurate comparison, Lyra points out the mismatched field without punishment. A single assist completes any comparison after inspection.

## Scene 4 — The people who are still there

At an authored safe migration point, control passes to the eighteen-year-old protagonist in the original mining refuge, genuinely playable and continuing from M11. The restored vent still moves its cloth streamer; medicine and corridor state reflect earlier progress. Mira is folding a blanket. Renn is helping a worker repair a shelf, and Tovin is awake or resting according to the existing ward staging; do not promise a cure. There is no Lessa, Neri or Calen here. The older hero feels a momentary urge to ask about a sunny window, but he has no coherent memory of meeting the younger family.

Mira: “You came back.”

Hero: “How long was I gone?”

Renn: “Long enough for the bread to cool.”

Hero: “That song you sing. Where did it come from?”

Mira: “My mother. I don't remember all her words.”

Hero: “And the necklace?”

Mira (turns the pendant so its worn pattern catches the lantern): “It was given to me before we left home. I wouldn't trade it.”

Hero: “Keep it.”

Optional dialogue: “Have you heard the name Lessa?” Mira answers that she does not recognize it; “Is there another verse?” she sings the same cadence and admits that the middle words change; or “How is the corridor?” Renn reports only completed M11 objectives. No character claims to be another family's ancestor. The hero can deliver an ordinary cup from the public water shelf, help hold a board while Renn fastens it, or simply stay for the song. These gestures are optional and do not duplicate medicine delivery, D14 toy repair or S16 rewards. Save the names, matching emblem and melody observation. No new family member death or hidden ancestry is invented.

## Scene 5 — What I remember, what I don't

The younger hero returns to Wolfveil at a safe resting anchor. Lessa is now outside with the apron folded over her knees. Calen rolls the hoop past the bench. The hero starts to ask whether the family has ever lost their home, but changes his wording.

Hero: “If you had to leave here, what would you take?”

Neri (looks at the apron, then at Calen): “Him. Everything else we'd decide after.”

Lessa: “And he'd insist on the hoop.”

Hero (a small smile): “Of course.”

Lyra later asks whether he has an answer. Player responses: “I remember them, but they don't remember me”; “The song could have traveled”; “I want to see what else survived.” Lyra replies respectively, “Then we should be careful with what we ask of them”; “It could. Let's see where the records lead”; or “The old sanctuary at Hollow Mountain has records carved in stone.” All routes unlock M13 through the historian's lead; no optional evidence required. The hero does not tell the family that they are dead people returned. The final journal asks: “Why do I remember a life that no one here remembers living?”
