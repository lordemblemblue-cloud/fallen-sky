# M03 — The Fractured Beast

R23 | Catalog: lunar clearing, age fifteen protects the Goddess as the wolf becomes a dragonlike abomination; she cannot release the fragment. Beginner boss and defense objective, shattered branches and faltering barrier, defeat then transition to M04. Age-eighteen species resemblance is a later recognition opportunity, not a cutaway in this quest. All combat values, staging and additional dialogue below are proposed, not final balance or new canon.

## Scene 1 — What stands up

Continue directly from M02_PROTECT_SAFE. The wolf rises without lifting its injured foreleg normally. Its shadow stretches across the inclined stone, then its shoulders follow the shape. The animal's recognizable outline is slowly lost: elongated limbs, torn-looking dark plates and a folded, dragonlike crest. The transformation uses a silhouette swap behind moving dust and a short skeletal animation, not an expensive full-body simulation. The young hero takes an involuntary half step backward. He has no name for what he is seeing. Keep the animal's suffering distinct from the invading presence: the wolf is a vessel, not a malicious animal deserving punishment.

Goddess (breath strained, never dropping the fragment): “If I release this, I cannot hold the seal.”
Hero (swallowing, eyes on the creature): “Then don't.”
Goddess: “You mustn't let it reach the light.”
Hero: “I can keep it looking at me.”

If the player selects hesitation: “I've never fought anything like that.” She answers, “Neither has anyone who first stood against it. Move when it moves.” If retreat is chosen, she points toward a protected perimeter shelf: “There. You can see it from there.” Both routes provide the same tutorial and readiness checkpoint; no shame, no invisible attack during dialogue. The player retains movement and camera once the boss is active. A basic loaner training weapon is available only if the player lacks a valid weapon; do not force equip over an existing selection. The protagonist acts despite fear, not because a deity instantly makes him a veteran.

## Scene 2 — Keep it away from her

The creature steps across the depression and strikes at the protective arc. A section of silver light bends inward, but the Goddess keeps both hands at the fragment. A fallen limb snaps against a rock. The encounter objective appears: “Keep the creature away from the woman.” A separate barrier integrity indicator is distinct from the boss's HP and the hero's own health. The boy initially thinks a damaged barrier means she is being injured; her stance and breath tell him otherwise. She is spending effort maintaining it, not merely waiting for the player to kill something.

Entity (two layered voices, articulated slowly): “You cannot keep them from us.”
Hero (startled by speech): “Who are you talking about?”
Goddess (without looking away): “Watch its hands.”

No explanation of “them.” The Entity does not recognize the hero, disclose the hostile goddess or name the original world's species. Reuse one distinctive silhouette feature later at M04 rather than a literal flashback. The boss aims at the hero when he interrupts or draws its attention; occasional barrier-directed attacks force a choice between moving to a safe interception position and dealing damage. The Goddess warns before integrity drops. If the player stays far away, her spoken hint points to safe engagement range instead of immediately failing.

## Encounter phases — proposed beginner tuning

Phase A, learning: roughly 100–70% HP. Creature uses a forward claw sweep with a visible shoulder windup (about 1.1 s), then a recovery opening (1.5 s). A short stomp marks a small ground circle before the strike. Clear floor markings must match hit volumes at low graphics. No forced lock-on or camera snap. Basic attack, block where equipment supports it, and dodge are taught in context; a distance attack can bait the same recovery window. A hit does not invalidate the lesson. Avoid overlapping patterns until the player has seen each once.

Phase B, protection: roughly 70–35% HP. The creature turns to the fragment, plants its forelimbs and charges a barrier-directed pulse. The player can interrupt with ordinary damage during a generous windup or use a visible safe lure anchor that draws attention. If an interrupt fails, one integrity segment is lost and the Goddess stabilizes after the attack; no instant wipe. She says, “It has to turn first. Watch where it looks.” A short bounded projectile uses a raised crest and a narrow ground path, never follows the player after release. A breakable fallen branch creates cover but not a required defense; debris collision is removed only after it rests, never while supporting a player. Proposed integrity recovery during successful interrupts or accessible assist mode; tune with actual playtests.

Phase C, final opening: below roughly 35%. Dragonlike crest extends, but no flying chase. It combines a single sweep with a delayed tail arc, separated enough for a beginner to reposition. After the sequence it stumbles near the original root; the hero can finish during an ordinary attack window, no compulsory cinematic QTE. The Goddess holds a steady contour rather than attacking for him. If the player struggles, expand recovery times and reduce sequence frequency in assist mode, not secretly alter story outcome. No immunity puzzle dependent on an optional discovery, Celestial Sight, expensive weapon or exact timing.

## Animation and combat contract

Use distinct anticipation, active and recovery clips for each attack; hand/crest orientation and planted feet convey direction. Damage windows are tied to validated animation events, not visual frame rate. Narrow claw hit volume, bounded tail arc and ground-marked stomp; no invisible full-circle sweep. Root motion may move the boss inside the arena but cannot teleport or push it through the Goddess; reconcile with collision and navmesh. Hero uses equipped role's normal combat animation, no bespoke forced camera or sudden snap to a dead target. On defeat clear targeting immediately, cancel pending hit events, and keep the fallen body nonblocking. Optional finishing flourish is skip-safe and never required to register victory. Separate audio: dry branch crack, low creature breath, clear warning tones, quieter pause after impact. Captions and shape-based indicators allow muted play; reduced motion disables camera shake and bright pulses without hiding threat cues.

## Scene 3 — The last sound

The creature's crest folds inward. Its shadow contracts a fraction too late. The body settles near the root without graphic detail. The boy stands with his weapon lowered, looking from the creature to the Goddess. He expects her to tell him what he has fought. Instead she closes her eyes for a moment and adjusts her grip on the fragment; the seal remains damaged, not magically restored by victory.

Hero (breathing hard): “Is it over?”
Goddess (after a pause): “This one is.”
Hero: “It sounded like someone was speaking through it.”
Goddess: “I heard.”
Hero: “What did it mean?”
Goddess (quietly): “Stay close.”

The player can ask about the wolf, offer help or remain silent. Wolf choice: “Was it still alive in there?” She answers, “I cannot tell you that.” Help choice: “Your hands are shaking.” She says, “I know. Give me a moment.” Do not resolve the vessel's fate or the seal's full history. The youth's exhaustion arrives after safety; the danger indicator disappears, and the forest returns imperfectly, with only one bird call. He starts toward her, then hears a distant sound that could be a branch or a shout. His sight loses focus; camera does not forcibly roll. Cut on a quiet loss of awareness. M04 owns the playable awakening at eighteen beneath the burning sky. Never display “dream ended,” a time travel label or explanatory menu.

## State, rewards and recovery

M02_READY → M03_READY → A → B → C → DEFEATED → AFTERMATH → M04_READY. Require actual boss defeat plus aftermath acknowledgment; checkpoint before encounter and at defeat. On player defeat or integrity failure reset encounter at safe start with ordinary health and temporary training kit; do not consume permanent resources or reset M02 observations. Offer retry, assist or return to pre-fight clearing. During active combat retreat to a clearly marked perimeter boundary triggers a reset confirmation, not endless pursuit into the forest. Save/reload at encounter start or after victory, not during an unsafe attack frame. At defeat award once proposed tutorial XP and progression, not a mandatory weapon rarity upgrade. No duplicate reward on retry; clean temporary kit according to existing inventory rules. Skip aftermath retains the essential observations in journal (“The creature spoke; the woman kept the fragment suspended”). Actual shared-life migration is developer truth; no confirmation in dialogue. M04 remains a new playable reality, not a noninteractive dream.
