# Reference quest map: notes in numbers

Research input for the three-empire war (session `three-empires`). Written 2026-11-21.

**What this is.** A structural study of one large reference open world: how many places it has and of what kinds, how far apart they sit, how its quests use the map, and how they are paced. It is for **structure only**. Reference place names appear here only as lookup labels so a later session can find the same spot on the source map or in the video. **Nothing from the reference game is proposed as content for ours.**

**Sources**
- Interactive map site (mapgenie, "skyrim/maps/skyrim"). Its page carries the full marker list (2,568 markers with positions, 76 categories). The counts and every distance below were computed from that data in the browser, not by eye.
- Video "I 100%'d Skyrim in 100 hours" (YouTube `WdebDXqxjG0`, 5 h 51 min 30 s), a run that completes every quest. Its automatic captions were read in the browser and searched, and frames were stepped through at the world-map moments. Times below are video times `h:mm:ss`.

**Scale used for every distance** (see section 2 for the check): one map cell = 4,096 game units ≈ 58.5 m, and one degree on the map site ≈ 182 cells ≈ **10.65 km**. Player run speed 370 units/s ≈ **5.3 m/s**, sprint 500 units/s ≈ **7.1 m/s** (vanilla values as published on modding pages; the conversion of a unit to 1.43 cm is the community convention, not an official figure).

**Totals.**
- **689** place markers of all kinds, of which **≈467 are out in the land** (not a house or temple inside a city).
- Removing temporary war camps, wayside shrines and the dragon burial mounds leaves **397 discoverable places**, which is close to what the game itself marks on its map (the commonly quoted figure is about 340 for the base game).
- Of those 397: **290 adventure spots** (caves, tombs, ruins, forts, towers, camps, lairs, mines used as dungeons, wrecks, strongholds, shrines, passes, groves) and **73 places to live** (cities, towns, farms, mills, inns, shacks, lighthouses).
- The runner counts **197 "clearable" dungeons** (a place with a boss whose death marks it cleared), video 1:40.

**Ratio to remember:** about **4 adventure spots for every place to live**, about **8 places to live per capital** (once farms, mills and inns are counted), and about **32 adventure spots per region**.

## 2. Spacing and density

All distances are straight lines computed from the map site's marker positions with the scale given at the top. Region ("hold") membership is approximated by **nearest capital**, because the map site carries no region borders; borders drawn that way are close to the real ones in the middle of the map and rougher at the edges.

### 2.1 Size and travel time

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Land with markers (bounding box) | **6.0 km × 4.2 km** (≈25 km²; real land ≈18–20 km² after sea in the north and the mountain rim) |
| Whole map including sea and rim | 7.0 km × 5.5 km (≈38 km²) |
| West–east crossing on foot, straight line | 6.0 km ÷ 5.3 m/s ≈ **19 min running**, ≈14 min sprinting |
| Same crossing on real paths (roads bend, mountains block; ×1.3–1.5) | **≈25–30 min running** |
| Mounted | **not verified**: no vanilla horse gallop figure found. If a gallop is 1.5–2× a run, a crossing is ≈12–18 min |
| Starting village → central capital | 533 m ≈ **1.7 min** running |
| Opening site → starting village | 497 m ≈ 1.6 min |
| Capital → nearest other capital | 659 m to 2.1 km, **median 1.1 km ≈ 3.5 min** running |
| Town → nearest capital (14 towns and settlements) | 216 m to 1.14 km, **median ≈ 575 m ≈ 1.8 min** |

The runner's own clock (shown beside the face-cam) says the full 100 % took a little under 100 hours of play; the video compresses that roughly **17 to 1**. The main line was finished at about **70 h** (video 4:57:00), because he cleared the regions first.

### 2.2 Distance between neighbouring places

Over the 397 discoverable places, the distance from each place to its **nearest neighbour**:

| percentile | 10 % | 25 % | **50 %** | 75 % | 90 % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| any place | 31 m | 74 m | **121 m** | 167 m | 206 m |
| adventure spot to nearest adventure spot | — | 105 m | **138 m** | 193 m | — |

So a new marker stands every **≈120–140 m**, about **25 seconds of running**. Over a two-minute run of ≈640 m along one line you pass **4 to 5 adventure spots** and about one place to live.

### 2.3 What is within reach of a typical point

Sampled 2,000 points on land (each 200–600 m from a random place) and counted places within a radius. Median, with the middle half in brackets:

| Radius (≈ time running) | all places | adventure spots | places to live |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 320 m (1 min) | 7 (5–9) | — | — |
| **640 m (2 min)** | **28 (21–33)** | **20 (14–24)** | **5 (3–7)** |
| 1 km (≈3 min) | 62 (46–72) | — | — |
| 1.3 km (4 min, or ≈2 min mounted) | 94 (71–112) | 69 (52–82) | 17 (13–22) |

From almost anywhere, about **20 dungeons, camps or ruins and 5 places to live** are within two minutes' run. You are never out of reach of a choice.

### 2.4 How density changes

- **From the capitals outward.** 142 places lie within 500 m of a capital, 205 between 500 m and 1 km, 48 between 1 and 1.5 km, 2 beyond. **87 % of all places are within 1 km of a capital.** The capitals are spread so evenly that each region is in effect a disc of about 1 km radius around its capital, and there is no empty wilderness between regions; the "wild" is the mountain rim and the far north.
- **From the start outward.** Measured from the starting village: 69 places within 1 km (≈22 per km²), 159 between 1 and 2 km (≈17 per km²), 151 between 2 and 3 km (≈10 per km²), 18 beyond 3 km. Density near the start is about **twice** the density at the far edges (part of the drop is sea and rim, so the designed drop is smaller than that).
- **Roads versus wild.** Along the nine main roads between capitals (15.8 km of road, a 400 m wide corridor ≈ 6.3 km², about a third of the land), there are 76 of 262 dungeon-type places, **29 %, about the same density as the whole land**. Dungeons are spread evenly, not strung on roads. What does cluster on roads is the living world: towns, inns, mills, farms, watchtowers and forts at crossings (seen on the world-map frames, and in that every town sits within 1.2 km of a capital on a road).
- **Mountains.** On the world-map frames at video 1:20:52 (the southern mountain wall) and 1:23:51 (the highest central peak), markers ring the foot of each mountain and thin out sharply up the slopes. On the high peak there is a single pilgrimage path with a few places spaced along it, then the summit. In the 1:23:51 frame, about 100 marker icons are visible at once around the peak.

### 2.6 What sits on the borders

Borders are not empty: they carry the **passes, forts and watchtowers that guard the crossings**, the roadside inns where travellers stop between regions, and a full share of dungeons.

## 3. How quests use the map

### 3.1 Where quest givers stand

The map site marks the start point of 37 side quests:
- **22 (59 %) stand inside a settlement** (within 150 m of a town, inn, farm or capital): a market stall, a temple, a ruler's hall, an inn.
- **11 (30 %) stand at a dungeon door** (within 80 m of a dungeon and away from settlements): someone camped outside a ruin, a survivor at a cave mouth, a ghost at a tomb.
- **4 (11 %) stand alone** on a road, at a shrine or at a landmark.

The runner's route shows the same thing: on entering each town he "starts talking to people" (video 12:00) and collects that town's whole stack at once; one city alone had **28 quests** (12:20).

### 3.2 How far the first step sends you

- For the 22 settlement givers, the **nearest dungeon is a median ≈250 m away** (under a minute running). The first step of a local quest usually goes to one of the 2–5 dungeons within ≈700 m of the town.
- Errands and deliveries deliberately send you **to another region**: a dagger from the south-east city to someone in the central city (video 23:00), about **2 km away**; a temple quest that has you run across the whole land reuniting couples (23:20).
- Faction and main-line steps jump **1–4 km** at a time (a capital to a far tomb, a far ruin, another capital).

Rule seen: **local quests stay within ≈0.7 km; errands cross one border; story lines cross the map.**

### 3.3 How chains hop between regions

- **The main line** starts in the south-centre, goes to the central capital (0.5 km), to the nearest tomb (≈0.5 km), out onto the plain for the first big monster fight, then up the central peak; later steps go to a far eastern tomb, back to the starting village, to a foreign embassy in the north-west, to the south-east city's sewers, to a temple in the western mountains, to the deepest ruin in the far north, back up the central peak to its summit, to a council in a monastery, to a trap in the central capital's palace, and to the enemy's mountain fortress. It **touches 7 of the 9 regions and returns to the central peak three times**; the central peak is its spine.
- **Each faction line is anchored to one capital**, where you report and where the final scene plays, but the steps go elsewhere. The thieves' line (section 4) is based in the south-east city and goes to an island estate next door, a meadery by the central capital, the north-west capital, a tomb in the north-west mountains, the mages' college in the far north-east, then the deepest machine ruin. About **two thirds of its steps are outside the home region**.
- **Its repeatable jobs pick one of the five big capitals at random**; after five jobs in one city that city gives a special job; four special jobs unlock the guild's top rank (video 4:01:40–4:02:00). That is a deliberate tour of the map.
- The war line moves region by region: a camp, then a fort, then the region's capital falls, then the next region (video 4:37:00–5:03:20).

### 3.4 What share of quests end where

Estimated from the runner's counts and the quests seen in the video (not a full tally):
- **Fetch, delivery, talk or collect-a-set: about half.** One city's 28 quests were half fetch quests, six of them collect-a-set (12:20).
- **Clear a dungeon, kill a target or win a fight: about 40 %.** Almost every faction, demon-prince and main-line quest ends in a dungeon or a fight.
- **Set pieces and choices: about 10 %** (a council, a wedding, an execution, a dream world).

### 3.5 One dungeon, several quests

- **197** clearable dungeons, only **26** with a self-contained quest of their own (video 2:44:00). The other ~170 exist to be **reused**: as the target of a faction job, a random bounty, a collectible's hiding place, a treasure-map spot, a shout word, a skill book.
- Examples from the run (labels only): one cave held both an unusual gem and a thieves' job item, picked up in one visit (4:11:40); a cave cleared for a warrior-guild job counted at the same time as a dungeon cleared (4:10:00); the first tomb of the main line was reused later for undead (2:04:40); a ride to one town passed two places needed by two different quests (11:40).
- Random jobs pick from a list of dungeons, so the same cave can be visited for three reasons on three different days.

### 3.6 Quests that start without a quest giver

- **Notes and books:** 12 treasure maps, each a picture of the place where the treasure hides (3:11:20); journals that start a hunt; a book that pulls you into a quest.
- **Bodies and scenes:** a burnt body on a coast near an abandoned cabin tells a whole tragedy without a word (1:54:40; the runner calls it "brutal and beautiful").
- **Strangers on the road:** random encounters in "zones" (each zone rolls one of a list when you enter, 5:39:20): a madman, a brother who only appears on the road (2:24:00), a courier who finds you at an inn with a letter (2:02:20, 3:36:40).
- **Walking in:** entering a stronghold under attack starts its quest (3:29:40); entering a dungeon finds its trapped or stranded quest giver (2:44:20).

## 5. What wears thin, and what is remembered

### 5.1 Tedious or pointless, and why

1. **Random assignment of repeatable quests.** You ask a giver for work and get any of several jobs at random, often one you just did; the runner calls this his "worst nightmare" (1:25:40) and lost time to repeats again and again (2:09:20, 5:31:40). *Lesson: the player should choose the job, or the job should come from the world state.*
2. **Fetch errands with nothing at stake.** Half of one city's quests were fetches (12:20); "a quick fetch quest", then the "largest collectathon in the game" (22:40). *Lesson: a delivery is only worth making if someone can stop it.*
3. **Collect-a-set with no clue.** 24 gems with "no quest marker", so you must "scan every inch of every location" (35:20); hunting behind waterfalls (1:44:20). *Lesson: every collectible needs a clue to where it is.*
4. **Treasure maps that repeat one pattern.** "Most of the treasure maps ended up just being repeats of this. Find treasure map, find treasure" (3:12:00).
5. **Quests that duplicate something you do anyway.** A quest to read a book you will read regardless is "one of the lamest quests" (2:34:00).
6. **Giant dungeons for a tiny goal.** "All we need to do is kill some bloke, but he's at the end of" a huge ruin (3:33:00); the longest dungeon in the game guards one item (1:30:40). *Lesson: a dungeon's size should match what is at stake.*
7. **Progress that depends on luck.** A random road encounter you must meet to continue (2:24:00, 2:34:00, 5:36:00); a letter that only comes at a certain inn after a certain level (3:36:20); a quest so rare the runner had never seen it (3:37:00).
8. **Backtracking and repeat trips.** Being sent to do the same ritual twice, after just travelling for it (4:06:40); going "back to annoying ruins again" (1:23:20); running across the map to undo your own trouble (3:39:00).
9. **Repeatable chores of guilds.** The warrior guild's "series of mostly boring simple side quests", animal extermination (4:07:00); thieves' jobs where "nothing interesting happens" (4:04:20).
10. **Level gates and missable quests** that block a chain with no in-world reason (1:04:40, 2:43:20, 49:20).

### 5.2 Remembered and praised, and what they share

- **Crashing a wedding to kill a guest:** "one of my top five quests in the game" (4:26:00). Public place, a crowd, several ways in (a loose statue to push), a clock running.
- **A god says it is the good one; you kill the servant anyway:** a dream-sickness in a coastal town solved in a castle above it (1:04:00–1:04:40). Reversal plus choice.
- **The inside of a mad king's mind:** a surreal place with its own logic (3:21:40).
- **The betrayal by the guild leader** (4:15:20) and **a partner who turns on you mid-dungeon** in a quest that does not even show in the journal (3:00:40).
- **The peace council:** who gets which region decides which later quests exist (4:43:40–4:54:40). The runner calls the war line "the largest curveball of the entire speedrun"; three quests per side exist only after certain choices.
- **A frost giant fight called back from an earlier game:** "one of the most memorable in the game" (2:31:20). A place with history.
- **A tragedy found on a beach** through a body and a cabin (1:54:40–1:55:00). No giver; the place tells it.
- **The demon-prince quests** are "the fan favorite quests" (3:20:40): each is one strange patron with one strange request and a twist.
- **A city conspiracy that ends in a prison uprising** (52:14–1:03:20): a chain inside one city, escalating until the city itself changes.

**What they share:** a **twist** that changes who the enemy is; a **choice** with a visible result; a **place with its own story** (a public stage, a dream, a ruined home); and **other people present** (a crowd, a council, a partner). None of them is "go there and kill N".

## 7. Rules of thumb for our valley

Our world: one valley of a few square kilometres in **five gated areas**, target **60–70 quests** and **40–60 places**, PvP at heart. The reference has ≈20 places per km² of land and a place every ≈120 m; at that density, **40–60 places fill 2–3 km²**, so our numbers are in proportion if the valley is about that size. Times below assume a running speed near 5 m/s; scale them by our own speed.

**Placing places**
1. **One hub per area, 8–12 places per area**: 1 hub, 1–2 small settlements or camps, 4–6 adventure spots (cave, ruin, fort, lair, camp), 1–2 landmarks or shrines. That is the reference's 1 : 8 : 35 region cut down by about four, keeping its **4 adventure spots to 1 place to live**.
2. **A new place every 100–150 m, about every 25–30 seconds of running**, and **10–15 places within two minutes' run** of any point. Never more than about a minute of running with nothing new in sight.
3. **Dungeons spread evenly; living places strung along the road.** Put settlements, inns and watch posts on the road through each area; put adventure spots in the land on both sides of it.
4. **Put a third of the places on the gates.** The reference keeps ≈30 % of its places on borders: passes, forts, towers, inns. Our gates are those borders, so each gate gets a fort or watch post, a crossroads stop and a dungeon close by. These are the natural **PvP choke points**.
5. **Densest near the start, thinner and harder toward the far end**, about twice as dense in the first area as in the last.
6. **One landmark the whole valley can see** (a peak, a tower, a burning city), visited by the main line **three times**, as the spine of the story.

**Making quests use the map**
7. **Givers stand in hubs (60 %), at dungeon doors (30 %), and alone on the road (10 %).**
8. **The first step of a local quest goes no further than ≈1 minute's run**; errands cross **one gate**; story steps cross **two or more**.
9. **Each faction line is anchored in its own hub but puts two thirds of its steps elsewhere**, so each empire's players keep travelling through the other empires' land.
10. **Every adventure spot serves at least three quests**, ideally one for each empire plus one neutral, so rival players arrive at the same cave for different reasons. With 40–60 places and 60–70 quests, plan about **25 shared target sites**.
11. **Never assign repeatable jobs at random.** Let the player pick the job, or tie it to the war's state (which side holds which fort), and make each repeat a **contest between empires** at a shared site, not a solo chore.
12. **No pure fetch or delivery.** If something is carried, it is carried through contested ground and can be intercepted by another empire's players: the delivery is the PvP.
13. **Collectibles get clues**: a picture, a riddle, a note. Each collect set leads somewhere the other empires also want.
14. **Quests without givers**: a note, a body, a ruined camp, a messenger on the road, placed at contested sites so whoever finds it first starts the hunt, and the others hear of it.

**Pacing and soul**
15. **A main line of about 12–15 quests** (scaled from 19): talk → travel → dungeon → set piece, a **twist every 4 quests**, **2–3 real choices**, never two long dungeons in a row.
16. **Each empire's line of about 8–10 quests** with the reference faction shape: three home quests to meet the people, a widening circle, a **betrayal or reversal at two thirds**, a hard dungeon, a ritual and a choice, a short tail (not hours of repetition).
17. **Every quest needs one of the four things the remembered quests share**: a twist, a choice with a visible result, a place with its own story, or other people present. For us, "other people" should often be **rival players**.
18. **Dungeon size matches the stake.** A side errand never ends at the bottom of the biggest ruin.
19. **No luck gates and few level gates.** Progress never waits on a random roadside encounter; an area's gate is the main level control.
20. **Turn in where the action is** (a messenger, a signal fire, the field camp), so finishing a quest does not mean walking back empty.
21. **Synchronise rivals by schedule, not by chance**: world events on a clock (a caravan leaves, a fort's garrison changes, a shrine opens at dusk) that several empires' quests point at, so rival players **arrive at the same place at the same time**.
22. **Let the war's map change the quest list**, as the peace council does in the reference: which empire holds a fort or a gate decides which quests appear there next.
