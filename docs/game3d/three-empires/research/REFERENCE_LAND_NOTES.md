# Reference land notes: how a large open-world RPG shapes its land and stages its main story

Research input for the three-empire war (Game 3D / Fallen Sky). Written 2026-11-21 by a side session of `three-empires`.

**Source studied:** YouTube `yMElCA8lLng`, "The Entire Main Quest Storyline" (4 h 9 min, 14 964 s), a cut-together playthrough of one well-known 2011 open-world fantasy RPG's main line, with burned-in subtitles and the uploader's short analysis at the end. Timestamps below are positions in that video (h:mm:ss) so later sessions can go back and look.

**Rule for using these notes.** The reference is studied for *craft only*: how ground is shaped, how a kind of place is laid out, how a scene is staged. Reference place names appear here only as lookup labels next to timestamps. Nothing of that game's map, places, names, characters, dialogue, art or music is proposed for our game. Every pattern card and rule at the end is written in generic terms for our own valley.

**How sizes were estimated.** Character height ≈ 1.8 m; a doorway ≈ 2.2–2.5 m; walk ≈ 1.4 m/s, run ≈ 4–5 m/s, horse ≈ 8–10 m/s. Sizes are estimates from screenshots, good to about ±25 %.

## What kind of video this is (read first)

The video is a **cut-together** main line, not one continuous run. The player fast-travels by map almost every time (map screen at 0:23:00, 2:10:00), so **long overland walks are mostly cut out**; what survives is the first escape walk, a few horse rides, the pilgrim stair, two night canyon/mountain approaches and the afterlife path. In contrast, **dialogue is kept in full**: about 45 % of the 4 h is standing conversation, reading books or menus. That ratio is itself a finding (see "How the story is staged").

## Close study, part 1: the opening, the first town, the first tomb, the first boss, the pilgrim climb

### 1. Opening road into a walled village (0:01:30–0:07:00)
- **Camera is locked in a moving cart** for ~3.5 min while four captives talk. The road does the storytelling: pine forest with snow patches → rock cut walls on both sides (≈4–6 m high, road ≈4 m wide) → a timber gatehouse in a log palisade → houses press in on the road (eaves 2–3 m from the cart) → a round stone tower closes the view. The player cannot move yet, so the *road itself is the first level*: it is staged as a funnel that narrows and darkens until the square opens. 0:01:40, 0:02:25, 0:02:55, 0:03:35.
- **Square:** ≈35 × 25 m, packed earth and cobble; the stone tower (≈8 m diameter, ≈18 m to the parapet) on one long side, timber-and-thatch houses (≈8 × 6 m footprint, eaves ≈3 m, ridge ≈6 m) on the others. Banners on 6 m poles mark the authority's side. ≈10 soldiers form a loose ring, prisoners in a line, the block on a 0.4 m dais. 0:04:10, 0:04:40, 0:06:10, 0:06:40.
- **The set piece announces itself by sound and sky first:** a roar before anything is seen, then the creature lands *on the tallest object in view* (the tower), 0:08:00. The escape then runs through the same square, now on fire, with collapsed houses forming a new path (burnt beams as walls) 0:08:20–0:10:20. *Lesson: the same small space played twice, calm then broken, costs no new geometry.*
- **Keep interior** 0:10:40–0:13:20: stone corridors ≈3 m wide, ≈4 m ceiling, torches in iron sconces every ≈6–8 m, banners on walls; a guard room with a portcullis worked by a wall lever; a torture room with 2 m cages. It ends in a natural cave (rough rock, ≈4–6 m wide), a sleeping bear the player can sneak past, and a **bright exit mouth** seen down a straight tunnel (0:13:20, 0:14:20): daylight as the goal.

### 2. First exit to the open world, and the walk to the first village (0:14:20–0:16:45)
- **The vista moment** 0:14:40–0:15:10: the cave mouth opens onto a ledge ≈10 m above the slope; a far snow peak is framed between two pine clumps; the path drops ahead. The player is given ≈20 s of nothing but view.
- **The companion points out the next dungeon** on a far ridge during the walk (0:15:35; the subtitle is about "that ruin up there"). Landmark → spoken reason → later quest. The ruin is a dark silhouette of stone arches on a mountain shoulder, ≈1–1.5 km off.
- **Trail:** worn earth with flat stone slabs showing through, **≈2–2.5 m wide**, no hard edge: grass, ferns, low boulders (0.5–1.5 m) and pine trunks thin out toward it. A rock outcrop ≈3 m tall shoulders one side. It bends roughly every 40–60 m, each bend hiding the next stretch behind a boulder or a pine clump. 0:15:20, 0:15:35, 0:15:50, 0:16:05.
- **Village arrival** 0:16:20: no wall; the trail simply becomes the street. First thing seen: a mill wheel and a timber house corner, then a chicken in the street, a fenced yard, a smith's shelter. 6–10 buildings strung along one street ≈5 m wide, a river on one side. 0:16:20–0:16:45.

### 3. The first town: outer farm, gate, market, upper stair, palace (0:18:05–0:19:20)
- **Road to town** 0:18:05: dirt with cobbled patches, **≈4 m wide**, tall dry grass and boulders on the verges, pines in clumps; companions run ahead in single file.
- **Outskirts** 0:18:15: a lone farm/brew-house with a signpost at the fork: the first building stands *outside* the wall to announce the town.
- **Outer gate** 0:18:20: a **timber-roofed gate arch ≈5 m wide, ≈6 m high**, set in a low stone wall ≈1.5 m, on a cobbled ramp; the inner city wall and the palace on its rock beyond. Then a big double timber door in a stone wall ≈8 m high (0:18:30).
- **Market** 0:18:40: a street ≈6–8 m wide, stalls under timber awnings, a fire brazier, 5–8 townsfolk.
- **Upper district** 0:18:50: a **wide stair ≈5 m wide, ≈20 steps, ≈4 m rise**, crowned by a **post-and-lintel stone gate ≈6 m tall** with a brazier on each side; low parapet walls; the palace above. *Height = rank: every step up the town is a step up in importance.*
- **Palace hall** 0:19:05–0:19:20: a long hall ≈40 × 15 m, a **sunken fire trench ≈10 × 2 m** down the middle, long tables either side, the ruler's throne on a dais at the far end up ≈5 steps. The fire trench is the light source and pulls the eye straight to the throne.

### 4. First tomb dungeon (0:23:05–0:29:30): the template dungeon
**Approach** (0:23:05–0:23:50): night, snow, pines; a hooded traveller stands on the trail (a living signpost); the trail climbs between rock walls; the "discovered" banner pops as the ruin's arches appear above, ≈100 m off. **Outside guards:** 3–4 bandits on the **wide stone stair** (≈8 m wide, ≈30 steps) that climbs to the entrance; one archer on top. The ruin's facade is a row of **tall stone arches (≈8–10 m)** silhouetted against the sky: the outline reads from a kilometre away.

**Interior sequence** (each step ≈20–60 s of walking):
1. **Entry hall** 0:23:55: big collapsed room ≈20 × 15 m, ceiling ≈8 m with a hole letting a light shaft in; bandit camp inside (bedrolls, a fire, crates). The light shaft is the room's landmark.
2. **Root tunnel** 0:24:10–0:24:25: ≈3 m wide, tree roots through cracked walls, lit by occasional braziers.
3. **Puzzle room** 0:24:40–0:25:10: ≈10 × 8 m, a portcullis gate on the far wall, a **lever**, three **rotating pillars with animal carvings**, and the answer carved above the gate (three heads on the lintel). A dead bandit who pulled the wrong lever lies in front (dart trap). *The room teaches by a corpse.*
4. **Round-arch corridor** 0:25:30: barrel vault ≈3 m wide, ≈4 m high, stone, warm torchlight.
5. **Web room** 0:25:50–0:26:30: a cave ≈15 m across, curtains of web, a **giant spider** drops from the ceiling; a man trapped in a web pleads (choice: free him or not).
6. **Burial corridors** 0:27:10–0:27:50: corpses rise from wall niches (≈2 × 0.8 m niches stacked two high) as the player passes: the walls themselves are the enemy spawner.
7. **Claw door** 0:28:10: a round stone door ≈3 m across with three rotating rings; the key item (looted earlier) carries the answer on its palm.
8. **Final cavern** 0:28:32–0:29:25: a natural cave **≈40 m across, ≈20 m high**; **two thin waterfalls fall from slits in the ceiling**, giving pale light and sound; a ruined stone platform up ≈6 steps; the **carved wall** curves across the back; a **sarcophagus** on a dais in front of it; chests left and right. The boss rises from the sarcophagus only when the player reaches the wall.
9. **Exit**: not shown (cut to map). The usual shortcut back to the entrance is a rising tunnel ending at a one-way door near the outside.
- **Light colours:** warm orange torches/braziers in built parts; cold blue-green daylight from ceiling holes in natural parts; the final hall is the brightest place in the dungeon.
- **Crossing time on screen:** ≈6 min including fights (the full dungeon is longer; cuts hide some).

### 5. Watchtower boss (0:34:10–0:39:00): the first open-air arena
- **Briefing on the move:** a squad of ≈6 guards gathers on a grassy rise with boulders, the captain speaks ≈20 s, then all run forward. 0:34:30.
- **Announcement from far:** **3–4 columns of black smoke** rise above the tower, visible ≈300 m away before the tower itself; burning grass embers along the approach. 0:34:30–0:34:45.
- **Arena:** a rocky knoll ≈60 m across; a **round tower ≈8 m diameter, ≈15 m tall with a broken top**; a paved ring around it; **ruined wall stubs 1–2 m high** scattered as cover; open, almost flat tundra all round so the flying enemy can be seen and shot. A wounded survivor waits inside the tower door with a warning. 0:34:55–0:35:55.
- **After the fight:** the guards walk up and stand in a semicircle around the corpse and the player (0:36:50–0:38:50); a ~2-minute conversation reveals what the player is. *The reward moment is staged as witnesses gathering, not as a menu.*

### 6. The pilgrim road: bridge, village, stair, monastery (0:44:40–0:56:00)
- **Stone arch bridge** over a river gorge (0:45:00): one span ≈25 m, deck ≈4 m wide with ≈1 m parapets; the road continues straight on the far side into autumn tundra. A bridge is a natural *gate between two kinds of country*.
- **Foot of the climb** 0:45:25: a small mountain village at the base; the stair begins behind it by a carved stone marker.
- **The stair** 0:46:40–0:49:15: flat stone slabs ≈2 m wide laid as steps into the slope, in **switchbacks**; snow piles on both sides; **carved stone tablets ≈2.5 m tall stand beside the path at intervals**, each telling one line of the place's founding story when read (≥7 tablets over the climb, roughly one per 60–90 s of walking). One monster blocks a switchback midway (0:48:25). Weather changes with height: dusk pine forest → bare rock → blizzard (0:47:40).
- **Arrival** 0:49:15–0:49:35: the last tablet stands by the last steps; the monastery is a **dark stone mass on the ridge**, one huge arched door ≈6 m tall in a blank wall; banner "discovered".
- **Inside** 0:49:55–0:54:00: dark stone halls, pillars, **square brazier pits on the floor** as the only light (orange); four robed elders stand in a loose ring; a long trial in the hall.
- **Courtyard** 0:54:40–0:56:00: snow court ≈30 × 20 m; an **iron gate between two stone pylons ≈5 m tall leads higher up the mountain and stays shut**: seen now, opened ≈1.5 h of play later (2:15:00). *Show the locked way early.*

### 7. Second tomb, the stolen prize (0:56:50–1:01:10)
- Entrance in a marsh (dead body by the water at 0:56:50). Dark round-arch corridors, then a large cave hall with stairs, then a **narrow stepped tunnel ≈2 m wide climbing toward warm braziers** (0:59:35): light pulls the player up.
- **Final chamber** 1:00:30: two huge carved beast-head statues flank a dais; the pedestal at its top is **empty**; a note lies in place of the prize, naming a meeting place (1:00:50). *The empty plinth is a twist told by a prop, and it hands over the next quest.*

### 8. Riding to the burial mound (1:08:50–1:12:50)
- On horseback through thin pine woods and snowy meadow; the companion talks the whole ride.
- **The objective is a creature in the sky**: a large winged silhouette circles over the trees ahead (1:09:20–1:10:05). The pair stop at the meadow edge **≈80 m away and watch** the creature raise a second one from an opened grave before the fight starts (1:09:43–1:09:58).
- Fight in open grass; afterwards the skeleton lies on the mound edge and the companion delivers ~2 min of explanation (1:11:35–1:12:52).

## Close study, part 2: embassy compound, canal city, sewer, hidden temple, summit, deep ruins, the last places

### 9. Walled embassy compound: a party, then a fight out (1:19:30–1:40:20)
- **Arrival at night by carriage**, torch-lit stable, an accomplice waits by the wagon to take the player's weapons (1:19:30–1:20:50). The compound sits alone in snow: a stone wall ≈4 m high, one gate with a guard, the **main hall** and a separate **guard house across a snowy courtyard** (≈25 × 15 m, a low walled garden/well in the middle, 1:28:30).
- **Inside:** a reception hall with arcades ≈20 × 12 m, pillars, a bar, 10–15 guests in small groups; a kitchen and a back corridor give the hidden route (1:21:10–1:22:00). The player must create a distraction to slip out of the party; then the upper floor and the guard house are a fight (1:27:30–1:29:00).
- **Exit:** cell under the guard house with a prisoner, a trap door down into a natural ice cave with bones and a cave beast, out into the wild (1:38:10–1:40:00). *A fort can be entered by the front as a guest and left by the back as an outlaw: the same footprint used twice.*

### 10. Canal city and its undercity (1:42:10–1:54:40)
- **Gate shakedown:** carriage drop at the gate at night; the gate guard demands a bribe (1:42:40). A town gate is a place for a scene, not only a door.
- **Market:** a square ≈25 × 20 m with a **small roofed well** in the middle, stalls with lattice fronts ≈3 m wide, timber houses on stone ground floors (1:43:10–1:46:40). The player asks around; an informer at a stall gives the lead.
- **Two levels of street:** wooden boardwalks at water level along a canal ≈8 m wide, stone streets ≈4 m above with arched bridges across (1:54:40). Cheap trick: the same canal seen from two heights reads as a large city.
- **Undercity:** entry by a door/hatch in the canal wall; dark **brick tunnels ≈3 m wide**; a dingy **underground tavern ≈15 × 10 m** lit by a few candles; a **locked door with a sliding peep-hatch** where the hidden person talks to the player before opening (1:49:10–1:49:40); the hideout room behind it, cluttered. Then an armed ambush by the hunters in the tavern (1:52:40).

### 11. City of stone, night canyon, hostile camp, hidden temple (1:58:45–2:08:00)
- **Stone city exterior** 1:58:45: a huge **stepped facade carved into the cliff**, ≈40 m wide, ≈30 m high, with a single dark doorway at the top of wide stairs; seen at night with a glow above. The building is the mountain.
- **Night canyon path** 1:58:58: cobbled path ≈3 m wide climbing a ravine between rock walls; boulders on the verges; hostile hill-folk ambush from the slopes (1:59:15–2:00:00).
- **The hostile camp** 2:00:22–2:01:02: a mountain bowl ≈150 m across with a **ruined stone tower** in it (a dragon perched on top), **twisted antler/branch totems** (≈2–3 m) at the edges, fires; the path into the mountain is marked by **two tall iron fire baskets on poles** (≈2.5 m) either side of a cave mouth hung with roots (2:01:02–2:01:25). *Totems tell who lives here before anyone is seen.*
- **Hidden temple:** inside the mountain lies a **forgotten temple in a cavern court** with trees growing in it, fog, a carved facade (2:01:30); a **three-pillar symbol puzzle** in front of it (2:01:52); a **blood seal** that only the right person can open (2:02:00–2:02:30); a **round floor seal** in a candle-lit room (2:03:20).
- **The carved wall** 2:04:00–2:05:00: a relief ≈20 m long, ≈6 m high; two companions walk along it with a torch and **read it panel by panel**, ≈2 min. *A wall that tells history replaces a cutscene; the player walks the timeline.*
- **Sanctuary** 2:05:20–2:08:00: a long hall with stone tables and racks; the team decides to make it their base (a new hub is gained at the end of the dungeon).

### 12. Summit via the gate of wind (2:14:40–2:27:00)
- The courtyard gate seen at 0:55 opens; beyond it, **a wall of storm fills the path** (2:15:40): a visual barrier the player clears a few metres at a time with a new power. *The climb is the lesson.*
- **Summit plateau** 2:15:55–2:16:50: ≈60 m across, snow and bare rock, a **stone cairn ≈2 m** as the last waypoint, a **rock spur with the carved wall on it** where the old dragon lands; pink-orange low sun. No props beyond rock and the cairn; the sky does the work.
- **≈10 min of talk on the summit** (2:17–2:26), then a mood shot of the peak at night under aurora (2:27:00).

### 13. Ice cave, frozen ruin, giant cavern, scroll chamber (2:34:40–2:49:20)
- **Ice cave mouth** 2:34:40: a tunnel of blue ice; a hermit camps inside beside a **round sealed metal door** (2:35) and sets the task.
- **Frozen ruin then brass ruin** 2:39:30–2:44:20: an icy hall with a stair, then **metal corridors ≈3 m wide**, round doors, pipes; automaton guards; blind cave dwellers; a large hall with a stair and a round door under cold blue lamps.
- **Giant cavern** 2:45:50–2:47:40: a cavern **several hundred metres across, ≈80–100 m high**, lit by **giant glowing blue-green fungi** (each ≈10–15 m tall, the only light sources, used as landmarks), domed brass buildings, raised walkways ≈3 m wide on brass pipes, black water below.
- **Scroll chamber** 2:48:20–2:49:20: a ring of brass cylinders around a central device, a **beam of light from the ceiling**; the final room is a hall with the item on a pedestal lit from above. *The goal is always the brightest thing in the room.*

### 14. Summit set piece twice more (2:49:40–3:00:00, 3:56:00–3:59:00)
- Reading the scroll at the summit triggers a **vision**: colour shifts to red, the same plateau becomes an ancient battlefield with ghost fighters (2:50:40–2:55:00). *The same arena reused with a different light becomes a different place.*
- The enemy arrives, lands, the fight uses the whole plateau (2:56–2:59); the old ally lands on the spur after.

### 15. Peace council and the palace trap (3:11:00–3:35:00)
- **Council:** a **round table ≈8 m across** in a dark stone hall, ≈12 seats, the war leaders on opposite sides, the host at the head, banners hung above each side; the player sits too (3:13–3:24). The player's choices trade land between the factions.
- **Trap:** the top floor of the palace is an **open porch ≈30 × 20 m with a great wooden yoke** at the far end, open to the sky on three sides; the creature is lured onto it and the yoke drops (3:28–3:35).

### 16. High temple ruin (3:36:20–3:43:10)
- **Exterior:** ruined stone arches and stairs in a high bowl in the rain; flying enemies perched on the ruins (3:36:20–3:36:50).
- **Interior** (3:37:20–3:41:10): dark corridors, braziers, **broken wooden scaffolding and stairs** inside the halls, an altar room with dozens of candles, the **claw puzzle door** again, then a great double door.
- **Out on top** 3:41:30: the dungeon climbs *through* the mountain and comes out on a high terrace; a **stair ≈10 m wide, ≈25 steps, between rock walls** rises to a **column of white light** (the portal) with a priest-boss on the top platform (3:41:50–3:43:10).

### 17. Afterlife valley and the last arena (3:43:30–3:55:00)
- **Path:** pale glowing stones ≈2 m wide through dark blue grass; **low mist fills the hollows and hides the middle distance**; the great hall is a silhouette on a far ridge; a small light ahead every ≈60 m (3:43:50–3:46:30). A spirit stops the player on the path for ≈2 min of talk (3:44:10–3:45:50).
- **Bone bridge:** giant curved ribs arch over the path where a **gatekeeper** tests the player (3:46:50–3:48:50).
- **Hall of heroes** 3:49:10–3:51:50: a tall facade at the end of the bridge; inside, long feast tables, stairs, heroes stand on the stairs and three join the player.
- **Final arena** 3:52:10–3:54:50: a **grass plateau ≈80 m across ringed by giant curved bone tusks and ruined arches**, a raised stone dais with steps at one end; mist covers it until the allies clear it with a shout; the enemy fights in and out of the mist.
- **Return** 3:56–3:59: back to the summit, dragons circle and land on the rocks around the player: the whole cast gathers for the ending.

### What makes the player feel brought into it (patterns, not content)
1. **Every quest is handed over by a person or an object standing in the world**, never by a menu: the ruler walks you to the wizard; a guard runs in mid-scene; an empty plinth with a note; files naming a city; a prophecy carved on a wall. *Our rule: the last beat of quest N is a person, letter, carving or sight that names the place of quest N+1.*
2. **Distant landmarks are introduced before they are quests.** The ruin on the ridge is pointed at in minute 15 and entered in minute 23; the shut courtyard gate is seen at 0:55 and opened at 2:15; the summit is visible from the first walk and is the final stage. *Show it, name it, then send them there.*
3. **Witnesses.** Big moments are staged with people gathering around the player: guards around the corpse (0:36:50), elders in a ring (0:50:10), heroes on the stairs (3:51), dragons landing around the player at the end (3:57). Allies fight alongside in nearly every boss fight. *A reward is someone's reaction.*
4. **Locked camera or slow walk while the stakes are explained.** The cart ride, the walk to the block, the walk along the carved wall, the misty path: the talk happens while the land moves past, not in a static room. Only the councils and teachers are static.
5. **The same place returns changed.** The square calm → burning; the summit for teaching → vision → battle → farewell (four visits); the inn cellar as a hub between missions (five visits). *Few places, revisited with new light, people and stakes, feel like a living world and cost less.*
6. **Each big location ends with a reveal tied to the place:** the carved wall's word, the empty plinth, the prophecy mural, the scroll's vision. *Our dungeons should end in a discovery about the war, not just a chest.*
7. **Light marks the goal.** Exit daylight in the cave, the brightest final hall, the beam on the scroll, the column of light on the temple top.
8. **What the reference does poorly (for us to avoid):** ≈42 % standing talk, 7 min of reading files, a 12-min council. For a browser action game: split long talk into walk-and-talk, show history on walls and tablets the player passes, keep any static scene under 90 s.

## (b) Pattern cards, one per kind of place

Each card is written generically for our valley. "Ref" timestamps point to where the video shows the pattern best.

### Road / trail
- **Widths:** main road 4 m (cart road, cobbles showing through dirt); trail 2–2.5 m (worn earth, flat slabs); pilgrim stair 2 m steps; ceremonial stair 5–10 m.
- **Edges:** no kerb. Grass/verge strip 1–2 m, then scattered boulders 0.5–1.5 m (one every 10–20 m), trees in clumps of 3–7, ferns. A rock outcrop or cut bank (3–6 m) on one side in hilly stretches.
- **Bends:** every 40–60 m; each bend has a "hider" (boulder, pine clump, rise) so the next stretch is revealed on turning.
- **Markers:** signpost at every fork (1 per junction); a farm or lone building before every town; carved tablets/stelae along a pilgrim route (one per ≈80–120 m of path); cairns on high bare ground; a lantern/brazier every ≈60 m on night or spirit paths.
- **Changes of country** happen at a bridge, a gate, a pass or a weather line, never mid-meadow.
- **Ref:** 0:15:20–0:16:05, 0:18:05, 0:45:00, 0:46:40–0:49:15, 1:58:58, 3:46:30.

### Approach to any adventure spot (last 300 m → door)
- **300 m:** a column of smoke, a perched creature, a tower top, a carved facade, or a glow seen over the ridge.
- **100 m:** the silhouette is fully seen; "discovered" moment; the path climbs or narrows into a ravine/stair.
- **50 m:** props tell who lives there (bedrolls and fires = bandits; antler totems = wild clans; banners = an army; bones = beasts); 2–4 guards outside, one on high ground (archer).
- **Door:** recessed mouth or framed arch in a cliff; a pair of braziers/fire baskets flanking it; wide steps up to it.
- **Ref:** 0:23:15–0:23:50, 0:34:30–0:35:05, 1:58:45, 2:00:22–2:01:25, 3:36:20.

### Cave (natural)
- **Shape:** mouth 4–6 m wide, 4–5 m tall, often hung with roots or ice; tunnels 3–5 m wide; chambers 15–40 m across; ceiling 6–20 m; ends at a cave hall or exits elsewhere (a cave as a *through* route: keep exit 0:14, embassy escape 1:39).
- **Parts:** mouth → twisting tunnel (1–2 bends to cut daylight) → beast den chamber (bones, nest) → optional shaft of daylight → exit or final chamber.
- **Light:** cold daylight shafts from ceiling cracks; glowing fungi in deep caves; otherwise darkness and the player's own light.
- **Enemies:** 1 large beast asleep (sneak option) or 3–6 small ones; webs and bones signal them.
- **Ref:** 0:13:20–0:14:40, 1:39:25–1:40:00, 2:34:40, 2:45:50.

### Tomb or ruin dungeon
- **Facade:** row of tall arches 8–10 m or a stepped carved front; stair 8 m wide, ≈30 steps; flanking braziers.
- **Sequence (9 spaces):** collapsed entry hall 20×15 m with a light hole → narrow tunnel 3 m → puzzle room 10×8 m (gate + lever + rotating pillars, answer carved nearby, a corpse showing the trap) → vaulted corridor 3×4 m → creature room 15 m → niche corridors (corpses rise from wall niches) → locked ring door (key found earlier) → final cavern 40×20 m with ceiling waterfalls/light, platform up 6 steps, carved wall, sarcophagus boss → one-way shortcut tunnel back to the outside.
- **Loot:** chests beside the boss dais; a book or note mid-way that explains the place; the main prize on a lit pedestal.
- **Light:** warm in built corridors (sconce every 6–8 m), cold daylight in natural parts, brightest at the end.
- **Crossing time:** 6–15 min.
- **Ref:** 0:23:55–0:29:25, 0:57:30–1:00:50, 3:37:20–3:41:30.

## (c) Rules for sculpting our own valley (five streamed areas, WebGPU)

**Terrain shape**
1. **Cut every area into rooms with ridges, not walls.** A 6–15 m terrain ridge or a rock-faced bank between two basins hides the next place and costs nothing but heightmap. Each area should be 3–6 such basins joined by passes.
2. **Use one big silhouette per area as its compass:** a peak, a carved cliff facade, a tower. It must be visible from most of that area and, through the gate, from the next one. Pick it before sculpting anything else.
3. **Put every adventure spot at a height change:** on a knoll, in a hollow, up a stair, behind a ravine. Flat placement reads as "random"; raised or sunken reads as "built here on purpose".
4. **Roads follow the land:** 4 m roads on valley floors and gentle slopes, 2–2.5 m trails over ridges, stairs only where the slope exceeds ≈30°. Bend every 40–60 m and put a hider (boulder or tree clump) on the inside of each bend.
5. **Steer with water, cliffs and tree lines, not fences.** A river with one bridge, a cliff band with one ramp, and a dense tree belt with one gap are three invisible walls that also look natural.
6. **Change country at a threshold only:** a bridge, a gate, a pass or a weather line. Inside one area keep one ground palette; the change of empire should land at the gate between areas.
7. **Flatten arenas to under 5° slope and 60–80 m across**, then break them with 4–8 cover pieces 1–2 m high and one perch 10–15 m tall. Leave the sky open over them.

**Cost per look (triangles and draw calls)**
8. **Paint roads, not model them:** a road is a terrain splat channel (dirt + cobble patches) plus a slight 0.1–0.2 m depression in the heightmap. Zero extra draw calls.
9. **A kit of ≈12 hero meshes per empire is enough**: arch, wall segment (straight, corner, broken), stair block, column, gate/lintel, brazier, banner pole, tent or lean-to, totem, tablet/stela. Build every camp, fort, shrine and ruin from that kit and draw each mesh as one instanced batch per area.
10. **Scatter boulders and trees as instanced sets of 3–5 variants**, in clumps (3–7 together), not evenly. Clumps give the "cultivated" look with fewer instances than uniform scatter.
11. **Silhouette beats detail:** a tall arch row, a tower stub or a stepped facade at 300 m is read by its outline. Spend triangles on the outline and on what is within 10 m of the player's path; use low LODs or impostor cards for anything beyond 150 m.
12. **Dungeons and caves go in their own interior scenes** behind a door/load point, so the open area never draws them. Their entrance is a facade mesh plus a dark recess (a black-faced plane) in the cliff.
13. **Fake the big cavern:** a large interior can be a few hundred metres across if the far walls are low-poly and dark, and lit only by 5–10 big emissive landmarks (glowing plants, crystals, braziers) instead of real lights.
14. **Use smoke columns, fire glow and sky-perched creatures as long-range markers.** One particle column (≈20 sprites) is cheaper than any building and reads from the gate of the area.
15. **Limit real lights:** one sun/moon, and per spot at most 2–4 point lights (braziers flanking the door, the campfire). Everything else is emissive material plus a baked vertex-colour glow on the ground.
16. **Fog does layout work:** low ground fog in hollows hides the middle distance (cheaper culling, and the hidden basin feels bigger); keep distance fog so the next area's silhouette is just visible at the gate.
17. **Reuse a place with new light rather than build a new one:** day/night, burning, vision-red, aurora. A light/fog preset per story beat is cheaper than new geometry.

**Readability from a distance**
18. **Each kind of spot has one colour and one shape signature**, kept the same across the valley: e.g. camps = warm fire + tents; tombs = dark arch row; shrines = a pale stone pylon pair; forts = a tower. The player learns to read the land.
19. **Fire baskets or braziers in pairs always mean "door here".** Never place a pair of braziers anywhere else.
20. **The goal is the brightest thing in view**: the exit of a cave, the final hall, the item on its pedestal, the portal. Keep the approach darker than the goal.
21. **Props tell who lives there within the last 50 m:** owner signs (banners, totems, skulls) on the approach, then 2–4 guards outside with one on high ground.
22. **Story tablets/stelae along the pilgrim and war roads:** 2.5 m stones, one every 80–120 m, each with one line of history. They give the walk a story without a cutscene and cost one instanced mesh.
23. **Show the locked way early.** Put a shut gate, a sealed door or a storm wall in a place the player passes in the first hour, and open it later in the story.
24. **Every spot has a vantage point** 50–150 m away (a rise, a ridge, a tower stub) from which the whole layout is visible before the player commits: guards, entrance, side path.
25. **Build loops:** every dungeon or camp gets a second way in or out (shortcut tunnel, trap door, back gate, cliff path), so the player can leave without backtracking and the space feels larger.
26. **Place a scene in every gate:** a guard who checks the player, a toll, a gatekeeper's test. Area gates in our valley are loading points anyway; give each one a person.
27. **Keep each quest's static talk under 90 s; move it onto a road, a wall or a climb.** Walk-and-talk along a story wall or down a misty path gives the same information with the land moving.
28. **Five areas, five reveals:** design each gate so that walking through it frames that area's big silhouette within the first 10 s (like the cave-mouth vista), then hide it again behind the first ridge.
29. **Revisit:** plan 2–3 places per area that the story returns to with changed light, people or damage (a hub cellar, a summit, a square). Budget for presets, not for copies.
30. **Measure with the body:** 1.8 m person, 2.2–2.5 m doors, 3 m corridors, 4 m roads, 8 m house footprints, 15–18 m towers, 60–80 m arenas. If a sculpted feature does not match these, it will read as toy-sized or empty.

Road rhythm, landmark spacing and how one kind of country turns into the next are judged from a few short stretches (0:15–0:18, 0:45–0:49, 1:09–1:10, 1:59–2:01, 3:43–3:47) and may not be typical.

## Build these five first (recommendation)
1. **The tomb/ruin dungeon template** (card "Tomb or ruin dungeon"): one interior scene with the 9-space sequence; every empire can reskin it with its own kit. Most reused shape in the reference (≥5 dungeons follow it).

5. **The shrine on a height** (switchback stair with tablets, shut gate, plateau with a perch): gives each area its vertical landmark and a place the story can revisit with changed light.

**Source:** a local copy the owner supplied of an uncut, no-commentary longplay of the same game's main quest (YouTube id `p0I7ccItmv8`, 480p, **21 h 08 min = 76 080 s**, 30 fps). Unlike the first source it keeps every walk, ride, shop visit, crafting session and side dungeon the player did along the way, so it shows the land between places. Timestamps below are positions in *this* file (h:mm:ss). Same boundary as before: structure and craft only; reference names appear only as lookup labels; nothing is proposed as content for our game; no frames were copied into the repo (frames were rendered to the session scratchpad only, with headless Blender's sequencer, and tiled into contact sheets).

**Tooling note for anyone going back:** Blender's sequencer clamps scene frames at 1 048 574 (≈9 h 42 m at 30 fps); every frame after that comes out identical. Adding a retiming key and moving the strip's last key to a quarter of the length (4× speed) puts the whole 21 h inside the limit.

**What these numbers mean for our valley.** Even on a horse, the reference puts **a "thing" (a building, a ruin, a camp, a bridge, a person or a monster) every 30–90 s of travel**: on foot that is every 50–120 m, on horseback every 250–700 m. A walk that takes 15–30 minutes passes 8–15 such things. Our valley is a few km², so a walk from one area gate to the next (≈1 km) should pass about 8–12 things, never fewer than one per 120 m of road.

### How the road is drawn (corrected from part one)
- **Main road:** cobbles laid flat in the ground, **3–4 m wide**, with grass growing between stones at the edges; no kerb; verge of tall grass, boulders and young trees. In farmland it is lined on one or both sides by **drystone walls ≈0.8–1.2 m high** (7:28:30, 3:22) or a **wattle/split-rail fence** (6:10:30, 7:26:30). In mountains a stone **retaining wall** holds the downhill side (3:22).
- **Trail:** bare earth 1.5–2.5 m wide, worn into the grass, with stones and roots (6:30–6:34:30, 10:55–10:57:30). Trails leave the road at a signpost or a cairn.
- **Bends and hiders:** roads bend every 60–150 m in open tundra, every 30–60 m in forest; the next stretch is hidden by a rise of 2–5 m, a boulder group, a tree clump or a ruined wall stub.
- **Junctions:** always marked by a **signpost** (a pole ≈2.5 m with 2–4 arms) and often by a farm or an inn standing at the fork (7:24, 13:11).
- **Cairns:** stacked-stone markers ≈1–1.5 m on trails and on mountain paths (3:36:30, 4:02).
- **Water crossings:** stone bridges of one to three arches with ≈0.8 m parapets, deck 4–5 m, often with **a pair of braziers on stone posts at the bridge head** (5:57); plank footbridges ≈1.5 m wide on trails (1:44:30); **fords** where a trail meets a shallow river (6:29:30, 11:00:30).
- **Change of country:** happened at a river or bridge (5:57, 8:56:30), at a pass (9:44, 15:10:30) or at a ridge line; snow begins on rising ground, and pine gives way to birch with autumn colour at a river valley (10:52).
- **Landmarks in view:** on open tundra a tower, a fort, a standing stone, a giant camp's smoke or a city wall is nearly always on the horizon; in forest the view closes to 50–100 m and the road itself is the guide.

## 2c. Forts, camps and military places in the uncut run

**Honest summary first.** The uncut main quest still does **not enter a war army's camp** (tents, standards, a command tent): those belong to the reference game's side war line, which this player skipped. What it does show, in full and on foot, is six fortified or camp places the road passes through. They are the material for the cards below. Close-up frames: every 6–10 s through each place.

### 1. Two-tower toll fort astride a gorge (3:25:00–3:33:00) — *the best fort in the video*
- **First seen** at 3:26:12 from ≈150 m: two square-ish stone towers on either side of a rocky gorge, **tied by a stone span at the top**, a timber hoarding (a wooden platform on brackets) sticking out of the near tower. The road runs along the bottom of the slope toward the near tower's foot.
- **Last 50 m** (3:26:24–3:26:30): the road narrows between a boulder and the tower base; the tower door is **on the road**, so the road *must* pass the guards.
- **Footprint:** each tower ≈7 × 7 m, ≈15–18 m tall, dry-laid rough stone; the gorge between them ≈20–25 m; the top bridge ≈2.5–3 m wide, **no parapet**, a long drop to a river (3:28:00–3:29:24).
- **At the door:** 2 bandits stand in the open in front of the tower door, a cooking tripod with a pot beside them (3:26:30–3:26:54). They stop the player and demand a toll: talk first, then fight or pay.
- **Inside:** narrow stone stairs up the tower (one room per floor, ≈4 × 4 m), a locked chest in a ground-floor room (3:27:24–3:27:42); at the top a **timber platform** with a lookout/archer and a lever; the fight moves out onto the top bridge (3:28:00–3:29:24); the far tower has more rooms, crates and a cell (3:29:30–3:32:06).
- **Exit:** down the far tower and out onto the **same road continuing on the other side** (3:31:36 shows the bridge from the far end; 3:32:12 the road leaving into pine forest). *The fort is a gate the road passes through, not a place off to the side.*
- **People:** ≈6 bandits: 2 at the door, 1–2 on the top platform, 1 on the bridge, 1–2 inside the far tower.

### 2. Log-palisade bandit fort beside a road (7:30:30–7:38:30)
- **First seen** at 7:31:00 from ≈60 m: a **timber gate tower over the road** (posts ≈6–7 m, a roofed lookout platform on top) built where the road squeezes between two rock outcrops. A bandit on the road at its foot asks for a toll (7:31:06–7:31:24).
- **Plan:** past the gate tower, a **log staircase** (≈1.5 m wide, ≈15 steps) climbs a rock face to the main camp; a **plank walkway ≈1.5 m wide** runs along the cliff (7:31:42–7:31:54); the camp proper is a yard ≈30 × 20 m on a rock shelf: a palisade of **vertical logs ≈3–4 m with sharpened tops** on the open sides, **a spiked log barricade** ("cheval-de-frise", 7:33:06–7:33:30) at the entry, **2–3 timber shacks** ≈6 × 4 m with plank walls and plank roofs (7:32:30, 7:33:54), tall dry grass inside the yard, a cooking spot.
- **Back:** behind the yard a **cave mouth** leads to the leader's room (bedroll, table, chest, a book) and a short cave with barrels and a pool (7:36:18–7:36:48). The leader stands in the back shack.
- **People:** ≈7: 1 at the road gate, 1 on the gate tower, 2 on the stair/walkway, 2 in the yard, 1 leader at the back.
- **Light:** daylight; no fires seen in the yard by day; lanterns in the cave.

### 3. Wild clan's camp in a canyon, built on plank decks (12:21:00–12:29:30)
- **Approach:** a red-rock canyon country; **standing stones ≈4–6 m** rise beside the trail before the camp (12:21:24–12:21:32), a dragon fight on open ground in front of it (12:21:40–12:23:16).
- **Plan:** the camp spreads over **wooden decks and stairs built against the canyon wall and across a gully** (12:25:56–12:27:08), ≈60–80 m long; a **ruined stone fort wall** forms the back; **hide tents ≈4 m tall** (a stretched-hide frame, 12:26:36), plank tables, barrels and crates, **a stone-lined round well ≈2.5 m across** in the middle of the main deck (12:27:48, 12:28:28), work benches with tools, **sharpened stakes** set in the slope on the approach (12:25:48).
- **People:** ≈8–10 clan fighters spread over different deck levels, so the fight climbs the decks; a few spell-casters keep back.
- **What is at the centre:** the well and the work benches; the chests are at the edges of the decks.
- **After:** the deck at the back of the camp is where the companion stops to point out the next place (12:28:52–12:29:08): the camp is a staging point, the temple cave opens below it (12:30:30–12:31:00).

### 4. Roadside traveller camps (1:57:20–1:58:00, 15:12:30–15:13:30, 18:38:00–18:40:30)
- **Night campfire on the open moor** (1:57:20–1:58:00): one big fire, a lean-to of hides on two poles, bones and a cooking spit, a chest by the fire; nobody home. Seen from ≈150 m **only by the fire glow**.

- **River hideout:** a camp in a cave mouth by a river: plank floors, tables, barrels, a skeleton in a crate, a lantern by the water (6:00:50–6:03:20).

### 7. City gates as the closest thing to a fort gate (4:30:00–4:31:20, 18:40:30–18:40:38, 2:54:18)

## 2d. Farms, mills and villages seen in passing

- **Farms** stand at road forks and outside every town wall: one longhouse ≈12 × 7 m with a shingle roof, a cabbage/vegetable field ≈20 × 15 m **fenced with wattle or split rails**, a cart, a well or a water trough, a hay or wood pile, 1–3 farm hands working (7:24, 7:26:30, 9:40:30–9:41:30, 11:01:30, 3:21). From the road the **smoke from the chimney** is seen first.
- **Mills:** a timber saw-mill with a water wheel on a stream, log stacks, a footbridge; the mill is always at the edge of a village where the river leaves it (6:28, 9:47).
- **Villages (corrected):** the river village has **≈10–12 buildings** along one road and a river, a mill, a stone bridge at one end and a wooden one at the other (6:28–6:28:30); the snow lumber village has **≈8 log houses** on a slope with a log palisade only on the side facing the road (9:51:20–9:55:30); the mountain-foot village has **≈8–10** buildings, a stone bridge and a drystone-walled lane (6:35). None have a full wall; a short palisade or a fence covers only the road side.
- **The inn** is the biggest building in every village (a long hall ≈20 × 10 m with a central fire pit, 7:25).

## 2e. Dungeon entrances, exits and loops (shown in full this time)

- **First tomb, the way out** (1:36:00–1:40:10): after the ring door, the final cavern (two thin waterfalls, sarcophagus, chests, the carved wall; 1:37:10–1:39:30); a **lever behind the wall** opens a **narrow rising stair tunnel** ≈2 m wide (1:39:40–1:39:50) that ends in a door marked as the way outside (1:40:10). The player comes out **on a hillside ≈100–200 m from and above the front entrance**, then walks down to a lakeside dock (1:41:20). Boss dead → outside: ≈2.5 min including loot.
- **Toll fort:** in by the near tower door, out by the far tower onto the onward road (above).
- **Palisade fort:** in by the road gate, out the same way, the cave at the back is a dead end with the leader.
- **Embassy:** in by the front door, out through a trap door into a cave and out into the wild elsewhere (8:48–8:53).
- **Hidden temple:** entered through a cave mouth below the clan camp (12:30:30–12:31:00), then a ruined court, a corridor, the face door, a stair (12:34:30–12:36:30); it ends in the sanctuary where the player stays (no exit run).
- **Brass ruin and the deep cavern:** entered from an ice-cave door, and the player leaves the cavern by **a lift/elevator up to a different exit** (index 17:39–17:45).
- **Rule:** every one of these has either **a second exit that comes out elsewhere** or **a gate on the far side**, so the player never walks back the way they came after the boss.

## 2f. How each quest hands the player from one place to the next when the walk is shown

- **The road is the handover.** After a quest-giver names a place, the player picks the road out of town, and within 2–5 minutes meets a **side thing on the road** (a stranger, a tomb door, a fort with a toll, a dragon) that is not the quest but is on the way. 1:42–1:59, 3:21–3:54, 7:24–7:43 all follow this pattern.
- **Every destination is announced by something seen first:** the town by farms and smoke (1:59:30, 4:30), the capital by a giant rock arch over the road under it (7:41), the village by a mill and a bridge (6:28), the clan camp by standing stones and a fight in front of it (12:21), the ice coast by a lone watchtower on a ridge at sunset (16:45).
- **Companions walk ahead and stop to talk at the threshold:** at the clan camp's back deck (12:28:52), at the temple door (12:35:00), at the stone city gate. The talk happens at the doorway of the next place, not in a room before leaving.
- **Fast travel cuts the second visit, not the first.** The player walks or rides to a place the first time (3:21–3:54, 6:27–6:35:30, 7:24–7:43) and fast-travels back to it later. *For our valley: the first trip to every place must be good on foot; the return trips can be gate-to-gate.*

## 2i. What this changes for our three army camps and the fort
1. **Put the fort across the road, not beside it.** Both forts in the reference make the road pass a guard who speaks first; the onward road leaves from the far side. Our fort should sit in a pass or at a river crossing between two areas, so every trip through that gate is a scene.
2. **Build camps on levels.** The war-band camp reads as a place because it climbs: decks, stairs and a back wall over 2–3 levels against a cliff. A flat ring of tents would read as scatter. Give each army camp at least one raised level (a platform, a terrace or a rampart walk) for an archer and the leader.
3. **Count small:** 3–4 tents or shacks, 4–8 m apart, around an open centre 10–15 m across with one focal object (fire, well, standard); 6–10 defenders; one stair or ramp in. More pieces would cost draw calls without reading better.
4. **Announce each camp by one tall thing:** a tower top on the skyline at ≈150 m, standing stones before the clan camp, fire glow at night. For three armies: one tall marker per army (banner pole, watch platform, standing stone), visible from the road 150 m out.
5. **Every fortified place has a second way out** (far tower, trap door, cave behind, rising tunnel), and the walk *to* it the first time is the reward, with 8–12 things on the way.
