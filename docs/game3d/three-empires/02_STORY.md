# Fallen Sky: The War of Three Roads

> **Retired on 2026-11-27.** The War of Three Roads story was removed from the game by the owner's decision; its tools were kept. This document is history. See [README.md](README.md).

Story for the open world. Step 2 of the three-empire plan. Draft for the owner, 2026-11-21.
Nothing here is built yet. Every name can be changed; the names I would most like you to confirm are listed at the end.

## The story in one breath

A piece of the Wolf Star fell into this valley. Three empires that have each watched that star for a thousand years found a road here in the same year, and each one needs the valley to survive: **Akitsu** needs its star-iron, **Tameri** needs its water and grain, **Remoria** needs its land. All three believe the Wolf that sleeps here is theirs. None of them is the villain. The people of the valley are caught in the middle, and one of them wants this war to happen.

The player is a soldier of one of the three, sworn at the gate, who slowly finds out that their own people have been here before, that the enemy across the river is here for a reason as good as their own, and that the last choice of the war will be put in their hands.

## 2. The legend

**The Wolf Star.** The brightest star in the sky. Three peoples who have never met each named it and built their calendar on it.

- In **Akitsu** it is the Heavenly Wolf, the guardian that walks the mountain roads ahead of travellers.
- In **Tameri** it is the Sharp One. On the morning it returns to the dawn sky, the great river begins to rise and the year begins.
- In **Remoria** it is the Mother. The she-wolf who raised their founders climbed into the sky when her sons quarrelled, and watches from there.

**The night the sky fell.** Long before any of today's empires, a piece of that star broke off and came down in this valley. The whirlpool lake in the north, the one the valley folk call the World Beneath the World, is where it struck.

**The Veil.** The fall tore something. In this valley the line between the living world and the world of spirits has been thin ever since. That is the Veil, and it has rules everyone here learns fast:

- Gunpowder will not catch. A spark dies in the pan.
- The dead do not rest unless someone gives them their rites. Left alone, they get up.
- Spirits take whatever shape people give them. The valley is full of beasts made of ink and ash, because its people have always painted.
- Those who fight under the Veil start to carry its light: the halos, wings and auras.

**The Wolf.** What fell was alive. It was shapeless and hurt, and the first people who found it painted it as a wolf, because all of them had a wolf in the sky. It has slept under the valley ever since, in the shape the paintings give it. The keepers of the valley have kept that painting unfinished on purpose, in four murals in four caves, for one reason: **a finished god chooses a people. An unfinished one belongs to everyone.**

**The Teeth.** Four shards of the star itself lie in the four caves, one set in each mural like a tooth in a jaw. They are star-iron: metal that takes an edge nothing else can, that sings near spirits, and that the Wolf feels like its own body.

**The Three Roads.** When a Tooth is drawn, the wound opens, and three roads open with it: one into the mountains of Akitsu, one onto the river of Tameri, one onto the northern frontier of Remoria. The valley's old records say it has happened before. Every few hundred years someone pulls a Tooth, three peoples find the valley, and they fight over it. **The people of Wolfveil are the descendants of the ones who stayed behind each time**: deserters, peacemakers and the wounded of all three.

A year ago someone drew the First Tooth. The star dimmed. Three empires saw it on the same night.

## 3. The three empires

Each empire is here because something at home is breaking. Each one is right from where it stands. Each has a quarrel inside it, and the player's own line of quests ends by taking a side in that quarrel.

### Akitsu, the Resolute (bear) — inspired by Japan in the Meiji years

**Who they are.** An island empire six years into the Dawn Reign. A young sovereign took the throne back from the sword houses that had ruled for centuries, abolished their privileges, and is building a modern country at a run: a conscript army in dark blue coats, railways, telegraph lines, foundries. Their beast is the moon-ring bear of their mountain shrines.

**Why they are here.** Foreign iron fleets, the Grey Ships, forced Akitsu's harbours open with treaties signed at gunpoint. Akitsu must become strong before it is carved up, and its own mines are worked out. Then the Heavenly Wolf dimmed, a road appeared in the western mountains that was on no map, and at the end of it lay star-iron. With it Akitsu can face the Grey Ships. The court has given the expedition sixty days to send the first shipment home.

**What they stand to lose.** Their country's freedom. And their peace at home: half the realm believes the Dawn Reign turned its back on the gods. If the sovereign's banner comes home with the Wolf's blessing, there is no civil war.

**The quarrel inside.**
- **The Banner Army**: farmers' and shopkeepers' sons with rifles that will not fire here, officers trained abroad, engineers who want to lay a railway through the valley. For them the reform is the first time their lives were worth anything.
- **The Old Houses**: the sword families who lost everything in the reform and were forbidden to wear their blades at home. In this valley powder is dead, so the army needs them again. For them this is the last place on earth their art matters.
- **The shrine wardens**, who say the Wolf is a mountain god to be asked, never mined.

**Their question: progress or reverence? What may you destroy to save your country?**

### Tameri, the Radiant (sun tiger) — inspired by Egypt in an age of war

**Who they are.** The Beloved Land, a river kingdom older than anyone's memory, ruled by a young queen, Meryt-Sepdet. Its army marches in divisions named for its gods; its temples keep the House of Life, where the rites for the dead are written. Their beast is the striped sun-cat, the Eye of the Sun in a hunting shape.

**Why they are here.** The river has not risen for three years. The Sharp One, whose dawn rising calls the flood, came late and dim. The granaries are nearly empty, and the Reed Kings, raiders and rebel governors together, are pressing the river mouths. The priests say the star has lost a tooth and the flood will not return until it is given back. The generals say star-iron will arm the chariots. The scribes say only this: **there are forty-one days of grain left at home.**

**What they stand to lose.** Their people's lives, this year. And their dead: in Tameri a person cannot pass on unless their mouth is opened with a blade of sky-iron. Every Tameri soldier who dies in this valley without the rite is lost for ever, and under the Veil, gets up again.

**The quarrel inside.**
- **The Temple**: the tooth is a piece of a god. It must be returned to heaven and no blade may be made from it. Restore the star and the flood returns.
- **The Army**: a kingdom that is conquered has no temples. Forge it.
- Between them, the scribes who count the grain, and a queen far away who writes that she trusts them both.

**Their question: faith or survival? What do you owe the gods and the dead while the living starve?**

### Remoria, the Vigilant (wolf) — inspired by Rome

**Who they are.** A republic that calls itself eternal. Its legend says twin brothers were raised by a she-wolf, and that the twin who lived built the city in her name. In its oldest temple hangs a shield that fell from the sky in the days of its second king. They call it **the Pledge of Rule**: while it is whole, Remoria stands. Their beast is the wolf.

**Why they are here.** Openly: land. Thirty thousand veterans of a ten-year war were promised farms and never got them, and a general who would like to be king is courting their anger. The Senate has sent the Ninth Legion, the Wolf-Sworn, to found a colony in the valley: twenty acres for every soldier. In secret: on the night the star dimmed, the Pledge of Rule cracked. The Senate has hidden it. The Ninth's commander carries a sealed order to find star-iron and mend the shield before the people learn of it.

**What they stand to lose.** Their republic. If the crack is known before it is mended, the general marches and the republic ends. And the veterans: men of forty-five who have nothing in the world except what they were promised.

**The quarrel inside.**
- **The Legate**: order, law, roads, walls. He believes a lie that keeps the peace is a duty, and he is an honest man being made to tell one.
- **The Tribune**: the soldiers and the people are owed the truth, and a republic that lies to itself is already gone.
- **The Augur**: the Wolf in this valley is the Mother herself. Bring her home and no shield is needed.
- **The veterans**, who want their twenty acres and are starting to notice that the land they were promised already has people on it.

**Their question: order or truth? And whose land is it: the one who was born on it, or the one who was promised it?**

## 4. The valley and its people

**Wolfveil Town** stands in the middle, around the Wolf-Spirit Fountain. Its people carry the blood of all three empires from older wars, which is why each army finds faces here that look like home.

**The Fountain Truce.** The valley's one law, older than the town: *no blood within sight of the fountain.* Every newcomer is counted and sworn to it at the Southern Wolf Gate. Soldiers of all three empires walk the market with their weapons tied, buy bread from the same baker, and kill each other past the bridges.

**The Fountain Watch.** Five archers sworn to no empire, who keep the Truce: **Lyra** the Seeker, their captain; **Vex** the Hunter; **Kira** the Scout; **Noor** the Wanderer; **Zephyr** the Silent, who has not spoken since he was a child.

**Sable Vey**, Keeper of the First Territory, keeps the Truce Stone and the town's law. **Elder Maren**, the Truce-Speaker, has spent a year keeping three armies talking.

**The Brush Keepers.** The painters who tend the four murals and keep the Wolf unfinished. Their master is **Hesper**, an old man with ink to the elbows, patient with everyone, loved in the town.

**The Unfinished Wolf.** Four murals, four strokes, one signature left blank. The Keepers' rule: every stroke may be renewed; the signature is never written.

## 5. The hidden hand

**Master Hesper drew the First Tooth.**

Seventy years ago, in a winter the town still calls the Hungry Winter, the passes closed and Hesper's family starved one by one while the Wolf slept under their feet and did nothing. He became the greatest of the Brush Keepers, and he has believed every day since that an unfinished god is a locked granary.

He means to finish the Wolf, so that it must answer when its people call. The painting needs two things: the four Teeth set back in the wolf's mouth, and ink ground from a real war, because **a god is signed in what its people bleed.** So he opened the Roads, brought three believing armies into his valley, and has been making sure they fight. The murder at the fountain is his. The clues that turn each empire against the others are his.

He is the villain of this story and he is the same as everyone else in it: he wants a god that answers. Each empire's line asks the player a version of his question, so that when he finally holds out the brush and says *finish it for your people; they will eat tonight,* the player understands exactly what is being offered.

**Zephyr the Silent** is Hesper's grandson, the last of that family. He has known all along.

## 6. The main story: twelve chapters in three acts

The main line is the same for all three empires and is given by the valley's people, so players of rival empires walk it side by side. What changes with your empire is who is with you, what is said to you, and which of the other two you owe a debt to.

### Act I. The Roads Open

**1. Sworn at the Gate.** *Southern Wolf Gate.* Lyra is swearing you in when ink beasts hit the line of new arrivals. You drag three strangers clear: one from each empire. One of the two foreigners presses a keepsake into your hand. You swear the Fountain Truce: your first pledge.

**2. Three Arrows.** *The Wolf-Spirit Fountain.* The weekly parley. Elder Maren is in the middle of a sentence when three arrows strike her: one fletched in each empire's style. You chase the archer over the rooftops; cornered, it bursts into ink. By nightfall the envoys have left town and the war is open. Sable Vey asks you to keep the Truce alive inside the town even if it dies outside.

**3. The First Stroke.** *Jade Lantern Grotto.* Master Hesper shows you the first mural: a wolf in grass strokes with one tooth missing. "Someone drew it a year ago. That was the night your roads opened." You fight down to the empty socket and its guardian, the Painted Stag, and find something left behind there. It points at one of the three empires.

**4. What the Wolf Stones Remember.** *The Wind-Carved Wolf Stones.* At the stones the token wakes an echo, and you are standing in a battle four hundred years old: three armies in older armour, on this same ground. You fight through it beside a ghost, a soldier of your own empire who threw down his standard to stop the killing. He founded the town. The Roads have opened before. Your people have been here before. Wolfveil is who was left.

### Act II. The Teeth

**5. Root Fire.** *Whispering Root Hollow.* The second Tooth. All three empires have a column at the cave mouth, and for the first time you fight the others in the open. Inside waits the Root Pyre. You carry the Tooth out, and the moment it leaves the mural the next cave wakes and stronger beasts walk the surface. Every Tooth drawn bleeds the Wolf a little more into the valley.

**6. The Drowned Ledger.** *Sunken Inkstone Cistern.* The Keepers' drowned archive: a ledger of every war fought here. Your empire is in it twice. The third Tooth is here too. Underwater, one of the rival champions saves your life or you save theirs, and neither of you says anything about it afterwards.

**7. The Burning Fields.** *Southern Gate Fields, at harvest.* All three armies come for the grain. Someone sets the fields alight, and the barn with the farm families in it is burning too. You can save the grain wagons for your people or the families in the barn. There is no time to do both well. Whatever you choose, someone thanks you and someone never forgives you.

**8. Four Hundred Lamps.** *Echoing Crystal Vault.* The last Tooth. All three commanders come in person, the standoff breaks, the lights go out and the roof comes down. You are sealed in with the champions of the other two empires and one lamp between you. On the way out through the dark, each tells you why they are here. When you reach the surface the Tooth is gone. Hesper took it while the armies fought.

### Act III. The Signature

**9. The Dusk Painter.** *The Wolf Sanctuary.* Hesper's workroom: seventy years of drawings of a finished wolf, and one child's drawing of a family at an empty table. On his desk, the stolen arrows and the planted clues. He tells you everything without shame. Zephyr stands beside him, and Lyra cannot loose at her own man. Hesper walks into the mountain with all four Teeth.

**10. The World Beneath the World.** *The Vortex Lake.* Where the sky fell. You go down through the whirl into the Wolf's own dream and watch the star fall, watch the first painters find it, watch it take the shape they gave it. It wears three faces now, Mother, Mountain God and Opener of the Ways, and all three are masks that people put on it. It says one thing to you. What it says depends on what you have done.

**11. One Night Under One Banner.** *The Howling Plateau.* Hesper's host rises: every soldier who has died in this war, painted back in ink, in all three uniforms. The Watch rings the Truce Bell and for one night three armies hold one line. The rival champions fight at your shoulder.

**12. The Last Stroke.** *The Wolf Rotunda, under the valley.* The Wolf, half signed and in agony. Hesper holds out the brush. **Finish it for your people**, and what they need most is answered tonight: the iron, the flood, the land. Or **break the brush**, and the Wolf stays free, the valley stays everyone's, and your empire's need is still there in the morning. Zephyr, silent beside you, makes his own choice from what he has seen you do.

**After the ending.** The Wolf does not let itself be finished: it marks the player instead, as its Witness. The Roads stay open because the three peoples now hold them open themselves. The need that brought each empire is still real, so the war goes on, now as a contest under the Truce for stewardship of the valley, round after round. That is the game's ongoing war between players. What the player chose at the end decides their epilogue, their title, and how the valley speaks to them afterwards.

## 7. Each empire's own story

Eight chapters each, on that empire's side of the valley. Each ends with the player choosing between two leaders, and neither choice is the wrong one. Chapters 3 to 6 of each line are deliberately pointed at the same places as another empire's chapter, so players doing them meet as enemies.

### Akitsu: The Iron Road (Western Cedar Wilds)

1. **A Farmer's Son in a Blue Coat.** Sergeant Gorō's recruits learn that a rifle is a spear now, the hard way, on the night the ink beasts come.
2. **The Sword Mistress's Price.** Lady Asagiri will train the conscripts if someone brings back her house banner, and the swords of the grandson who carried it.
3. **Survey Stakes.** Mizuhara's railway survey across the west. At the shrine, Kohaku shows you which trees the line runs through. *(Remoria is sent to pull the stakes; Tameri to copy the map.)*
4. **Moon-Ring.** A mother bear poisoned by a splinter of star-iron. The Colonel wants her shot. Kohaku wants her saved. You decide how the fight ends.
5. **The Grey Ships' Letter.** Sixty days. Escort the first ore wagons to the Road. *(Tameri raids them.)*
6. **Seven Swords at the Stones.** House Asagiri holds the frontier at the Wolf Stones against a Remorian cohort. Seven named swordsmen. How many live depends on you. *(Remoria is ordered to take it.)*
7. **The Cedar Order.** The Colonel orders the shrine cedars felled for the railway. Lady Asagiri refuses. Kohaku stands at the trees. **You choose: the railway or the grove.**
8. **First Train Through the Mountain** or **The Last Stand of House Asagiri.** The cost of what you chose, and what the other one says to you afterwards.

### Tameri: Forty-One Days (Eastern Riverlands)

1. **The Count.** Unloading the relief barge under attack. Paneb shows you the tally: forty-one days. From now on it changes with what you do.
2. **Opening of the Mouth.** Tameri's dead lie in the ravine without their rites, and at night they rise. Recover the priestess's sky-iron blade and free five soldiers. Each one has a name, and you read it.
3. **Eyes on the Iron Road.** Sabu takes you west to learn what Akitsu is building. *(Akitsu guards its survey; Remoria is there too.)*
4. **The Quarry of the Ancestors.** In the old quarry lies an obelisk cut by Tameri hands four hundred years ago. Clear what nests there and raise it. *(Remoria wants the stone for its fort.)*
5. **The Iron That Should Be Bread.** The General sends you to take Akitsu's ore. The Priestess asks what you think you are stealing. *(Akitsu escorts it.)*
6. **The Night Barge.** Grain for home, down the river in the dark, past a floating bridge the other armies would like to sink it from. If it arrives, the count goes up nine days.
7. **Iron or Flood.** You are carrying a Tooth. The General wants a hundred blades. The Priestess wants it given to the river. **You choose.**
8. **A Hundred Blades** or **The River Rises.** The forge defended, or the procession to the waterfall, and an answer from the water that the player must decide how to read.

### Remoria: Twenty Acres (Northern Highlands)

1. **Old Vine's Furrow.** The first furrow of the colony is ploughed on cold thin soil, and the children of the wolf are attacked by wolves. The Augur is delighted.
2. **The Crack.** A courier, a sealed order, and something you were not meant to overhear.
3. **Pull the Stakes.** Akitsu's railway is aimed at the northern pass. Stop it at the survey. *(Akitsu guards it; Tameri watches.)*
4. **The Stone Is Ours.** Kallias needs dressed stone for the fort and the aqueduct. Tameri is already in the quarry. *(Tameri is raising its obelisk.)* Kallias and the Tameri master mason turn out to like each other.
5. **The Water Is Ours.** An aqueduct from the whirlpool lake to the fort, raised arch by arch. Downstream, it is Tameri's river. *(Tameri is sent to break it.)*
6. **Take the Stones.** The cohort goes against seven swords at the Wolf Stones. *(Akitsu holds them.)*
7. **The Pledge.** The Tribune has found out about the crack and means to tell the legion. The Legate begs for thirty days. The Augur wants her silenced. **You choose: the secret or the truth.**
8. **The Shield Made Whole** or **The Assembly of the Ninth.** The patch forged at the hot springs under attack, or the first free vote of a new colony, and the Augur's answer to it.

## 8. The valley's own stories

Sixteen side stories given by the people of the valley, open to every empire, each tied to one place and one person. A few, to show the tone (the full list is in the quest plan):

- **The Lantern Count.** Every dusk the old lamplighter lights one lantern for each person who died that day, whatever their uniform. He has run out of oil. The only oil is in the three camps.
- **The Deserters' Hearth.** Somewhere in the western woods, men who walked away from all three armies are sharing one fire. All three commanders have put a price on them.
- **The Unsent Coin.** A Remorian soldier dead four hundred years cannot rest at the ruined watch station: his pay never reached his family. His family are still in the valley. They bake the town's bread.
- **The Gravedigger's Three Fields.** One woman buries all three peoples, each in their own way. She needs cedar for the pyres, salt for the wrappings and coins for the eyes, and someone to stand guard while she works.
- **Fishing the Vortex.** A fisherman at the whirlpool lake keeps hooking old helmets. One from each empire. Each commander has to be told.
- **Bread and Salt.** A wedding between a town girl and a soldier who stayed. It needs a gift from each of the three peoples, and a night without a fight.

## 9. Why a quest matters here: the Pledge

You asked for the "pledge system" to be expanded. I could not find a system by that name in the code, so I have read it as the monsters, caves and quests side of the game. I liked the word enough to build the quest system around it, and it fits the story: Remoria's sacred shield is literally called the Pledge of Rule. If you meant something else, tell me and I will adjust.

**Every quest is a Pledge.** You do not pick it off a list. Someone asks you, face to face, for a reason, and you give your word.

- **It is sworn to a person, for a cause.** Every pledge states what it is for and who pays if it fails. "Kill five skeletons" becomes "five of our own dead are walking the ravine because nobody gave them their rites; their names are on these tags; set them free."
- **It can be kept, bent or broken.** Kept: done as sworn. Bent: done another way when the story gives you a choice. Broken: abandoned or betrayed. All three are remembered.
- **Three things are remembered.** *Renown*, what your empire thinks of you. *Trust*, what the valley thinks of you. And the *Wolf's Regard*, which is never shown as a number and comes only from mercy and truth in the story's choices; it decides your ending.
- **It is staged.** Each pledge has something you see before anyone speaks, a person who asks, a place with its own history, a turn you did not expect, and a moment at the end where someone looks at you differently.

**How quests make players fight.** For every war pledge one empire can swear, the other two can swear the opposite at the same place and time: escort the wagon, raid the wagon, toll the wagon. Swearing a war pledge raises your banner, which is the game's existing rule for fighting other players: both sides have chosen it. Story pledges send you through the same passes, bridges, fields and cave mouths, so the war is always in sight even for someone who has not raised a banner yet.

**The rules of war.** The Truce gives fighting between players a code: no blood within sight of the fountain; only the sworn may be attacked; the dead are owed their rites, and whoever cuts down someone performing them carries a mark of shame for a while.

## 10. Monsters, and why they are here

No monster is in the valley without a reason, so that every fight means something.

- **Ink beasts.** Spirits that took the shape of the valley's paintings: hares, stags, koi, moths, and the ink-strays, wolves made of brush strokes. They grow stronger each time a Tooth is drawn.
- **The unrested dead.** Soldiers of the three armies nobody gave rites to. Each rises in the manner of its people: Akitsu's as hollow banner-bearers, Tameri's as the Unopened, Remoria's as grey shades still keeping formation. Killing them is a mercy, and the quests treat it as one.
- **What came down the Roads.** Each Road let in creatures from that land's own stories: from Akitsu's mountains, moon-ring bears, lantern spirits, crow-monks of the cedars, river imps; from Tameri's river and desert, river-jaws, stone scorpions, jackal shades, scarab swarms; from Remoria's old world, cliff harpies, a one-eyed giant, a bronze forge guardian.
- **The painted host.** In the last act, Hesper's ink soldiers in all three uniforms.

These are original designs in the spirit of each people's folklore. The full list, with which ones share a skeleton so they stay cheap to run, is in the quest plan.

## 12. Room left for later

- **Spells.** Fire and lightning are already hinted at in the valley's lore as arts of the Veil. Each empire has a natural home for one (Tameri's sun-fire, Akitsu's storm, Remoria's iron and stone). Nothing in this plan depends on them.
- **Beyond the Roads.** Each Road leads somewhere. Later, each empire's home side can open as its own area.
- **Seasons.** The war for stewardship can change with each round: which banner hangs over the fountain, which empire guards the Sanctuary.

## 14. What is inspired by what

The three empires are inspired by real history and folklore, which belong to everyone: Japan's years of reform, Egypt's wars and river, Rome's legions and founding legend. Every person, place and event in this story is original. The two walkthrough videos and the map site were used only to learn how a quest map is structured; nothing from that game's world, names or quests is used here.
