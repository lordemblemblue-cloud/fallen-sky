## 3. The places

Each place is one adventure spot: something visible from a distance, a reason to go there, and one to four quests that use it. "New" means it has to be built; "Here" means the land or landmark is already in the game and gets dressed.

Every place follows the same few rules, taken from the land study (`research/REFERENCE_LAND_NOTES.md`):

- **It sits on a change of height**: on a knoll, in a hollow, up a stair, behind a fold in the ground. Nothing is placed on flat open grass.
- **It can be read from far off**: smoke, a banner, a tower top or a carved front seen from about 300 m; its whole outline at 100 m; in the last 50 m, things on the ground that say who lives there.
- **A pair of fire baskets always means "the way in is here"**, and is used for nothing else.
- **Each area has one big shape that works as its compass**: the great cedar in the west, the obelisk and quarry cliff in the east, the fort towers and aqueduct arches in the north, the mill in the south, the Oath Tree and fountain in the town.
- **Each kind of place looks the same wherever it is**: a camp is a fire and tents, an old battlefield is standing banners, a shrine is a pair of pale stones.
- **Each empire's places are built from one small kit of about twelve pieces** (tent, palisade, gate, banner pole, fire basket, crate stack and so on), so that a whole area's camps and forts draw as a handful of batches.

## 4. The main story (12)

Given by the valley's people. The same for every empire, so rivals walk it side by side.
Each entry: level, who asks, where, the steps, how it is staged, and the kind of reward (the server decides every reward).

**M01 · Sworn at the Gate** — level 1 · Lyra · Oath Tree, then the Southern Wolf Gate
1. Speak with Lyra at the Oath Tree, two steps from where you first stand. Tie your empire's ribbon to the tree: your first pledge, the Fountain Truce.
2. The gate bell rings. Run down the main road to the Southern Wolf Gate.
3. Ink-strays are among the refugee wagons. Clear them and free three trapped travellers, one from each empire.
4. Walk them to Lyra at the gate arch.
*Staging:* the run down the main road passes all three lodges with their banners out, so the player sees the three empires before anyone explains them. Each traveller thanks you in their own way; one of the two foreigners gives you a keepsake you will meet again in chapter 6.
*Reward:* experience, Trust, the keepsake, the pledge boards open.

**M02 · Three Arrows** — level 2 · Sable Vey · the fountain
1. Before the parley, hear at least two of the three envoys state their case.
2. Stand in the ring while Elder Maren speaks. Three arrows.
3. Chase the archer along the ink trail over the roofs and lanes before it fades.
4. The archer bursts into ink. Pick up what is left: three arrows, three kinds of fletching.
5. Bring them to Sable Vey and watch the three envoys leave by three roads at once.
*Staging:* the only death in the story that happens on Truce ground, in front of everyone. The chase is a race, with no fighting.
*Reward:* experience, Trust, the title Truce-Sworn. Your own empire's story and the war pledges open.

**M03 · The First Stroke** — levels 6 to 10 · Kira the Scout, then Master Hesper · Jade Lantern Grotto
1. Kira sends you to the grotto, where Hesper is renewing the first mural. Captain Arlen of the Jade Watch is at the mouth.
2. Go down through the Ink Hares to the painted chamber.
3. Defeat the Painted Stag at the empty socket.
4. Take what was left in the socket.
5. Speak with Hesper under the mural.
*Staging:* Hesper never stops painting while he talks. The mural is lit by the player's own fight: every hare that falls puts a stroke of light back on the wall.
*Reward:* experience, a skill point, Trust.

**M04 · What the Wolf Stones Remember** — levels 8 to 10 · Noor the Wanderer · the Wolf Stones
1. Carry the token to the stones.
2. Touch it to the tallest stone. The world turns to ink wash and an old battle begins around you.
3. Hold with the ghost soldier through three waves, one from each old army.
4. He plants his standard, speaks, and walks to the cairn.
5. Return to Noor.
*Staging:* the ghost wears your own empire's colours, four hundred years out of date. The enemies already living at the stones today are the echoes of this fight.
*Reward:* experience, Trust, a keepsake (the Thrown Standard). The valley stories and discoveries open.

**M05 · Root Fire** — levels 12 to 16 · your empire's commander, with Vex watching for the Watch · Whispering Root Hollow
1. Muster under your camp's standard.
2. Reach the cave mouth. All three columns arrive together; players who have raised their banner fight for the mouth.
3. Inside, cut through the Root Embers to the Root Pyre and defeat it.
4. Draw the second Tooth from the mural.
5. Carry it out to your commander.
*Staging:* the first time the three empires are in one place under arms. When the Tooth leaves the wall the murals go dark behind you all the way to the surface.
*Reward:* experience, a skill point, Renown, a gear roll. Stronger ink beasts wake for you from here on.

**M06 · The Drowned Ledger** — levels 18 to 24 · Lyra · Sunken Inkstone Cistern
1. Climb past the Wolf-Ear peaks to the cistern mouth.
2. Go down through the Ink Koi to the flooded archive.
3. Read three pages of the ledger: three older wars. The third names your own empire's column.
4. Defeat the Drowned Ink.
5. In the water, a rival champion and you save each other. Take the third Tooth to your commander.
*Staging:* the rival who pulls you out belongs to the empire whose keepsake you have carried since the gate.
*Reward:* experience, a skill point, Trust, the ledger pages as a keepsake.

**M07 · The Burning Fields** — level 20 and up · your empire's quartermaster and Sable Vey · the Gate Fields
1. Reach your empire's wagons on the Threshing Floor.
2. Bring in five sheaves, or guard those who do. Rival players are doing the same.
3. The fields are fired. Cut down the saboteurs, who wear all three uniforms.
4. The barn is burning with the farm families inside and so are your wagons. Ninety seconds: pull the wagons clear, or break the barn doors and lead the families out.
5. Stand in the ash and hear what each side has to say to you.
*Staging:* the fire reaches wagons and barn at the same moment, in sight of each other. Whatever is not saved burns in front of the player.
*Reward:* experience, and Renown or Trust depending on the choice. Saving the families is remembered by the Wolf.

**M08 · Four Hundred Lamps** — levels 28 to 34 · your commander · Echoing Crystal Vault
1. Join your column at the vault mouth, where all three empires are fighting to get in.
2. Go down among the Glass Moths, whose light is the only light. Each one killed makes the vault darker.
3. Stand with your commander at the three-way standoff.
4. The Last Stroke wakes and the roof comes down.
5. Sealed in with the two rival champions: get out through the dark with one lamp between three. Whoever carries it cannot fight.
6. At the surface, the Tooth is gone.
*Staging:* three enemies taking turns to carry one lamp. Each tells why they are here while it is their turn to be defenceless.
*Reward:* experience, a skill point, a gear roll, the title Lamp-Bearer.

**M09 · The Dusk Painter** — level 34 · Lyra · the Wolf Sanctuary
1. Search Hesper's workroom: the drawings of the finished wolf, the child's drawing, the stolen quivers, the planted tokens.
2. Hesper comes in, with Zephyr. He tells you everything.
3. He walks out toward the mountain. This is Truce ground, and you swore.
4. Report to Sable Vey. The Truce Bell is brought out.
*Staging:* the first pledge of the game is the reason you cannot stop him. A player who strikes anyway finds Lyra's bow turned on them, and loses Trust; nothing else changes.
*Reward:* experience, Trust.

**M10 · The World Beneath the World** — level 35 · Noor · Vortex Lake
1. Reach the lake and get past its dragon, by fighting or by patience.
2. Go down through the whirl.
3. Walk five halls, five memories: the Fall, the first painters, the first war, the Hungry Winter, tonight.
4. In the last hall the Wolf speaks, in three voices at once.
5. Come back up through the water.
*Staging:* the fourth hall is Hesper as a boy. Nothing attacks the player there.
*Reward:* experience, a skill point, the title Who Went Down.

**M11 · One Night Under One Banner** — levels 36 to 40 · Sable Vey and all three commanders · Howling Plateau
1. Carry the Bell's summons to your commander. What they answer depends on how your own empire's story ended.
2. Muster on the plateau, three armies in one line.
3. Hold through three waves of the painted host. Fighting between players is suspended on the plateau for this night.
4. The rival champions fight beside you.
5. The host breaks, back toward Root Hollow. Follow it.
*Staging:* a shared event for everyone in the area, whatever their empire: the one place the three sets of players stand in a single line.
*Reward:* experience, Renown and Trust together, war tokens.

**M12 · The Last Stroke** — level 42 · Lyra and the Watch · the Wolf Rotunda
1. Go down the long painted hall from Root Hollow to the Rotunda.
2. Zephyr is at the door. What he does depends on what you have done.
3. The Wolf, half signed: three faces, three fights, one in each empire's way of fighting.
4. Hesper holds out the brush. Finish it for your people, or break it.
5. Walk up into the town at dawn.
*Staging:* the brush is offered with your empire's need spoken aloud, in the voice of the person from your own story you cared about most.
*Reward:* experience, skill points, the title Wolf's Witness, a mark of light on the character. The ongoing war for stewardship opens.

### How each chapter hands you to the next

No chapter ends in a menu. The last thing in each one is a person, an object or a sight that names the next place, and each place is shown before the player is sent there.

| After | What sends you on |
| --- | --- |
| M01 | Lyra: the parley is at the fountain at noon, and the sworn have a place in the ring. |
| M02 | The three arrows on Sable Vey's table are wet with ink. Kira: the only ink like that is in the Jade Lantern Grotto. |
| M03 | The token from the socket. Hesper: the Wolf Stones remember hands. |
| M04 | The ghost points his standard at a red glow on the far ridge: Root Hollow, already burning. |
| M05 | As the Tooth leaves the wall, the next mural lights: a koi under water. Lyra is waiting at the mouth. |

| M07 | A glass moth lands on a burnt post, far from its cave. Your commander's horn sounds. |
| M08 | An ink handprint on the empty socket. Only one man in the valley has ink to the elbows. |
| M09 | The child's drawing shows a wolf under the lake. Noor: the lake remembers. |
| M10 | You surface to the Truce Bell ringing across the valley and smoke over the plateau. |
| M11 | The host breaks and runs back down into Root Hollow. |

**Places that come back changed.** The fountain square: calm, then the murder, then empty of envoys, then the dawn walk. The Gate Fields: green, burning, ash, then stubble with graves. The Wolf Stones: quiet, the echo of the old war, Seven Swords, a shrine in someone's colours. The quarry: a pit, then an obelisk on the skyline. The plateau: a furrow, a battlefield, one line of three armies. The same land with new light and new people costs far less than new land, and it is what makes a place feel lived in.

**A person in every gate.** Each of the eleven gates between areas gets a warden of the Fountain Watch. Going into the town they tie your weapon with a cord; going out they cut it. The gates are loading points already; this turns each one into the Truce being kept in front of you.

## 5. Akitsu: The Iron Road (8) — west

**A01 · A Farmer's Son in a Blue Coat** — level 3 · Sergeant Gorō · Camp of the Western Road
Drill three recruits at the straw posts. That night ink-strays come over the picket: hold it, and choose which wagon to stand by, the powder that will not burn or the rice. Look for a quiver missing from the armoury.
*Reward:* experience, Renown.

**A02 · The Sword Mistress's Price** — level 6 · Lady Asagiri · her Hall, then the Wolf Stones
Her grandson carried the house banner to the stones and did not come back. Take the banner and his two swords from the echo that holds them, carry them home on foot, and pour the water at his grave.
*Staging:* she does not thank you. She corrects your grip.
*Reward:* experience, Renown, a sword lesson.

**A03 · Survey Stakes** — level 10 · Lieutenant Mizuhara · Railhead, Wintering Shrine, the Commons
Plant three survey stakes; each takes a few seconds in the open. At the shrine Kohaku shows you which trees the line runs through.
*Rivals:* Remoria pulls stakes (R03); Tameri copies the survey (T03).
*Reward:* experience, Renown, the railway drawn on your map.

**A04 · Moon-Ring** — level 14 · Kohaku · Wintering Shrine, then the Bear's Den
Follow three claw-marked cedars to a mother bear driven mad by a splinter of star-iron. The Colonel wants her dead. When she is nearly beaten, lower your weapon while Kohaku sings to her, or finish it.
*Reward:* experience; Trust, or Renown. Sparing her is remembered by the Wolf.

**A05 · The Grey Ships' Letter** — level 18 · Colonel Hayasaka · the Singing Seam to the Cedar Road
A telegram from home: sixty days. Take two ore wagons from the mine to the Cedar Road.
*Rivals:* Tameri raids the wagons (T05).
*Reward:* experience, Renown, war tokens.

**A06 · Seven Swords at the Stones** — level 24 · Lady Asagiri · the Wolf Stones
House Asagiri holds the frontier for three minutes against a Remorian cohort. Seven swordsmen, each with a name. Every one still standing at the end speaks to you.
*Rivals:* Remoria is ordered to take the stones (R06).
*Reward:* experience, Renown, the title Eighth Sword if five or more live.

**A07 · The Cedar Order** — level 30 · the Colonel, Lady Asagiri, Kohaku · the Wintering Shrine
The order comes to fell the shrine cedars for the trestle. **Mark seven trees, or stand in front of them.**
*Reward:* Renown, or Trust. Decides chapter 8.

**A08 · First Train Through the Mountain** *or* **The Last Stand of House Asagiri** — level 34
The railway: guard the first engine along its first two hundred metres through an ambush. The grove: hold the shrine against the detachment sent to arrest Lady Asagiri, without killing them, until the Colonel comes alone.
*Reward:* a title and a coat to match the choice; Renown or Trust.

## 6. Tameri: Forty-One Days (8) — east

**T01 · The Count** — level 3 · Paneb the Scribe · the Landing
Carry four grain jars from the barge to the stack while river-jaws come up the bank. Then Paneb shows you the tally: forty-one. Look for a quiver missing from the armoury.
*Reward:* experience, Renown, a tally shard that always shows the count.

**T02 · Opening of the Mouth** — level 6 · Priestess Meresankh · Scarred South, then Shadow Ravine
Take back the sky-iron blade from the jackal shade that stole it. In the ravine, free five of Tameri's unrested dead: defeat each, read the name on its tag, perform the rite. Guard the Priestess through the last chant.
*Staging:* each name is spoken aloud. A sixth body in the ravine wears another empire's colours; the player may free it too.
*Reward:* experience, Renown. The sixth is remembered by the Wolf.

**T03 · Eyes on the Iron Road** — level 10 · Sabu · the west
Cross the town with your weapon tied, then watch three of Akitsu's survey points long enough to draw them.
*Rivals:* Akitsu plants the stakes (A03); Remoria pulls them (R03).
*Reward:* experience, Renown.

**T04 · The Quarry of the Ancestors** — level 14 · Meresankh and Hori the master mason · the Quarry
Clear the stone scorpions from the cutting, read the old writing on the fallen obelisk, and guard the crew through three heaves on the ropes while it rises.
*Rivals:* Remoria is cutting stone on the far side of the pit (R04).
*Staging:* the obelisk rises in three stages in front of the player. If no blood is spilt between the two crews, the masons share their bread.
*Reward:* experience, Renown, Trust if peace held.

**T05 · The Iron That Should Be Bread** — level 18 · General Nakhtamun · Akitsu's ore road
Raid an ore wagon in the west and carry a crate of splinters to Sabu's cache by the Commons. The Priestess is waiting when you come back.
*Rivals:* Akitsu escorts the wagons (A05).
*Reward:* experience, Renown, war tokens.

**T06 · The Night Barge** — level 24 · Paneb · the Landing to the River Road
Ride the grain barge down the river by night, fighting from its deck, and hold it for a minute at the Pontoon Bridge while the crew drops the mast. If it arrives, the count goes up nine days.
*Rivals:* the other empires can take a war pledge to sink it.
*Reward:* experience, Renown, nine days.

## 10. War pledges (8 kinds) — how quests turn into fights between players

Sworn at your empire's lodge or camp. Swearing one raises your banner until it is kept or broken, which under the game's existing rule means other bannered players can fight you. For each pledge an empire can swear, the other two can swear its opposite at the same place and hour.

| # | Pledge | Your side | The other sides | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W01 | Wagons | Escort your empire's convoy | Raid it, or stop it for toll | Akitsu's ore road (west), Tameri's grain barge (east), Remoria's stone carts (east to north) |
| W02 | Hold the Shrine | Stand at a shrine, uncontested, until it takes your colours | The same | Wolf Stones, Hidden Waterfall, Field of Standards' three outliers |
| W03 | Signal Fires | Light your beacon | Put it out, light your own | Cedar Crown, Highfall Crown, Wolf-Ear |

| W06 | Harvest | Reap sheaves and get them to your wagon | Reap, or rob the reapers | The Threshing Floor |
| W07 | Rites for the Fallen | After a battle, give the fallen their rites | Interrupt, or do the same for your own | Wherever players have just fought |
| W08 | Writ of the Wolf | Hunt a player the Watch has named for a killing spree | Be the one named, and survive | Anywhere outside the town |

**Rules of war.** No fighting in the town. Only bannered players can be attacked. A player performing rites can be attacked, but whoever does it carries a mark of shame for a time. Kills, captures and rewards are decided by the server, never by the player's own browser.

**What this depends on.** In the published game today, open fighting between players in the wild areas is designed but not switched on: the switch waits for a reply from a world server that is not running. Duels between two players do work, and the server decides them. So war pledges can be built in two stages: first on duels (two players sworn to opposite pledges at the same place are thrown into a duel there, and the winner's side advances), then on open fighting once the world server is running. Today's rule also has no notion of sides: two players of the same empire can fight if both chose to. War pledges need "same empire cannot fight".

## 13. What the quest system has to learn

For the builders. Today's system knows "kill N" and "find a place", gives quests from five archers, spawns quest enemies in a ring around the player, and lets the browser grant experience. This plan needs:

- **Steps in order**, each with its own place and its own line of dialogue.
- **Step kinds**: talk; go to; defeat (a named kind, at a place); defeat a boss; pick up; carry (on foot, dropped if hit); deliver; use (hold to perform, interruptible); escort (a person, a wagon, a barge along a path); defend (a place or a person for a time); hold a zone; choose; spare (win without killing).
- **Enemies at a place**, not around the player, and kinds other than the one model per area the game has now.
- **Quest givers anywhere**, with a model, a voice and a marker, per area scene.
- **Empire-only quests and empire-specific lines** in shared quests (the hooks exist: quests can already name a faction and require story flags).
- **Choices that are remembered**: story flags, and the three standings (Renown already exists as contribution; Trust can use the existing reputation store; the Wolf's Regard is new).
- **War pledges**: a shared objective with opposing sides, a time window, and the banner raised while sworn.
- **The server decides** acceptance, progress that matters, completion and every reward. The game has a private server path for this that the published game does not call yet; until it does, new quests can be played but must grant nothing of value from the browser.

**Room on screen.** The game's own budget for one area in a browser is 500 draw calls and 1.5 million triangles, of which scenery may use 350 and 1.0 million. Measured before this work, the worst single view already uses 207 to 280 draw calls and 0.77 to 1.24 million triangles; the west is over its triangle budget and the east is at it. Grass is 55 to 70 percent of everything drawn. So:

- Each new place gets at most about 10 draw calls and 15,000 triangles in view, and a low-poly version at about a third of that.
- All of one empire's kit pieces in an area draw as instanced batches: about twelve draw calls for every camp and fort in the area together.
- The west and the east have to give back triangles (thinner grass inside camps, on roads and on rock) before they take new ones.
- At most 30 animated enemies exist at once today; each new kind of monster is measured against that before more are added.
- Interiors (caves, the barrow, the shaft tomb) are separate scenes, so the open areas never draw them.
- Every place is measured in the browser, before and after, on WebGPU.

## 15. Gliding: land shaped for jumping off

Added 2026-11-22. The owner asked for the ability to "glide and fly", to "jump off hills", with combat unchanged and the glide under the player's control. Another session (the one that owns the player's movement) is building the ability. This plan's part is the land: heights worth jumping from, landings worth reaching, and a few places that are easier or only reachable from the air.

**Rules until the glide's reach is known.** No distance below is fixed: how far a glide carries for each metre of height comes from the session building it, and every launch and landing is measured against that number before it is built. No glide route crosses a border between areas, because the far side of a border is not loaded. A player who is carrying something for a pledge, or walking someone home, cannot glide. Seen from the air a place is read by its outline, so each landing has one thing that shows from above: a fire, a banner, a ring of stones, pale ground.

**Measured, 2026-11-22.** The glide is built (branch ). It carries about 4.2 m forward for each metre of height (read  in ), about 2.6 m when diving. Ground more than 2 m above the glider is a wall: a glide never lifts the hero onto a higher shelf, and it ends on ground at or below the height it arrives at. An area's border is a wall away from a gate. One consequence the table below had not counted on: the glider sinks at about 13 degrees, so over ground that falls more gently than that it lands again within a few metres. A glide carries off an edge (a cliff, a gorge rim, a ridge's side), not down a road or an even slope. **Every row of the table below has to be walked again with that in mind before it is built**: the heights are right, but a launch counts only where the land drops away faster than 1 in 4.2, and the ridge path from the Vigil to the hot springs falls at 7 to 12 degrees, which is too gentle.

**Where the land already wants it** (heights from the game's own ground):

The Gate Fields are flat: they are where glides end, never where they begin. That suits them as the open battlefield.

**In the quests.**
- The Kite Line (V07): the kite hangs on a ledge that can only be reached by gliding from the Vigil; the way home is the glide down.
- Wolf-Ear Vigil (V13): at dawn the player leaps from the peak and sees the three camps from the air.
- The World Beneath the World (M10): the way down into the lake is a leap.
- The Rookery (X03) and the Harpy Roost (X07): reached from above.
- Signal Fires (W03): the beacons stand on the three crowns, so whoever lights one leaves by air, and whoever comes to put it out may arrive that way.

**For the builders.** A player in the air sees much further than one on the ground. The rule "nothing is drawn beyond 150 m unless it is a landmark" will show as things appearing below the glider, so each area's landmarks and each landing's marker have to be in the drawn-from-anywhere set, and frame time is measured from the air as well as from the ground.
