## Sworn at the Gate

Main story, chapter 1. Asked by Lyra the Seeker.

- **Sworn to:** Lyra of the Fountain Watch, at the Oath Tree
- **Why it is asked:** Nobody walks under the Veil unsworn, and the people at the gate have no one else.
- **What keeping it costs:** A sworn hand may never draw blood within sight of the fountain, whatever is done in front of it.

**When it is offered**

- **Lyra the Seeker:** Stop there. Nobody walks under the Veil unsworn. I'm Lyra. I keep the count, and you are number four hundred and twelve this month. The month is young.
- **Lyra the Seeker:** Three armies drink from this fountain. The only reason the water isn't red is one law, older than the town: no blood within sight of the fountain.
- **Lyra the Seeker:** You swear it the way everyone has. Take a ribbon in your people's colour and tie it to the tree. Your hands, your knot. Then it's yours to keep or to break.

**Step 1. Tie your ribbon to the Oath Tree.** *(Oath Tree and Truce Stone)*

- **Lyra the Seeker:** Good. Remember how that felt. One day it will be the only thing standing between you and something you badly want to do.
- *[gate bell]*
- **Lyra the Seeker:** That's the gate bell. Twice means trouble on the road. You're sworn now, so it's your trouble too. Go!

**Step 2. Run to the Southern Wolf Gate.** *(Southern Wolf Gate and wagon yard)*

- **Gate Warden:** Ink on the wagons! It came up out of the ditch. There are people under there, three at least, and I can't leave the arch!

**Step 3. Drive the ink shades off the wagons.** *(Southern Wolf Gate and wagon yard)*

- **Gate Warden:** That's the last of them. Get those people out before the axles give.

**Step 4. Free the three travellers pinned under the wagons.** *(Southern Wolf Gate and wagon yard)*

- **Tome:** My leg! No. No, it's only the rice. Forty years I've carried it, and today it sat on me. Thank you, soldier.
- **Henut:** The jar isn't broken. Thank the Sharp One. And thank you. I didn't think anyone would stop.
- **Livilla:** Don't fuss. I've been under worse than a wagon. My husband marched twenty years for a field he never saw. I've come to see it for him.

**Step 5. Bring them up the road to the Oath Tree.** *(Oath Tree and Truce Stone)*

- **Tome:** They told us in the hills that powder won't burn here. My grandson laughed. Then his rifle clicked like a cricket, and he stopped laughing.
- **Henut:** At home the river has not risen in three years. I weave, but nobody buys cloth when there's no bread. So I came to where the grain is.
- **Livilla:** See those three banners? Each one is sure this valley was promised to it. I buried a man who was sure of things.
- **Tome:** Is it true about the fountain? A blue coat and a red cloak can stand side by side, and nothing happens?
- **Henut:** It's true. I asked the warden. He said the law is older than his grandmother's grandmother.

**Step 6. Speak with Lyra.**

- **Henut:** Here. It's only a bead, river blue, from my mother's loom. Where I come from, you give something to the one who opened a door for you. *(to a player of Akitsu)*
- **Livilla:** Take this. A pebble from the edge of the field he was promised. I carried two. I only need one. *(to a player of Tameri)*
- **Tome:** For you. A paper charm from the shrine in my village. The bear on it keeps travellers. It kept me today, so now it's yours. *(to a player of Remoria)*

**When it is finished**

- **Lyra the Seeker:** Three more for the count, and none of them yours. You pulled strangers out from under a wagon an hour after you got here.
- **Lyra the Seeker:** You kept the Truce before you understood it. That's the only way anyone ever has.
- **Lyra the Seeker:** Sable Vey holds the parley at the fountain at noon. The sworn have a place in the ring. Stand in it. You should see what you just swore to.

## Three Arrows

Main story, chapter 2. Asked by Sable Vey.

- **Sworn to:** Sable Vey, Keeper of the Truce Stone
- **Why it is asked:** Someone has to stand in the ring who has heard all three sides.
- **What keeping it costs:** To stand in the ring you tie your weapon, whatever happens in front of you.

**When it is offered**

- **Sable Vey:** So you're Lyra's four hundred and twelfth. I'm Sable Vey. I keep the stone the Truce is cut into, which mostly means keeping three armies from reading it too closely.
- **Sable Vey:** The envoys meet at noon, as they have every week for a year. Before you stand in the ring, hear them. All three. Anyone can love their own side. I need the sworn to understand the other two.

**Step 1. Hear the three envoys at their lodges.**

- **Lieutenant Mizuhara Jun:** Mizuhara, Lieutenant of Engineers. Ask me what Akitsu wants and I'll show you a drawing. One railway, mine to mountain. Iron for a country that will be carved up without it.
- **Lieutenant Mizuhara Jun:** We pay for every tree. I counted them myself. I am told that is not the same as asking.
- **Paneb the Scribe:** Paneb, scribe. I'll save you the speech. There are forty-one days of grain in the Beloved Land. I count them every morning. This morning there were forty-one.
- **Paneb the Scribe:** Tameri asks for the harvest of the Gate Fields at a fair price, and for the river to be left as it runs. That is all. It is everything.
- **Tribune Aelia Sabina:** Aelia Sabina, tribune. Thirty thousand soldiers were promised land for ten years of war. Remoria keeps its promises or it is nothing. The Ninth is here to mark out farms.
- **Tribune Aelia Sabina:** Yes, I know there are people on that land. I have said so in the tent. I will say so again today.

**Step 2. Take your place in the ring at the fountain.** *(Oath Tree and Truce Stone)*

- **Sable Vey:** The ring is closed. Weapons tied. Elder Maren speaks for the valley.
- **Elder Maren:** A year. Fifty-two noons. I have heard each of you say what your people cannot live without. And do you know, I believed every one of you.
- **Elder Maren:** So here is what the valley asks back. Not land. Not iron. Not water. Only that when you take what you need, you look at who is standing...
- *[three arrows]*
- **Sable Vey:** Maren! Nobody moves. NOBODY draws! You, sworn one: it went west, along the lane. Go!

**Step 3. Follow the ink trail before it dries.**

- **Lyra the Seeker:** It's leaving ink! Follow it before it dries!
- *[ink burst]*

**Step 4. Pick up what the archer left.**

- **Lyra the Seeker:** Gone. It was never a person. Look at the fletching, though. Crane feather. Goose. Reed. One from every quiver in this valley.

**Step 5. Bring the arrows to Sable Vey.**

- **Sable Vey:** Three arrows. Three peoples. One old woman who trusted all of them. Whoever did this wanted every one of us to be guilty.
- **Tribune Aelia Sabina:** The Ninth did not do this. But I can't prove it, and by tonight nobody will want me to.
- **Paneb the Scribe:** Forty-one days. And now a war.
- **Lieutenant Mizuhara Jun:** I drew her portrait last week. She asked me to make her look taller.
- **Sable Vey:** They're going back to their camps, and tomorrow they'll send soldiers where they sent envoys. The Truce is dead past the bridges. I am asking you whether it is dead in here.

**Step 6. Answer Sable Vey.**

- *Sable Vey holds out the Truce Stone.*
- Choice: "Swear it again, on the stone."
  - **Sable Vey:** Then there are two of us. That's twice what I had this morning.
- Choice: "Keep your first oath, and say nothing more."
  - **Sable Vey:** Fair. One oath honestly kept is worth more than two given in a hurry.

**When it is finished**

- **Sable Vey:** Your own people will call you to their camp before dark. Go when they do. And when Kira comes looking for you about the ink, listen to her.

## The Lantern Count

Valley story. Asked by Bram.

- **Sworn to:** Bram the lamplighter, at the Lantern Wall
- **Why it is asked:** So that everyone who died today is counted the same, whatever they wore.
- **What keeping it costs:** You ask three armies for a favour on the day they went to war.

**When it is offered**

- **Bram:** Every dusk since I was a boy, I've lit one lantern for each soul the valley lost that day. It used to be a slow job. I ran out of oil on Thursday.
- **Bram:** The only oil left is with the armies' wagons, down on the Gate Fields. They won't give it to an old man. They might give it to one of their own, if you told them what it's for.

**Step 1. Ask for lamp oil at each of the three wagon lines on the Threshing Floor.** *(Threshing Floor)*

- **Sergeant Gorō:** Lamp oil? For the old lamplighter? He lights one for ours too? Huh. Take the good jar, then. Not the kitchen one.
- **Paneb the Scribe:** Oil is counted like grain. One lantern for each, whoever they were? Then I'll write it under rites. Nobody questions that column.
- **First Centurion Titus Petronius:** Twenty-five years, and nobody ever lit a thing for a dead legionary but his own tent. Take it. Tell him the Ninth lost a boy called Decius on Tuesday.

**Step 2. Bring the oil to Bram at the Lantern Wall.**

- **Bram:** All three? All three gave. Well. Come on, then. You carry the taper. My hands shake.

**Step 3. Light five lanterns with him.** *(Lantern Wall)*

- **Bram:** Maren, of the valley.
- **Bram:** A boy of the Ninth. Decius, was it? Decius.
- **Bram:** Two of Akitsu. Brothers, they said. One lantern each. They'd have argued over sharing.
- **Bram:** A Tameri carter. Nobody gave me a name, so I say 'the carter', and I mean him.
- **Bram:** And one for whoever loosed those arrows. They're somebody's, too.

**When it is finished**

- **Bram:** That's today. If you're passing at dusk tomorrow, I could use the hands.

## The Gravedigger's Three Fields

Valley story. Asked by Dalla.

- **Sworn to:** Dalla, gravedigger of the Three Fields
- **Why it is asked:** So that every people here is buried with the care its own would give.
- **What keeping it costs:** You stand guard over the dead of the armies that are trying to kill you.

**When it is offered**

- **Dalla:** You're standing on Tameri. Two steps left is Akitsu. Behind you, Remoria. I keep them apart because their gods asked for different things. The worms don't care.
- **Dalla:** Akitsu burns theirs, and I need cedar. Tameri wraps theirs in salt, and I'm out. Remoria wants a coin on each eye for the ferry, and I've been using buttons. Fetch what I'm short of, and I'll show you how each is done.

**Step 1. Fetch two bundles of cedar from the farmsteads' woodpile.**

**Step 2. Fetch a sack of salt from the granary.**

**Step 3. Ask Old Vine at the Remorian wagons for the ferryman's coins.**

- **First Centurion Titus Petronius:** Coins for the ferryman? Who for? Ours and theirs? She buries theirs? Hm. Here. Proper ones, with the wolf on. Don't tell the paymaster.

**Step 4. Bring it all to Dalla.**

- **Dalla:** They come when I dig. The ink wants the dead before the ground has them. Keep it off me. I work fast.

**Step 5. Keep the ink off Dalla while she buries the day's dead.** *(The Three Fields)*

- **Dalla:** Done. Fire for the hills, salt for the river, a coin for the road. Now. There's one more. No coat, no tags, nothing in the pockets. Somebody's. I don't know whose.

**Step 6. Decide how the last one is buried.**

- *Dalla holds out the spade.*
- Choice: "Bury them the way your own people would."
  - **Dalla:** Then they'll have that, and someone who meant it. That's more than most get this year.
- Choice: "Cedar, salt and a coin. All three."
  - **Dalla:** All three. Hah. That's how my mother did the ones nobody claimed. She said, let the gods sort out whose turn it is.
- Choice: "Ask Dalla how the valley buries its own."
  - **Dalla:** The valley's way? We plant a tree on them. In thirty years somebody sits in the shade and doesn't know why it's a good spot.
